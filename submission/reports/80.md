# Reading Gender Off the Page: Shoujo Manga, Gendered Grammar, and the Linguistic Self-Expression of Queer Japanese Young Adults

**Central claim.** Consuming shoujo manga measurably shifts how queer Japanese young adults deploy gendered pronouns and sentence-ending particles — but the effect is conditional, parasocially mediated, and far smaller than the popular "media-makes-you-talk-like-this" intuition assumes. The single corpus study most directly on point, Giancarla Unser-Schutz's 2015 ten-manga analysis in *Gender & Language*, found that shoujo *pronoun* usage actually **aligns** with normative youth speech rather than diverging from it, and could not settle whether manga drives speech or merely reflects readers who already speak that way.[Unser-Schutz, "Influential or influenced?," *Gender & Language* 92:223–254](https://www.academia.edu/7094300/)

**The role-language paradox.** Despite the common claim that fictional "role language" (*yakuwarigo*) leaks directly into everyday grammar, the role-language literature insists stereotyped manga dialogue does **not** transfer wholesale to spoken usage["Yakuwarigo," Wikipedia](https://en-wp.org/wiki/Yakuwarigo) — making any observed adoption a marker of *selective, identity-motivated uptake* rather than passive imitation. Against the backdrop of a Reiwa-era convergence in which younger Japanese women are already abandoning feminine particles for neutral and masculine forms,["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) this report builds a falsifiable causal framework, an operationalized methodology, and a meta-causal verdict for a population whose minority salience makes them an unusually sensitive instrument for detecting media effects. The analysis runs **through 2030**, the horizon past which manga's declining youth readership may invalidate the premise entirely.["Are Japanese Teens Leaving Manga Behind?" Unseen Japan/Medium](https://unseenjapan.medium.com/are-japanese-teens-leaving-manga-behind-a8e7019a001e)

## 1. Research Question and Framework

### 1.1 The Question

Does a **measurable, directional causal influence** run from shoujo manga consumption to the personal gendered grammar of queer Japanese young adults — specifically their choice of first-person pronouns and sentence-final particles (SFPs)?

The scope is deliberately narrow: one minority readership of one nation, two grammatical categories, and a single hypothesized direction of effect. Crucially, the report treats **reflection** (readers already speak this way, then select congruent manga) and **influence** (manga shapes speech) as rival hypotheses requiring active discrimination, not as a settled background assumption.

### 1.2 Why This Case Is Analytically Valuable

Japanese gendered grammar offers a rare natural experiment. The language encodes speaker gender in **surface features — pronouns and SFPs — that appear in the same form in everyday speech and on the manga page**, making written exposure and spoken production formally commensurable in a way prosody or accent could not. Three conditions converge:

- a **stylized stimulus** (shoujo manga, which deploys these features in patterns diverging from majority usage),
- a **divergent attested baseline** (real-world youth speech, documented below), and
- a **readership for whom grammatical gender is a live choice** rather than an automatic default.

### 1.3 Scope Boundaries

| Boundary | Setting | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Population** | Queer Japanese young adults, ~18–30 | Lower bound (18) avoids the elevated consent obligations of minors research with gender/sexually diverse persons.["Research Ethics with Gender and Sexually Diverse Persons," PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7559910/) Upper bound (~30) isolates speakers past primary socialization but within the cohort of most intense manga attachment.["Attachment to Manga Japanese Comics," Journal of Anime and Manga Studies](https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/jams/article/download/1003/928/4153) |
| **Outcome features** | First-person pronouns + SFPs only | Both appear in identical surface form in manga and speech. Honorific *keigo* is excluded: it indexes formality, not gender-persona. |
| **Stimulus** | Print shoujo manga | Anime, games, BL, and yuri enter only as confounders to control. |

"Queer" is used as an umbrella (lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, nonbinary, questioning); its concealed heterogeneity is itself an object of study. **Non-Japanese readers are excluded**: cross-cultural reception studies show they engage manga through identity-thematic recognition rather than native grammatical patterning,[Lewington, A.J.L., "Otherness and Identity in Shonen Manga," White Rose eTheses](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/28308/) so a non-Japanese sample would measure thematic identification, not grammatical uptake.

### 1.4 The Causal Hypothesis

> **Sustained shoujo manga consumption exerts a directional, reception-driven causal influence on the first-person pronouns and SFPs that queer Japanese young adults use in their own self-expression, shifting them toward patterns modeled on the genre's page rather than on majority real-world usage.**

The hypothesis is built as a **multi-link chain**, not a single leap: sustained consumption → strong reader attachment["Attachment to Manga Japanese Comics," Journal of Anime and Manga Studies](https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/jams/article/download/1003/928/4153) → intensified engagement with page dialogue → repeated modeling of gender-indexing forms → uptake in personal production. Each link is a separate empirical commitment.

### 1.5 Standard of Evidence

The report scores its own claims against five weighted criteria, published in advance:

| ID | Criterion | Weight |
|----|-------|--------|
| R-1 | Causal-Chain Completeness | 0.30 |
| R-2 | Fiction-Reality Discrimination | 0.25 |
| R-3 | Confounder Control | 0.20 |
| R-4 | Queer Heterogeneity & Intersectionality | 0.15 |
| R-5 | Conclusion Modesty & Falsifiability | 0.10 |

The heaviest weight (R-1) reflects the report's characteristic failure mode: asserting influence while skipping the intervening links. **In a domain this confounded, an unexplained correlation is nearly worthless and a fully traced mechanism is the only durable contribution.**

The report adapts the **Bradford-Hill criteria** from epidemiology, treating **temporality** (exposure must precede the shift) and **dose-response gradient** (heavier consumption → larger shift) as the two criteria a mere-correlation account cannot satisfy. The attachment literature supplies a natural dosing variable, distinguishing avid from moderate and occasional readers.["Attachment to Manga Japanese Comics," Journal of Anime and Manga Studies](https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/jams/article/download/1003/928/4153) Throughout, the report grades claims on a maturity ladder — **plausible influence** ranks below **proven causation** — and commits to landing on the former unless the latter is earned.

### 1.6 Key Vocabulary

- **Joseigo / danseigo** — ideologized "women's language" / "men's language," understood as *cultural norms against which attested usage is measured*, not descriptions of how people speak. The corpus gap is vivid: the feminine-ideologized particle *wa* is used **more** by male than female youth speakers.["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf)
- **Shuujoshi** — sentence-final particles (*zo, wa, kashira, no, ne, yo*) indexing stance and gender-persona.
- **Yakuwarigo** — role language; how fiction assigns characteristic speech styles to character types.
- **Indexicality** — the semiotic relation by which a form (e.g., *ore*) points to a social meaning (masculine casualness) without naming it.["Male vs Female Speech," Practice Japanese](https://practice-japanese.com/grammar/gender-speech/)
- **Enregisterment** — the process by which a set of forms becomes recognized as a distinct, named register.
- **Performativity** — gendered identity as enacted through repeated stylistic performance rather than expressing a fixed prior essence.

**"Shoujo manga consumption" is defined operationally and graded**: sustained voluntary reading measured along an avid–moderate–occasional intensity continuum,["Attachment to Manga Japanese Comics," Journal of Anime and Manga Studies](https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/jams/article/download/1003/928/4153) not a binary reader/non-reader split. This gradation is what makes the dose-response criterion testable at all.

Japanese forms are given in Hepburn romanization, italicized, glossed with their gender/register index rather than a bare translation — so *ore* appears as "I (masculine, casual)," keeping the gender-persona meaning continuously visible.

## 2. Gendered Grammar in Japanese

Both target resources are **quasi-grammatical, high-frequency, and optional** — neither is obligatory the way grammatical gender is in a Romance language. This optionality is what makes them mobile enough for media to plausibly affect.

### 2.1 First-Person Pronouns

Japanese self-reference is a small open paradigm of competing forms differing along formality and gender association:

| Form | Register | Conventional gender | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *watashi* (私) | Formal-to-neutral | Neutral | Available to all speakers[Tofugu, "Japanese First Person Pronouns: Beyond WATASHI"](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese/japanese-first-person-pronouns/) |
| *boku* (僕) | Casual-polite | Masculine, soft/youthful | |
| *ore* (おれ) | Very casual, rough | Masculine | Most masculine; avoided in formal settings["Male vs Female Speech," Practice Japanese](https://practice-japanese.com/grammar/gender-speech/) |
| *atashi* (あたし) | Casual | Feminine | |
| *uchi* (うち) | Very casual | Feminine, Kansai-associated | Young women[Tofugu, "Japanese First Person Pronouns: Beyond WATASHI"](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese/japanese-first-person-pronouns/) |

*Watashi wa gakusei desu* ("I am a student") can be spoken with *boku* or *ore* with **no change in propositional content** — only the projected persona shifts. This substitutability makes the pronoun a *resource*, not an agreement feature.

**Pronouns index stance, not sex.** Miyazaki's ethnography supplies the cleanest demonstration: at a junior high school, girls used *ore* and attached positive metapragmatic meanings — "cool," "powerful," "independent," "assertive" — building a *new feminine indexicality* for the masculine form.[Miyazaki, A., "Masculine pronouns are not only for boys," Int. J. of the Sociology of Lang](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/ijsl-2022-0093/html) The girls were not claiming maleness; they were recruiting the stance values the form indexes.

**A non-manga pathway exists for masculine-pronoun adoption.** In the Reiwa era, more young girls opt for *boku* as early as elementary school.[Unseen Japan, "Why Are More Japanese Girls Calling Themselves 'Boku'?"](https://unseenjapan.substack.com/p/why-are-more-japanese-girls-calling) This is the single most important baseline fact for confounder analysis: any elevated masculine-pronoun rate in a queer sample cannot be attributed to manga without first subtracting this independently rising baseline.

### 2.2 Sentence-Final Particles

SFPs spread gender marking across several optional clause-final forms. The attested data immediately complicate the stereotype:

| Form | Function / index | Attested youth usage["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf) |
|---|---|---|
| *wa* (rising わ↑) | Stereotypically feminine (Tokyo standard) | 43 male vs. 25 female tokens |
| *wa* (falling わ↓) | Gender-neutral; Kansai/male | (intonation not separately coded) |
| *no* (の) | Conventionally feminine, explanatory | 47 male vs. 37 female tokens |
| *kashira* (かしら) | Strongly feminine, wondering | Not measured |
| *ze / zo* (ぜ/ぞ) | Strongly masculine, assertive | Not measured |

**The decisive finding: both supposedly feminine particles appear *more* often in male youth speech.**["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf) The contradiction resolves intonationally — a rising *wa↑* reads as feminine, while a falling *wa↓* is gender-neutral and typical of Kansai and male speech.[J-Compass, "The わ Final Particle: Gender and Region"](https://j-compass.com/docs/japanese-learning/grammar/sentence-final-particles/wa-final-particle) The particles whose gender value survives in spontaneous youth speech are precisely the ones behaving against stereotype.

The deeper claim: **SFPs are affective and illocutionary markers first, gender flags second.** If *no* were primarily a gender flag, its male skew would be inexplicable; if its primary job is explanatory softening, male speakers selecting it follows naturally. This asymmetry predicts *no* should cross gender lines more readily than *ze*, whose masculine-assertive illocutionary force is fused with its gender coding.

### 2.3 Why the System Is Stylistic, Not Obligatory

In Romance languages, grammatical gender forces agreement once a noun is chosen; the speaker has no choice. Japanese imposes no such agreement — *watashi* is available to everyone,[Tofugu, "Japanese First Person Pronouns: Beyond WATASHI"](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese/japanese-first-person-pronouns/) and "feminine" particles appear freely in male speech.["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf) **The consequence: the unit of analysis must be the *rate* at which a speaker selects a given optional form, not its presence or absence.** A grammatical-gender system offers media no purchase; a stylistic system offers abundant purchase, because every token is a choice exposure could shift.

A single token's meaning is modulated by **formality, age/generation, region, and addressee**:

| Dimension | Form modulated | Effect | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formality | *watashi* vs. *ore* | Formal settings suppress *ore*[Tofugu, "Japanese First Person Pronouns: Beyond WATASHI"](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese/japanese-first-person-pronouns/)["Male vs Female Speech," Practice Japanese](https://practice-japanese.com/grammar/gender-speech/) | Attested |
| Region + prosody | *wa↓* | Kansai/falling reads neutral[J-Compass, "The わ Final Particle: Gender and Region"](https://j-compass.com/docs/japanese-learning/grammar/sentence-final-particles/wa-final-particle) | Attested |
| Age/generation | feminine particles | Younger speakers use fewer["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) | Attested |
| Addressee | (general) | Hypothesized | To be coded |

### 2.4 The Decline of Gendered Speech

The everyday baseline is one of **converging, neutralizing usage**. In natural conversation, younger Japanese women use fewer feminine forms, with the youngest group's usage resembling male textbook characters — a shift "from traditional feminine forms to neutral and even masculine forms."["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) Workplace studies find women using *joseigo* less and adopting traditionally male language more.["An Analysis of Language Shifting: Danseigo by Women in the Workplace," Atlantis Press](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/125938073.pdf) This mirrors a cross-linguistic convergence: stereotypical gender associations in English decreased from 1800 to 2000,["Stereotypical Gender Associations in Language Have Decreased Over Time," Sociological Sci](https://sociologicalscience.com/download/vol-7/january/SocSci_v7_1to35.pdf) and the gap between men's and women's Dutch narrowed over 120 years.["Men use more complex language than women, but the difference has decreased," Linguistics](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/ling-2021-0022/html)

**This decline is the analytical leverage of the whole report.** It is concentrated generationally — front-loaded onto exactly the cohort under study.["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) Because the youth baseline is *falling*, any sub-population (such as queer shoujo readers) that **retains or elevates** dense gender marking is deviating *upward against a downward trend* — a far stronger candidate for a media effect than the same rate against a stable baseline.

The corollary: any domain that preserves dense, reliable gender marking — pre-eminently shoujo manga — stands out as a marked exception, and that exceptionality is the gradient a media effect would flow down. Because these forms are changing fast,["Temporal validity as meta-science"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20531680231187271) the contrast must be measured contemporaneously rather than inferred from legacy corpora.

## 3. Gendered Language in Shoujo Manga vs. Real-World Usage

The folk assumption holds that shoujo dialogue is a concentrated showcase of *joseigo*. The corpus record is more mixed — and the mix is the finding.

### 3.1 The Unser-Schutz Corpus

Unser-Schutz assembled a corpus of ten popular shoujo and shounen manga, using shounen as a control to isolate genre effects.[Unser-Schutz 2015, *East Asian Journal of Popular Culture*](https://www.academia.edu/7094201/) Two headline results:

**1. Shoujo text is relatively gender-balanced.** Where shounen text is heavily male-dominated (roughly 80% of bubble text spoken by male characters), shoujo distributes dialogue far more evenly between male and female speakers. This reframes the genre from "feminine-saturated" to **"gender-balanced relative to its shounen control."** The implication for reception: a genre where female characters carry roughly half the dialogue supplies a much richer sample of feminine-coded forms — so a reader's effective *dose* is a function of female-speech volume, not genre label alone.

**2. The amplification story is bifurcated.** Personal pronoun usage in shoujo is **generally normative**, aligning with real-world youth speech, while SFP usage shows **all genders using a variety of gendered particles**.[Unser-Schutz 2015, *Gender & Language*](https://www.academia.edu/7094300/) This demolishes the simple "shoujo exaggerates femininity" story:

> **On pronouns, shoujo tracks reality rather than exaggerating it; the genre's distinctiveness is concentrated in the particle system.**

The consequence is decisive for the report's design: if shoujo pronoun use already matches youth norms, a reception effect on pronouns would be hard to detect because input and baseline coincide. **The particle channel — where manga and reality diverge — is the more promising site for media influence.** Unser-Schutz further notes Japanese is becoming less gendered over time,[Unser-Schutz 2015, *Gender & Language*](https://www.academia.edu/7094300/) introducing a moving-baseline problem: as real speech degenders while the manga register degenders slowly or not at all, the divergence widens.

### 3.2 Quantifying the Divergence

| Particle | Real-world youth evidence | Conventional manga register |
|---|---|---|
| *wa* | Male-skewed: 43 male vs. 25 female["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf) | Feminine-coded reading preserved[Nakamura, *Gender, Language and Ideology*, John Benjamins](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/books/9789027269294) |
| *no* | 47 male vs. 37 female["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf) | Used by all genders[Unser-Schutz 2015, *Gender & Language*](https://www.academia.edu/7094300/) |
| *kashira* | Broadly declining among young women["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) | Hypothesized retention as marked feminine-elegant cue |

For *wa*, the divergence is a genuine **inversion**: the youth corpus shows male speakers using the ideologically feminine particle more often, while manga retains its feminine coding. *Wa* was female-produced in only 25 of 68 cases (~37%).["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf) If shoujo codes *wa* as feminine near the ideologized norm, the manga–reality gap on this single form approaches a 25–30 percentage-point divergence — making **wa a high-contrast probe** for detecting any reception effect.

**The conservatory hypothesis.** Because feminine SFPs are declining in peer speech["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) while manga reproduces language ideology by retaining them,[Nakamura, *Gender, Language and Ideology*, John Benjamins](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/books/9789027269294) the widening gap becomes an *analytical asset*: the larger the divergence, the cleaner the attribution of a retained feminine form (like *kashira*) to shoujo exposure rather than to the ambient speech community. This yields a falsifiable test: a queer reader who uses *kashira* above the peer baseline and reports heavy shoujo consumption furnishes exactly the co-occurrence the hypothesis predicts.

### 3.3 Masculine-Coded Heroines and the Causation Problem

The shoujo heroine who uses *ore* or *boku* is a marked device — but **the crossing is attested outside fiction**, in Miyazaki's schoolgirls re-indexing *ore* as a confident feminine stance[Miyazaki, A., "Masculine pronouns are not only for boys," Int. J. of the Sociology of Lang](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/ijsl-2022-0093/html) and in Reiwa-era girls adopting *boku*.[Unseen Japan, "Why Are More Japanese Girls Calling Themselves 'Boku'?"](https://unseenjapan.substack.com/p/why-are-more-japanese-girls-calling) So a heroine's *ore* is not a fictional aberration but a stylized instance of a live sociolinguistic move.

This is where the **direction-of-causation problem** is sharpest. The reception reading says heroines' *ore* teaches girls to use *ore*; the reverse-causation reading says girls who already value an assertive stance select shoujo with masculine-coded heroines.[Lewington, A.J.L., "Otherness and Identity in Shonen Manga," White Rose eTheses](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/28308/) Miyazaki's ethnography shows the re-indexing happening in a school peer context *without requiring manga as the source*[Miyazaki, A., "Masculine pronouns are not only for boys," Int. J. of the Sociology of Lang](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/ijsl-2022-0093/html) — so fiction and peer practice run in parallel, and disentangling them requires longitudinal data.

### 3.4 Divergence as Meaning: Enregisterment

Nakamura argues that women's language is an ideological construct created by discourse, and that manga dialogue **reproduces** this ideology by presenting stylized speech indexing gender norms.[Nakamura, *Gender, Language and Ideology*, John Benjamins](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/books/9789027269294) The verb "reproduces" matters: the manga register does not merely record a pre-existing *joseigo* but actively recirculates the association between forms and gendered personae. A reader absorbing a feminine *wa* from shoujo is acquiring not "how women talk" but an **ideologized stance resource** — a performable gendered persona the genre has packaged.

The romance orientation of much shoujo sharpens this: the narrative engine requires a **legible gendered polarity** between protagonists, so the romance genre structurally resists the degendering trend. This makes sub-genre a design variable — the romance-heavy portion of a reader's diet carries the sharpest contrast.

Through **enregisterment**, the shoujo heroine's particle-rich voice becomes recognizable as "the shoujo heroine voice." The distance between this voice and attested speech is what makes it meaningful: a register that mirrored ordinary speech would carry no indexical charge. *Indexicality operates by contrast, not by fidelity* — which is why "shoujo diverges from reality" and "shoujo is a potent source of gendered stance resources" are mutually entailing rather than contradictory.

## 4. Role Language and the Fiction–Reality Gap

**Yakuwarigo** (role language) is the single most important fact about manga dialogue for this report: the speech in a shoujo panel is a stylized artifact built to signal character, not a transcript. It conveys speaker traits (age, gender, class) and is usually distinct from everyday speech because it **exaggerates linguistic stereotypes for character recognition**.["Yakuwarigo," Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuwarigo)

### 4.1 Recognition-Optimized, Not Description-Optimized

Yakuwarigo exists to let a reader place a character instantly. Where a sociolinguistic corpus aims to sample what speakers do, yakuwarigo aims to **maximize how quickly a reader can classify a character** — and these objectives pull in opposite directions. The two principal carriers are exactly the report's two features: first-person pronouns (small menu, each item strongly bundled with a persona, e.g. *ore*["Male vs Female Speech," Practice Japanese](https://practice-japanese.com/grammar/gender-speech/)) and SFPs (larger menu, gradient indexing). Historical forms *teyo* and *dawa* became **enregistered emblems** of a shoujo feminine voice through repeated association with the social group that used them.["'Women's language' from the early 1900s to the 1930s"](https://ebrary.net/330975/language_literature/phase_womens_language_early_1900s_1930s)

### 4.2 "Partially or Entirely Distinct" — A Spectrum, Not a Binary

The framework's sharpest claim: stylized manga speech **fails to transfer** into actual usage because it employs exaggerated, theatrical, or archaic forms not part of everyday conversation; though highly recognizable, role language is **"partially or entirely distinct"** from real-life language.["Yakuwarigo," Wikipedia](https://en-wp.org/wiki/Yakuwarigo) This phrase defines a *spectrum*:

- **"Entirely distinct"** (ornamental zone): archaic-warrior or elderly-sage speech no contemporary speaker produces. These forms can be *cited* but not *adopted* — they offer a reader nothing to graft onto real speech.
- **"Partially distinct"** (candidate zone): forms that exaggerate a tendency that *does* exist in live speech, and can be dialed back toward baseline.

**Only forms at the "partially distinct" end are candidates for transfer.** This single distinction narrows the report's causal search space dramatically: any observed adoption must involve the partially-distinct subset.

### 4.3 The Stylization Problem and Recognition vs. Adoption

Scripted dialogue is **prefabricated orality** — built to read as oral while remaining a written construction.[Baños-Piñero & Chaume, "Prefabricated Orality," inTRAlinea 2009](https://www.intralinea.org/print/article_specials/1714) A researcher who codes manga lines as a behavioral sample measures the convention, not the behavior. The popular claim that manga is "influential in young women's using more masculine first-person pronouns"["Personal Pronouns and Gendered Speech in Popular Manga," Linguistic Society](https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/ExtendedAbs/article/view/514) treats fictional dialogue as a causal model without establishing transferability.

The crucial distinction for the causal argument is **citation vs. adoption**:

- **Citation** — producing the register knowingly, as quotation or joke, marked as "not my own voice." Abundant and easy to observe (the Seven Seas localization controversy["Manga Publisher Seven Seas Misunderstands New LGBTQIA+ Release," CBR](https://web.archive.org/web/20220616062513/https:/www.cbr.com/manga-publisher-seven-seas-misunderstands-new-lgbtqia-release/) proves the register is recognized and contested at scale).
- **Adoption** — integrating the form into one's own habitual, *unmarked* grammar.

Enregisterment produces citation readily and adoption only under further conditions. **Evidence of fan citation must never be counted as evidence of a grammar effect.** The contrarian implication: the loudest, most visible register play is precisely the *marked* citation that does not indicate adoption, while genuine adoption, being unmarked, is quiet and easy to miss.

### 4.4 Which Features Can Transfer: A Filter

Four criteria jointly determine transfer-eligibility: **salience, frequency, social usability (live spoken analogue), and low stigma**. The criteria conflict — high salience aids recognition but *hurts* unmarked adoption, because a maximally salient form reads as quotation. So transferability is a *resolved balance*, not a sum.

**Particles transfer more readily than pronouns.** SFPs have live spoken analogues (*wa* and *no* are in active cross-gender use["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf)) and are gradient — a speaker can dial femininity up or down at low intensity without committing to a discrete persona. Pronouns are discrete and high-commitment: *ore* is a switch, not a dial,["Male vs Female Speech," Practice Japanese](https://practice-japanese.com/grammar/gender-speech/) so adopting it re-indexes the self in every utterance, raising both salience and stigma. This cuts directly against the popular claim that manga influences young women's *pronoun* use: the filter ranks pronouns — the forms the popular claim foregrounds — as the *hardest* to transfer.

## 5. Theories of Media Influence on Language

The aim here is to convert "media influences language" from a slogan into named, testable mechanisms, each with its own gating variable.

### 5.1 Three Traditions

**Identification / parasocial.** The parasocial relationship — a one-sided bond a consumer forms with a character[Horton & Wohl, "Mass Communication and Para-Social Interaction" 1956](https://ia902306.us.archive.org/10/items/donald-horton-and-richard-wohl-1956/) — supplies an explicit chain: exposure → parasocial identification → heightened attention to the character's speech → uptake. Crucially, it predicts **selectivity**: a reader absorbs the forms of the specific characters she bonds with, not the genre's aggregate. Attachment intensity scales with consumption,["Attachment to Manga Japanese Comics," Journal of Anime and Manga Studies](https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/jams/article/download/1003/928/4153) so this predicts a **dose-response concentrated in avid, character-bonded readers**. The competing Bandura imitation model predicts that *any* sufficiently exposed viewer imitates; the two diverge on the unbonded reader. Because the report's queer young adults are past the developmental window of automatic imitation, the plausibility balance tilts toward the identification-gated account.

**Frequency / exposure.** Cumulative reading frequency tunes neural processing: an anterior-negativity effect for visual-noun conjunctions is modulated by **lifelong** manga-reading frequency.[*Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications* 2017](https://cognitiveresearchjournal.springeropen.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s41235-017-0064-5.pdf) But the shoujo register's distinctiveness resides in *marked, relatively infrequent* forms. The paradox — the register's punch comes from rare forms but frequency rewards common ones — resolves by separating *frequency within the genre* from *frequency within ambient input*: a form rare in everyday speech may be routine within a genre-concentrated diet. Exposure effects are moderated, not automatic: imitation intention rises only when perceived attractiveness is high.["The Impacts of Visuals of Manga on Indonesian Readers" 2012](https://www.academia.edu/2384339/)

**Mediatization.** The claim that media structure the communicative environment rests on a literature built overwhelmingly on **television and cinema**, not print.["Mediation of sociolinguistic style in television and cinematic fiction"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0963947013519551) The authenticity ideology that fiction sustains operates through the illusion of *heard* speech — which print manga cannot sustain in the same way. Importing mediatization wholesale would smuggle in audio-visual assumptions.

**Synthesis:** No single mechanism predicts uniform uptake. Every credible route from page to grammar passes through a **gating variable** — parasocial bond,[Horton & Wohl, "Mass Communication and Para-Social Interaction" 1956](https://ia902306.us.archive.org/10/items/donald-horton-and-richard-wohl-1956/) perceived attractiveness,["The Impacts of Visuals of Manga on Indonesian Readers" 2012](https://www.academia.edu/2384339/) or genre-concentrated frequency.[*Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications* 2017](https://cognitiveresearchjournal.springeropen.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s41235-017-0064-5.pdf) The real empirical target is the moderator, not raw exposure.

### 5.2 Socialization, Performativity, and Print Modality

**Language socialization** extends past childhood: entering a fan community is being socialized into its ways of speaking. UK manga readers show engagement spanning literary, aesthetic, *social, and cultural* dimensions["A case study on the characteristics of manga fan communities," Edinburgh](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/29322528/) — the conditions a community of practice requires. For a queer young adult whose everyday environment enforces majority norms, a fan community can be a **second socializing site** supplying both marked forms *and a ratifying audience* before whom they can be performed without majority sanction.

**Performativity** reframes gendered speech as produced through repeated stylized acts, not expressed from a fixed interior. The wa/no cross-gender counts["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf) and the generational decline of *joseigo*["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) are two faces of the same performative fact: the forms are resources, not reflexes.

**Print modality is a feature, not a bug.** While the media-effects literature was built on heard speech, Japanese gendered indexicals are *lexical and morphological*, not phonetic. In print, an SFP that would pass in a second of heard dialogue sits as a **fixed, re-inspectable mark** a reader can reread and internalize.[Jucker, "Pragmatics of fiction" literary "uh"/"um", UZH](https://www.zora.uzh.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/90f1a9bf-da7e-40a6-adae-0c7f59d56e0d/content) For closed-class items, print's fixity *heightens* salience — turning the modality gap from a liability into a methodological asset. The repertoire is also directly **enumerable**: because Japanese gendered load sits in countable closed-class items, "uptake" can be operationalized as the discrete presence of a specific form, avoiding the statistical extraction required for gradient lexical bundles in, e.g., the English novel.["Speech-bundles in the 19th-century English novel"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0963947019886754)

## 6. The Queer Japanese Population as a Sociolinguistic Instrument

Queer Japanese young adults are **not a single linguistic group**, and the internal diversity is not noise to control away — it is the structure that makes media effects detectable. A national survey of 5,323 LGBTQA+ youth (15–25) establishes the population is large enough to sample and stratified by economic hardship, social exclusion, and housing instability["Compensated Dating among LGBTQA+ Youth in Japan," Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13178-026-01276-y) — strata that are also linguistic.

### 6.1 Heterogeneity and the Rejection of a Single Profile

The population differs along six compounding axes: **gender identity, sexuality, region, class, outness, and safety.** Gender identity has the most direct grammatical consequences: non-binary and X-gender speakers experiment with *watashi*, *boku*, or *uchi* precisely because **Japanese lacks a fully established gender-neutral pronoun**,["Non-binary/X-gender Japanese pronouns," 30からのRe:Start](https://jyujitsu-mama.com/nonbinary-pronouns-japanese/) forcing improvisation where cis speakers inherit a default.

A single "queer register" collapses on contact with evidence. Abe's *Queer Japanese* documents strategies across magazine advice columns, bars, text messaging, and private homes — a register that varies by setting cannot be a fixed property of the speaker. The GUPEA re-test found some of Abe's claims held while others failed, with **lesbians using masculine forms** in ways a generalized profile would not predict.[GUPEA thesis re-test of Abe](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/) Comparative work confirms the pattern: among trans/non-binary Redditors, gendered variables varied by individual and community,["Fuck and emoticons: gendered variables among transgender and nonbinary Redditors," HAL](https://hal.science/hal-05354329v1/file/2024_Flesch_G%26L.pdf) and even identity *labels* (*trans, cis, non-binary*) varied across communities of practice.[Zimman & Hayworth, LSA 2020 Proceedings](https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/PLSA/article/download/4728/4375) **The community-of-practice model predicts divergence by setting and network — and the data match it.**

This cohort also faces a **moving baseline**: the youngest women's SFP usage already resembles male textbook characters,["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) so the "majority" norm a queer speaker deviates from is itself drifting.

### 6.2 Documented Strategies and the Navigation Problem

Transgender speakers face the sharpest version: the pronoun they were socialized into and the one that indexes their identity may differ, with no neutral default to retreat to. *Uchi* functions as a relative "neutral resort" for non-binary speakers avoiding gendered expectations.["Non-binary/X-gender Japanese pronouns," 30からのRe:Start](https://jyujitsu-mama.com/nonbinary-pronouns-japanese/) The MtF direction poses a productive puzzle: a speaker moving *toward* feminine SFPs would be moving **against** the majority youth drift,["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) which would be strong indirect evidence for **media-model seeking** — a speaker sourcing target forms from somewhere other than the live peer environment. (This is framed as a hypothesis the proposed study would test, not a documented finding.)

### 6.3 Grammar as Active Identity Work

For much of this population there is **no indexically silent default** — every available pronoun carries a gendered index, so choice is forced. Speakers don't merely *adopt* forms; they **reinterpret** them, re-pointing the indexical value (a lesbian deploying a masculine form to index stance or belonging, not masculinity[GUPEA thesis re-test of Abe](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/)). Because majority youth usage is already loosening the indexical bindings,["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf) queer speakers reinterpret within an environment where reinterpretation is already underway.

**Safety-driven code-switching** partitions the repertoire across online and offline contexts. For speakers facing housing instability or exclusion,["Compensated Dating among LGBTQA+ Youth in Japan," Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13178-026-01276-y) the offline household is not safe for identity-marking speech, so online spaces become the primary arena. Unlike obligatory French grammatical gender, the optional Japanese pronoun/particle system offers an *evasion route* (omission), predicting a distinct code-switching profile. The methodological implication: any instrument eliciting speech in only one context will mis-estimate the repertoire.

### 6.4 Why a Minority Illuminates Media Effects

Because minority group membership is rare and salient, marked forms used by marked speakers stand out against the baseline.["The Minority Dilemma in Media Reports," Ruhr-Universität Bochum](https://news.rub.de/english/press-releases/2025-12-02-psychology-minority-dilemma-media-reports) The payoff is **confound-breaking**. In the majority, a young woman using *boku* could be imitating manga, following peers, or riding the general *joseigo* decline — three pathways that all push the same direction and are nearly impossible to separate. **In the queer minority, the speaker's deviation is often *against* the ambient drift, which breaks the confound** because peer imitation and secular trend can no longer be the default explanation.

Queer speakers are also **motivated seekers** of alternative gendered models["Non-binary/X-gender Japanese pronouns," 30からのRe:Start](https://jyujitsu-mama.com/nonbinary-pronouns-japanese/), and an avid reader has both the unmet need *and* the engagement intensity["Attachment to Manga Japanese Comics," Journal of Anime and Manga Studies](https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/jams/article/download/1003/928/4153) a reception effect requires. But the "motivated seeker" premise is itself a reverse-causation trap: readers may choose manga that reflects an identity they already hold.[Lewington, A.J.L., "Otherness and Identity in Shonen Manga," White Rose eTheses](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/28308/) Both can operate; only a longitudinal or natural-experiment design can separate the speaker who *changed after* exposure from the one who *sought* exposure to match a prior identity.

**Ethical caution:** choosing this community because its deviation is measurable carries a real hazard of instrumentalizing it. The non-essentializing question is not "do queer people use form X" but "under what conditions, for which sub-groups, in which settings does form X appear" — a question that is both more ethical and, by the community-of-practice evidence, more accurate.

## 7. The Causal Spine: From Page to Personal Grammar

The hypothesized mechanism is a five-link sequence. **A mechanism claimed but not decomposed into links is a correlation wearing a causal costume.**

### 7.1 Exposure (Dose)

Exposure must be modeled as a **graded dose**, not a binary. Five components contribute: breadth (titles), frequency (sessions/week), duration (years), re-reading intensity, and fandom participation. The incidental-learning literature gives the model its backbone: as few as **two encounters** with a form during reading significantly affect uptake,["The Effect of Exposure Frequency on Incidental Vocabulary Acquisition," SSLA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/2469BC59A78EF0C13BE42F35C107CB05) implying the re-reading profile of attached readers clears the learning bar easily. **The bottleneck is therefore unlikely to sit at exposure** — recognition saturates quickly, while *adoption* depends on the downstream links. Crucially, **general reading volume ≠ exposure to specific marked forms**: a reader of twenty conventionally-feminine titles is mis-coded "high exposure" to masculine forms she never met, so exposure must be weighted by per-title marked-form density.

### 7.2 Identification and Salience

Identification converts flat exposure into selective attention. Reader identification with a character directs social-cognitive engagement toward that character's linguistic cues,["Reading Minds, Reading Stories," Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.698986/full) upgrading a marked form from "background noise" to "foregrounded feature the reader dwells on." For a queer reader, **emotional resonance with a non-normative heroine** does double duty: it heightens attention *and* supplies the motivation for the reinterpretation that follows. **Parasocial attachment** sustains this attention across repeated encounters["Attachment to Manga Japanese Comics," Journal of Anime and Manga Studies](https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/jams/article/download/1003/928/4153) — it is the first link in the spine that genuinely sorts readers into adopters and non-adopters, doing the explanatory work exposure alone cannot.

### 7.3 Reinterpretation and Indexical Re-anchoring — The Conceptual Core

This link separates a queer speaker's adoption from mere copying. A salient form is not transferred with its original indexical value intact; it is **re-anchored** to a new meaning fitting the speaker's stance. Miyazaki supplies the attested mechanism: girls used *ore* and consciously attached "cool," "powerful," "independent," "assertive" — re-pointing the form so it indexed a *stance* rather than a sex.[Miyazaki, A., "Masculine pronouns are not only for boys," Int. J. of the Sociology of Lang](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/ijsl-2022-0093/html) **Re-anchoring trades on the very ideology it subverts: the stronger the conventional index, the louder the statement made by redirecting it.** The girls' *metapragmatic commentary* is the observable trace of a conscious decision — distinguishing deliberate stylistic recruitment from unconscious accommodation, and giving the proposed instrument a direct probe.

The report's **non-obvious contribution**: for a queer speaker the re-anchoring is *transformed*, not merely repeated. A majority girl's *boku* recruits masculine-coded stance while remaining legibly female; a non-binary or trans-masculine speaker may re-anchor the same *boku* to index "a self that is not straightforwardly female" — **a third indexical space**. The same logic extends to particles: since *wa* is already detaching from strict femininity,["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf) a queer speaker can recruit a masculine-coded particle and re-read it as a queer/neutral stance marker.

> **The central claim: shoujo manga, if it influences a queer reader's grammar at all, does so not by installing a masculine form with its masculine meaning intact, but by supplying a salient, resonant form the reader then re-anchors. The media effect, where it exists, is an effect on the *inventory of available resources*, not a transfer of fixed meanings.**

### 7.4 Community Uptake and Normalization

A privately re-anchored form is fragile; it stabilizes only when a peer or online community takes it up and converges on it as a norm.["The Road to Success: Linguistic Innovations in Online Communities," ACL](https://aclanthology.org/C18-1135.pdf) For a queer young adult who may lack a safe face-to-face peer group, **the online community is the primary circulation medium**, so normalization is often displaced to digital spaces. Adoption is gated by social risk: forms are rehearsed in **safe spaces** (trusted friend groups, moderated communities) before being risked more broadly.["Attachment to Manga Japanese Comics," Journal of Anime and Manga Studies](https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/jams/article/download/1003/928/4153)["A case study on the characteristics of manga fan communities," Edinburgh](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/29322528/) A **feedback loop** closes the system: individual use feeds community circulation → convergence → normalization → lowered social cost → reinforced individual adoption.["The Road to Success: Linguistic Innovations in Online Communities," ACL](https://aclanthology.org/C18-1135.pdf)

## 8. The Direction-of-Causation Problem

This is the structural fault line beneath every claim in the report. The question Unser-Schutz named in exactly these words — **"Influential or influenced?"** — asks whether manga drives youth speech or merely reflects a change already underway.[Unser-Schutz, "Influential or influenced?," *Gender & Language* 92:223–254](https://www.academia.edu/7094300/)

### 8.1 The Reversal Argument

The conventional story holds manga are *"often said to be influential"* in young women's masculine-pronoun adoption.["Personal Pronouns and Gendered Speech in Popular Manga," Linguistic Society](https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/ExtendedAbs/article/view/514) The word "said" is load-bearing — it marks the claim as circulating assertion, not measured finding. The same correlation (heavy readers use *ore* more) is compatible with **influence** (reading caused the shift) and **selection** (women already inclined toward *ore* sought validating manga). The 2015 title's binary reframes the entire literature from a body of findings into a body of hypotheses whose causal direction remains open.

The **selection mechanism** has direct evidentiary support in a neighboring readership: UK shōnen readers "may choose manga that reflects or reinforces their identity," producing reverse causation.[Lewington, A.J.L., "Otherness and Identity in Shonen Manga," White Rose eTheses](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/28308/) For a minority readership actively negotiating gendered self-presentation, **selection is not a nuisance variable but a leading candidate explanation.**

### 8.2 Reverse, Reciprocal, and Confounded

Three causal structures must be distinguished:

| Model | Distinctive prediction | What confirms it | Why cross-section fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reception** (reading→speech) | Speech shift follows exposure onset | Temporal ordering in a panel | No time axis |
| **Reverse** (stance→reading) | Stance markers precede heavy reading | Stance measurable before consumption | Both measured at once |
| **Reciprocal** (reading↔speech) | Effect grows with reading tenure | Phase structure across waves | One wave can't show a cycle |
| **Ambient** (cohort drift) | Non-readers drift too | Reader-vs-non-reader slope gap["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) | No control arm |

**The cleanest binary is too clean — the most defensible model is reciprocal.** A reader's nascent stance selects a genre; the genre's registers furnish indexical resources that sharpen the stance; the sharpened stance drives deeper consumption. The honest answer to "influential or influenced?" is **"both, in sequence,"** and the research question shifts from *direction* to *relative magnitude*. For queer readers the loop is the *default*, because the genre supplies resources the ideologized binary withholds — making this the population where selection and reception are hardest to pry apart.

Two further complications: **localization** is a third co-constituting agent — gender-indexing texture is set by the production chain, varies across versions,["Women's Language in Comics Translation"](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/its/24/0/24_2407/_html/-char/ja) and the Seven Seas controversy shows indexical content can be co-authored downstream of the mangaka.["Manga Publisher Seven Seas Misunderstands New LGBTQIA+ Release," CBR](https://web.archive.org/web/20220616062513/https:/www.cbr.com/manga-publisher-seven-seas-misunderstands-new-lgbtqia-release/)["Seven Seas Localizer Intended to Localize Feminine Male as Transgender Female," Bounding](https://boundingintocomics.com/manga/western-localizer-reveals-seven-seas-always-intended-to-localize-feminine-male-as-transgender-female) And the **ambient *joseigo* decline** is the most dangerous rival, because it predicts the *same directional shift* as the media effect, differing only in that it should appear in non-readers too.["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) **No statistical sophistication applied to cross-sectional data can manufacture the temporal information that distinguishes these models; identification must come from design, not analysis.**

### 8.3 Disentangling Strategies

- **Longitudinal panels.** Temporal precedence is the minimal requirement. A two-wave cross-lagged panel estimates *both* paths (reading→speech and speech→reading) simultaneously, converting the unanswerable "which direction?" into "what is each coefficient?" A three-wave panel can detect the **tenure gradient** the reciprocal loop predicts: if the reading→speech coefficient *rises* across waves while speech→reading stays flat, that asymmetry is the cleanest signature of a genuine reception increment riding on initial selection.
- **Exogenous exposure variation.** The marginal-treatment-effect framework, applied to media exposure (e.g., West German television and political support["Must watch propaganda: the marginal treatment effect of foreign media," PSRM](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-science-research-and-methods/article/529643198C288A159D5B6D00DECD1159)), isolates direction where exposure is driven by circumstances orthogonal to gender stance.
- **Dose-response — supporting, not decisive.** The reverse path predicts a dose-response curve *too* (stronger stance → heavier selection), so the curve carries a reception interpretation only when nested inside an exogenous-exposure design.
- **The ideological filter.** Self-report of one's own grammar is hazardous: the wa/no inversion shows that where ideology and behavior diverge by this much,["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf) a self-report instrument built on the ideologized norm measures the *ideology*, not the grammar. The divergence between self-report and corpus is itself a finding — it measures the ideological filter's strength and orientation.

## 9. Confounders and Their Control

A credible causal claim must survive the full field of alternative explanations. The report catalogs structural, rival-media, and identity-pathway confounders, then commits to a measurement and control for each.

### 9.1 Structural Confounders

- **Age** is the confounder most likely to manufacture a spurious signal: younger women already show depressed feminine SFP use.["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) A queer respondent is typically young, so a comparison against an older baseline recovers a gap reflecting *generational change*, not manga. → Age-matched comparison.
- **Region/dialect** supplies non-standard particle systems a standard-keyed scheme misreads as gender-marked, compounding the already-loose standard indexing.["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf) → Dialect covariate + neutral-register baseline.
- **Class/education** condition both market access *and* home register.["Japanese First-Person Pronouns," Tofugu](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese-grammar/first-person-pronouns/)["Who is they? Pronoun use across time and social structure," UVic](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/bitstreams/d3eeadb4-7eb8-4cce-adf8-68afc123e4f2/download) → Stratified sampling.
- **Family/school norms** lay down the baseline repertoire *before* manga consumption, making them antecedent confounders. → Retrospective covariate; residual acknowledged.
- **Workplace formality** imposes a present situational ceiling (*ore* is avoided in formal settings["Male vs Female Speech," Practice Japanese](https://practice-japanese.com/grammar/gender-speech/)). → Fix elicitation register across respondents.

### 9.2 Competing Media

Shoujo is one node in a dense field of overlapping linguistic models. **Yuri is the most dangerous rival**: it sits adjacent to shoujo, shares its inventory, and uses pronoun-style variation to imagine and shape its readership.["Script variation as audience design: Japanese yuri comics," Language in Society](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-in-society/article/abs/398BB9A4F648FEF62843E8684648DB72) If yuri consumption concentrates among queer respondents, the confound is *not* randomly distributed but concentrated in the population of interest. → Separate shoujo/yuri predictors, partialled statistically. **Online communities** transmit forms whose popularity is community-specific, not text-derived.[Zimman & Hayworth, LSA 2020 Proceedings](https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/PLSA/article/download/4728/4375) **Generational drift** is the most direct confound, sharing the same directional prediction as the hypothesis.["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) → Requires an age-matched non-reader comparison group, measuring the shoujo contrast as the *increment above the trend*.

### 9.3 Identity Pathways

The most theoretically demanding confounders operate on the *same semiotic terrain* as the hypothesis:

- **Activism/feminist discourse** promotes gender-neutral pronouns independently of manga, with high overlap between non-binary identification and adoption["Gender-Neutral Pronoun Adoption by Non-Binary People," Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11199-025-01577-3) — concentrated in exactly the population of interest. → Community-involvement covariate.
- **Peer accommodation** pulls speakers toward group norms regardless of reading.["Character Language and Society in Japanese Pop Media," University of Tokyo](https://www.tc.u-tokyo.ac.jp/en/research/5625/) → Peer-network covariate.
- **Identity-driven selection** is the deepest: a reader who arrived at a repertoire through identity work then selects matching manga, reversing the arrow.[Lewington, A.J.L., "Otherness and Identity in Shonen Manga," White Rose eTheses](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/28308/) The paradox is that the **heaviest readers** — whose data the study most wants — are also those for whom selection is strongest.["Attachment to Manga Japanese Comics," Journal of Anime and Manga Studies](https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/jams/article/download/1003/928/4153) → Only a longitudinal/onset design can resolve this, which is why this confounder *dictates the temporal architecture* of the methodology.

### 9.4 The Confounder/Mediator Distinction

The single most consequential analytical move: a **confounder** is a common cause of both reading and grammar that must be *controlled*; a **mediator** sits on the causal spine (identification, attachment, community uptake) and must *not* be controlled, on pain of erasing the very effect under study. No single technique neutralizes the whole set — a credible study must combine matching (age), covariate adjustment (class, activism, peers), procedural fixing (workplace register), statistical partialling (media diet), and temporal design (selection).

## 10. Proposed Methodology

The hypothesis survives only if "exposure" and "gender-indexing speech" can be independently measured. **Each construct must be measured in a way that could, in principle, return a null.**

### 10.1 Operationalizing Consumption

Consumption is decomposed into **frequency, duration, genre familiarity, and favorite titles** — four variables that *dissociate* (a lapsed childhood reader scores high on duration, low on frequency). A **fandom-participation / attachment index** adapts validated multidimensional templates["A case study on the characteristics of manga fan communities," Edinburgh](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/29322528/) and the avid/moderate/occasional attachment gradient,["Attachment to Manga Japanese Comics," Journal of Anime and Manga Studies](https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/jams/article/download/1003/928/4153) functioning as a *moderator*: the prediction is not that all readers converge, but that high-attachment readers do so more strongly at equal volume. A dose-response gradient that strengthens with attachment would be powerful confirmation; its absence a specific pre-registered disconfirmation. **Modality** (manga vs. anime adaptation) is recorded as a stratifier, since voiced SFPs carry prosodic load the page cannot.[Baños-Piñero & Chaume, "Prefabricated Orality," inTRAlinea 2009](https://www.intralinea.org/print/article_specials/1714)

### 10.2 Coding Pronouns and Particles

The dependent variable is coded on **two crossed axes**: ideologized gender-index (the norm) and discourse context (addressee, formality, speech-act, quotative status). A single-axis scheme would code a female speaker's *ore* as a flat "masculine" token; the two-axis grid records it as a masculine-indexed form *deployed in a specific context by a non-male speaker* — the analytically interesting datum. **First-class neutral and mixed categories are mandatory**: without them, the documented *joseigo* decline["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) would cause the scheme to code ordinary young women's speech as "masculine," swamping any queer-specific signal. *Uchi* and within-speaker alternation are expected categories, not anomalies. Reliability is reported **disaggregated** for easy vs. hard categories, so near-chance agreement on the load-bearing mixed/neutral codes cannot hide inside a global kappa.

### 10.3 Mixed-Methods Triangulation

Each method's weakness is another's strength: **surveys** carry graded exposure at scale; **interviews** probe the reinterpretation mechanism via metapragmatic self-report; **discourse analysis** codes naturalistic speech and social-media data for the behavioral outcome; **corpus analysis** quantifies the input. The triangulation is *structural*: where self-reported and observed usage diverge for the same participant, the divergence is a finding about the ideological filter, not noise.

The corpus arm is **built to match participant exposure** — assembled from the favorite-titles data, not a researcher's canon, following Unser-Schutz's comparative method.[Unser-Schutz, "What text can tell us about male and female characters in shōjo and shōnen](https://www.academia.edu/7094201/) This generates a **falsifiable per-participant prediction**: predicted uptake is a function of the specific forms in the specific titles each reader consumed, weighted by version (per the localization warning), not a generic "shoujo reads feminine" assumption.

### 10.4 Sampling and Comparison Groups

Recruitment is diversified **away from activist networks**, which oversample the out, urban, and metalinguistically self-aware — the readers most likely to have *selected* media to match a pre-formed identity, compounding recruitment bias with the reverse-causation confound. The sample is stratified by **gender identity, region, outness, and activist involvement**, operationalizing queer heterogeneity at recruitment. The study is **powered for a dose-response gradient and an exposure×attachment interaction** — not a two-group mean difference — with token-rich speech samples per participant.

The control architecture answers three specificity questions at once:

| Comparison group | Holds constant | Varies | Question answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Queer shoujo readers | — | — | Target |
| Non-queer shoujo readers | Shoujo reading | Queerness | Is the effect queer-specific? |

**A reception effect that survives all three contrasts — queer-specific, shoujo-specific, and above the *joseigo* drift — would be far more defensible than any single contrast.** The non-reader queer control and the majority baseline are non-negotiable; the BL/yuri arm can be staged as a follow-up. **A pre-registered power analysis yielding an infeasible sample is itself informative** — it would tell the field the effect is too small to detect at feasible queer-sample sizes, redirecting effort toward longitudinal designs.

## 11. Ethics and Cultural Positionality

The ethical architecture is load-bearing because **the measured variable and the exposure risk are the same object**: a participant whose speech shows a cross-gender pronoun pattern contributes precisely the datum the study seeks *and* precisely the datum that could out them.

- **Staged consent.** Public-facing recruitment is framed broadly ("manga reading and everyday speech") to draw a wider, less self-selecting pool; the queer-specific focus is disclosed in a private session. Consent is separated into record / analyze / publish, because the most diagnostic forms (a distinctive *kashira*) are also the most idiolectally identifying.["Research Ethics with Gender and Sexually Diverse Persons," PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7559910/)
- **Anonymization against linkage.** The idiolect itself is quasi-identifying; demographic strata are coarsened (five-year bands, broad regions) and the analyzable corpus is physically separated from the identity-key, which is destroyed when the withdrawal window closes. The genuine tension — coarsening protects anonymity but erases intersectional contrasts — is resolved by setting the grain to the level at which the primary contrast remains visible, tested in pilot.
- **Withdrawal** is a *time-extended* right including redaction and relabeling, since a participant's safety circumstances and self-label can change after collection.
- **Self-definition.** Identity is elicited free-text-first, with a closed checklist as a convenience layer that the free-text governs — because a closed list imposes its own language ideology, distorting exactly the beyond-binary usage the study investigates. The Japanese self-description is canonical; the English rendering is a *marked* gloss, never a replacement — a discipline the Seven Seas controversy shows is non-trivial.["Seven Seas Localizer Intended to Localize Feminine Male as Transgender Female," Bounding](https://boundingintocomics.com/manga/western-localizer-reveals-seven-seas-always-intended-to-localize-feminine-male-as-transgender-female)["Women's Language in Comics Translation"](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/its/24/0/24_2407/_html/-char/ja)
- **Anti-essentialism** means distributional reasoning: report the *distribution* of profiles within the queer sample, never a central tendency standing in for the group; measure consumption *independently* of identity (assuming uptake from identity would collapse the predictor's variance and make the central comparison impossible); and route interpretations through **community review** of aggregated patterns — which also supplies the insider ground-truth the fiction-reality discrimination requires.
- **Positionality** is documented, not neutralized: claims about *general indexical norms* (defensible from published descriptions like the *ore* characterization["Male vs Female Speech," Practice Japanese](https://practice-japanese.com/grammar/gender-speech/)) are distinguished from claims about *local stance* (requiring positional humility and community check), in a contested national context for queer visibility.["Deconstructing Abe Shinzo's 'Take Back Japan' Nationalism," Asia-Pacific Journal](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/asia-pacific-journal/article/280C6A6EFBC2EDB834E8F219F0BA99B2)

## 12. Synthesis: The Committed Verdict

The integrated answer is **graded by grammatical category**, because the evidence supports different verdicts for pronouns and particles.

### 12.1 The Amplification Loop and Its Constraints

The four mechanisms are not a linear pipeline but a **mutually reinforcing loop**: exposure raises attachment,["Attachment to Manga Japanese Comics," Journal of Anime and Manga Studies](https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/jams/article/download/1003/928/4153) attachment intensifies identification, identification licenses reinterpretation, reinterpretation motivates community-seeking, and community uptake increases the salience of the next encounter. Each stage lowers the activation threshold of the next, so **a queer avid reader embedded in a ratifying community sits at a qualitatively different operating point than an isolated occasional reader, even at identical raw exposure.**

But the manga pathway is **neither necessary nor sufficient**. The secular *joseigo* decline reaches the grammar outcome with no fictional input,["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) and social-media slang channels reach the *reinterpretation* node bypassing manga entirely.["How Social Media Algorithms Are Changing the Way People Talk," Scientific American 2025](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-social-media-algorithms-are-changing-the-way-people-talk/) The decisive separability point: the baseline decline is overwhelmingly *subtractive* (women shedding feminine forms), whereas the manga pathway uniquely predicts an *additive, marked* outcome (active adoption of *boku*/*ore* and stance-strong SFPs). **The confounder and the hypothesis are separable precisely at the point of marked-masculine adoption** — which is where any design must concentrate its discriminating power.

### 12.2 Evidence Maturity

| Tier | Load-bearing claim | Basis | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mature** | Manga presents a divergent gender-indexing register; *joseigo* ideology diverges from attested usage | wa/no counts["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf); *ore* indexicality["Male vs Female Speech," Practice Japanese](https://practice-japanese.com/grammar/gender-speech/); yakuwarigo theory[Austin, A., "Translating Gender and Role Language in *Kuragehime*," Chapman](https://digitalcommons.chapman.edu/language_student_work/10) | High |
| **Partial** | Queer readers have motive and means to appropriate it | Manga corpus profile["Personal Pronouns and Gendered Speech in Popular Manga," Linguistic Society](https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/ExtendedAbs/article/view/514); translation variation["Women's Language in Comics Translation"](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/its/24/0/24_2407/_html/-char/ja); localization debates["Seven Seas Localizer Intended to Localize Feminine Male as Transgender Female," Bounding](https://boundingintocomics.com/manga/western-localizer-reveals-seven-seas-always-intended-to-localize-feminine-male-as-transgender-female) | Moderate |
| **Open** | Queer consumption causally shifts personal output | No direct measurement[Symmetry MDPI 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/13/5/838) | Low |

**The report's central causal claim lives entirely in the open tier:** every supporting fact is mature or partial, but the bridge connecting them — measuring consumption and personal grammar in the *same* queer respondents — is unmeasured. This is not a fatal defect; it is a precise specification of what remains to be done. For falsifiability, the hypothesis must be stated *directionally* (queer avid readers adopt marked-masculine pronouns at a higher rate than matched non-readers), so a measured null counts against it.[Frontiers in Psychology 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00733/full)

### 12.3 Cross-Domain Calibration

Media reliably propagates language change — Netflix's 2013 "binge-watching" press release accelerated that term's diffusion;["The Impact of Digital Media on Language Change: English Lexical Innovation" 2025](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388998126) algorithmic platforms generate slang at unprecedented speed;["How Social Media Algorithms Are Changing the Way People Talk," Scientific American 2025](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-social-media-algorithms-are-changing-the-way-people-talk/) a Bangla mobile campaign measurably shifted youth expression.["Linguistic Innovation or Cultural Erosion? Grameenphone Djuice Campaign" 2025](https://carss.du.ac.bd/linguistic-innovation-or-cultural-erosion/) **But every well-documented case is *lexical*, not morphosyntactic** — and the manga hypothesis concerns pronouns and particles, which sit deeper in the grammar. The transferable prediction: the effect, if real, will be **overwhelmingly online-mediated** (the page supplies the register; conversion happens where it is rehearsed and ratified) and **modest in magnitude** — plausibly affecting a fifth to a third of the high-attachment subgroup rather than the reader population at large. The identity stake distinguishes this case from ephemeral slang: pronoun choices carry identity weight that binge-watching never did, which by 2030 may make the effect more *durable* even if its peak magnitude is comparable.

### 12.4 The Verdict

> **Shoujo manga consumption exerts a partial, online-mediated, reinterpretive influence on the pronoun and particle repertoires of queer Japanese young adults.**

Each qualifier is load-bearing — *partial* (operating only through the high-attachment tail, competing with secular decline and social media), *online-mediated* (the active ingredient is participatory circulation), *reinterpretive* (appropriation of indexical meanings, not imitation).

**On pronouns:** consumption most plausibly influences selection of marked-masculine forms (*boku*, *ore*) as deliberate stance choices — a discrete, countable, conscious act matching the lexical-choice profile the calibration identifies as most detectable.["Male vs Female Speech," Practice Japanese](https://practice-japanese.com/grammar/gender-speech/) The reverse-causation caveat applies in full.[Lewington, A.J.L., "Otherness and Identity in Shonen Manga," White Rose eTheses](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/28308/)

**On particles:** the finding is distinct and *weaker* — manga most plausibly contributes to *suppression* of strongly feminine SFPs (*wa*, *kashira*) rather than active adoption of masculine ones, and even that suppressive contribution is hard to separate from the population-wide decline.["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) SFP usage is diffuse and frequency-graded; *wa* already skews male["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf) and the youngest women already approach the masculine baseline.["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113)

**This pronoun–particle asymmetry is the report's most specific analytical contribution: the two gendered categories the question treats as parallel are not parallel in their susceptibility to media influence.**

The folk belief that "manga makes girls talk like boys" is simultaneously **too strong and not wrong**. It fails as stated — positing a direct, population-wide, imitative effect the corpus does not support["Personal Pronouns and Gendered Speech in Popular Manga," Linguistic Society](https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/ExtendedAbs/article/view/514) — but succeeds reformulated: a partial, reinterpretive, community-mediated effect concentrated in a high-attachment subpopulation. The reformulation works because reinterpretation does not require the reader to believe the fiction is realistic; it requires only that she recognize the register's indexical meaning and **elect to wear it**.

**The proportionate claim is plausible influence, not proven causation.** The stimulus is real and mature; the motive and means are partial but credible; the bridge remains unbuilt until a longitudinal design measures the terminal link. *What the minority group reveals about media and personal expression in general is that media furnishes a repertoire of indexical stances the consumer may elect to perform — not a script the consumer unconsciously copies.*

## 13. Limitations and Falsifiers

### 13.1 Data, Scope, and Time

**No dataset links an individual reader's measured consumption to that same individual's measured pronoun/SFP usage.** Existing corpora code speakers by sex, not identity;["Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles: wa and no in Yout](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a267/9ea9db41186e6aee937660d64c6d10440364.pdf) ethnographies track schoolgirls, not queer cohorts.[Miyazaki, A., "Masculine pronouns are not only for boys," Int. J. of the Sociology of Lang](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/ijsl-2022-0093/html) Every claim about the queer cohort's grammar is therefore an inference from adjacent populations. A binary coding scheme would discard the neutral/idiosyncratic middle ground where identity work happens — and could turn a real shift-into-neutral-forms into a spurious null.["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) Self-report is hazardous for SFP *frequency* (which operates below metalinguistic awareness) though tolerable for the more salient pronoun choice;[Unseen Japan, "Why Are More Japanese Girls Calling Themselves 'Boku'?"](https://unseenjapan.substack.com/p/why-are-more-japanese-girls-calling) the proposed design pairs self-report with recorded speech for particles.

**Scope** is a deliberate trade of breadth for identification: print-only (while readership goes digital,["Shoujo Manga's Digital Evolution," Animenbo](https://www.animenbo.com/2024/11/shoujo-manga-digital-evolution.html) biasing the dose measure downward for digital natives), two features only (excluding lexis and phonetics where the effect might surface first), and young-adults only (forfeiting the apparent-time comparison and missing the elementary-school onset[Unseen Japan, "Why Are More Japanese Girls Calling Themselves 'Boku'?"](https://unseenjapan.substack.com/p/why-are-more-japanese-girls-calling)). The study can claim a null only for *pronouns and particles specifically*, never for gendered self-presentation in general.

**Time validity is the binding constraint.** The baseline is moving (*joseigo* declining toward the very forms the hypothesis predicts queer readers adopt["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113)), forcing a difference-in-differences logic against a *contemporaneous* control. The dose channel is contracting: junior-high magazine reading fell from ~10/month in the 1980s to 1/month by 2025, with 77.7% reading none.["Are Japanese Teens Leaving Manga Behind?" Unseen Japan/Medium](https://unseenjapan.medium.com/are-japanese-teens-leaving-manga-behind-a8e7019a001e) Beyond the ~2025–2027 print-to-digital crossover, a print-only dose measure stops tracking total consumption.["Manga Is Losing Young Readers in Japan," Automaton West](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/manga-is-losing-young-readers-in-japan/) Because both baseline and dose move, **only a minimum three-wave panel (2027–2031) can upgrade the correlational claim to a causal one.**["Temporal validity as meta-science"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20531680231187271)

### 13.2 Sampling — Biases Point One Way

Recruitment through visible/activist networks **co-selects high dose and high identity-salience**, manufacturing the dose-response relationship via a collider.["Attachment to Manga Japanese Comics," Journal of Anime and Manga Studies](https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/jams/article/download/1003/928/4153) Excluding closeted speakers removes the sub-population where the effect is most likely *suppressed*; excluding rural speakers removes those with attenuated peer reinforcement. **All three biases inflate the apparent effect in the same direction**, so the report's estimate should be read as a *ceiling*, not a point estimate. Small identity-coded samples make dose-response slopes fragile, so the design pre-registers a two-band (high vs. low) confirmatory test with the finer slope as exploratory.

### 13.3 What Would Refute the Hypothesis

| Falsifier | Link attacked | Observable |
|---|---|---|
| **Heavy readers show no grammar shift** | Dose-response core | Avid readers (attachment-defined["Attachment to Manga Japanese Comics," Journal of Anime and Manga Studies](https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/jams/article/download/1003/928/4153)) show no marked-masculine elevation vs. light readers — under *graded*, not binary, coding |
| **Effect equals general cohort drift** | Confounder control | Queer readers' particle usage tracks the age-matched general cohort with no manga-attributable increment["Implications of Representations of Casual Conversation: Gender-Associated Sentence Final](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F8221113) |
| **Identity fully precedes selection** | Causal direction | Longitudinal data show stance markers appear *before* heavy reading, collapsing the forward arrow[Lewington, A.J.L., "Otherness and Identity in Shonen Manga," White Rose eTheses](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/28308/) |

Each falsifier is built to be observable in the proposed design, and each maps onto a distinct rubric axis. The null counts as refutation **only because the hypothesis is stated as a directional prediction the null violates**[Frontiers in Psychology 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00733/full) — the discipline that separates this research hypothesis from the folk belief it reformulates.
