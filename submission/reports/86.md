# Manufacturing Technology Options for Hollow Motor Shafts in NEV Electric-Drive Units: Forming Techniques, Comparative Evaluation, and Route Selection

## Executive Summary

Cross-wedge rolling and rotary swaging dominate the near-net forming of hollow NEV motor shafts, but the highest-volume answer is increasingly the **cold-formed modular two-piece shaft**, not any single monolithic route. The global electric-motor rotor-shaft market underwrites this contest at **US$2.2 billion in 2024, growing to US$3.0 billion by 2030**, with hollow architectures capturing a disproportionate share of new e-drive programs in the 140–360 kW band.

Despite the common claim that hollow shafts are best produced by deep-hole drilling a solid forging, the evidence here shows **machining-from-solid is the costliest and most material-wasteful route**, surviving only where volumes are low or geometries defeat forming. Through 2030, the decisive selection driver shifts from raw formability toward integrated rotor-cooling duct geometry and motor–gearbox interface tolerance, favoring assembled and flow-formed routes.

**The committed verdict:** for mainstream NEV traction shafts at high volume, **cross-wedge rolling and rotary swaging are co-leaders, radial forging ranks third**, flow forming and cold extrusion are strong volume-conditional contenders, and machining-from-solid is relegated to prototype and low-volume niches. The ranking is analytical, conditioned on a steel-centric material scope and high-volume economics, and flips only under three named boundary conditions: sub-crossover volume (favoring drilling), cooling-bore geometry (favoring CWR), and high-strength through-thickness demand (favoring AHD hollow forging).

The ten routes evaluated, by family:

| Route | Core forming principle | Scope tier |
|---|---|---|
| Rotary Swaging | Incremental radial die blows over a mandrel | Lead contender |
| Cross-Wedge Rolling (CWR) | Wedge tools reduce a rotating billet to a stepped profile | Lead contender |
| Radial Forging | Two/four dies forge radially with axial feed | Tier-1 / medium-volume |
| Three-Roll Skew Rolling | Three skewed rolls compress a heated billet | Near-term contender |
| Rotary Compression / Forging | Localised orbital compression | Emerging |
| Cold / Composite Extrusion | Material pushed through a die to form profile | Specialist (high-volume) |
| Flow Forming (Spinning) | Rollers thin a tubular preform over a mandrel | Near-term (low-volume) |
| Hollow Forging (AHD-steel route) | Forging a hollow body from air-hardening ductile steel | Emerging / calibration |
| Deep-Hole / BTA Drilling | Bore drilled into solid bar, then turned | Low-volume fallback |
| Rotary Friction Welding | Tube and solid ends joined by friction | Two-piece option |

## 1. Scope and Evaluation Framework

### 1.1 The component in scope

A **hollow rotor shaft** is a thin-walled steel tube that supports the rotor lamination stack of an NEV traction motor and carries its drive torque to the downstream reduction gear set. Its defining geometric feature is the central bore, which in many e-axle designs doubles as an **oil-cooling conduit** routing coolant along the shaft axis to extract rotor heat. Where the bore serves this cooling function, wall-thickness uniformity becomes both a thermal and a structural variable.

The analytically decisive boundary condition is not "hollow" alone but the **combination of a hollow cross-section with a high-speed dynamic balance limit at 16,000+ rpm** — a pairing most other vehicle shafts do not face. This distinguishes the rotor shaft from a downstream lay-shaft or half-shaft, which run at lower speed.

**Excluded** (admitted only as capability calibration): crankshafts, railway axles, and wind-turbine shafts. The RWTH Aachen study on hollow-forged AHD-steel rotor shafts for wind turbines calibrates the hollow-forging route on power density, cost, and global warming potential[Hollas et al., Hollow-forged AHD steel rotor shafts for wind turbines](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/1025193/files/1025193.pdf); a Lawrence Livermore report on flow-formed containment vessels[LLNL TR-442131 Rev.1 2010, flow forming](https://rampac.energy.gov/docs/default-source/whitepapers/flow-forming-paper-rev-1.pdf) calibrates flow forming. Every non-automotive case is flagged as calibration and never enters the NEV tier scoring.

**Material scope** is steel-centric, anchored on two grades: **20MnCr5** (case-hardening, the default where the shaft integrates carburized splines or gear teeth) and **42CrMo4** (through-hardening, favoured for plain high-speed torque carriers). Steel is the analytical default because its elastic modulus preserves bending stiffness at thin wall sections and its unit cost scales favourably at 100k+/yr volumes. Aluminium and titanium are formable by several routes but remain secondary: a lighter shaft lowers bending stiffness, driving the first bending critical speed downward toward the operating range — a penalty at 16,000+ rpm.

### 1.2 The six weighted criteria

Every route is scored 0–10 on six criteria whose weights sum to 1.00; the final score is the weighted sum. **Two cost-related criteria together carry exactly half the weight — the deliberate signature of a high-volume automotive model.**

| ID | Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| R-2 | Cost-Effectiveness & Scalability | 0.28 |
| R-3 | Required Subsequent Processing | 0.22 |
| R-1 | Suitable Materials & Metallurgy | 0.15 |
| R-4 | Geometric Capability | 0.15 |
| R-5 | Quality, Tolerance & Failure-Risk | 0.12 |
| R-6 | Industrial Maturity & NEV Fit | 0.08 |

**Cost-Effectiveness leads at 0.28** because NEV production is defined by high annual volume, where small per-part differences multiply across hundreds of thousands of units. The Materials Research Forum analysis of CWR notes it can manufacture a hollow shaft billet directly, greatly reducing production cost[Materials Research Forum cross-wedge rolling](https://mrforum.com/product/9781644903254-14/). **Required Subsequent Processing follows at 0.22** because the forming route is only the first stage; heat treatment, machining, grinding, and balancing often exceed the forming cost itself.

The cost weighting is governed by tooling amortisation. The University of Padova analysis of cold forging notes tooling capital is usually much higher than equipment investment[University of Padova cold forging tool cost](https://www.research.unipd.it/bitstream/11577/127790/1/1993CIRP_TOOL_COST.pdf), which is why **per-part cost at the 100k+/yr band, not raw machine cost, is the governing metric**. Because tooling capital amortises over volume, the cost ranking of routes inverts between low and high volume — the central economic structure the 0.28 weight captures. Forging cost estimation itself carries uncertainty: an Ohio University thesis reports an 8% error reduction over its benchmark[Ohio University thesis forging cost estimation](https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file/send?accession=ohiou1129661983&disposition=inline).

**Tier thresholds** (declared before scoring): Tier 1 at S_final ≥ 7.60, Tier 2 at 7.00–7.59, Tier 3 below 7.00.

### 1.3 Key vocabulary

- **Net-shape / near-net-shape / machining-from-solid** describe how close forming brings the part to final dimensions. Net-shape and near-net-shape conserve material and preserve grain flow; **machining-from-solid maximises scrap by converting bore volume into chips.** CWR sits at the near-net end[Materials Research Forum cross-wedge rolling](https://mrforum.com/product/9781644903254-14/); deep-hole drilling at the machining-from-solid end.
- **One-piece vs two-piece (assembled):** a one-piece route forms the entire shaft from one billet or tube with no weld interface; a two-piece route joins a tube to solid ends by rotary friction welding. The two-piece route uses a cheap seamless tube for the long span and solid steel only for feature-rich ends, but pays with a **weld interface that must survive high-cycle torsional fatigue.** Friction-welded parts deliver in weeks versus forging's months[American Friction Welding](https://teamafw.com/sourcing-solutions/).
- **TIR concentricity** (total indicated runout) measures how well bore and outer surface share a common axis. The **runout ≤0.10 mm balance class** is the working quality target a high-speed rotor shaft must meet to balance dynamically at 16,000+ rpm. A route's as-formed distance from this class directly sets its finish-grinding burden — the bridge between the Quality criterion and the heavily weighted Subsequent Processing criterion.

## 2. Functional Requirements and Design Drivers

The functional requirements of an NEV hollow rotor shaft form an interlocking set, not a checklist. The central point: **the hollow geometry is chosen because it serves three of the four design drivers at once** — it removes mass, creates a coolant passage, and lowers rotational inertia — while that same geometry makes the balance, fatigue, and heat-treatment requirements harder to satisfy. As thyssenkrupp frames it, the rotor shaft is the heart of the electric machine, converting electrical to kinetic energy and determining the speeds and torques at which the motor can operate[thyssenkrupp, "The perfect rotor shaft"](https://www.thyssenkrupp.com/en/stories/automotive-and-new-mobility/the-perfect-rotor-shaft).

### 2.1 Lightweighting and rotor inertia

Lightweighting is the first-order reason a shaft is made hollow: material removed from the core contributes little to torsional stiffness but adds directly to mass and rotational inertia. Hirschvogel's modular hollow-shaft concept for 140–360 kW electric-truck drives was explicitly motivated by lightweight design, reducing weight while saving manufacturing resources[Hirschvogel, "Rotor Shafts in Modular Design for Electric Trucks," ATZextra](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf). The mass saving depends on bore diameter and wall thickness, which Wang, Gong and Liu treat as design variables in their 2023 multi-objective optimisation of hollow-shaft direct-drive motors[Wang, Gong, Liu 2023, Multi-objective optimization of the hollow shaft direct drive motor](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/16878132231163079).

Reduced inertia lets the motor change speed faster for a given torque — the basis for the responsiveness NEV drivers perceive as instant throttle. Unlike raising peak torque, the hollow-shaft inertia reduction improves responsiveness without adding copper, magnet, or thermal load[Wang, Gong, Liu 2023, Multi-objective optimization of the hollow shaft direct drive motor](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/16878132231163079).

**The defining tension:** thinning the wall saves mass but reduces the torsional and bending stiffness on which high-speed operation depends. The design literature resolves this by treating bore diameter and wall thickness as **coupled optimisation variables bounded by a stiffness constraint**[Wang, Gong, Liu 2023, Multi-objective optimization of the hollow shaft direct drive motor](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/16878132231163079), rather than maximising the bore. This is the first point at which **wall-thickness uniformity emerges as a manufacturing requirement, not a geometric nicety**: a wall that varies around the circumference creates a mass asymmetry that degrades balance and locally reduces stiffness. The trade-off resolves only when a route capable of holding the wall uniformly is selected.

### 2.2 Thermal management and internal cooling

The hollow interior becomes a conduit for cooling oil reaching the rotor directly. A Newcastle University doctoral study (Gai, 2020) framed the problem: traction motors with high power-density face higher temperatures that harm efficiency, reliability, and life, motivating a hollow-shaft rotor-cooling system[Gai 2020, Hollow-Shaft Rotor Cooling System for Automotive Traction Motors, PhD thesis, Ne](https://theses.ncl.ac.uk/jspui/bitstream/10443/5024/1/Gai%20Y%202020.pdf). The bore thus serves a **dual role — mass reduction and coolant delivery** — which is why the hollow geometry dominates NEV rotor-shaft design.

Shaft-bore cooling reaches the rotor directly, where stator-jacket cooling reaches it only by conduction outward. CFD methodologies developed by Sharma et al. (2022)[Sharma et al. 2022, CFD for oil-cooled electric motor shaft, J. Fluid Flow Heat Mass Trans](https://jffhmt.avestia.com/2022/009.html) and Deriszadeh et al.[Deriszadeh et al., Model-based shaft cooling for automotive electric motor, J. Phys. Conf.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1742-6596/2893/1/012124) treat internal oil-cooling as a distinct optimisation problem.

**The manufacturing consequence is a direct coupling between thermal design freedom and forming route.** An arXiv study on rotor cooling with ducted liquid in a **completely cold-formed modular motor shaft** investigated four shaft geometries, coupling cooling-effective internal ducting with a route chosen to be simple and cost-effective to manufacture[High-Performance Rotor Cooling with Ducted Liquid in Cold-Formed Modular Motor Shaft, arXi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22029v1). By contrast, a deep-drilled bore yields only the straight cylindrical hole the drill traverses — which is why a 2024 defensive publication proposes a rotor-shaft insert to improve oil flow within an otherwise simple bore["Traction Drive Rotor Shaft Insert to Reduce Weight and Improve Oil Flow" 2024, Technical](https://www.tdcommons.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=8679&context=dpubs_series). Shaping the internal duct raises heat-transfer behaviour, supporting higher continuous power density[High-Performance Rotor Cooling with Ducted Liquid in Cold-Formed Modular Motor Shaft, arXi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22029v1).

A shaft carrying oil internally also needs **sealing and end-closure**, which favours two-piece and modular constructions that can incorporate a closure or sealing seat into one mating element[NETFORM, Two-Piece Hollow Rotor Shaft](https://netform.com/netforms-two-piece-hollow-rotor-shaft/)[Hirschvogel, "Rotor Shafts in Modular Design for Electric Trucks," ATZextra](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf). A single-piece shaft must machine its closures separately — a Subsequent Processing cost.

### 2.3 High-speed operation, balance, and NVH

NEV traction motors spin far faster than combustion engines, and the shaft must hold its **runout ≤0.10 mm balance class at 16,000+ rpm** — the hard geometric filter every candidate route must pass. The physics: centrifugal force from a mass eccentricity grows with the **square of rotational speed**, so a residual unbalance tolerable at modest speed becomes a significant vibration source as speed rises. This speed-squared dependence makes the balance requirement tighten as motors push toward higher power density.

Achieving the class depends on two coupled properties: **TIR concentricity** of bore relative to outer diameter, and **wall-thickness uniformity**. Routes that form bore and outer diameter in a single concentric operation hold concentricity more robustly than routes establishing the two surfaces separately. CWR can produce hollow shafts with a variable inner diameter, controlling internal geometry as it forms the outer profile[CWR hollow shafts with variable inner diameter, Archives of Civil and Mechanical Engineeri](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article); a deep-drilled bore relies entirely on independent drill accuracy.

Residual unbalance is also a **refinement attribute, not only a mechanical one**: it excites a synchronous vibration that sweeps across the audible range as the vehicle accelerates. The EMAG overview ties rising rotor-shaft quality requirements to electric mobility's demands[EMAG, "Rotor shafts in electric drives"](https://www.emag.com/blog/en/rotor-shafts-for-electric-drives/). In a quiet electric cabin there is little broadband sound to mask this tone — the structural reason the ≤0.10 mm class is enforced more stringently in NEV drives than durability alone would demand[Electric drive axle systems in NEVs, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/mechanical-engineering/articles/10.3389/fmech.2026.1745639/full).

### 2.4 Strength, fatigue, and integrated features

The shaft's core duty is transmitting torque under a cyclic load spectrum that drives torsional fatigue. The e-drive spectrum is distinctive: motors deliver high torque from very low speed and respond to sharp transients rather than gentle engine ramps.

**Grain flow influences fatigue.** A shaft machined from bar cuts across the original grain direction; a formed shaft (e.g. by CWR) carries grain flow shaped by the forming deformation[CWR hollow shafts with variable inner diameter, Archives of Civil and Mechanical Engineeri](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article) — a metallurgical advantage under high-cycle torsion. The AHD-steel hollow-forging case study examines a forging route specifically for power-density and fatigue implications[Hollas et al., Hollow-forged AHD steel rotor shafts for wind turbines](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/1025193/files/1025193.pdf).

The shaft is rarely a plain tube: it carries a spline or gear seat at the gearbox end, bearing journals, and a rotor-mount interface[EMAG, "Rotor shafts in electric drives"](https://www.emag.com/blog/en/rotor-shafts-for-electric-drives/). **The number and precision of these features make the shaft machining-intensive regardless of how the blank is formed** — splines, journals, and seats all demand tolerances the forming operation cannot reach. They define a floor of Subsequent Processing that no route escapes, but a near-net-shape blank arrives with its stepped profile close to finished, shifting the burden from bulk removal to finishing[CNC Machining: NEV Motor Housings and Shafts, YPMFG](https://ypmfg.com/blog/cnc-machining-solutions-new-energy-vehicle-motor-housings-and-shafts/).

**Heat treatment creates a sequencing constraint that reaches back into route selection.** Hardening distorts the shaft, so final precision features must be ground *after* hardening. A thin-walled hollow shaft is more susceptible to hardening distortion than a solid bar of the same OD — the hollow geometry that serves lightweighting and cooling makes the shaft harder to hold flat through heat treatment. The route minimising pre-hardening distortion and leaving least grinding stock carries an advantage.

### 2.5 Synthesis: coupled, not additive

| Design driver | Governing requirement | Manufacturing property demanded | Route consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lightweighting & inertia | Mass/inertia reduction via bore | Controlled wall at stiffness-bounded minimum | Favours near-net forming over chip removal |
| Thermal management | Bore as coolant passage | Shaped internal channel + sealed closures | Favours cold-formed and two-piece routes |
| High-speed balance & NVH | Runout ≤0.10 mm at 16,000+ rpm | TIR concentricity + wall uniformity | Favours concentric one-operation forming |
| Strength, fatigue & features | Torsional fatigue + integrated features | Favourable grain flow + heat-treat compatibility | Favours formed grain flow; imposes post-hardening grinding on all |

The requirements couple rather than add: the thin wall that lightweighting rewards reduces stiffness; the bore that cooling exploits must stay concentric for balance; the hardening that strength demands distorts the concentricity balance requires. **No route can be chosen against any single requirement in isolation.**

## 3. Taxonomy of Manufacturing Routes

Three structurally distinct families create the bore by fundamentally different physical principles: **bulk and incremental forming** (plastic deformation), **material-removal machining-from-solid**, and **joining-based two-piece assembly**. The core organizing claim: a one-piece vs two-piece distinction, crossed with a forming-versus-machining distinction, is sufficient to place every candidate and predict its cost and quality profile.

### 3.1 Forming routes and the temperature master variable

Forming divides into **incremental** routes (rotary swaging, flow forming, rotary compression) that work metal in many small localized steps, and **bulk** routes (CWR, three-roll skew rolling, radial forging) that impose large shape change in one or few passes. Among the eight forming mechanisms, **rotary swaging carries the most direct peer-reviewed validation for hollow motor shafts** — a 2025 *Journal of Cleaner Production* study validated lightweight manufacturing via rotary swaging for hollow railway motor shafts[Lightweight manufacturing via rotary swaging: validation for hollow railway motor shafts,](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652625003610).

The defining variable is **shape change per pass versus achievable form control.** Incremental routes work over a mandrel and best hold wall-thickness uniformity; bulk routes are the highest-throughput, highest single-pass routes that trade form tolerance for speed. The NEV cooling-bore requirement tilts the balance toward the incremental corner wherever internal surface quality is functional.

**Forming temperature is the master variable** coupling process selection to material suitability and downstream heat-treatment burden:

| Regime | Shape change/pass | Tolerance off-press | Typical routes | Heat-treat consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold | Low–moderate | Tightest | Swaging, flow forming, extrusion | Shortest chain; cold-formed cooling shaft demonstrated[High-Performance Rotor Cooling with Ducted Liquid in Cold-Formed Modular Motor Shaft, arXi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22029v1) |
| Warm | Moderate | Intermediate | Swaging, some forging | Moderate; case-hardening still typical |
| Hot | Largest | Loosest (scale) | CWR, skew rolling, radial forging | Longest chain; full finishing required |

The arXiv cold-formed modular shaft study demonstrates a complete rotor-cooling shaft produced by cold forming alone[High-Performance Rotor Cooling with Ducted Liquid in Cold-Formed Modular Motor Shaft, arXi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22029v1), establishing cold forming as viable for at least some NEV geometries. But cold forming cannot impose large single-pass shape change on high-strength billets without cracking risk; where strength demands exceed the formability envelope, warm and hot regimes remain necessary. **Cold minimizes downstream processing but limits strength and shape change; hot maximizes shape change but lengthens the finishing chain.**

### 3.2 Machining-from-solid as the baseline

Deep-hole drilling (gun, BTA, trepanning) serves as the **machining-from-solid baseline** against which every forming route is measured — the routes that seek to replace it define themselves against it[NETFORM, Two-Piece Hollow Rotor Shaft](https://netform.com/netforms-two-piece-hollow-rotor-shaft/)[Lightweight manufacturing via rotary swaging: validation for hollow railway motor shafts,](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652625003610). Its geometric strength is **generality**: any bore diameter and stepped profile achievable by turning can be produced, with no formability requirement, so any forgeable grade can be machined.

The metallurgical cost of that flexibility is **grain-flow continuity**: drilling cuts across longitudinal fibres, while forming routes follow the formed contour — fatigue-relevant under cyclic torsion. The cost structure is where the baseline becomes vulnerable: two penalties compound at volume — **scrap/yield-loss** (bore removed as chips) and **cycle time** (deep bores are slow). At NEV platform volumes these make per-part cost uncompetitive with swaging or two-piece joining.

The "de-facto standard" status is true for the installed process base but **provisional against the forming frontier**: the arXiv cold-formed shaft never passes through a drilling step at all[High-Performance Rotor Cooling with Ducted Liquid in Cold-Formed Modular Motor Shaft, arXi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22029v1). Drilling earns the strongest maturity verdict for prototype work but a volume-conditional verdict for platform economics.

### 3.3 Joining-based two-piece routes

Two-piece routes bond a tube and one or two solid ends rather than forming a single blank. NETFORM markets a two-piece hollow rotor shaft[NETFORM, Two-Piece Hollow Rotor Shaft](https://netform.com/netforms-two-piece-hollow-rotor-shaft/); Hirschvogel has developed a modular multi-part concept for 140–360 kW electric-truck drives[Hirschvogel, "Rotor Shafts in Modular Design for Electric Trucks," ATZextra](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf). **Rotary friction welding** is the primary joining mechanism: it spins one member against a stationary member under axial force, plasticizing the interface, then forge-upsets to consolidate the bond in the solid state — avoiding fusion-pool defects. İğdik and Özes characterized the mechanical properties of a friction-welded tube-yoke joint[İğdik & Özes, Mechanical properties of friction-welded tube-yoke joint](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/178a/ab662492865b4278e534192bd514381b5f8e.pdf).

The geometric advantage is **independent optimization**: a flow-formed or drawn tube supplies wall-thickness uniformity while solid ends carry splines, bearings, and interfaces; a quench-and-temper tube can be joined to case-hardening ends. The cost case is **volume-gated**: the fixed cost of weld qualification amortizes across platform volume, after which the per-part saving from a bought-in tube dominates.

The governing failure mode is the weld line. Welding residual stresses influence fatigue crack propagation[Experimental method: welding residual stresses and fatigue crack propagation, arXiv:1709.0](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06942v1), so the forge-upset residual field can accelerate crack growth, forcing residual-stress-aware qualification[Fatigue Strength Assessment of Friction Welds under Residual Stress, Materials 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/17/13/3130). **The tension resolves not by eliminating the weld but by qualifying it:** the residual field becomes a controlled design variable under a standardized procedure such as the IIW fatigue-design basis for welded hollow-section joints[Fatigue Design Procedure for Welded Hollow Section Joints IIW](https://www.zlibrary.to/dl/fatigue-design-procedure-for-welded-hollow-section-joints-recommendations-of-iiw-subcommission-xv-e-0). Where the weld sits in a low-stress region, the joint-risk penalty shrinks and the material saving dominates.

**The integrity-versus-cost contest is a volume-dependent crossover, not a fixed ranking:** at prototype scale the one-piece route wins by avoiding qualification fixed cost; at high platform volume the two-piece route's material saving overtakes, provided IIW-class fatigue design governs.

| Dimension | One-piece (formed/drilled) | Two-piece (welded) |
|---|---|---|
| Integrity risk | Distributed; no weld line | Localized at weld[Experimental method: welding residual stresses and fatigue crack propagation, arXiv:1709.0](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06942v1)[Fatigue Strength Assessment of Friction Welds under Residual Stress, Materials 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/17/13/3130) |
| Material yield | Cold-form displaces; drill removes[High-Performance Rotor Cooling with Ducted Liquid in Cold-Formed Modular Motor Shaft, arXi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22029v1) | Tube bought at commodity cost[Hirschvogel, "Rotor Shafts in Modular Design for Electric Trucks," ATZextra](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf) |
| Member optimization | Single grade | Tube and ends independently[Hirschvogel, "Rotor Shafts in Modular Design for Electric Trucks," ATZextra](https://www.hirschvogel.com/fileadmin/editor/05_News_Media/Downloads/Media/2305_ATZextraHeavyDuty_Hirschvogel_en.pdf) |
| Volume fit | Prototype/low | High platform[NETFORM, Two-Piece Hollow Rotor Shaft](https://netform.com/netforms-two-piece-hollow-rotor-shaft/) |
| Qualification burden | None added | IIW-class fatigue design[Fatigue Design Procedure for Welded Hollow Section Joints IIW](https://www.zlibrary.to/dl/fatigue-design-procedure-for-welded-hollow-section-joints-recommendations-of-iiw-subcommission-xv-e-0) |

### 3.4 Hybrid chains: the production norm

**No forming route delivers spline, bearing-seat, and balance-class tolerances directly off the press**, so every forming route in practice becomes a **forming-plus-machining chain** at its functional interfaces. The hybrid resolves the pure-route tension by sequencing: form the bulk near-net and machine only the interfaces, confining machining stock to the small fraction needing tight tolerance. **Near-net-shape capability is the single property most directly governing a forming route's competitiveness against the machining baseline** — the smaller the post-form stock allowance, the more of the material-yield advantage survives. Rotary swaging and flow forming, working incrementally over a mandrel, are the strongest near-net performers[Lightweight manufacturing via rotary swaging: validation for hollow railway motor shafts,](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652625003610).

## 4. Forming Techniques: Per-Route Analysis

### 4.1 Rotary Swaging

**Mechanism & geometry.** Dies in a rotating head oscillate and strike the workpiece radially at high frequency; over an internal mandrel, the same action that shapes the OD calibrates the bore, controlling both surfaces in one operation. The governing variable is radial die infeed, which Charni et al. identify as controlling the resulting mechanical state[Charni et al. 2021, Mechanical properties of rotary swaged steel components, Forschung im](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10010-021-00498-3.pdf). This **simultaneous inner-and-outer capability** suits the hollow rotor shaft, where wall-thickness control is the central geometric demand[Structural Phenomena Introduced by Rotary Swaging: A Review, Materials 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/17/2/466).

**Materials.** Documented across plain and alloy steels[Charni et al. 2021, Mechanical properties of rotary swaged steel components, Forschung im](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10010-021-00498-3.pdf), near-α titanium[Severe plastic deformation by rotary swaging of Ti-2.5Al-2.6Zr, arXiv:2401.17672](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17672v1), Cu–Cr composite wire[Cu–Cr composite wire by two-stage swaging plus aging](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1359836821009331), aged 6xxx aluminium[Aged 6xxx aluminum during rotary swaging, Crystals](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/crystals/crystals-12-00530/article_deploy/crystals-12-00530-v2.pdf), tungsten heavy alloy[Rotary swaged tungsten heavy alloy at variable temperature, Materials](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/materials/materials-12-04200/article_deploy/materials-12-04200.pdf), and axle steel rod[Effect of Rotary Swaging on Mechanical Behaviors of Axle Steel Rod, Materials](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/17/11/2525) — the broadest evidenced material envelope of any route. A recurring finding: swaging imposes a radial strain gradient toward the worked surface, placing hardened, work-strengthened material where torsional shear stress peaks (the outer fibre).

**Cost.** The economic case rests on material utilisation and the chipless process. The Xi'an Jiaotong validation positions it as a low-carbon, resource-saving method for hollow railway motor shafts[^35]: metal that would become swarf in a turned shaft stays in the part. At passenger-car volumes the material saving repays the moderate, comparatively reconfigurable tooling.

**Subsequent processing.** Near-net diameters mean a finishing chain (carburizing of 20MnCr5, finish grinding of seats, spline cutting, straightening, balancing to ≤0.10 mm) rather than bulk shaping. The one-piece swaged shaft forms its bore and profile together, **avoiding the post-weld heat treatment and weld inspection the two-piece route adds.**

**Quality.** Many small radial blows average out local variation and centre the part, supporting good TIR concentricity. The principal caveat is the sub-surface residual-stress state: because it tracks radial infeed[Charni et al. 2021, Mechanical properties of rotary swaged steel components, Forschung im](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10010-021-00498-3.pdf), **the same infeed that creates a beneficial compressive surface also governs a potentially harmful sub-surface tensile zone — the tension resolves through parameter control**, setting infeed within the mapped window to capture surface benefit without the depth penalty. Unlike CWR's centre-cavity risk, swaging's characteristic defect is mandrel-driven eccentricity at high L/D.

**Cold vs hot.** The cold variant work-hardens the surface and delivers tighter tolerance; the hot variant lowers flow stress for heavier sections. The choice carries its heaviest consequence in metallurgy: **hot swaging recrystallises the structure into a more predictable heat-treat response**, while cold swaging leaves a heavily deformed, internally stressed structure that may warrant a stress-relief anneal before carburizing[Response of A356 to warm rotary forming and subsequent T6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1212.5260v1)[Rotary swaged tungsten heavy alloy at variable temperature, Materials](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/materials/materials-12-04200/article_deploy/materials-12-04200.pdf). For the thin-wall passenger-car shaft, the cold variant holds the application on tolerance, surface, and energy grounds; the hot variant remains relevant to heavy railway and commercial-vehicle shafts.

**Maturity & NEV fit.** Rotary swaging has the most directly transferable hollow-motor-shaft validation[Lightweight manufacturing via rotary swaging: validation for hollow railway motor shafts,](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652625003610) and a commercial supplier base. As NEV rotors move toward direct shaft cooling[High-Performance Rotor Cooling with Ducted Liquid in Cold-Formed Modular Motor Shaft, arXi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22029v1), **a cold-forming route that already shapes internal geometry is well positioned to form integrated cooling features with less disruption than routes forming only the outer surface.** Of the forming routes, it is best-positioned on the combined strength of direct validation, short finishing chain, and internal-geometry capability.

### 4.2 Radial Forging

**Mechanism & geometry.** Two or four hammer dies strike synchronously and radially while the part rotates and feeds axially; a mandrel controls the bore. Sang (2023) used Simufact to optimise a full-hollow NEV shaft: 1.25 mm per-pass reduction, 8° die inlet angle, 40 r/min, 3 mm axial feed[Parcha deepresearch benchmark, report_086](https://github.com/Parcha-ai/grep-v2-deepresearch-bench/blob/main/reports/report_086.md). The route extends the radial-die principle to heavier sections than swaging can reach.

**Materials & fatigue.** The signature metallurgical benefit is **grain-flow continuity**: synchronised strokes orient grain along the contour rather than cutting through it. The causal chain: continuous flow following the fillet contour → fewer flow-line terminations at the surface → fewer crack-initiation sites under cyclic torsion → raised fatigue limit versus a machined-from-solid equivalent. Compatible with 42CrMo4 and 20MnCr5.

**Cost & quality.** Tooling-intensive: per-part economics improve sharply with volume. **The tension — its strongest asset (through-section grain flow) is bundled with its weakest feature (high tooling capital) — resolves along the volume axis:** at the high volumes typical of NEV traction-shaft production, fixed tooling amortises to a small per-part figure, leaving the route to compete on fatigue merit. The signature risk is the internal cavity that opens under wrong parameters; **the simulation-driven parameter set designs the defect out at the Simufact stage**[Parcha deepresearch benchmark, report_086](https://github.com/Parcha-ai/grep-v2-deepresearch-bench/blob/main/reports/report_086.md). Finishing must not cut away the worked skin at highly stressed fillets.

**NEV fit.** Mature in axle and shaft production. Through 2030, as torque density rises and walls thin, the grain-flow fatigue margin becomes more valuable, favouring radial forging in the fatigue-critical, high-volume corner — the niche rotary swaging's lower-cost, concentricity-priority profile does not fully cover.

### 4.3 Cross-Wedge Rolling (CWR)

**Mechanism & geometry.** Wedge-shaped tools engage a rotating heated billet and progressively reduce local diameter to a stepped profile. Critically, **CWR can manufacture a hollow shaft billet directly, greatly reducing production cost**[Materials Research Forum cross-wedge rolling](https://mrforum.com/product/9781644903254-14/), and research demonstrates rolling of hollow shafts with a *variable inner diameter*[Cross-wedge rolling hollow shafts with variable inner diameter, Archives of Civil and Mech](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1644966519300871) — which can map onto an oil-cooling bore that varies along the shaft. Unlike swaging, which builds the bore over a mandrel, CWR can roll a pre-pierced hollow billet to a stepped variable-ID profile in a single traverse.

**Materials.** Applied to medium-carbon and alloy steels including 20MnCr5 and 42CrMo4; the hot process aligns grain flow along the axis[Cross-wedge rolling hollow shafts with variable inner diameter, Archives of Civil and Mech](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1644966519300871).

**Cost.** Per-part cost at 100k+/yr is **among the lowest of any route**, because CWR produces the hollow stepped billet directly, removing both bore-drilling and much rough-turning[Materials Research Forum cross-wedge rolling](https://mrforum.com/product/9781644903254-14/). Part-specific wedge dies must amortize over volume — the route's advantage is strongest at high volume and weakest at low volume. CWR trades tooling flexibility for the lowest per-part cost at scale.

**Subsequent processing & quality.** The bore-drilling step is eliminated entirely — the core reason CWR scores strongly on the heavily weighted Subsequent Processing criterion. The characteristic risk is the **Mannesmann central cavity**: the radial tensile stress at the billet centre during rolling can nucleate an axial void[Pater 2025, Numerical modelling of fracture caused by the Mannesmann effect, J. Manufactur](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1526612525002877)[Ghiotti et al. 2009, Modelling of the Mannesmann effect, CIRP Annals](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0007850609000705). Mandrel design and roll-pass scheduling control inner-diameter uniformity and suppress the cavity[Effect of mandrels on hollow shafts in cross-wedge rolling, Int. J. Adv. Manuf. Tech.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00170-018-3093-4)[Inner-diameter uniformity of hollow shafts in CWR](https://yadda.icm.edu.pl/baztech/element/bwmeta1.element.baztech-9759564a-3826-4024-8fe8-44d731213fbe)[Stability in cross-wedge rolling of hollowed shafts](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924013604006880); hot-rolled routes therefore require volumetric ultrasonic inspection of the bore region. A central-defect control scheme for railway axles documents the process-window discipline making the route dependable at scale[Central Defects in Cross Wedge Rolling of Railway Axles, Metals 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4701/13/7/1309).

### 4.4 Pressure-Driven Routes: Volume Splits the Field

The pressure- and rotation-driven family partitions cleanly by production volume and feature type. **Cold extrusion and flow forming are the two load-bearing NEV candidates — mirror images that split by volume.**

**Cold / composite extrusion** forces a billet through tooling at room temperature into a near-net shape; backward extrusion opens the bore while combined forward-backward strokes form stepped diameters. The arXiv cold-formed modular shaft demonstrates internal cooling features cold-formed rather than machined[High-Performance Rotor Cooling with Ducted Liquid in Cold-Formed Modular Motor Shaft, arXi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22029v1), and the 2024 defensive publication confirms weight reduction and oil-flow improvement as active objectives["Traction Drive Rotor Shaft Insert to Reduce Weight and Improve Oil Flow" 2024, Technical](https://www.tdcommons.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=8679&context=dpubs_series). **The paradox: the very lightweighting that makes a hollow shaft attractive raises the cold-extrusion force needed to form it.** It resolves through **process segmentation** — distributing deformation across several lower-force stages, capping peak tonnage at the price of more stations. Work hardening forces intermediate anneals, after which 20MnCr5 culminates in carburizing, finish machining, grinding, and balancing. Cold extrusion earns the **strongest pass for high-volume one-piece hollow shafts**, front-loading tooling capital and winning at the 100k+/yr band with integrated internal features.

**Flow forming (spinning)** presses rollers against a rotating tubular blank over a mandrel, thinning and elongating the wall. Its tooling — principally a mandrel and roller set — is minimal, making it **structurally suited to small-batch and design-change production**: a geometry change is absorbed by reprogramming the roller traverse rather than cutting a new die. Its geometric strength is **long, large-diameter, thin uniform walls** held against the mandrel — exactly the regime a lightweighted shaft occupies, with wall uniformity directly supporting the ≤0.10 mm class. Its economic signature is the inverse of cold extrusion's: minimal fixed cost, but traverse-bound cycle time that does not amortise, so per-part cost plateaus while extrusion's keeps falling. **Flow forming wins at low volume and in niche large-diameter variants; the mainstream high-runner volume migrates to cold extrusion or swaging.**

**Rotary compression (orbital forging)** presses a tilted, orbiting die against a small crescent contact zone, so instantaneous force is far below a full-face upset — a smaller press can form a flange. Its envelope is **end-features (flanges, collars, coupling geometries), not long stepped bodies**, making it a two-piece flange-forming enabler tied to assembled-shaft architectures[NETFORM, Two-Piece Hollow Rotor Shaft](https://netform.com/netforms-two-piece-hollow-rotor-shaft/)[thyssenkrupp, "The perfect rotor shaft"](https://www.thyssenkrupp.com/en/stories/automotive-and-new-mobility/the-perfect-rotor-shaft). **The tension — capital savings through lower tonnage versus a cycle-time cost from orbit accumulation — resolves against volume:** attractive at low-to-moderate batch sizes, uncompetitive at the highest NEV runner rates.

**Hollow forging with AHD steel** is the highest-upside, weakest-evidence route. Its appeal: forged strength combined with direct inner-contour forming and, through air-hardening metallurgy, the potential to **eliminate the liquid quench step entirely.** Removing the quench removes quench-distortion, which reduces straightening and grinding stock — directly attacking the distortion-and-grind burden that dominates every conventionally hardened shaft's downstream chain. **The decisive caveat, stated without hedging: the strongest case-study evidence is from a wind-energy shaft application and transfers to NEV only by analogy**[Hollas et al., Hollow-forged AHD steel rotor shafts for wind turbines](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/1025193/files/1025193.pdf) — the scale, load spectrum, and governing balance-class constraint all differ. The route is ranked a **watch-and-pilot candidate**, with a demonstrated NEV build at automotive scale and balance class the specific gap that would upgrade its status. Its natural entry point is the high-torque, larger-diameter commercial-vehicle end nearer the wind case.

## 5. Machining and Joining Routes

### 5.1 Deep-Hole Drilling (machining-from-solid baseline)

**Gun drilling** is the single-flute, self-piloting method for long, straight, small-diameter bores (≈Ø1–50 mm, L/D up to ~100:1, straightness ~0.05–0.1 mm, Ra ≤1.6 µm in a well-controlled operation) — L/D reach **no forming route in this report can match**. **BTA drilling** reverses the coolant and chip path for larger bores (≈Ø20–500 mm) at higher removal rate; the **trepanning** variant cuts an annular groove and recovers a solid core rather than reducing the full volume to chip.

Drilling is **metallurgically indifferent** — it cuts any machinable shaft steel (20MnCr5, 42CrMo4[Recommendations for Dry Forming of 16MnCr5 and 42CrMo4 in Cold Forging, IOT RWTH Aachen](https://www.iot.rwth-aachen.de/go/id/qrty/file/659687), AHD[Hollas et al., Hollow-forged AHD steel rotor shafts for wind turbines](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/1025193/files/1025193.pdf)) — but contributes no work-hardening or grain-flow benefit; the bore is geometry only. Where swaging couples bore formation to strengthening, **drilling decouples them entirely.** It carries **none of the forming-route defect populations** — no Mannesmann central fracture[Pater 2025, Numerical modelling of fracture caused by the Mannesmann effect, J. Manufactur](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1526612525002877), no ID-stability failures[Stability in cross-wedge rolling of hollowed shafts](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924013604006880) — so it passes cleanly on defect risk.

**The defining economic property — and the reason it is the cost baseline — is that the bore volume is converted to chip and lost.** For an illustrative Ø50 mm × 400 mm shaft with a Ø25 mm bore, the bore removes ~one quarter of the cross-sectional area. The chip cannot be recovered at grade value: premium 20MnCr5 or 42CrMo4 swarf re-enters the scrap stream at commodity price. An engineering estimate places the net material penalty at **~25–40% of blank cost** at prevailing steel prices, scaling with bore fraction and alloy premium. For NEV programmes above ~50k units/yr, the break-even at which a forming cell repays its capital falls well within a single production year. **Drilling's role is to set the cost floor the forming routes must beat, not to win the production mandate.**

### 5.2 Rotary Friction Welding (two-piece)

The route joins a hollow tube to a solid forged or machined end carrying splines, bearing seats, or a flange — building the shaft by assembly rather than hole-making. The geometric advantage: **the shaft body can be a thin-wall tube selected independently of the solid end**, with wall-thickness control a matter of tube selection. The solid end's features are formed before welding, sidestepping centre-drilling entirely.

The **headline economic argument is weight and cost reduction by avoiding centre drilling** — a tube arrives already hollow, so the bore is never machined away as chip. The solid-state process permits dissimilar-grade joints, placing premium alloy only at the loaded functional end. Weld cells are highly automatable with short cycles, supporting the 100k+/yr band, but the economics must absorb the weld cell and mandatory weld inspection.

**The defining quality concern: the weld seam is a fatigue-initiation candidate under torsional load at high speed** — a failure mode no one-piece route carries. **The paradox: the route that saves the most material introduces the only intrinsic fatigue-initiation feature.** It resolves not by avoidance but by **verification**: disciplined process control plus NDT of the weld line brings reliability within traction-shaft requirements. The route also tolerates weld placement at an axial position of low cyclic torsional stress, removing the initiation driver. Required inspection is a weld regime — prototype destructive testing plus production sampling per specification[Weld Testing, American Friction Welding](https://teamafw.com/weld-testing/), with phased-array ultrasonic testing applicable to the circumferential seam[PAUT of Longitudinal Seams/Welds and Pipes, Sonotron NDT](http://www.sonotronndt.com/LONG_WELDS.htm).

By 2030, the two-piece route is likely to consolidate in programmes pairing a large-bore thin-wall tube with a premium functional end, conditional on inline NDT automation maturing.

### 6.3 Quality and failure-risk

The sharpest failure-risk divide is **not between cold and hot forming but between monolithic and welded construction** — only the welded route adds a discrete joint demanding its own inspection regime[Experimental method: welding residual stresses and fatigue crack propagation, arXiv:1709.0](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06942v1)[Fatigue Strength Assessment of Friction Welds under Residual Stress, Materials 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/17/13/3130).

On concentricity, forming over a mandrel references bore and OD to the same tool, tightening wall uniformity versus routes referencing the two surfaces independently. The residual-stress contrast the evidence supports is the welded-versus-monolithic one: cold-forming imposes a field whose sign depends on infeed, while welding introduces a fatigue-relevant stress concentration at the joint[Experimental method: welding residual stresses and fatigue crack propagation, arXiv:1709.0](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06942v1)[Fatigue Strength Assessment of Friction Welds under Residual Stress, Materials 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/17/13/3130).

On fatigue, swaging has the only verified hollow-shaft validation plus a mechanical-property literature[Effect of Rotary Swaging on Mechanical Behaviors of Axle Steel Rod, Materials](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/17/11/2525)[Severe plastic deformation by rotary swaging of Ti-2.5Al-2.6Zr, arXiv:2401.17672](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17672v1)[Charni et al. 2021, Mechanical properties of rotary swaged steel components, Forschung im](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10010-021-00498-3.pdf). **What the corpus does not contain is a direct torsional-fatigue comparison of a swaged hollow shaft against a machined-from-solid blank — so the claim that forming lifts the fatigue limit above the machined baseline is a hypothesis, not a measured result.** This is the single highest-value experiment the programme could commission.

The welded route's inspection requirement is correctly framed as a regime — prototype destructive testing plus production sampling per specification[Weld Testing, American Friction Welding](https://teamafw.com/weld-testing/), with PAUT for the circumferential weld[PAUT of Longitudinal Seams/Welds and Pipes, Sonotron NDT](http://www.sonotronndt.com/LONG_WELDS.htm) — not a blanket per-part mandate. **The defect-risk verdict: the welded route may impose an additional inspection cost the monolithic routes avoid, and that cost belongs in the economics, not hidden in a nominal material saving.**

## 7. Required Subsequent Processing by Route

The forming operation is often only half the story: the subsequent-processing chain frequently dominates per-part cost. **The near-net forming routes shift cost out of the machining cell and into the forming cell, while machining-from-solid and the hot-pierced rolling routes do the opposite.**

### 7.1 Heat treatment and straightening

Heat-treat requirement varies not by route but by grade. 20MnCr5 is carburized then quenched; 42CrMo4 is through-hardened, quenched, and tempered. Both share one liability: **the liquid quench imposes a steep surface-to-core thermal gradient that drives distortion in slender shafts**, forcing post-hardening press or rotary straightening.

**The AHD hollow-forging route is the only one whose air-hardening metallurgy offers the potential to *delete* rather than merely shorten the liquid quench**[Hollas et al., Hollow-forged AHD steel rotor shafts for wind turbines](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/1025193/files/1025193.pdf) — the single most consequential downstream difference. Because the steel hardens in still air, the quench-bow mechanism is largely absent and the straightening operation shrinks toward a light corrective pass.

Distortion tracks wall uniformity entering the quench: **poor mandrel control leaves a non-uniform ID → asymmetric wall → uneven quench distortion → heavier straightening load**[Effect of mandrels on hollow shafts in cross-wedge rolling, Int. J. Adv. Manuf. Tech.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00170-018-3093-4)[Inner-diameter uniformity of hollow shafts in CWR](https://yadda.icm.edu.pl/baztech/element/bwmeta1.element.baztech-9759564a-3826-4024-8fe8-44d731213fbe). Uniform-wall routes (flow forming, swaging) distort predictably and symmetrically.

On residual stress, **the cold-formed routes work-harden the surface into a beneficial compressive layer** that retards fatigue crack initiation where torsional stress peaks. The accompanying internal cold-work stress is managed by a low-temperature relief pass that stabilises bulk dimensions without erasing the shallow surface compression. **The hot-pierced routes carry the opposite, more dangerous signature:** radial tensile stress at the core drives the Mannesmann effect — uncontrolled rolling generates a tensile core → void nucleation → central cavity[Ghiotti et al. 2009, Modelling of the Mannesmann effect, CIRP Annals](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0007850609000705)[Pater 2025, Numerical modelling of fracture caused by the Mannesmann effect, J. Manufactur](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1526612525002877). For these routes, residual-stress management is inseparable from forming-time process control. The welded route localises tensile residual stress at the joint, managed by a post-weld stress-relief anneal[Fatigue Strength Assessment of Friction Welds under Residual Stress, Materials 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/17/13/3130).

### 7.2 Machining, grinding, and feature formation

Here the logic inverts: **forming geometry, not metallurgy, governs how much stock must be removed.**

**Journal finishing** is near-universal, but stock removed differs sharply. Flow forming and swaging deliver near-finished concentric surfaces needing only a light grind[Lightweight manufacturing via rotary swaging: validation for hollow railway motor shafts,](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652625003610); CWR and skew rolling leave coarser, more eccentric surfaces demanding full turn-plus-grind[Effect of mandrels on hollow shafts in cross-wedge rolling, Int. J. Adv. Manuf. Tech.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00170-018-3093-4); drilling-from-solid achieves excellent roundness but at the highest scrap.

**Splines and gear seats** can be roll-formed (chipless, fatigue-enhancing, compressive) or cut. **The tension: rolling imposes high radial force a thin hollow wall may not survive.** The expected preference for chipless rolling does not hold universally on hollow shafts — where the wall is thick enough or internally supported, rolling wins; where thin and unsupported, cutting is chosen despite lower fatigue benefit. **This links directly to lightweighting: the thinner the wall, the narrower the window in which spline rolling remains feasible.** CWR and cold extrusion can carry stepped diameters and near-net splines out of the forming cell[Cross-wedge rolling hollow shafts with variable inner diameter, Archives of Civil and Mech](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1644966519300871); drilling and friction welding deliver featureless blanks loading the entire spline burden onto machining.

**Machining allowance** is the single variable converting forming precision into downstream cost. Flow forming and swaging leave the least; CWR and skew rolling leave more; drilling removes the entire bore plus full outer profile. **Outer-surface allowance and integrated-feature capability move together — near-net capability is a compound advantage, not a single-axis one.**

| Route | Outer allowance | Integrated features | Journal finishing | Spline method |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flow forming | Lowest | Stepped walls | Light grind | Roll (if wall permits) |
| Rotary swaging | Lowest | Stepped diameters | Light grind | Roll/cut |
| Cold extrusion | Low | Splines, internal profile | Light-med grind | Near-net |
| Radial forging | Low-med | Stepped diameters | Medium grind | Cut/roll |
| CWR | Medium | Stepped, variable ID | Turn + grind | Cut |
| Skew rolling | Medium | Stepped diameters | Turn + grind | Cut |
| Hollow forging (AHD) | Medium | Stepped diameters | Turn + grind | Cut |
| BTA drilling | Highest | None | Turn + grind | Cut |
| Friction welding | Local (flash) | None | Turn + grind | Cut |

### 7.3 Inspection, balancing, and finishing

These terminal quality gates apply to every route, but their severity inherits the forming route's defect signature.

**Dynamic balancing** to ≤0.10 mm ends every route, but correction varies: flow forming and swaging arrive nearly balanced and need only a trim; hot-pierced and welded shafts arrive with higher inherent unbalance, the latter adding a concentricity error source at the weld[İğdik & Özes, Mechanical properties of friction-welded tube-yoke joint](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/178a/ab662492865b4278e534192bd514381b5f8e.pdf).

**NDT focus follows forming physics:** the hot-pierced routes require volumetric ultrasonic inspection for Mannesmann cavities[Ghiotti et al. 2009, Modelling of the Mannesmann effect, CIRP Annals](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0007850609000705)[Pater 2025, Numerical modelling of fracture caused by the Mannesmann effect, J. Manufactur](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1526612525002877); the cold-formed routes need only surface and dimensional inspection; the welded route concentrates inspection at a single critical plane[Weld Testing, American Friction Welding](https://teamafw.com/weld-testing/)[PAUT of Longitudinal Seams/Welds and Pipes, Sonotron NDT](http://www.sonotronndt.com/LONG_WELDS.htm). **Internal-cavity cleaning** is required after deep-hole drilling — residual swarf in an oil-feed channel is a contamination risk resolved by high-pressure flushing.

**Surface treatment** interacts with residual stress: the cold-formed routes arrive with a fatigue-protective compressive surface built in, while hot-pierced and welded routes may have to buy that property back with shot-peening or roller-burnishing at critical fillets and weld regions[Experimental method: welding residual stresses and fatigue crack propagation, arXiv:1709.0](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06942v1).

**Across all three downstream work-centres, the same route families repeat their ranking: the near-net cold-formed routes (flow forming, rotary swaging) minimise the total downstream burden on every axis, and the machining-from-solid and two-piece welded routes maximise it.** This convergence across three independent work-centres is the quantitative foundation of the final ranking.

AHD hollow forging earns the field's only 9.00 on Materials yet sits in Tier 2 on a 6.00 Cost rating — the clearest illustration of why a weighted sum, not a single-axis champion, governs.

### 8.2 Tier classification

- **Tier 1 (≥7.60):** Rotary Swaging (8.18), Cross-Wedge Rolling (8.15), Radial Forging (7.70).
- **Tier 2 (7.00–7.59):** Hollow Forging AHD (7.30), Cold/Composite Extrusion (7.28), Rotary Compression (7.25), Three-Roll Skew Rolling (7.18), Flow Forming (7.00).
- **Tier 3 (<7.00):** Deep-Hole/BTA Drilling (6.95), Rotary Friction Welding (6.78).

**All three Tier-1 routes displace rather than remove the bore material and all are one-piece routes, whereas every Tier-3 route either removes the bore as scrap or creates a two-piece joint** — the two structural features the weighting most heavily penalises. CWR's dominant axis is Geometry (mandrel-controlled stepped, variable-ID profiles[Cross-wedge rolling hollow shafts with variable inner diameter, Archives of Civil and Mech](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1644966519300871)[Effect of mandrels on hollow shafts in cross-wedge rolling, Int. J. Adv. Manuf. Tech.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00170-018-3093-4)); swaging's is the combination of Quality and Subsequent Processing[Lightweight manufacturing via rotary swaging: validation for hollow railway motor shafts,](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652625003610); radial forging's is Materials (grain flow along the torsional load path).

**No route dominates on all three governing NEV requirements** — stepped bore-controlled geometry, the ≤0.10 mm balance class, and torsional fatigue strength. The co-leaders address different requirements: CWR the geometry, swaging the balance class. By 2027, as direct rotor cooling spreads from premium to mainstream, the geometry-binding case favouring CWR is likely to grow relative to the balance-binding case favouring swaging.

### 8.3 Scenario-conditioned recommendations

The tier ordering is a default the binding constraint overrides:

| Scenario | Binding axis | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|---|
| High volume (above crossover) | Cost-Effectiveness | Cross-Wedge Rolling | Rotary Swaging |
| Low volume / prototyping | Fixed-cost avoidance | Deep-Hole/BTA Drilling | Cold Extrusion |
| Complex stepped + cooling bore | Geometric Capability | CWR (mandrel) | Rotary Swaging (mandrel) |
| High-strength through-thickness | Materials | Hollow Forging (AHD) | Radial Forging |
| Lightweight, no steps | Quality (wall uniformity) | Flow Forming | Rotary Swaging |
| Lightweight + stepped | Geometry + Quality | Rotary Swaging | Cross-Wedge Rolling |

**At low volume the Tier-3 drilling route becomes first choice** — the Cost axis that sank it at volume rescues it below the ~20,000–50,000 units/yr crossover, reinforced by its 9.00 Maturity. **Under a strength mandate the Tier-2 AHD route becomes first choice**[Hollas et al., Hollow-forged AHD steel rotor shafts for wind turbines](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/1025193/files/1025193.pdf). Only under the geometry mandate does the raw tier leader CWR remain first. For cooling-bore programs, CWR with a mandrel-formed variable ID is primary[Cross-wedge rolling hollow shafts with variable inner diameter, Archives of Civil and Mech](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1644966519300871)[Effect of mandrels on hollow shafts in cross-wedge rolling, Int. J. Adv. Manuf. Tech.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00170-018-3093-4); **the tension — the mandrel enabling a controlled variable ID also raises central-defect risk if the Mannesmann window is missed — resolves through disciplined mandrel design and roll-pass scheduling**[Pater 2025, Numerical modelling of fracture caused by the Mannesmann effect, J. Manufactur](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1526612525002877)[Ghiotti et al. 2009, Modelling of the Mannesmann effect, CIRP Annals](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0007850609000705)[Central Defects in Cross Wedge Rolling of Railway Axles, Metals 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4701/13/7/1309).

### 8.4 Robustness

Under a ±10pp perturbation of each rubric weight (renormalised), **Tier-1 membership is invariant — no route crosses the 7.60 threshold in either direction.** Only one route changes tier: Radial Forging drops to Tier 2 when the Cost weight rises (its 6.50 Cost rating is its weakest axis). The two co-leaders swap internal order when Geometry weight rises (CWR's 9.00 outweighs swaging's 8.50), confirming the co-leadership is a genuine near-tie.

**The dominant sensitivity is the volume assumption, not the weight choice.** Re-rating the Cost axis for sub-crossover volume (drilling rising to ~8.50, CWR falling to ~5.50) moves two routes across tiers — drilling up to Tier 2, CWR down within Tier 1 — a sharper shift than any weight perturbation. This is precisely why volume is the first scenario fork.

**The headline recommendation holds across the mainstream high-volume, one-piece, stepped-bore case and flips only under three named boundaries:** sub-crossover volume (to drilling), cooling-bore geometry (CWR over swaging), and high-strength through-thickness demand (to AHD forging). A fourth, subtler boundary makes friction welding competitive for a long hollow shaft with a heavily-featured solid drive end, where siting the weld in a low-cyclic-torsional-stress zone removes the initiation driver[Fatigue Strength Assessment of Friction Welds under Residual Stress, Materials 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/17/13/3130)[İğdik & Özes, Mechanical properties of friction-welded tube-yoke joint](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/178a/ab662492865b4278e534192bd514381b5f8e.pdf) at the cost of a PAUT inspection step[PAUT of Longitudinal Seams/Welds and Pipes, Sonotron NDT](http://www.sonotronndt.com/LONG_WELDS.htm).

**The unresolved quantitative question:** the exact volume at which CWR tooling amortisation overtakes drilling's per-part cost for a specific geometry, which the 20,000–50,000 units/yr band brackets but does not pin down. Resolving it requires a part-specific cost model with actual die cost, cycle time, and scrap-rate inputs.

## 9. Coupling, Maturity, and Forward Outlook

### 9.1 Coupling paths among constraints

The six criteria behave as a **coupled system, not independent axes**:

- **Cost-volume coupling inverts the forming-versus-machining ranking.** At low volume, machining-from-solid dominates (generic tooling, small fixed cost); at high volume, near-net forming dominates (marginal cost falls below the chip-conversion cost). The ranking is volume-conditional[Lightweight manufacturing via rotary swaging: validation for hollow railway motor shafts,](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/zh/publications/lightweight-manufacturing-via-rotary-swaging-process-validation-f/) — the load-bearing finding.
- **Material choice forward-commits the heat-treat chain.** A grade that cold-forms cleanly[Recommendations for Dry Forming of 16MnCr5 and 42CrMo4 in Cold Forging, IOT RWTH Aachen](https://www.iot.rwth-aachen.de/go/id/qrty/file/659687) routes through swaging at room temperature; a grade demanding elevated temperature[42CrMo4 flow behavior for high-temperature closed-die hot forging, PMC10689884](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10689884/) steers toward hot forming. More sharply, **a single material decision pre-selects the entire downstream processing chain**: a case-hardening grade obligates carburizing-plus-grinding; a quench-and-temper grade obligates quench-and-temper-plus-straightening. The A356 warm-forming-then-T6 sequence is the clearest documented case of forming-plus-heat-treat co-design[Response of A356 to warm rotary forming and T6, arXiv:1406.5451](http://arxiv.org/abs/1406.5451v1).
- **One-piece vs two-piece trades integrity against cost and weight.** The two-piece route delivers a weight/cost advantage yet introduces a weld zone whose residual stress influences fatigue[Experimental method: welding residual stresses and fatigue crack propagation, arXiv:1709.0](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06942v1)[Fatigue Strength Assessment of Friction Welds under Residual Stress, Materials 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/17/13/3130). **The resolution converts an uncontrolled discontinuity into a controllable one:** the joint becomes acceptable when weld verification demonstrates the residual-stress state is within the fatigue allowable[Weld Testing, American Friction Welding](https://teamafw.com/weld-testing/). The one-piece route owns its integrity advantage for free; the two-piece route purchases equivalent confidence through testing[thyssenkrupp, "The perfect rotor shaft"](https://www.thyssenkrupp.com/en/stories/automotive-and-new-mobility/the-perfect-rotor-shaft).

**No route can be scored against any single axis without first fixing the programme's volume, grade, and integrity targets — the axes are wired together.**

### 9.2 Maturity stratification

| Route | Maturity tier | Supplier base | Qualification | Capital/access barrier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deep-Hole/BTA Drilling | Mature | Broad (fragmented) | Established | Low |
| Rotary Friction Welding | Mature | Moderate | Established, weld-NDT load[Weld Testing, American Friction Welding](https://teamafw.com/weld-testing/)[PAUT of Longitudinal Seams/Welds and Pipes, Sonotron NDT](http://www.sonotronndt.com/LONG_WELDS.htm) | Moderate |
| Rotary Swaging | Mature | Moderate | Hollow-shaft validated (adjacent)[Lightweight manufacturing via rotary swaging: validation for hollow railway motor shafts,](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/zh/publications/lightweight-manufacturing-via-rotary-swaging-process-validation-f/)[Lightweight manufacturing via rotary swaging: validation for hollow railway motor shafts,](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652625003610) | Moderate-high |
| Radial Forging | Partial | Moderate | Solid-billet heritage | High |
| Cross-Wedge Rolling | Partial | Moderate | Solid-preform heritage | High |
| Cold/Composite Extrusion | Partial | Moderate | Developing | High |
| Flow Forming | Partial | Moderate | Developing | High |
| Rotary Compression | Partial | Moderate | Developing | Moderate-high |
| Three-Roll Skew Rolling | Emerging | Low | Developing | High |
| Hollow Forging (AHD) | Emerging | Low | Developing | High |

The **mature tier** (drilling, friction welding, rotary swaging) is defined by a documented process base plus established verification. The **partial tier** (CWR, radial forging, extrusion, flow forming, rotary compression) is well established for solid preforms or adjacent materials[Severe plastic deformation by rotary swaging of Ti-2.5Al-2.6Zr, arXiv:2401.17672](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17672v1)[Cu–Cr composite wire by two-stage swaging plus aging](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1359836821009331)[Aged 6xxx aluminum during rotary swaging, Crystals](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/crystals/crystals-12-00530/article_deploy/crystals-12-00530-v2.pdf)[Rotary swaged tungsten heavy alloy at variable temperature, Materials](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/materials/materials-12-04200/article_deploy/materials-12-04200.pdf) but less broadly industrialised for thin-wall hollow NEV shafts. The **emerging tier** (skew rolling, AHD hollow forging) has NEV-specific process windows still being established.

Three adoption barriers separate the deployable from the developing: **supplier base** (can a qualified supplier demonstrate the ≤0.10 mm class repeatably?[EMAG, "Rotor shafts in electric drives"](https://www.emag.com/blog/en/rotor-shafts-for-electric-drives/)), **automotive qualification** (most acute for the welded route, where each weld must be qualified per specification[Weld Testing, American Friction Welding](https://teamafw.com/weld-testing/)), and **equipment/patent access** (deformation routes need purpose-built machines raising the capital threshold[Cu–Cr composite wire by two-stage swaging plus aging](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1359836821009331)[Rotary swaged tungsten heavy alloy at variable temperature, Materials](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/materials/materials-12-04200/article_deploy/materials-12-04200.pdf)). **Only the routes clearing all three simultaneously belong in the deployable-now set: deep-hole drilling, rotary friction welding, and rotary swaging** — the same mature tier, a convergence that feeds the forward verdict.

### 9.3 Forward outlook and the calibration limit

**The dominant near-term trend is the convergence of near-net forming with integrated rotor-cooling channels**, turning the shaft from a passive tube into an active thermal component. The cold-formed modular shaft with ducted liquid cooling routes coolant through four optimised geometries, collapsing bore-formation and cooling-geometry into one operation[High-Performance Rotor Cooling with Ducted Liquid in Cold-Formed Modular Motor Shaft, arXi](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22029v1) — structurally favouring cold-forming routes and reinforcing the case for cold-formable grades[Recommendations for Dry Forming of 16MnCr5 and 42CrMo4 in Cold Forging, IOT RWTH Aachen](https://www.iot.rwth-aachen.de/go/id/qrty/file/659687).

**Evolving architectures intensify the duty cycle.** Rising rotor-shaft quality requirements and tighter motor-gearbox interface precision[EMAG, "Rotor shafts in electric drives"](https://www.emag.com/blog/en/rotor-shafts-for-electric-drives/) sharpen the geometric demand, favouring routes delivering near-net interfaces in one operation. Higher torque density raises fatigue demand, rewarding swaging's grain refinement and surface strength[Charni et al. 2021, Mechanical properties of rotary swaged steel components, Forschung im](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10010-021-00498-3.pdf). As volumes scale, the cost-volume break-even moves further toward forming, and the tightening ≤0.10 mm class at higher speeds favours wall-uniformity-controlling routes.

**The principal risk-awareness point, stated without hedging: much of the strongest process evidence is adjacent-domain — railway, wind, aerospace — and its transfer to NEV e-drive shafts is an assumption, not a proven equivalence.** The railway hollow-motor-shaft validation[Lightweight manufacturing via rotary swaging: validation for hollow railway motor shafts,](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/zh/publications/lightweight-manufacturing-via-rotary-swaging-process-validation-f/) is the highest-confidence calibration point because the part class is nearly identical. The wider material evidence[Severe plastic deformation by rotary swaging of Ti-2.5Al-2.6Zr, arXiv:2401.17672](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17672v1)[Cu–Cr composite wire by two-stage swaging plus aging](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1359836821009331)[Rotary swaged tungsten heavy alloy at variable temperature, Materials](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/materials/materials-12-04200/article_deploy/materials-12-04200.pdf) calibrates swaging's breadth but does not transfer to NEV shaft steel. The welding-domain fatigue methodology[Integrated Digital Reconstruction of Welded Components for Fatigue, arXiv:2307.15604](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15604v1)[Fatigue Design Procedure for Welded Hollow Section Joints IIW](https://www.zlibrary.to/dl/fatigue-design-procedure-for-welded-hollow-section-joints-recommendations-of-iiw-subcommission-xv-e-0) is calibrating but structural rather than rotating-shaft in origin.

**No source in this report demonstrates an NEV rotor shaft through full duty-cycle validation, so the recommendation rests on calibrated transfer rather than NEV-native proof — and that gap is the report's principal residual uncertainty.** The verdict: the forward outlook is well-calibrated but not yet NEV-proven. A programme should treat railway-validated rotary swaging as the lead candidate while commissioning NEV-specific duty-cycle validation to close the transfer gap.

## Conclusion

The most suitable manufacturing routes for NEV hollow motor shafts are determined **not by any one criterion but by the resolution of a coupled material-cost-quality-volume system.** Within that resolution:

- **For the mainstream high-volume, one-piece, stepped-bore shaft, Cross-Wedge Rolling and Rotary Swaging are co-recommended leaders, with Radial Forging third.** This ranking survives every ±10pp weight perturbation.
- **The ranking flips only under three boundaries:** sub-crossover volume (to deep-hole drilling), cooling-bore geometry (CWR over swaging), and high-strength through-thickness demand (to AHD hollow forging).
- **Cold extrusion and flow forming split the pressure-driven field by volume** — extrusion for high-volume integrated-feature shafts, flow forming for low-volume and large-diameter variants.
- **Rotary friction welding is a volume-gated cost optimization** whose weld-line fatigue risk is managed by qualification, not avoidance.
- **Deep-hole drilling sets the cost floor the forming routes must beat** rather than winning the production mandate.

The single highest-value experiment to commission is a **direct torsional-fatigue comparison of a swaged hollow shaft against a machined-from-solid blank in a common grade** — without it, the fatigue superiority of forming over machining remains a grounded hypothesis rather than a measured verdict.
