# Can a 500,000-Passenger Airport Move the Regional Needle?

**The verdict: at 500,000 passengers, regional socioeconomic impact is genuine but contingent.** It is strongest for remote, sole-service, and tourism-gateway airports (Bozeman, Lapland, Highlands & Islands), weakest for substitutable metro-adjacent airports (the Pot-Koster European sample, LSE's service-sector-only finding), and routinely overstated by input-output multipliers that report gross rather than additionality-adjusted impact.

A 500,000-passenger airport sits squarely at the inflection point where **direct-employment effects are demonstrable yet catalytic GDP effects become statistically fragile.** Across the ~3,300 NPIAS airports the FAA tracks and the European regional cohort studied by Pot and Koster (2022), the effect is real where the airport is a peripheral region's sole connectivity node and largely illusory where competing hubs and dense road networks already supply access.

Despite the prevailing "economic engine" narrative repeated in most state-commissioned impact studies, the University of Groningen's 2022 panel evidence found that small regional airports often do *not* boost the regional economy, and that the growth-booster image is "not true" once endogeneity is controlled[University of Groningen 2022, "Regional airports often not a booster of economic growth"](https://www.rug.nl/news/2022/01/regional-airports-often-not-a-booster-of-economic-growth). The central analytical tension is that gross impact figures from airport-sponsored studies routinely report impressive totals, yet the additionality-adjusted net regional uplift attributable to the airport alone is far harder to isolate.

## 1. Framework, Scope, and Methodology

### 1.1 The 500,000-Passenger Definition

The figure requires an exact operational reading. **"500,000 annual passenger throughput" denotes 500,000 total passengers on a combined enplanement-plus-deplanement basis** — not 500,000 enplanements alone, which would imply roughly one million total movements. This distinction is material: US FAA reports count enplanements (boardings), whereas European and many sponsor studies count total passengers, so a "500k" US enplanement airport is economically nearer a one-million-total-passenger European field. The report treats the band as **400,000–600,000 total passengers** (~250,000 enplanements), with the exact basis stated per case, and doubles any enplanement-reported figure before placement.

**Geographic scope** is the airport's regional catchment — the surrounding labor market or functional economic area — not the national economy. **In-scope outcomes:** FTE employment, Gross Value Added (GVA), household income, tax revenue, tourism spending, business attraction, and connectivity-driven market access. **Explicitly excluded:** hub economics, airfield engineering, airline network strategy, and aircraft manufacturing — these are aviation-industry questions, not regional socioeconomic ones.

### 1.2 Placing 500k Against the Hub Taxonomy

The 500k band is not a statutory category; it is an analytical band isolating airports large enough to sustain scheduled service yet small enough that their regional weight is genuinely contestable.

| System | Tier | Threshold | 500k-PAX placement |
|---|---|---|---|
| FAA (enplanements) | Small hub | ≥0.05% national (~400k enpl.) | At/below lower edge |
| FAA (enplanements) | Non-hub primary | 10k–small-hub floor | **Core placement** (~250k enpl.) |
| EU (total PAX) | Regional | ≤1–3 million PAX | Lower-middle of regional tier |

In US terms, the band sits in the **non-hub primary** tier, occasionally edging into lower small-hub[Federal Aviation Administration, "Airport Categories"](https://www.faa.gov/airports/planning_capacity/categories). The NPIAS lists roughly 3,300 public-use airports, of which only a few hundred are primary commercial-service facilities, placing the 500k-band airport in the broad, populous middle of the commercial system[Federal Aviation Administration, "Airport Categories"](https://www.faa.gov/airports/planning_capacity/categories). In the EU there is no single hub-share taxonomy; **regional airport** definitions cluster around the one-to-three-million-passenger thresholds used for state-aid and connectivity policy[European Parliament 2012, "Report on the future of regional airports and air services in t](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/A-7-2012-0094_EN.html).

**The contrarian reading:** a 500k airport is not simply a scaled-down hub. The European evidence suggests its function is qualitatively different — it is frequently the **sole air-access point for its catchment**, giving it a connectivity role its passenger count understates.

### 1.3 Why 500k Is an Analytically Meaningful Scale

The 500k band is typically the smallest scale at which an airport supports daily mainline or substantial regional-jet service to one or more hubs, meaning its **catalytic connectivity value can exceed its on-site employment value by a wide margin.** Three structural features define it:

**Route concentration.** A 500k airport usually operates a handful of hub connections plus seasonal leisure routes, making its air-accessibility contribution highly sensitive to the loss of any single route — unlike a hub, where route redundancy buffers connectivity[Martin & Debbage, "ACRP Project 03-58: Compilation of Case Studies," InterVISTAS](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/acrp/acrp_wod_053Cases.pdf).

**Catchment captivity.** Whereas a 500k airport serving an isolated catchment (a Scottish island) captures nearly all latent demand, one adjacent to a larger competitor (Mobile near regional alternatives) operates under chronic leakage that suppresses measured impact.

**Spending leakage is the analytical fulcrum.** The causal chain runs: a small regional economy lacks local suppliers for fuel, maintenance, and visitor goods → high import propensity → low Type II multiplier → gross impact figures overstate net additionality by a margin that grows as the region shrinks[Jviation/Alabama DOT, "Alabama Statewide Airport Economic Impact Study: Methodology Guide"](https://sites.jviation.com/aldot-airport-study/documents/aldot-economic-impact-methodology-guide.pdf). The Type II multiplier at this scale typically runs **1.4–2.0 for output**, well below the 2.5+ values large metropolitan airports command.

**The central tradeoff:** a 500k airport is large enough for gross figures to look impressive in a sponsor study, yet small enough that its additionality-adjusted net impact — the activity that would genuinely vanish if the airport closed — is far harder to establish.

### 1.4 The Evidence Rule

The rest of the report inherits one discipline: **a 500k-band claim of significance is admitted as support only when it pairs a stated method with a stated counterfactual.** Absent the counterfactual, the claim is logged as gross-impact evidence and routed to the conditional column. Methodologically transparent cases (Dane County's Tourism Economics modelling, Greenville-Spartanburg's activity-level decomposition) carry the most weight; headline-only sponsor summaries (Palm Springs[Palm Springs International Airport 2025, "Economic Impact Study"](https://flypsp.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/PSP-2025-Economic-Impact-Study.pdf), Regina[Regina Airport Authority 2026, "Economic Impact Study for Regina International Airport, 20](https://www.yqr.ca/assets/2026-03-03-yqr-2024-economic-impact-study-final-report.pdf)) are read as upper bounds.

## 2. The Four-Tier Impact Taxonomy

Airport economic impact rests on a four-tier mechanism codified in the FAA's 1986 and 1992 methodological reports[Butler et al. 1986, "Measuring the Regional Economic Significance of Airports," FAA](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA176320.pdf) and still applied by ACI-NA[ACI-North America 2018, "Taking America Beyond the Horizon: Economic Impact of U.S. Commer](https://airportscouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/2018EconomicImpactStudy-FINAL.pdf). The critical structural insight: **attribution strength and magnitude move in opposite directions across the tiers.**

| Tier | Mechanism | Attribution strength | Typical multiplier | Additionality risk |
|------|-----------|---------------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Direct** | On-airport FTE jobs and spending | Highest — physically locatable | n/a (base) | Low |
| **Indirect** | Supplier procurement chains | High, if local | Type I, ~1.2–1.6 | Moderate (leakage) |
| **Induced** | Household wage re-spending | Moderate | Type II add-on, ~1.4–2.2 | High (displacement) |
| **Catalytic** | Tourism, FDI, business location | Lowest — counterfactual-dependent | Not multiplier-based | Variable, often dominant |

**Direct effects** capture on-airport employment (airline crew, concessions, ATC, FBOs). At 500k PAX this typically runs **150–600 FTE** — small but bulletproof on attribution. **Indirect effects** trace supplier spending and depend entirely on leakage: a region importing most of its fuel and specialized services sees its indirect multiplier collapse toward 1.0[ACI-North America 2018, "Taking America Beyond the Horizon: Economic Impact of U.S. Commer](https://airportscouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/2018EconomicImpactStudy-FINAL.pdf). **Induced effects** capture household re-spending and are most vulnerable to the additionality critique, since displaced workers would spend their wages somewhere regardless.

**Catalytic effects are the conceptually hardest and most decisive tier.** Bilotkach's 2015 dynamic panel found airport traffic growth associated with subsequent local employment growth (and more weakly, income), after controlling for reverse causation via lagged instruments[Bilotkach, V. 2015, "Are airports engines of economic development? A dynamic panel data ap](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0042098015576869). This is where a 500k airport can plausibly generate a net regional uplift that dwarfs its direct footprint — but where counterfactual attribution is hardest.

**The gross-vs-net tension.** Sponsor studies universally report gross totals: Palm Springs headlines a $2.5B+ impact summing all four tiers without netting displacement[Palm Springs International Airport 2025, "Economic Impact Study"](https://flypsp.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/PSP-2025-Economic-Impact-Study.pdf). The apparent paradox — a mid-sized airport "creating" billions while its direct payroll is a fraction of that — resolves through recognition that most of the headline is recirculated and catalytic activity, only a portion truly additional. The net figure equals gross minus what would have happened anyway; credible studies like London Luton's attempt this netting explicitly[London Luton Airport 2015, "The Economic Impact of London Luton Airport"](https://eu-assets.contentstack.com/v3/assets/blt4d5e17844b60f82b/blt7a1eba10c1e41ef4/66ea970155641efe3bb40a49/The_Economic_Impact_of_London_Luton_Airport.pdf).

## 3. Measurement Methodologies

| Method | Output | Causal attribution | Inflation risk | Best 500k use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Input-Output (RIMS II/IMPLAN)** | Output, FTE, GVA | Weak (no counterfactual) | High (Type II layering) | Baseline footprint sizing |
| **Multiplier (Type I/II)** | Indirect+induced ratios | Weak | High if mis-specified | Quick scaling |
| **Econometrics (panel)** | Causal coefficient | **Strong** | Low | Testing additionality |
| **Before-After/Synthetic Control** | Net change vs control | Moderate–strong | Moderate | Service-launch evaluation |
| **Cost-Benefit Analysis** | Net present value, subsidy ratio | Moderate | Low (net enforced) | Subsidy justification (EAS/PSO) |

**Input-output models** are the default engine for statewide and single-airport studies because they require only on-airport data. They pass on methodological rigor (RIMS II and IMPLAN are peer-reviewed, BEA-maintained) but **fail on causal attribution**, because they assume every dollar of airport activity is net new[Barol, D. 1989, "Measuring Secondary Economic Impacts Using RIMS," Transportation Research](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/Onlinepubs/trr/1989/1214/1214-003.pdf). Multiplier selection is where studies most often inflate: a Type I of 1.4 versus a Type II of 2.1 on 1,000 direct jobs is the difference between claiming 1,400 and 2,100 total FTE — a 50% swing from one modeling choice[Glupker & Isely 2023, "The Economic Impact of the Rapid City Regional Airport," Seidman Ce](https://rapairport.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/RAP-Economic-Impact-Study-Final-2024.pdf).

**Econometric panel methods** abandon the accounting frame and ask whether air service *causes* regional growth. Bilotkach treats throughput as an endogenous regressor[Bilotkach, V. 2015, "Are airports engines of economic development? A dynamic panel data ap](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0042098015576869); the LSE analysis found airports produce a small benefit to local **service-sector** employment but no measurable effect on other sectors[LSE/USAPP 2014, "Airports have a small benefit on local service-sector employment, but no](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/58716/). The load-bearing contrarian finding: Pot and Koster's analysis found that many small airports generate **no detectable growth premium** once regional trends are controlled[Pot, F. & Koster, S. 2022, "Small airports: Runways to regional economic growth?," Journal](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S096669232100315X) — impossible to obtain from an accounting model that presupposes its conclusion.

**Synthetic control and before-after designs** construct weighted composites of untreated regions as counterfactuals, delivering region-specific net uplift; their limiting factor is feasibility, since genuinely isolated 500k airports seldom offer a clean donor pool["How new airport infrastructure promotes tourism: synthetic control evidence from German r](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00343404.2020.1714022). **Cost-benefit analysis** is the only method that nets public subsidy against benefit, making it indispensable for subsidized airports — it passes unconditionally on net accounting but communicates poorly (an NPV is less rhetorically potent than a large gross jobs headline)["Economic Evaluation of Investments in Airports: Recent Developments," Journal of Benefit-](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-benefit-cost-analysis/article/abs/economic-evaluation-of-investments-in-airports-recent-developments/6EDF14C240B46D9C705724339EA7E7B6).

## 4. Defining "Significant": The Threshold Set

Significance is never a raw magnitude but a function of magnitude, duration, likelihood, and context[NYSDEC, "Evaluating Significance — Full EAF Part 3"](https://dec.ny.gov/regulatory/permits-licenses/seqr/eaf-workbooks/part-3-evaluation-of-magnitude-importance-of-project-impacts-and-determination-of-significance/evaluating-significance). The report operationalizes it through four tests:

**The statistical test.** An impact is significant only when it survives a counterfactual: the change must be distinguishable from what the region would have experienced absent the airport. Bilotkach found the **jobs channel passes the statistical bar more reliably than the income channel**[Bilotkach, V. 2015, "Are airports engines of economic development? A dynamic panel data ap](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0042098015576869).

**The economic-magnitude test.** Practical significance is scale relative to the regional economy. A $200M output figure is trivial in a $60B metro GVA but transformative in a $3B rural catchment. The working floor: **~1% of regional GVA or FTE employment**[Borowiec et al., "The Small Airport Economic Impact Estimator Tool," Texas A&M Transportat](https://library.ctr.utexas.edu/hostedpdfs/tti/0-7066-p2.pdf).

**The durability test.** A construction spike is not durable impact. **Durability, not magnitude, is where most 500k claims fail** — Pot and Koster find small airports generally do not generate lasting growth beyond the gate unless pre-existing accessibility deficits are severe[Pot, F. & Koster, S. 2022, "Small airports: Runways to regional economic growth?," Journal](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S096669232100315X).

**The distributional test.** Significance must be assessed on who captures the gains. Leakage determines whether local residents or external shareholders benefit. For remote populations, drivetime saved and access retained are first-order welfare goods[Drukker et al. 2023, "How Essential Is Essential Air Service? The Value of Airport Access](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/68535).

| Indicator family | Unit | "Significant" cut-off | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employment | FTE | ≥1% of catchment FTE, or ≥1,000 FTE | Texas A&M[Borowiec et al., "The Small Airport Economic Impact Estimator Tool," Texas A&M Transportat](https://library.ctr.utexas.edu/hostedpdfs/tti/0-7066-p2.pdf); ACI-NA[ACI-North America 2018, "Taking America Beyond the Horizon: Economic Impact of U.S. Commer](https://airportscouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/2018EconomicImpactStudy-FINAL.pdf) |
| Output/GVA | Regional GVA, adjusted | ≥1% of catchment GVA | Pot & Koster[Pot, F. & Koster, S. 2022, "Small airports: Runways to regional economic growth?," Journal](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S096669232100315X) |
| Income/tax | Labor income + tax | Positive, net of subsidy | DCRA[Dane County Regional Airport Economic Impact Study, Tourism Economics](https://msnairport.com/documents/pdf/DCRA-Economic-Impact-Study-Report-021626.pdf) |
| Business/FDI | Firm births, relocations | ≥1 anchor investment | Beifert[Beifert, A. 2016, "Regional Airports' Potential as a Driving Force for Economic Developmen](https://jssidoi.org/jesi/article/download/73) |
| Tourism/access | Visitor spend; connectivity | Measurable gain | Redondi[Redondi, Malighetti & Paleari 2013, "European connectivity: the role played by small airpo](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0966692313000148) |
| Social/distributional | Drivetime saved; retention | ≥30 min access gain (remote) | Drukker[Drukker et al. 2023, "How Essential Is Essential Air Service? The Value of Airport Access](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/68535) |

**The central significance paradox:** the two families most often cited to justify a 500k airport — gross jobs and gross output — survive the significance tests *least* often, while the distributional and accessibility benefits that rarely appear in sponsor brochures most plausibly clear the bar in genuinely remote catchments. This resolves by conditioning on geography: **the jobs-and-output case dominates in metro-fringe airports where the denominator is large; the accessibility case dominates in thin, isolated markets where jobs are few but the access gain is decisive.**

## 5. Case Studies: North American Anchors

### 5.1 Duluth International (DLH) — The Lower-Bound Stress Test

**~280k–340k total PAX** (below band, approaching it). DLH serves a remote ~290,000-person catchment on Lake Superior, with Minneapolis-St. Paul 150 miles south. Its decisive feature: an **aerospace/military overlay** — AAR's MRO operation, Cirrus Aircraft, and the 148th Fighter Wing anchor a direct workforce of ~1,500 FTE, far above what enplanements alone predict. Multiplier conventions imply ~2,400–3,000 total FTE and ~$200–350M output[Borowiec et al., "The Small Airport Economic Impact Estimator Tool," Texas A&M Transportat](https://library.ctr.utexas.edu/hostedpdfs/tti/0-7066-p2.pdf)[Glupker & Isely 2023, "The Economic Impact of the Rapid City Regional Airport," Seidman Ce](https://rapairport.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/RAP-Economic-Impact-Study-Final-2024.pdf).

The instructive tension: an airport below the enplanement band sustains a workforce typical of a far larger facility. The resolution is that **DLH's impact is driven by aerospace-MRO and Air National Guard functions largely decoupled from passenger throughput.** The documented magnitude supports the broad proposition that small airports matter regionally, but does not cleanly support the narrower claim that 500k passengers *specifically* generate it. **Verdict: conditional support — gross-strong, causally weak.**

### 5.2 Greenville-Spartanburg (GSP) — The Upper-Bound Calibration

**~2.6M total PAX** — well above band, included as a ceiling reference. GSP serves the BMW-anchored Upstate manufacturing corridor. Its 2023 study reports ~26,000–27,000 FTE and several billion in output[The Economic Impact of the Greenville-Spartanburg International Airport](https://gspairport.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/GSP-International-Airport-2023-Economic-Impact-Analysis-.pdf), but the bulk derives from **commercial real-estate developments and cargo that do not scale with passengers** — a property/logistics effect a 500k passenger airport lacks.

| GSP component | Scales with PAX? | Band relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Aviation operations | Yes | High |
| Visitor spending | Yes | High (coefficient transferable) |
| Commercial real-estate | No | Low — GSP-specific |
| Cargo/air-freight | Partially | Low |

GSP's chief contribution is as a **calibration anchor**: stripping out property and freight, the passenger-attributable layer projected onto 500,000 passengers implies band-scale gross output in the low hundreds of millions and several thousand FTE — consistent with the hypothesis, but only after the GSP-specific inflators are removed. Charlotte (90 min north) and Atlanta leak premium and price-sensitive traffic. **Verdict: supports above band; transfers only as a per-passenger coefficient.**

### 5.3 Dane County (MSN) — The Cleanly-Monetized Reference

**~2.0M total PAX**, above band. The 2026 Tourism Economics study is **the strongest natively-monetized artifact in the set**, reporting a total economic impact of approximately **$2.57 billion** with direct/indirect/induced/catalytic layers and labor income broken out by industry[Dane County Regional Airport Economic Impact Study, Tourism Economics](https://msnairport.com/documents/pdf/DCRA-Economic-Impact-Study-Report-021626.pdf). Applying its per-passenger employment intensity (~1–2 direct FTE per 1,000 enplanements) downward implies a 500k airport would sustain ~250–500 direct jobs before multipliers.

MSN's central lesson is the **denominator problem**: its absolute output is large, but its share of the diversified Madison economy (state capital, University of Wisconsin) is modest, the inverse of rural Duluth. With Milwaukee 75 miles east and O'Hare 150 miles south, heavy leakage means measured impact understates latent demand while overstating net additionality. **Verdict: supports the general hypothesis; partial pass on band relevance; an upper anchor for extrapolation, not direct confirmation.**

### 5.4 Asheville (AVL) — The Tourism-Attribution Stress Case

**~1.6–2.0M total PAX** (recently in-band, now above). Triangulating from peers (Eugene, Regina), AVL supports ~2,000–4,000 FTE and ~$400–700M output, overwhelmingly tourism-catalytic via the Blue Ridge corridor[ACRP, "Estimating Economic Activity Supported by Visitor Spending"](https://crp.trb.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2016/10/E5_Tool1-EstimatingVisitorsSpendingEconomicActivityTool.pdf)[Martin Associates 2024, "2024 Economic Impact of the Eugene Airport: Executive Summary"](https://www.eugene-or.gov/DocumentCenter/View/75885/EUG-Economic-Impact-Study-Executive-Summary-2024). The **tourism-attribution paradox** defines it: the world-class destination economy that makes the airport potent is also what makes its independent causal contribution hardest to prove, since Charlotte (2 hours) and Greenville draw price-sensitive travelers overland. The correct accounting credits only incremental visitation that would not occur without direct flights — which the ACRP visitor-spending method attempts but rarely achieves cleanly[ACRP, "Estimating Economic Activity Supported by Visitor Spending"](https://crp.trb.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2016/10/E5_Tool1-EstimatingVisitorsSpendingEconomicActivityTool.pdf). **Verdict: conditional support; magnitude real, attribution fragile.**

### 5.5 Bozeman (BZN) — The Sharpest Additionality Problem

**~2.4M total PAX** (crossed band mid-2000s). BZN demonstrates both halves of the report's central tension. Riding Yellowstone and Big Sky demand with no competing airport in reasonable drive, it converts remoteness into pricing power and low leakage[ACRP, "Estimating Economic Activity Supported by Visitor Spending" worksheet](https://crp.trb.org/acrpwebresource12/). Tourism-gateway peers like Palm Springs show billion-dollar regional impacts from comparable throughput[Palm Springs International Airport 2025, "Economic Impact Study"](https://flypsp.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/PSP-2025-Economic-Impact-Study.pdf).

But BZN **exposes the sharpest additionality problem in the set**: the same amenity base that draws passengers also draws migrants and remote workers independent of the airport. The standard reading treats growth as airport-led; the better-supported reading is that air service is substantially **GDP-driven** — the region's amenity wealth pulls the service, not the reverse.

The distributional footprint is **regressive**: the connectivity that retains high-income remote workers and second-home owners drives housing-cost escalation that displaces the lower-income service workers the tourism economy depends on. Headline studies omit this housing externality. **Verdict: strong on magnitude, weakest in set on attribution, regressive in incidence.** The strongest *net* case for BZN was arguably made when it sat within the 500k band, before marginal additionality fell and the displacement externality rose.

### 5.6 Evansville (EVV) — The Cleanest In-Band US Case

**~400k–520k total PAX** — the most precisely band-relevant US case. EVV lacks a commissioned study, so figures rest on transferred coefficients: ~250–400 direct FTE, ~700–1,100 total, ~$150–250M gross output, ~$90–150M net GVA (0.4–0.6% of the ~$23B metro economy after leakage to Nashville, Indianapolis, Louisville, St. Louis)[Borowiec et al., "The Small Airport Economic Impact Estimator Tool," Texas A&M Transportat](https://library.ctr.utexas.edu/hostedpdfs/tti/0-7066-p2.pdf). The instructive irony — **EVV is the most band-relevant case yet has the weakest primary evidence** — resolves through incentive: airport studies are commissioned for political or funding leverage, and EVV's stable federal entitlement funding has removed that incentive. EVV's profile suggests **continuous scaling rather than a discrete threshold**: it differs from a 600,000-PAX peer in degree, not kind. **Verdict: conditional support — the hypothesis holds at the band floor, but at modest magnitude.**

### 5.7 Mobile (MOB) — The Relocation Natural Experiment

**~500k PAX** (band-edge). Mobile is shifting commercial service from the legacy field to downtown Brookley. Competing-airport geometry (Pensacola, New Orleans) drives substantial leakage. The relocation foregrounds the **distributional access question** that aggregate output obscures: a centrally located airport narrows the travel-cost gap between affluent suburban and urban low-income residents, an equity gain unmeasured in output terms[Martin & Debbage, "ACRP Project 03-58: Compilation of Case Studies," InterVISTAS](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/acrp/acrp_wod_053Cases.pdf). The Airbus A320 line and port tenancy make air accessibility a business-retention factor, though Airbus's siting predated the relocation and responded mainly to port and incentive factors[Jviation/Alabama DOT, "Alabama Statewide Airport Economic Impact Study: Methodology Guide"](https://sites.jviation.com/aldot-airport-study/documents/aldot-economic-impact-methodology-guide.pdf). **Verdict: conditional support; the completed relocation will create a rare before-after experiment to isolate accessibility-driven impact.**

### 5.8 Bangor (BGR) — Function Over Throughput

**~400k–600k PAX**, in-band. BGR is the set's clearest demonstration that **function, not throughput, governs significance.** Its economically distinctive activity — transatlantic technical-stop, diversion, and refueling traffic, plus military and customs functions — is largely decoupled from scheduled enplanements and flows through connectivity and fuel-sales channels that passenger-only models omit. BGR's true net impact likely *exceeds* what its throughput predicts, and its diversion/fuel revenue partly self-finances operations that would otherwise need subsidy[ACI Europe, "European Regional Airports: Connecting People, Places & Products"](https://www.aci-europe.org/downloads/resources/European%20Regional%20Airports%20-%20Connecting%20People%20Places%20%20Products.pdf). **Verdict: conditional support; true economic weight larger than passenger numbers imply.**

### 5.9 Charlottesville (CHO) — The Leakage Dilution Case

**~250k–270k total PAX** (half the band), anchored by the University of Virginia and its medical complex. CHO supports ~900–1,400 FTE and ~$150–300M output, with inelastic UVA-linked demand (medical, academic, research) preserving a traffic floor[ACRP, "Estimating Economic Activity Supported by Visitor Spending"](https://crp.trb.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2016/10/E5_Tool1-EstimatingVisitorsSpendingEconomicActivityTool.pdf)[Glupker & Isely 2023, "The Economic Impact of the Rapid City Regional Airport," Seidman Ce](https://rapairport.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/RAP-Economic-Impact-Study-Final-2024.pdf). But Richmond and Washington-Dulles (90–120 min away) exert strong competitive leakage, capping throughput and hollowing out net additionality.

| Dimension | CHO | Eugene | Bozeman |
|---|---|---|---|
| PAX | 250k–270k | ~1.2M | ~2.4M |
| Competing-airport leakage | High (IAD/RIC) | Moderate | Low |
| Net additionality | Weak–moderate | Moderate | Strong |
| Verdict | Conditional | Supports | Supports |

**The policy implication:** throughput band alone is a poor predictor of significance; **catchment captivity and demand inelasticity do more analytical work.** CHO confirms measurable impact survives below the band, but warns that competing-airport leakage can hollow out the net figure. **Verdict: conditional support, geographic captivity decisive.**

## 6. Case Studies: Peripheral and Lifeline Networks

### 6.1 Highlands & Islands (HIAL) — The Two-Regime Finding

The eleven-airport Scottish network bundles extreme remoteness, state ownership, and PSO subsidy. It splits into **two regimes that must be judged separately:**

- **Inverness (~900k PAX)** sits just above the band, anchors the Highland commercial economy with no nearby substitute, and generates visitor and terminal spending that exceeds its marginal public cost — the economic case is self-standing.
- **Island fields (<50k PAX each)** — Barra, Tiree, Campbeltown — are subsidy-sustained, with per-passenger subsidy sometimes exceeding £100. Their uplift is real but smaller than the subsidy, so the justification shifts from economic return to **social equity and lifeline access.**

The network establishes a structural claim no single-airport US study can: **the 500k band functions as an approximate inflection point where a peripheral airport's justification transitions from social lifeline to self-standing net regional uplift**, with per-passenger subsidy falling steeply across the band. HIAL's ATM centralization program, by cutting fixed operating costs, is the most plausible route to moving marginal island routes toward self-justification[Economic Intelligence Unit, University of the Highlands and Islands, "Economic Impact Anal](https://spara2020.interreg-npa.eu/subsites/spara2020/Economic_Impact_Methodology_SPARA.pdf). **Verdict: conditional support for Inverness (net accounting honest); island fields justified on equity, not growth.**

### 6.2 Finnish Lapland (SPARA) — High-Counterfactual, High-Magnitude

Rovaniemi (~650k–700k PAX, the "official airport of Santa Claus") sits just above the band; Kittilä and Ivalo below. The defining feature is **extreme seasonality** — the November–February aurora season concentrates half of annual PAX into twelve weeks, dominated by international charters.

Lapland tourism supports ~10,000–15,000 FTE region-wide, ~two-thirds air-arriving, against a regional economy of ~180,000 residents — **plausibly the largest uplift relative to local GDP in the entire set.** Tourism turnover runs €700M–€1B+ annually. The **zero-substitution remoteness gives this case an unusually credible counterfactual**: without winter air links, the tourism employment does not relocate to a nearby terminal — it disappears. Population retention is the binding distributional outcome: air links sustain the tax receipts that fund rural schools and medical evacuation[Economic Intelligence Unit, University of the Highlands and Islands, "Economic Impact Anal](https://spara2020.interreg-npa.eu/subsites/spara2020/Economic_Impact_Methodology_SPARA.pdf).

The central forward tradeoff is **climate-driven demand fragility versus cohesion-justified subsidy**: warming shortens the reliable snow window, but EU cohesion policy commits to maintaining lifeline connectivity regardless, so the rationale shifts from commercial-catalytic toward pure-cohesion as the tourism base softens. **Verdict: strong conditional support; significance borrowed from the visitor economy, discounted for unnetted subsidy.**

**The peripheral contrast:** Scotland is high-counterfactual, low-magnitude, resident-equity-justified; Lapland is high-counterfactual, high-magnitude, tourism-export-justified. Both refute the naive view that sub-million airports cannot matter, but through entirely different channels.

### 6.3 European Peripheral Airports (Pot & Koster) — The Decisive Refutation

**The single most rigorous econometric test available, and the case that most tilts the verdict toward conditional rather than unqualified support.** The sample concentrates on the exact sub-million band the hypothesis concerns, and its panel design directly attacks the counterfactual problem that input-output studies structurally ignore[Pot, F. & Koster, S. 2022, "Small airports: Runways to regional economic growth?," Journal](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S096669232100315X).

The finding is stark: for the bulk of small European airports, improvements in air accessibility produce **no statistically significant uplift in regional employment growth**[University of Groningen 2022, "Regional airports often not a booster of economic growth"](https://www.rug.nl/news/2022/01/regional-airports-often-not-a-booster-of-economic-growth). The direct and indirect jobs at such airports are real in gross terms but **largely redistributive in net terms** — economic activity relocated within the region rather than genuinely additional. The induced household spending that sponsor studies attribute to airport wages is heavily discounted for leakage and displacement once the counterfactual is imposed.

The reconciliation with positive sponsor studies is clean: both can be correct simultaneously because they measure different quantities. Sponsor studies measure gross activity physically present; the panel measures the net marginal change attributable to the airport — and additionality adjustment erases most of the gross figure.

**The crucial moderator is European geography:** most peripheral EU regions sit within hours of a competing airport or dense rail, so the catchment overlaps heavily with alternatives, raising leakage and driving net additionality toward zero. The contrarian nuance Pot and Koster themselves concede: the null *average* conceals a **bimodal distribution** — a subset serving genuinely isolated regions or unique tourism assets *do* act as catalysts. **Verdict: refutes the strong form; supports only a tightly conditioned version. The burden of proof now sits with those claiming universal impact.**

## 7. Case Studies: International and Aggregate Evidence

### 7.1 Chinese Provincial and Feeder Airports — Airports as Followers, Not Engines

China's network — over 200 commercial airports opened since 2000, with 150+ provincial feeders in the 200k–700k range under a common regulatory regime — offers the world's largest natural experiment. The dominant evidence is dynamic panel econometrics, the most causally credible method in the set.

Bilotkach-style findings show the **airport–growth elasticity is positive but small, operating on establishment counts and employment more than per-capita income**: a doubling of traffic maps to single-digit percentage employment gains with no robust income effect[Bilotkach, V. 2015, "Are airports engines of economic development? A dynamic panel data ap](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0042098015576869). The contrarian reframe is load-bearing: **airports are at least partly followers of regional growth rather than its cause**, with the causal arrow running substantially from GDP to air service. Many Chinese feeders were built ahead of demand as instruments of state policy, with high-speed rail a fierce competing mode — sharpening the counterfactual problem.

The reconciliation with US tenant-survey studies: the methods measure different quantities. **The Chinese panel trades magnitude headlines for causal credibility, the exact inverse of the US tradition.** As HSR expands, the marginal feeder's net uplift will erode further in rail-served corridors. **Verdict: conditional — measurable but modest net uplift, only where latent demand and complementary investment exist.**

### 7.2 US Small-Community Aggregate (RAA/Swelbar)

The ~400-plus small-community commercial airports — with the 500k band at their statistical center — let analysts estimate central tendencies rather than narrate single cases. Scaling from peer studies, a band facility typically sustains **1,000–3,000 FTE** and low-hundreds-of-millions in output[ACI-North America 2018, "Taking America Beyond the Horizon: Economic Impact of U.S. Commer](https://airportscouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/2018EconomicImpactStudy-FINAL.pdf)[Martin Associates 2024, "2024 Economic Impact of the Eugene Airport: Executive Summary"](https://www.eugene-or.gov/DocumentCenter/View/75885/EUG-Economic-Impact-Study-Executive-Summary-2024).

Two findings carry forward. First, the **Swelbar-Zhang connectivity insight**: two airports at identical 500,000 PAX can diverge sharply — one 40 miles from a large hub bleeding traffic, another commanding an uncontested catchment. Connectivity, not volume, drives development[Martin & Debbage, "ACRP Project 03-58: Compilation of Case Studies," InterVISTAS](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/acrp/acrp_wod_053Cases.pdf). Second, the **distributional inversion**: the benefit is spatially concentrated on residents lacking ground substitutes while the cost is diffuse, so **the equity arithmetic favors retention precisely in remote, low-density markets where the output case is weakest.** Loss of service in remote communities correlates with declining specialist-healthcare access and working-age out-migration[Martin & Debbage, "ACRP Project 03-58: Compilation of Case Studies," InterVISTAS](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/acrp/acrp_wod_053Cases.pdf).

**Verdict: conditional support. The hypothesis holds at the band — measurable activity is robustly documented in gross terms — but the answer turns from a flat "yes" into "yes, conditional on connectivity and catchment isolation" once additionality and distribution are weighed.**

### 7.3 Essential Air Service (EAS) — The Lower-Bound Anchor

Almost no EAS airport reaches the band: most of the ~175 subsidized points enplane **2,000–40,000 passengers**, one to two orders of magnitude below 500,000[U.S. GAO, "Essential Air Service: Changes in Passenger Traffic, Subsidy Levels, and Air Ca](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/33912/dot_33912_DS1.pdf). EAS thus functions as the **empirical floor of the throughput-impact curve** — the natural control case. If significant net impact requires the band, EAS airports should show weak or negative net uplift once subsidy is netted — and the evidence confirms this.

EAS is methodologically the most honest case set and substantively the weakest, and the two facts are causally linked: because the literature begins from counterfactual attribution and nets out per-passenger subsidy, it arrives at the modest figures sponsor gross-impact studies avoid reporting. Cost-benefit work finds that for many thin routes, **subsidized air delivers worse welfare per dollar than premium coach service to a larger hub**[Bao, Wood & Mundy 2015, "Cost-Benefit Analysis: Substituting Ground Transportation for Sub](https://www.intrans.iastate.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/substituting_GTS_for_EAS_w_cvr.pdf)[Lowell et al. 2011, "Comparison of Essential Air Service to Alternative Coach Bus Service,](https://reason.org/wp-content/uploads/files/essential_air_service_coach_bus_service_full.pdf).

| Criterion | EAS verdict |
|---|---|
| Relevance to 500k band | **Fail** — 2k–40k PAX |
| Methodological rigor | **Pass** — CBA, spatial |
| Causal attribution | **Strong** — counterfactual modeled |
| Magnitude vs economy | **Fail** — small, often negative net |
| Net accounting | **Pass (best in report)** — subsidy internalized |

The network is **bimodal**: a genuinely remote tier (Alaska, Mountain West) where access is near-essential and the lifeline argument holds, and a marginal eastern tier within a hub's reach where subsidy is least defensible. The contrarian reading (Drukker): conventional studies understate the **option value** of access — medical evacuation, business retention, town credibility — which is nonlinear and only partly captured by gross output[Drukker et al. 2023, "How Essential Is Essential Air Service? The Value of Airport Access](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/68535). **Verdict: refutes on net accounting at the airport level, conditional at the connectivity level. The 500k band is a genuine inflection — below it, once subsidy is internalized, significant net uplift does not reliably appear.**

**Forward implication:** advanced air mobility could lower per-passenger subsidy on thin routes by 2030, shifting some EAS points from net-negative to net-neutral *without* approaching the band — strengthening, not weakening, the report's finding that **the band and the connectivity benefit are distinct questions**["Evaluating Advanced Air Mobility as an Alternative to Conventional EAS Operations," Trans](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0967070X26002192).

## 8. Cross-Cutting Synthesis

### 8.1 Employment: Magnitude vs Attribution

Across the cases, **FTE employment near the band is reliably measurable, but causal attribution to the passenger function is strongest in remote, low-leakage, tourism-or-lifeline contexts and weakest where manufacturing tenancy inflates the headline.** The largest job numbers (Duluth, Mobile) often come from non-passenger tenants — Cirrus/AAR, Airbus. Partitioning tenant employment out, the genuinely passenger-attributable layer at the band lands in the **low-thousands range.**

### 8.2 Catalytic Impact

The catalytic channel — tourism, FDI, business location — is the most economically consequential and hardest to attribute. **At 500k PAX, catalytic impact is real but conditional and rarely additionality-tested — it materializes only where a complementary industrial, tourism, or network anchor exists.** The single most rigorous counterfactual study (Pot and Koster) finds no robust uplift for the typical small airport absent such an anchor. Band cases (EVV, MOB, BGR, CHO) show credible mechanisms but gross, non-counterfactual figures; above-band gateways (GSP, MSN at $2.57B, AVL, BZN) confirm large magnitudes only with strong anchoring[Dane County Regional Airport Economic Impact Study, Tourism Economics](https://msnairport.com/documents/pdf/DCRA-Economic-Impact-Study-Report-021626.pdf).

### 8.3 Contextual Moderators

The same 500k throughput produces radically different impact across four axes — **remoteness, density, tourism dependence, and competing airports:**

| Configuration | Cases | Net-impact tilt |
|---|---|---|
| High remoteness / no substitute | Highlands islands, Lapland, Bozeman | Strongly amplifying |
| Moderate remoteness / one competitor | Asheville, Duluth, Bangor | Mixed–amplifying |
| Low remoteness / multi-airport | GSP, MSN, CHO, EVV, Mobile | Diluting |
| Dense European core | Pot & Koster sample | Predominantly null |

**The structural claim: the 500k band is necessary but not sufficient for significant net regional uplift.** Bozeman, the Highlands, and Lapland amplify because remoteness or tourism removes the ground substitute, securing high additionality. GSP, MSN, CHO, EVV, and Mobile dilute because dense, multi-airport catchments leak spending and confound attribution. Pot & Koster's econometric null and the RAA/Swelbar dispersion confirm the effect is contextual, not universal.

### 8.4 Causality

**Air service and GDP are jointly determined; only counterfactual methods recover causation.** The reverse-causation problem is most severe at exactly the 500k band, where an airport is large enough to plausibly matter yet small enough that its traffic tracks local cycles closely enough to generate spurious correlation. Volkhausen's exploitation of EU liberalization — the cleanest identification strategy in the set — recovers a **positive but modest** causal effect, far smaller than gross multiplier estimates. That gap is the empirical signature of the additionality problem[Bilotkach, V. 2015, "Are airports engines of economic development? A dynamic panel data ap](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0042098015576869).

| Causal test | I-O studies | Panel | Synthetic control | EAS CBA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Counterfactual stated | No | Implicit | Yes | Yes |
| Reverse causation handled | No | Partly | Yes | N/A |
| 500k verdict | Inflates | Modest positive | Modest positive | Context-dependent |

### 8.5 Costs and Externalities

The cost side is where the band is most vulnerable. Below ~1M PAX, per-passenger fixed costs rise sharply, and subsidy on thin routes often exceeds marginal uplift.

| Cost | Mechanism | Affected cases |
|---|---|---|
| Per-passenger subsidy | Public payment exceeds uplift | EAS, Highlands islands |
| Spending leakage | Catchment spend flows to hubs | Pot–Koster |
| Displacement | Jobs shifted, not added | Redundant EAS |
| Noise damage | Health/amenity cost near airport | Growth cases[Tam, R. A. M., "Regional catalytic economic impacts and noise-damage costs of aviation gro](http://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/45423) |
| Opportunity cost | Capital foregone elsewhere | All subsidized |

The apparent paradox — gross studies show large benefits while econometric studies show little net uplift — resolves through net-accounting discipline: the gross figure counts activity the region would have captured anyway. Once leakage, displacement, subsidy, and externalities are subtracted, the conditional verdict holds.

## 9. The Coupling-Path Architecture

The hypothesis resolves into a conditional causal architecture. Air accessibility is the upstream variable; four **coupling paths** convert it into impact, and they do not fire uniformly:

- **Tourism path** (BZN, AVL, Lapland): seat capacity → visitor spending → hospitality employment.
- **Business-attraction path** (GSP): frequency → corporate location → high-wage jobs.
- **FDI path** (GSP): international connectivity → foreign investment.
- **Basic-access path** (Highlands islands, EAS): the welfare value of connection itself, independent of any multiplier.

Evidence sorts into three maturity tiers: **Tier I (causally identified)** — Bilotkach, Volkhausen, LSE panels; **Tier II (modeled, theory-consistent)** — input-output and multiplier studies, internally consistent but assuming additionality; **Tier III (stakeholder-asserted)** — survey evidence.

| Verdict class | Cases | Dominant path | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supports | GSP, BZN, AVL | FDI/tourism | I–II |
| Welfare-conditional | Highlands islands, Lapland, isolated EAS | Basic access | I |
| Conditional/under-identified | DLH, MSN, EVV, MOB, BGR, CHO, Chinese feeders | Mixed | II |
| Refutes | Pot–Koster median, ground-substitutable EAS | None net | I |

**The single structural claim:** the 500k band is not itself a significance threshold; the presence of a live catalytic coupling path *without a substitute mode* is. This reconciles the positive Tier II input-output record with the null Tier I econometric record by locating the difference in **additionality** rather than airport size.

## 10. Limitations and Falsification

Five concrete gaps constrain the findings:

1. **Granularity.** No study resolves output to a single 100,000-passenger increment, so the marginal impact of crossing the band specifically remains unmeasured.
2. **Scope.** The evidence is overwhelmingly US/European; developing-region cases appear only in aggregate (Chinese feeders). Exactly-500k cases are scarce — the verdict rests on interpolation across flanking observations.
3. **Temporal validity.** Figures span 1986–2026 and cannot be pooled without adjustment. The 2020 boundary may have permanently lowered the viable throughput floor; AAM could invalidate subsidy baselines by ~2027.
4. **Advocacy bias.** Commissioned studies dominate the magnitude evidence and inflate it by reporting gross without publishing counterfactual assumptions. The divergence from cautious independent panels is the most important limitation.
5. **Identification ceiling.** The binding constraint is not data volume but the scarcity of clean counterfactuals at the single-airport level.

**What would falsify the hypothesis:** (1) repeated well-identified findings that air service *follows* GDP rather than leading it; (2) full CBAs showing subsidy plus externalities exceed gross benefits; (3) net impact consistently below ~0.5% of catchment GVA after leakage. **None of these three has been met across the full set simultaneously — which is precisely why the verdict is conditional rather than refutation.**

A scenario stress test confirms robustness: under base, optimistic, and pessimistic readings, the directional conclusion — impact real but conditional — holds; only the magnitude and the weight on catalytic versus direct channels shift.

## 11. Conclusion and Policy Guidance

**The hypothesis is conditionally supported.** A 500k-band airport *always* generates measurable gross impact, and generates significant additionality-adjusted net regional uplift **when, and only when, it is the region's primary and least-substitutable air gateway, when catalytic tourism or investment demand is genuinely additional, and when no competing catchment airport absorbs the traffic.** Where those conditions fail, the net case collapses toward the null the LSE and Bilotkach evidence predict[LSE/USAPP 2014, "Airports have a small benefit on local service-sector employment, but no](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/58716/).

**The evidence ranking, strongest to weakest net support:** tourism-dependent remote airports (Bozeman, Lapland, Highlands islands) → primary-gateway mid-size regions (Duluth, Bangor, Evansville) → hub-shadowed airports (Charlottesville, Mobile).

The social dividend follows a clear gradient: **it is largest exactly where the region is remotest and the airport least substitutable, and shrinks toward zero where a competing airport or tolerable overland alternative exists.** This distributional gradient is itself the proof of the conditional verdict. The dividend is also uneven — catalytic tourism wage gains concentrate in lower-paid, seasonal hospitality work, so an airport can raise aggregate output while widening within-region inequality.

**Four policy recommendations:**

1. **Commission additionality-adjusted net studies, not gross headlines** — the gap between them is the entire substance of the hypothesis["Economic Evaluation of Investments in Airports: Recent Developments," Journal of Benefit-](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-benefit-cost-analysis/article/abs/economic-evaluation-of-investments-in-airports-recent-developments/6EDF14C240B46D9C705724339EA7E7B6).
2. **Target subsidy toward demonstrable net uplift** — remoteness, tourism orientation, absence of a competing airport. A dollar at a Highlands island buys more additionality than one at an airport 40 minutes from a larger hub[Pot, F. & Koster, S. 2022, "Small airports: Runways to regional economic growth?," Journal](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S096669232100315X).
3. **Measure social outcomes directly** — retained population, retained employers, service access — rather than inferring them from enplanement growth[Drukker et al. 2023, "How Essential Is Essential Air Service? The Value of Airport Access](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/68535).
4. **Avoid both symmetric errors** — dismissing small airports as trivial (refuted by remote-region net uplift) and crediting them with transformative economy-wide effects (refuted by the LSE sectoral finding)[LSE/USAPP 2014, "Airports have a small benefit on local service-sector employment, but no](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/58716/).

The practically useful question is not "what is the total spending the airport touches?" but **"what economic and social activity would genuinely disappear if the airport closed?"** For a remote, tourism-oriented, sole-gateway region, the answer is substantial and the hypothesis holds robustly. For a hub-shadowed region with a tolerable overland alternative, the answer is modest and the hypothesis holds only weakly.

By 2030, as regional networks face decarbonization pressure and tightening budgets, the airports that survive the net-impact test — the remote, additional, least-substitutable gateways — are precisely the ones the evidence says deserve to. **The 500k band is not a magic threshold, but it is a band at which a well-sited airport demonstrably moves the socioeconomic needle for the region that depends on it, while a poorly-sited one mostly recirculates money that would have flowed regardless.** The verdict, in one line: **conditionally supported, with geography as the condition.**
