# Stealing Genes Across Kingdoms: Horizontal Gene Transfer in Plants and Animals and Its Evolutionary Significance

Horizontal gene transfer (HGT) in multicellular plants and animals is real, repeatedly documented, and functionally consequential — but it is a **rare, episodic phenomenon** constrained by a stacked series of cellular barriers, not the routine evolutionary force it is in bacteria. Across the best-curated genomic surveys the signal is small and uneven: foreign genes constitute roughly **0.16–1.44% of phytoplankton gene inventories**, about **4.4% of the choanoflagellate *Monosiga brevicollis* nuclear genome**, and an outlier **~8–10% of the bdelloid rotifer transcriptome**.

The field has shifted, in the words of *Trends in Genetics*, from asking **"if" to "how much"**[*Horizontal Gene Transfer in Eukaryotes: Not if, but How Much?*, Trends in Genetics](https://www.cell.com/trends/genetics/fulltext/S0168-9525(20). Many headline claims — including widely cited reports of dozens of bacterial genes in the human genome — collapsed under contamination-controlled reanalysis, recasting them as artefacts[Genome Biology 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-017-1214-2). Through 2030, the measured frequency of eukaryotic HGT is expected to rise chiefly because long-read and phylogenomic detection are improving, not because the underlying biology is changing.

The rarity itself is the most informative signal, because it localizes exactly which cellular and ecological barriers a foreign gene must cross to survive. This report scores fourteen recurring candidate cases against a single weighted rubric, so that each claim is evaluated rather than merely narrated.

## Scope and Definitions

HGT (synonymous with lateral gene transfer, LGT) is the **non-sexual movement of genetic information between genomes**, in which incoming DNA or RNA either replaces an existing gene or introduces a new one[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotic evolution*, Nature Reviews Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrg2386). This report's recipients of interest are **multicellular plants and animals** — organisms that sequester their reproductive cells, so that a foreign gene landing in a leaf or gut epithelium cannot be inherited unless it also reaches the germline.

Two boundaries define the analysis:

- **Bacterial conjugation enters only as a contrast baseline.** In prokaryotes, every recipient cell is a founder, so uptake equals heritability; the prokaryotic consensus rests partly on compact genomes that make detection tractable[*Horizontal Gene Transfer in Eukaryotes: Not if, but How Much?*, Trends in Genetics](https://www.cell.com/trends/genetics/fulltext/S0168-9525(20). Eukaryotic HGT, by contrast, trades off somatic uptake against germline fixation — and only the latter is heritable.
- **Protists and microbial eukaryotes are context-only.** A single-celled eukaryote that takes up foreign DNA takes it up in the cell that also reproduces, collapsing the somatic-versus-germline distinction. They sharpen the comparison without being scored cases.

The **donor universe is broad in principle but bacterially weighted in evidence**, because bacterial genomes are the best reference set against which a xenolog can be recognized. The report tracks five donor classes: bacteria (best documented — e.g., sweet potato T-DNA[*The genome of cultivated sweet potato contains Agrobacterium T-DNAs with expressed genes*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1419685112)), plants (e.g., whitefly BtPMaT1[*Whitefly hijacks a plant detoxification gene that neutralizes plant toxins*, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33770502/)), bryophytes (fern neochrome[*Horizontal transfer of an adaptive chimeric photoreceptor from bryophytes to ferns*](https://d.docksci.com/horizontal-transfer-of-an-adaptive-chimeric-photoreceptor-from-bryophytes-to-fer_5ae8e7fad64ab2c9af8a1b5a.html)), transposable elements[*Promiscuous DNA: horizontal transfer of transposable elements and why it matters*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2940939/), and — more weakly — fungi, viruses, and arthropods.

## The Evaluation Rubric

Every case is scored against a fixed, weighted six-item rubric, published here so the report functions as an evaluation rather than a survey.

| ID | Label | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| R-1 | Evidence Quality | 0.25 |
| R-2 | Mechanistic Plausibility | 0.15 |
| R-3 | Functional Integration | 0.20 |
| R-4 | Adaptive Benefit | 0.20 |
| R-5 | Frequency and Distribution Context | 0.10 |
| R-6 | Contamination and Artifact Control | 0.10 |

Two design choices carry the report's thesis:

- **Contamination control (0.10) is scored separately from evidence quality (0.25).** A case can have excellent phylogenetic signal that is nonetheless an artefact — the bdelloid rotifer episode, where a 2018 re-analysis of a 2016 study revealed possible contamination[*Horizontal Gene Transfer in Bdelloid Rotifers Questioned*, The Scientist](https://www.the-scientist.com/horizontal-gene-transfer-in-bdelloid-rotifers-questioned-64483), is the canonical proof that these dimensions are independent. This separation prevents a technically clean phylogeny from laundering a dirty sample.
- **Adaptive Benefit and Functional Integration together carry 0.40 — double the weight of mechanism.** The animating question is not whether genes move but whether their movement matters. A foreign gene that is present, phylogenetically clean, and inert is, for this report's purposes, a weaker result than a messier case that demonstrably changes the recipient's fitness[*Horizontal gene acquisitions by eukaryotes as drivers of adaptive evolution*, BioEssays](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/bies.201300095).

Frequency is weighted low (0.10) deliberately: **rarity is the report's conclusion, not its scoring criterion.** A single well-documented adaptive transfer (sweet potato) is more valuable than a recurrent but functionally obscure one. The discriminating power shows in a direct comparison: the sweet potato scores near-maximally on both evidence and contamination control — 291/291 accessions is a distribution no contaminant produces[*The genome of cultivated sweet potato contains Agrobacterium T-DNAs with expressed genes*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1419685112) — while the bdelloid complement scores high on functional narrative but is penalized on contamination control.

## The Causal Spine

The report's analytical core is a single causal chain that the middle chapters build link by link:

```
Ecological contact → DNA uptake → Somatic integration → Germline access
→ Fixation → Expression & benefit → Evolutionary significance
```

The defining property is that this chain is **serial and multiplicative**: a transfer succeeds only if every link is cleared, so the overall probability is the product of several small probabilities. This is the **weak-link model** (Huang): each step — uptake, escape from vacuoles, nuclear import, recombination, expression — is individually inefficient, so fixation is rare in eukaryotes[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: the weak-link model*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033532/). The model's master insight is that rarity is *quantitative, not categorical*: no link is impassable, but their conjunction yields a low per-lineage acquisition rate that nonetheless remains nonzero over evolutionary time.

Crucially, the **germline-access step is the link multicellular animals impose but protists do not** — the single most important asymmetry between the plant and animal cases.

## Controlled Vocabulary

Precise terminology matters because a large share of retracted HGT claims failed when an author used "HGT" where a cheaper process was meant:

- **Endosymbiotic gene transfer (EGT):** movement of genes from an organelle genome (mitochondrion, plastid) into the nucleus of the same cell — an active, ongoing process[*Endosymbiotic gene transfer: organelle genomes forge eukaryotic chromosomes*, Nature Revi](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrg1271). EGT matters mainly as a confounder: the fixed and shrinking gene pool of organelles cannot account for all, or even most, bacterial-origin genes in eukaryotes, so a residue of genuine HGT must remain[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: the weak-link model*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033532/).
- **Intracellular gene transfer (IGT):** the broader category of compartment-to-compartment movement within a cell, of which EGT is the organelle-to-nucleus special case.
- **HT-of-TEs:** horizontal transfer of transposable elements between species, treated as a distinct process[*Promiscuous DNA: horizontal transfer of transposable elements and why it matters*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2940939/).
- **Phylogenetic incongruence** (gene tree–species tree mismatch): the primary evidentiary signature of HGT.
- **Germline sequestration (the Weismann barrier):** the developmental separation of somatic and reproductive lineages in animals. Plants lack a strictly sequestered germline — their reproductive cells differentiate late from somatic meristem — whereas most animals set aside the germline early. This becomes the load-bearing causal variable in the plant-versus-animal synthesis.

The **mobilome** — the full complement of mobile genetic elements — reframes the donor question: the relevant unit of transfer is often not a tidy protein-coding gene but a mobile element carrying genes as passengers[*Genetic exchange in eukaryotes through horizontal transfer: connected by the mobilome*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5791352/).

# Distinguishing HGT from Its Mimics

The hardest problem in eukaryotic HGT research is not finding candidate events but excluding cheaper explanations. The definition's two clauses generate the entire agenda: "non-sexual" separates HGT from hybridization and introgression; "between genomes" forces a decision about whether organelle-to-nucleus movement counts[Keeling, Nature Reviews Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00688-5).

## HGT versus Vertical Inheritance

Vertical inheritance is the null expectation against which every HGT claim is measured. A **xenolog** — a horizontally acquired gene — is expected to resolve phylogenetically with its inferred donor lineage rather than with the recipient's close relatives[Keeling, Nature Reviews Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00688-5)[The American Naturalist](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/600082). Whereas vertical inheritance predicts every gene traces the same branching history as the organism, HGT predicts a small minority trace a different history entirely; the two make opposite predictions about a single measurable quantity, which is the only positive test the field possesses.

A consequential distinction follows: incoming DNA can **replace** an existing gene (xenologous replacement — ancestry changes, repertoire doesn't) or be **additive** (introducing a capability the recipient lacked). Both documented flagship cases are additive: the sweet potato's *Agrobacterium* T-DNAs entered as new material[*The genome of cultivated sweet potato contains Agrobacterium T-DNAs with expressed genes*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1419685112), and the whitefly's BtPMaT1 conferred a detoxification capacity the insect did not previously possess[*Whitefly hijacks a plant detoxification gene that neutralizes plant toxins*, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33770502/). Additive events are plausibly the ones most likely to produce a demonstrated fitness benefit, because they supply a capacity selection could not readily generate from existing variation[Keeling, Nature Reviews Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00688-5).

## The EGT–HGT Continuum

EGT, endosymbiont-to-host insertion, and free-living-donor HGT are better understood as **positions on one donor-proximity continuum** than as three separate phenomena. Organelle-to-nucleus transfer is the quantitatively dominant form of intracellular gene movement[*Endosymbiotic gene transfer: organelle genomes forge eukaryotic chromosomes*, Nature Revi](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrg1271) precisely because the donor DNA already sits in the cytoplasm, bypassing the uptake and vacuolar-escape steps that throttle free-living-donor HGT[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: the weak-link model*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033532/).

By a strict reading of "between genomes," EGT satisfies the HGT definition — it differs only in the donor's *residence status*, not in the underlying molecular process. The recombination and expression steps are identical whether the DNA originated in a plastid or a free-living bacterium[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: the weak-link model*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033532/). This generates a testable prediction: **HGT contribution should scale inversely with donor distance** — intracellular donors (organelles, endosymbionts) contribute most, intimately associated donors (parasites, prey) an intermediate amount, fully free-living donors the least. The clustering of documented cases around parasitic plants and plant-feeding insects is consistent with this ordering.

## Hybridization, Introgression and ILS

The processes most likely to masquerade as HGT also produce gene tree–species tree incongruence without horizontal movement[^17]:

- **Introgression** is a *sexual* process operating between crossable (typically congeneric) lineages. This is the mechanistic reason the most secure HGT cases are *interkingdom* — bacterium-to-plant, plant-to-insect, bryophyte-to-fern — where introgression is excluded by biology rather than by argument. Intra-kingdom cases between crossable lineages must clear the introgression bar by explicit quantitative argument or fail.
- **Incomplete lineage sorting (ILS)** — retention of ancestral polymorphism across speciation events — produces genuine incongruence with no gene movement. The discriminating signal is *where* the discordant gene branches: ILS can only shuffle topologies among lineages sharing a recent common ancestor, so an ILS-induced discordance places the gene among near relatives, whereas HGT places it with a donor too distant to have shared any ancestral polymorphism. **A single discordant gene tree is never, by itself, sufficient evidence for HGT.**
- **Convergence and gene loss** can also generate look-alike signals. The neochrome photoreceptor illustrates both coexisting in one gene family: NEOCHROME arose twice by convergent assembly[*A chimeric photoreceptor gene, NEOCHROME, has arisen twice during plant evolution*, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.0504734102), yet a separate appearance in ferns is a genuine horizontal acquisition[*Horizontal transfer of an adaptive chimeric photoreceptor from bryophytes to ferns*](https://d.docksci.com/horizontal-transfer-of-an-adaptive-chimeric-photoreceptor-from-bryophytes-to-fer_5ae8e7fad64ab2c9af8a1b5a.html). The resolution is topological — convergence does not nest one lineage's gene inside another's donor clade, whereas transfer does.

The master discriminator across all these mimics is **donor distance**: none of the cheaper explanations can place a gene with a donor too distant to cross sexually or to share ancestral variation.

## Horizontal Transfer of Transposable Elements

HTT is **simultaneously the most frequent and the least host-adaptive** form of horizontal movement in eukaryotes. A transposon confined to a single vertically inherited lineage decays through mutational inactivation; one that jumps to a new host escapes that decay[Gilbert & Feschotte, Curr Opin Genet Dev 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6069605/). Critically, a successful HTT seeds a *self-amplifying* element that can proliferate to thousands of copies — so HTT can dominate the quantitative budget of horizontally acquired sequence even where single-gene HGT is rare.

This report counts HTT as HGT for definitional and frequency purposes (it satisfies both clauses of the definition[Gilbert & Feschotte, Curr Opin Genet Dev 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6069605/)) while **scoring it separately on Adaptive Benefit**, because a transferred TE is in the first instance a selfish element whose spread serves its own replication, not the host's fitness. The open question this raises — whether the mobilome is predominantly a passenger or a vehicle that ferries flanking host genes across species boundaries[*Genetic exchange in eukaryotes through horizontal transfer: connected by the mobilome*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5791352/) — determines whether HTT belongs in the same causal account as adaptive HGT or in a parallel one.

# The Microbial Baseline: Why HGT Is Routine in Prokaryotes

The microbial baseline is the report's **control condition**: before judging whether eukaryotic HGT is genuinely rare or merely hard to see, we need a reference case where the process is known to be frequent.

## Prokaryotic Mechanisms

Prokaryotes possess **dedicated, characterised delivery infrastructure**. Two routes are documented step-by-step:

- **Transduction:** a bacteriophage accidentally packages fragments of host DNA into viral capsids during assembly; on infecting a new cell, it injects donor bacterial DNA that can recombine into the recipient genome[*Mechanisms of horizontal gene transfer*, AskFilo 2024](https://askfilo.com/user-question-answers-smart-solutions/explain-three-mechanism-of-horizontal-gene-transfer-and-5-3135323239333037).
- **Conjugation:** a donor cell extends a sex pilus forming a conjugative bridge, through which plasmid DNA transfers by rolling-circle replication, the recipient synthesizing the complementary strand[*Horizontal Gene Transfer*, Microbial Genetics 2025](https://open.maricopa.edu/microbialgenetics/chapter/horizontal-gene-transfer/).

The transferred cargo — chiefly **plasmids** — arrives as a coherent, independently establishing unit rather than as a fragment that must integrate before functioning[*Horizontal Gene Transfer*, Microbial Genetics 2025](https://open.maricopa.edu/microbialgenetics/chapter/horizontal-gene-transfer/). The clinical face of this is antibiotic-resistance spread: any gene on a conjugative plasmid rides the same route[*Horizontal Gene Transfer*, Microbial Genetics 2025](https://open.maricopa.edu/microbialgenetics/chapter/horizontal-gene-transfer/). The analytically important contrast: **no comparable routine vector is known to deliver DNA into a sequestered animal germline.**

## Why Microbes Exchange Genes Freely

Three structural enablers make delivered DNA more likely to survive, integrate, and function in microbes:

1. **No germline sequestration.** The cell that takes up foreign DNA is the cell that reproduces, so any integrated gene is directly heritable — collapsing the three-step animal barrier (soma–germline isolation, restricted germ-cell entry, reduced inheritance[*Assessing the effects of a sequestered germline on interdomain lateral gene transfer in M](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27139503/)) into a single permissive event.
2. **Single-cell exposure to environmental DNA.** A prokaryotic genome sits far closer to environmental DNA, and lacks the vacuolar trafficking and nuclear-envelope steps that the weak-link model identifies as eukaryote-specific bottlenecks[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: the weak-link model*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033532/).
3. **Compact, operon-organised genomes.** A transferred operon delivers a co-transcribed *working module* rather than a fragment requiring independent regulatory assembly. Keeling notes documented plant HGT cases include bacterial operon transfers[Keeling, Nature Reviews Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00688-5), and such modules can confer novel traits enabling niche exploitation and adaptive radiation[Nature Reviews Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrg3962).

## Eukaryote-Specific Barriers

Each microbial permissiveness has a eukaryotic mirror image:

- **Germline sequestration (animals).** The 2016 study frames the sequestered germline as an *argued* obstacle to interdomain LGT, noting that few studies had specifically assessed the claim[*Assessing the effects of a sequestered germline on interdomain lateral gene transfer in M](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27139503/). The barrier raises the threshold rather than forbidding transfer — which is why successful animal cases concentrate in lineages with unusual germline biology, like the bdelloid rotifer with its asyngamous recombination and loss of heterozygosity[*Recombination in bdelloid rotifer genomes*, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168952524000283).
- **Nuclear envelope and expression incompatibility.** Foreign DNA must cross a membrane barrier absent in prokaryotes, and even integrated genes may fail to express because their regulatory grammar misaligns with eukaryotic machinery[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: the weak-link model*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033532/) — the operon advantage inverts into a eukaryotic liability.
- **Sexual recombination** can dilute a newly acquired gene before fixation. The bdelloid's asyngamous recombination[*Recombination in bdelloid rotifer genomes*, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168952524000283) is a departure from standard sex that changes how foreign genes propagate.

## Reframing 'Rarity'

How much of eukaryotic HGT's apparent rarity is biological and how much is a detection artefact? Both forces operate. **Detection bias** makes HGT appear rarer in eukaryotes because large, complex genomes hinder assembly and gene prediction, whereas compact prokaryote genomes have yielded thousands of complete sequences[*Horizontal Gene Transfer in Eukaryotes: Not if, but How Much?* Rutgers version](https://dbm.rutgers.edu/news/Van%20Etten%20and%20Bhattacharya_Trends_Genetics-2020.pdf). The *BMC Biology* "tip of the iceberg or of the ice cube?" framing captures the uncertainty[*Lateral gene transfer in eukaryotes: tip of the iceberg or of the ice cube?*, BMC Biology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12915-016-0330-x).

But a **real biological difference** remains after correcting for detection: the weak-link multiplication has no counterpart in the permissive microbial cell[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: the weak-link model*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033532/). The lifestyle dependence is documented in fungi, where parasites show the highest HGT-acquired gene counts, saprotrophs intermediate, and symbionts lowest[mBio](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/mbio.02855-24) — cautioning that ecological intimacy alone does not predict HGT load.

# Mechanisms and Ecological Routes in Multicellular Eukaryotes

## The Seven-Link Spine

A successful eukaryotic transfer must clear seven serial gates: **donor exposure → cell entry → germline access → integration → expression → selection → fixation.** Because the gates are multiplicative, the compound probability is the mechanistic root of rarity.

Not all gates are equally restrictive. **In plants, cell-entry and integration are demonstrably permissive** — the *Agrobacterium* T-DNA route routinely crosses the plant wall and integrates into chromatin[*Traversing the Cell: Agrobacterium T-DNA's Journey to the Host Genome*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3355731/). **In animals, the germline-access link is the dominant bottleneck**: germline sequestration isolates germ cells, limits foreign-DNA entry, and reduces inheritance[*Assessing the effects of a sequestered germline on interdomain lateral gene transfer in M](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27139503/). A second filter operates after integration: an unexpressed or useless xenolog degrades into a pseudogene, which means **intact, expressed xenologs are disproportionately likely to be genuine transfers rather than contaminants**, because contamination does not integrate and express in the host's regulatory context.

## Intimate-Contact Routes

Direct tissue contact solves the donor-exposure link but does nothing on its own for germline access:

- **The haustorium** is the best-characterised natural conduit for plant-to-plant HGT. This invasive organ connects parasite to host vasculature, allowing exchange of water, nutrients, proteins, nucleotides, pathogens, and retrotransposons[*The Haustorium, a Specialized Invasive Organ in Parasitic Plants*](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-arplant-043015-111702). Germline access is partially relaxed because the parasite's meristematic tissue can lie near the feeding interface.
- **Grafting** recreates cell-to-cell proximity experimentally but is mostly human-made and developmentally distant from reproductive tissue, so its contribution to the natural record is marginal.
- **Herbivory/feeding** is the animal analogue: a plant-eating insect processes enormous quantities of plant DNA at the gut epithelium. The whitefly BtPMaT1 acquisition is the best-documented case[*Whitefly hijacks a plant detoxification gene that neutralizes plant toxins*, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33770502/), though *how* the gene bridged from gut to germline remains the open mechanistic problem[*Plant to insect horizontal gene transfer: empowering whiteflies*](https://univ-angers.hal.science/hal-03219146v1/document).

## Vector-Mediated Routes

Vectors supply dedicated machinery that actively performs cell entry and integration, which is why they dominate the highest-confidence cases:

- **Agrobacterium T4SS** is the only eukaryotic HGT mechanism with every link from secretion to chromatin integration experimentally reconstructed: the type IV secretion system exports single-stranded T-DNA across the cell wall and plasma membrane, traffics it to the nucleus, and integrates it into chromatin[*Transfer of DNA from Bacteria to Eukaryotes*](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/mbio.00863-16)[*Traversing the Cell: Agrobacterium T-DNA's Journey to the Host Genome*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3355731/). Even so, natural fixation is rare because the germline-access lottery remains.
- **Viruses and retroelements** can integrate *directly* into the germline, bypassing the bottleneck that defeats every other animal route: a virion infects a host cell, reverse-transcribes, and integrates; when this occurs in a germ-line cell, the sequence is inherited as a host allele[*Endogenous Viral Elements in Animal Genomes*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2987831/). This is why retroelement families like LINE-1 and BovB are over-represented in animal HGT[*Horizontal transfer of BovB and L1 retrotransposons in eukaryotes*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29983116/) — they are self-integrating agents the Weismann barrier was never designed to exclude. The tradeoff: this self-integrating property makes them mostly selfish rather than adaptive, with only a minority domesticated into host function.
- **Endosymbionts** like *Wolbachia* live permanently inside host cells, often within reproductive tissue, collapsing donor-exposure and germline-access into a single condition. This connects to EGT, where organelle-to-nucleus translocation has been ongoing since organelles arose from free-living prokaryotes[*Endosymbiotic gene transfer: organelle genomes forge eukaryotic chromosomes*, Nature Revi](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrg1271). Because *Wolbachia* infects a vast fraction of insect species and resides in the germline, it is the single most probable source of future-documented bacterium-to-animal transfers.

**Vectors do not abolish the seven-link chain — they relocate the bottleneck.**

## Environmental and Organellar Routes

Two further routes lack both a living donor in contact and dedicated machinery:

- **Environmental DNA uptake / phagocytosis** combines donor-exposure and cell-entry into a single act of engulfment, which is why phagotrophic protists and early-diverging animals (sponges, placozoans) show elevated HGT[Keeling, *Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: aligning theory with data*, Nature Revie](https://www3.botany.ubc.ca/keeling/PDF/24HGT.pdf). But a phagocytosed fragment arrives with no machinery to guide it to the nucleus, so it faces a steeper integration link[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: the weak-link model*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033532/).
- **Mitochondrial genome capture in plants** exploits the unusually recombinogenic, HGT-permissive plant mitochondrial genome, where the integration link is comparatively permissive.

# Documented Plant Cases

Plant genomes furnish the richest, best-validated catalogue of eukaryotic HGT, because several plant lineages maintain physical conduits placing foreign DNA in direct contact with recipient cells.

## Parasitic Plants: Cuscuta and Broomrapes

**Cuscuta (dodder)** is the clearest plant-to-plant case. The 2020 convergent-HGT analysis reports **108 transcribed, probably functional candidate HGT events**, plus 42 additional host-derived transposon, pseudogene, and non-coding regions[*Convergent horizontal gene transfer and cross-talk of mobile nucleic acids in parasitic p](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31332314/). Because *Cuscuta* is a generalist, the inferred donor varies across events — itself diagnostic, since a gene tracing to many different hosts is far harder to explain by vertical descent than by repeated capture. Several captured genes localize to the haustorial feeding interface, operating in the very tissue through which they entered.

The case passes Evidence Quality and Functional Integration (at transcription level) clearly; Adaptive Benefit remains inferred for most genes. Its **most striking feature is recurrence**: 18 of the captured genes were independently acquired in the same gene families by **Orobanchaceae** parasites (broomrapes and witchweeds)[*Convergent horizontal gene transfer and cross-talk of mobile nucleic acids in parasitic p](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31332314/). This convergent acquisition is among the most persuasive arguments against artefact — a contaminant would not independently recapitulate the same family-level pattern in two separately sequenced lineages. The shared mechanism is the haustorium, which moves nucleic acids passively through plant plumbing rather than via a bacterial secretion system. The convergence reveals the route as **directional rather than idiosyncratic**: it repeatedly delivers members of functionally coherent families to the recipient.

**Frequency in plant HGT tracks anatomy**: lineages with standing conduits occupy a high-frequency regime; those without show mainly ancient transfers.

## Land-Plant Colonization: Bryophyte Gene Acquisition

The moss ***Physcomitrella patens*** carries **57 nuclear gene families** acquired anciently from prokaryotes, fungi, or viruses[*Widespread impact of horizontal gene transfer on plant colonization of land*, Nature Comm](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms2148) — true interkingdom transfers, distinct from the intra-kingdom parasitic cases. The functional roster is the analytical heart: xylem formation, plant defence, nitrogen recycling, and starch, polyamine, hormone, and glutathione biosynthesis — each mapping onto a challenge of terrestrial life that an aquatic ancestor would not have faced[*Widespread impact of horizontal gene transfer on plant colonization of land*, Nature Comm](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms2148).

The case passes Functional Integration strongly (resident nuclear families, not transient insertions) and is strengthened against contamination by its embedding in genuine plant genomic context[*Widespread impact of horizontal gene transfer on plant colonization of land*, Nature Comm](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms2148). Its significance is uniquely broad: where parasitic transfers improve a specialized lifestyle, the *Physcomitrella* transfers are framed as having helped build the metabolic and structural toolkit for **an entire habitat shift — the colonization of land**. A necessary caution: "widespread" describes the *cumulative historical footprint* across 57 families, not a high contemporary rate — a low per-lineage rate can produce a large cumulative count over hundreds of millions of years.

## Transkingdom and Ancient Transfers

- **Fern neochrome (hornwort → fern)** is one of the cleanest adaptive HGT stories in any eukaryote. NEOCHROME is a chimeric photoreceptor fusing red-sensing phytochrome and blue-sensing phototropin modules[*Horizontal transfer of an adaptive chimeric photoreceptor from bryophytes to ferns*, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1319929111). The functional payoff is enhanced low-light adaptation, which matters for shade-dwelling understory ferns. The complete chain — transfer, integration, expression conferring advantage, selective fixation[*Horizontal transfer of an adaptive chimeric photoreceptor from bryophytes to ferns*, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1319929111) — is precisely the one the weak-link model says should rarely complete, which makes it a showcase. The chimeric architecture is itself an internal control against contamination. (Note: the chimeric architecture *also* arose independently twice in plant history[*A chimeric photoreceptor gene, NEOCHROME, has arisen twice during plant evolution*, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.0504734102), so convergence and transfer coexist in this gene's history.)

- **Penelope-like retroelements (arthropod → conifer)** is the first documented animal-to-plant transposable-element transfer[*An Ancient Transkingdom Horizontal Transfer of Penelope-Like Retroelements from Arthropod](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4860704/). After transfer, the PLEs amplified to become a major component of conifer genomes. This is a **structurally important counterexample to the assumption that fixed HGT equals adaptive HGT**: a selfish element with no host benefit became a defining feature of conifer genome architecture, because intragenomic self-replication drives copy-number increase independently of organismal fitness[*Promiscuous DNA: horizontal transfer of transposable elements and why it matters*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2940939/). The frequency profile is one rare crossing magnified into large copy number — the opposite of *Cuscuta*'s many independent events.

- **Mitochondrial gene capture** between flowering plants exploits the recombinogenic plant mitochondrial genome, distinct from EGT.

# Documented Animal Cases

Animal HGT clusters around a few ecological lifestyles that repeatedly place foreign DNA near a germline — chiefly feeding relationships, desiccation-driven uptake, and ancient captures. The cases fall along a steep evidential gradient.

## Insect HGT

- **Whitefly *Bemisia tabaci* (BtPMaT1).** The clearest demonstration of repeated plant-to-insect capture: **49 plant-like genes from at least 24 independent HGT events** in the MEAM1 genome[*Multiple Horizontal Acquisitions of Plant Genes in the Whitefly Bemisia tabaci*, PubMed 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36155788/). The signature gene, BtPMaT1, is a phenolic glucoside malonyltransferase that **mediates resistance to plant phenolic glucoside toxins**, letting the insect feed on chemically defended tissue[*Whitefly hijacks a plant detoxification gene that neutralizes plant toxins*, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33770502/)[*Multiple Horizontal Acquisitions of Plant Genes in the Whitefly Bemisia tabaci*, PubMed 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36155788/). This is among the few animal cases with an *experimentally demonstrated* adaptive benefit. Contamination is untenable: 24 phylogenetically independent acquisitions plus a functional assay cannot be produced by a contaminant. Recurrence within a single lineage sets it apart from the single-event pattern typical of most animal cases.

- **Pea aphid (fungal carotenoid pathway).** An ancestral aphid acquired the entire carotenoid-biosynthesis pathway — including phytoene synthase and desaturase — from a fungus, giving it endogenous pigment production most animals lack[*Lateral Transfer of Genes from Fungi Underlies Carotenoid Production in Aphids*, Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1187113). Where the whitefly gene *removes a barrier* (toxins), the aphid pathway *adds a capability* (biosynthesis): two distinct modes by which HGT raises fitness. The genes group phylogenetically with fungi and produce functional pigment, ruling out contamination. This is a single ancient fixation — the modal frequency pattern for well-supported animal HGT.

- **Coffee berry borer (mannanase).** A seed-boring beetle proposed to have acquired a plant-cell-wall-degrading enzyme. This case is **supported only by analogy** to the well-documented nematode cell-wall enzymes[*Horizontal gene transfer of microbial cellulases into nematode genomes*, BMC Evolutionary](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2148-11-13), not by case-specific data marshalled here. It passes Mechanistic Plausibility (a gut-microbiome route is plausible) but sits a tier below the flagship cases on Evidence Quality.

**Sustained feeding relationships — with plants, fungi, or gut microbes — are the dominant route to insect HGT.**

## Microscopic Animals

- **Bdelloid rotifers (foreign-gene complement).** The broadest multi-kingdom donor complement of any animal case: foreign genes derive mainly from eubacteria but also fungi, protists, and algae, with ~10% of ~29,000 transcripts matched to horizontally acquired origins[*Biochemical Diversification through Foreign Gene Expression in Bdelloid Rotifers*, PLOS G](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1003035). The mechanism is the most explicit of any animal case: **desiccation causes double-strand breaks, repair incorporates environmental DNA, foreign genes are expressed, and selection fixes beneficial acquisitions**[*Biochemical Diversification through Foreign Gene Expression in Bdelloid Rotifers*, PLOS G](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1003035) — a direct genomic uptake route absent in the insect cases, reinforced by signatures of asyngamous recombination[*Recombination in bdelloid rotifer genomes*, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168952524000283). The case is the field's canonical contamination lesson: a 2018 re-analysis revealed possible contamination in the 2016 data[*Horizontal Gene Transfer in Bdelloid Rotifers Questioned*, The Scientist](https://www.the-scientist.com/horizontal-gene-transfer-in-bdelloid-rotifers-questioned-64483). The resolution is **partition, not refutation** — the integrated, expressed fraction holds while the contaminant fraction is removed, so the headline percentage should be treated as an upper bound pending per-gene verification.

- **Plant-parasitic nematodes (cell-wall enzymes).** A functionally focused microbe-to-animal transfer: cellulases, hemicellulases, and pectinases (including GH45 cellulases and pectate lyases) that degrade plant cell walls — a key adaptation for parasitism[*Horizontal gene transfer of microbial cellulases into nematode genomes*, BMC Evolutionary](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2148-11-13). The 2011 analysis documents not just presence but **functional assimilation and gene turnover**[*Horizontal gene transfer of microbial cellulases into nematode genomes*, BMC Evolutionary](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2148-11-13), hallmarks of genuine integration a contaminant cannot exhibit, placing this among the most securely integrated animal transfers. The causal chain is direct: acquired enzymes enable cell-wall degradation, which enables tissue invasion, which underpins the entire plant-parasitic lifestyle — the animal parallel to parasitic-plant HGT.

The **desiccation-asexuality link** is mechanistically special: desiccation supplies the uptake opportunity while asexuality removes the recombinational dilution that might otherwise purge acquired sequences[*Biochemical Diversification through Foreign Gene Expression in Bdelloid Rotifers*, PLOS G](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1003035)[*Recombination in bdelloid rotifer genomes*, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168952524000283). This identifies a lifestyle that systematically raises the per-lineage acquisition rate, and generates a testable prediction: **other desiccation-tolerant asexual animals should, with rigorous contamination controls, show elevated foreign-gene complements.** It also offers a resolution to the paradox of a persistent asexual lineage — HGT-supplied novelty may partly compensate for the absent genetic mixing of sex.

**Lifestyle, not feeding alone, drives microscopic-animal HGT**, confirming that the distribution of animal HGT is not random but concentrated in lineages whose biology repeatedly places foreign DNA near the genome.

# Detection Methods and Evidence Standards

Establishing genuine transfer is a problem of converging independent evidence, because every diagnostic signal has a non-horizontal explanation capable of producing the same pattern. The collapse of the human-HGT claims and the bdelloid reanalysis both turned on exactly these ambiguities[Genome Biology 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-017-1214-2).

## Phylogenetic Incongruence

The foundational signal: if a gene were inherited vertically, its tree should mirror the organism's tree; a xenolog declares itself when the two disagree[PLOS Computational Biology 2015](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004095). The approach is mechanism-independent — the signature is the same whether DNA arrived by haustorial contact or by feeding. But the claim graduates from suggestive to load-bearing only when a **formal statistical test rejects the constrained topology** that would place the gene with its host's relatives[PLOS Computational Biology 2015](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004095). The distance between a point-estimate tree difference and a formally tested rejection is exactly where many early claims failed on reanalysis[Genome Biology 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-017-1214-2).

The chief pitfall is **long-branch attraction**: rate variation across lineages can drive tree-building methods to group two rapidly evolving lineages by coincidental shared substitutions rather than ancestry, manufacturing false incongruence[Nature Reviews Genetics 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00620-x). Incongruence must therefore be interrogated under models that accommodate rate heterogeneity before it is accepted as biological — a claim cannot be stronger than the model that generated its support.

## Genomic Context, Synteny and Composition

Where phylogeny reads the gene's history, genomic context answers whether the gene is *physically embedded* in the host genome rather than floating in a contaminant:

- **Scaffold embedding + expression.** The sweet potato is the cleanest illustration: T-DNA assembled within the nuclear genome of all 291 accessions and transcribed in the Huachano clone[*The genome of cultivated sweet potato contains Agrobacterium T-DNAs with expressed genes*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1419685112) — a combination a free-floating contaminant cannot produce.
- **Compositional signatures (GC content, codon usage; the 70% rule).** The weakest category, because composition *decays* — a transferred gene ameliorates toward the host equilibrium over time. The 70% rule flags any gene with >70% amino-acid identity to a prokaryotic homolog as potential contamination, on the reasoning that a genuine ancient transfer should have diverged below that threshold[*A natural barrier to lateral gene transfer: the 70% rule*, PMC 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5067920/). Composition is a *recency detector*; phylogeny is a *history detector*.
- **Synteny.** The most contamination-resistant evidence. *Loss* of synteny around a candidate witnesses HGT (the gene sits where its donor's relatives never occupied)[BMC Genomics 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12864-019-6395-5)[*Detecting horizontal gene transfer: a probabilistic approach*, PubMed 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32138652/), while *shared* insertion context across related taxa dates the event before their divergence. The sweet potato shows this: IbT-DNA1/2 extend across the series *Batatas*, indicating an ancient event predating diversification[*The horizontal gene transfer of Agrobacterium T-DNAs into the series Batatas*, Scientific](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-48691-3). Crucially, **synteny does not decay the way composition does**, making it the decisive test for ancient transfers.

The sweet potato IbT-DNA case is the only entity in the matrix satisfying the full embedding stack simultaneously, which is why it serves as the field's reference standard.

## Expression and Functional Validation

Demonstrating presence answers *whether* a transfer occurred; functional validation answers *whether it matters*, on a ladder of increasing strength:

1. **Transcription (RNA-seq):** the sweet potato T-DNA is expressed[*The genome of cultivated sweet potato contains Agrobacterium T-DNAs with expressed genes*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1419685112); the whitefly BtPMaT1 is a functioning enzyme, not a silent remnant[*Whitefly hijacks a plant detoxification gene that neutralizes plant toxins*, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33770502/).
2. **Knockout/knockdown:** the gold standard. CRISPR/Cas9 knockout of the integrated sweet-potato Ib-rolB/C genes significantly reduced chlorogenic acid derivatives, with transient expression confirming the effect on polyphenol content[*Functional Analysis of Naturally Integrated Rol Genes in Sweet Potato via CRISPR/Cas9*, P](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41470592/). The whitefly case reads on an ecological phenotype (toxin tolerance), the sweet-potato case on a metabolic one — a well-designed assay must target the *specific* activity hypothesized.
3. **Distinguishing transcribed pseudogenes:** a knockout phenotype that changes a named trait is what certifies a functional acquisition over a transcribed relic, because a pseudogene has no functioning product to remove[*Functional Analysis of Naturally Integrated Rol Genes in Sweet Potato via CRISPR/Cas9*, P](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41470592/).

## Contamination Controls

Contamination is the dominant source of false claims, because contaminant reads assemble with host DNA, creating false positives[*Within-species contamination of bacterial whole-genome sequence data*, Genome Biology](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s13059-019-1914-x.pdf), and within-species contamination influences clustering *more* than between-species contamination[*Within-species contamination of bacterial whole-genome sequence data*, Genome Biology](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s13059-019-1914-x.pdf) — the hardest contaminants to catch are those most closely related to the host. The **70% rule** is the most widely applied filter, deliberately conservative (accepting some false negatives to sharply reduce false positives)[*A natural barrier to lateral gene transfer: the 70% rule*, PMC 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5067920/), deployed as a pre-phylogenetic screen in **INTERCHANGE-style pipelines** that run cheap decontamination first and expensive phylogenetic tests only on survivors[*A natural barrier to lateral gene transfer: the 70% rule*, PMC 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5067920/).

# Frequency and Distribution

The most defensible answer to "how common?" is **10–243 non-redundant HGT regions per species** across diverse eukaryotic genomes[bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.07.26.501571v3) — the chapter's quantitative anchor. The band's two-orders-of-magnitude width is itself the finding: a species at 10 and one at 243 occupy opposite ends of what "common" could mean.

## Survey Evidence

The unit matters — "non-redundant regions," not raw copies[bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.07.26.501571v3). Lineage-specific tallies sit within this band: the whitefly contributes **49 plant-like genes from ≥24 events**[*Multiple Horizontal Acquisitions of Plant Genes in the Whitefly Bemisia tabaci*, PubMed 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36155788/), while the phagotroph *Monosiga brevicollis* carries **405 genes (~4.4% of its nuclear genome)** of algal and prokaryotic origin[BMC Genomics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2164-14-729). These illustrate a **counting-frame tension**: whether the whitefly or *Monosiga* is "more HGT-rich" depends on whether one counts raw events or genomic proportion. The resolution is methodological transparency — report both the count and the denominator, and the apparent contradiction dissolves.

| Survey frame | Tally | Frame |
|---|---|---|
| Eukaryote cross-species | 10–243 regions/species | Distinct regions |
| *Bemisia tabaci* | 49 genes, ≥24 events | Raw genes/events |
| *Monosiga brevicollis* | 405 genes, ~4.4% | Genomic proportion |
| Most animals | Low; scarcer than plants | Qualitative |

## Variation Across Lifestyles

Lifestyle predicts where a lineage falls within the band. **Parasites > saprotrophs > symbionts** among fungi is one of the cleanest signals[mBio](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/mbio.02855-24), and HGT frequency rises with heterotrophy and contributes to parasite adaptation[PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1608765113). The mechanism connects to the weak-link model: raising the frequency of the *first* step (encounter with foreign DNA) shifts the whole chain's output upward. The phagotroph extreme — *Monosiga*'s 405 genes[BMC Genomics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2164-14-729) — follows because internalising whole donor cells maximises the rate-limiting encounter step.

**Asexuals** (bdelloids) are theoretically enriched because HGT can substitute for the genetic novelty sex provides, though the bdelloid empirical tally is contested after the 2018 reanalysis[*Horizontal Gene Transfer in Bdelloid Rotifers Questioned*, The Scientist](https://www.the-scientist.com/horizontal-gene-transfer-in-bdelloid-rotifers-questioned-64483). **Mammals sit at the low end** — Keeling states HGT is scarcer in animals[Keeling, Nature Reviews Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00688-5), and large-bodied obligate sexuals compound two factors: weak-link inefficiency makes arrival rare, and weak selective premium (abundant internal variation) makes retention rarer still[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: the weak-link model*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033532/)[Keeling, Nature Reviews Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00688-5).

The **plant–animal asymmetry** is most plausibly a *route-availability* difference, not intrinsic receptivity. A nucleus that routinely imports organellar DNA via EGT[*Endosymbiotic gene transfer: organelle genomes forge eukaryotic chromosomes*, Nature Revi](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrg1271) continuously exercises the same import-and-recombination machinery the weak-link model identifies as rate-limiting[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: the weak-link model*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033532/) — a hypothesis (not a directly measured route comparison) that distinguishing it from looser plant germline sequestration or crop-genome study bias requires further comparative data to confirm.

## Detection Bias and the True Rate

Every count is of *detected* events, distorted by three biases:

- **Sampling and publication bias.** Sequencing concentrates on crops, pathogens, and models, so the HGT map reflects where we have looked. Publication bias foregrounds clean adaptive cases (BtPMaT1, sweet potato, neochrome), inflating apparent functional importance while ambiguous candidates go unreported.
- **Ancient-transfer under-detection.** Phylogenetic methods capture only **0.8–1.5% of possible events** and probably underestimate the true number[BMC Genomics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2164-9-136), because the incongruence signal erodes as transferred genes diverge and intermediate lineages lose the gene. Method improvement matters dramatically: long-read co-barcode sequencing yielded **>50-fold more detected events after correction** in one study[Microbiology Spectrum](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.03602-23).
- **The "not if, but how much" reframing**[*Horizontal Gene Transfer in Eukaryotes: Not if, but How Much?*, Trends in Genetics](https://www.cell.com/trends/genetics/fulltext/S0168-9525(20) concedes existence and moves the debate onto magnitude — implying that counting famous cases cannot answer the frequency question, which the genome-wide band addresses better[bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.07.26.501571v3).

## Reconciling 'Widespread' versus 'Rare'

The two camps are **not disagreeing about data but about what to count**. The dynamic-HGT (Yue) view emphasises that every surveyed genome carries ≥10 foreign regions[bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.07.26.501571v3) — HGT is pervasive across taxa. The small-but-significant (Keeling) view emphasises that the per-lineage rate is low and retention selective[Keeling, Nature Reviews Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00688-5). The contradiction dissolves once one notices that **"widespread" refers to distribution across taxa while "rare" refers to rate within a lineage** — a phenomenon can be near-universal in presence yet low in per-lineage frequency. The "iceberg or ice cube"[*Lateral gene transfer in eukaryotes: tip of the iceberg or of the ice cube?*, BMC Biology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12915-016-0330-x) question — how much lies beneath the detected surface — remains unsettled by the data alone.

# Functional Integration and Adaptive Benefit

Functional integration and adaptive benefit are **two analytically separable questions** that the literature frequently conflates.

## The Retention Gauntlet

From insertion to working component, a xenolog passes three filters:

1. **Regulatory capture.** A transferred coding sequence arrives without recipient promoters; expression is one of the inefficient weak-link steps[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: the weak-link model*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033532/). The sweet potato sidesteps this — *Agrobacterium* T-DNA is built to function in plant cells, and the CRISPR knockout of Ib-rolB/C reducing chlorogenic acid derivatives is the gold-standard proof that regulatory context was established[*Functional Analysis of Naturally Integrated Rol Genes in Sweet Potato via CRISPR/Cas9*, P](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41470592/).
2. **Machinery compatibility.** Codon and intron features must suit the recipient. The nematode cellulases show "functional assimilation and gene turnover"[*Horizontal gene transfer of microbial cellulases into nematode genomes*, BMC Evolutionary](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2148-11-13) — behaving as integrated members of the genome, subject to duplication and loss like native genes — the signature distinguishing a resident xenolog from a contaminant.
3. **Selective retention versus drift.** A xenolog can reach fixation by **drift without conferring advantage** — this is the null hypothesis adaptive claims must defeat. Detectability and retention do not establish benefit; only a **positive molecular signature** — a gene-specific sweep or elevated transfer rate for an ecologically relevant gene[Nature Reviews Microbiology 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41579-021-00650-4) — licenses the adaptive inference. The sweet potato passes because its T-DNA is present in every cultivated line but absent from wild relatives[*The genome of cultivated sweet potato contains Agrobacterium T-DNAs with expressed genes*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1419685112), a distribution best explained by selection during domestication.

**Clearing the integration gauntlet is logically prior to, and distinct from, conferring a fitness benefit.**

## Documented Adaptive Benefits

The three flagship animal benefit cases share a theme — each supplies a biochemical capacity the recipient lineage characteristically lacks:

- **Whitefly BtPMaT1** neutralises plant phenolic-glucoside defences, plausibly widening host range[*Whitefly hijacks a plant detoxification gene that neutralizes plant toxins*, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33770502/); functional association is firmer than a measured selection coefficient.
- **Nematode cell-wall enzymes** are explicitly "a key adaptation for parasitism," with a clear causal chain from enzyme to cell-wall breach to a new trophic lifestyle[*Horizontal gene transfer of microbial cellulases into nematode genomes*, BMC Evolutionary](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2148-11-13) — the clearest case where HGT enabled an *entire ecological strategy*.
- **Aphid carotenoid pathway** delivers both pigmentation and oxidative-stress protection[*Lateral Transfer of Genes from Fungi Underlies Carotenoid Production in Aphids*, Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1187113) via a single ancient acquisition.

These reveal a graded evidentiary structure: the nematode case links genes to a niche transition through an explicit chain, while the whitefly and aphid establish functional association more firmly than a *quantified* fitness differential.

## Niche Expansion and Metabolic Novelty

Niche-expanding HGT operates through several mechanisms: enabling a major life-history transition (terrestrialization, light capture — fern neochrome's adaptive photoreceptor[*Horizontal transfer of an adaptive chimeric photoreceptor from bryophytes to ferns*](https://d.docksci.com/horizontal-transfer-of-an-adaptive-chimeric-photoreceptor-from-bryophytes-to-fer_5ae8e7fad64ab2c9af8a1b5a.html)[*A chimeric photoreceptor gene, NEOCHROME, has arisen twice during plant evolution*, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.0504734102)), supplying a wholesale new pathway (aphid carotenoids[*Lateral Transfer of Genes from Fungi Underlies Carotenoid Production in Aphids*, Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1187113)), modulating a resident network (sweet potato rol genes shifting polyphenol output[*Functional Analysis of Naturally Integrated Rol Genes in Sweet Potato via CRISPR/Cas9*, P](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41470592/)), and providing defence/parasitism tools (nematode enzymes, whitefly detox).

The mobile-element strand reveals the **limit of the adaptive framing**: transposable elements frequently spread as *selfish* sequences whose persistence reflects their own replicative success, with any host consequence arising as a byproduct[*Promiscuous DNA: horizontal transfer of transposable elements and why it matters*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2940939/). Distinguishing selfish persistence from domesticated function requires evidence of co-option — a higher bar than mere presence.

## Avoiding the Adaptationist Trap

The most important analytical discipline is **resisting the assumption that every retained xenolog confers a benefit.** The "iceberg or ice cube" framing[*Lateral gene transfer in eukaryotes: tip of the iceberg or of the ice cube?*, BMC Biology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12915-016-0330-x) poses exactly this: whether detectable cases represent a vast functional phenomenon or something largely inert. Two errors must be avoided: treating detection as evidence of importance (detection biases toward functionally distinctive genes), and treating survival of the rarity filter as proof of selection (drift also carries genes to fixation). The burden of proof rests on a *positive* signal, not on mere survival.

# Evolutionary Significance: A Meta-Causal Synthesis

The three axes earlier kept distinct — mechanism, frequency, significance — **do not vary independently.** They are linked by shared upstream variables: host lifestyle, germline architecture, and the detection filter itself.

## Three Couplings

1. **Lifestyle amplifies both opportunity and retention** — as two separable multipliers, not one. Parasitism raises *opportunity* (chronic tissue intimacy maximises donor-DNA delivery); asexuality plausibly raises *retention* (asyngamous recombination changes how foreign tracts are inherited[*Recombination in bdelloid rotifer genomes*, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168952524000283)). The whitefly reaches the well-supported end through dietary donor exposure; the bdelloid through a retention-favouring recombinational regime — different terms of the same compound probability.

2. **Germline-access constraints feed back into observed rarity.** The plant–animal developmental asymmetry is a primary structuring variable: a strict early-sequestered germline reduces fixable somatic acquisitions, which lowers fixed events per animal lineage, which registers as observed rarity, which raises the evidentiary bar any new animal claim must clear — a self-reinforcing chain. This predicts (a falsifiable projection, not a measured forecast) that the strongest candidate taxa for newly validated animal events are obligate parasites, chronic feeders, and ancient asexuals.

3. **Detection bias couples to perceived importance.** The three detection criteria — high similarity, incongruence, patchy distribution[*Horizontal Gene Transfers in Plants*, PubMed 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34440601/) — preferentially recover transfers from distant donors encoding distinctive functions, which **inflates the apparent adaptive fraction** because functionally bland neutral insertions are under-detected by construction. The same property that makes a transfer easy to validate (its distinctness) correlates with the property that makes it look important. Frequency estimates from these criteria are therefore defensible for the adaptive tail but systematically conservative about the neutral background.

**Rarity, detectability, and apparent significance are not three findings but three readings of one underlying process.**

## Maturity Tiers

The case record sorts cleanly by how many detection and integration criteria a case clears[^57]:

**Tier 1 — Well-validated** (strong phylogeny + genomic context + expression):
- *Whitefly BtPMaT1* — plant→insect, toxin neutralisation, single lineage-specific event; the multiplicity of independent events plus a functional assay makes contamination untenable[*Whitefly hijacks a plant detoxification gene that neutralizes plant toxins*, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33770502/)[*Multiple Horizontal Acquisitions of Plant Genes in the Whitefly Bemisia tabaci*, PubMed 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36155788/).
- *Sweet potato T-DNA* — bacterium→plant, 291/291 cultivated accessions with cultivated/wild segregation excluding contamination, expressed, likely domestication-selected, ancient and fixed[*The genome of cultivated sweet potato contains Agrobacterium T-DNAs with expressed genes*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1419685112)[*The horizontal gene transfer of Agrobacterium T-DNAs into the series Batatas*, Scientific](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-48691-3).
- *Bdelloid complement* — placed at the *bottom* of Tier 1 on the verified subset only; a qualified pass on contamination control after the 2018 reanalysis[*Horizontal Gene Transfer in Bdelloid Rotifers Questioned*, The Scientist](https://www.the-scientist.com/horizontal-gene-transfer-in-bdelloid-rotifers-questioned-64483), demonstrating that asexual lineages can accumulate foreign material[*Recombination in bdelloid rotifer genomes*, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168952524000283).

**Tier 2 — Plausible but incomplete** (genuine signal + nameable phenotype, one unresolved step):
- *Tunicate cellulose synthase* — microbe→animal supplying a capacity animals lack, tying a defining body-plan feature (the cellulose tunic) to a single acquisition; the germline route remains inferred.
- *Fern neochrome* — bryophyte→fern adaptive photoreceptor[*Horizontal transfer of an adaptive chimeric photoreceptor from bryophytes to ferns*](https://d.docksci.com/horizontal-transfer-of-an-adaptive-chimeric-photoreceptor-from-bryophytes-to-fer_5ae8e7fad64ab2c9af8a1b5a.html); strong on incongruence and adaptive characterisation, but the twice-arisen convergent origin[*A chimeric photoreceptor gene, NEOCHROME, has arisen twice during plant evolution*, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.0504734102) complicates the distributional argument.

**Tier 3 — Contested and open:**
- *Human/mammalian claimed HGT* — bacterium→vertebrate into a strictly sequestered germline, invoking housekeeping genes indistinguishable from microbiome contamination. Fails Contamination Control and Mechanistic Plausibility. Its value is methodological: it calibrates how aggressively contamination must be filtered, the "iceberg or ice cube" problem in numerical form[*Lateral gene transfer in eukaryotes: tip of the iceberg or of the ice cube?*, BMC Biology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12915-016-0330-x) — a lenient filter yields many apparent events, a strict filter yields few, and the data alone cannot adjudicate.

**The strength of an HGT claim and the clarity of its adaptive story rise together, not independently** — which is the evidence–benefit coupling expressed as a ranking.

# Limitations, Controversies and Falsifiers

The field's confidence is appropriately asymmetric: a handful of validated systems are robust to every known artefact, while a long tail of candidates remains contingent on sequencing and sampling decisions the next five years will change.

## Genome-Quality Limits

**Genome quality, not biological rarity, is the binding constraint** on current frequency claims:

- **Short-read chimeric contigs** splice host DNA to exogenous DNA, producing a *false* synteny signal — and genome contamination can inflate HGT inferences[Nature Communications](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12865159/). The unresolvable observable was the **integration junction** itself; deeper short-read coverage of a contaminated library simply assembles the contaminant more confidently.
- **Sparse taxon sampling** inflates apparent incongruence: where the nearest sampled relative is many branches removed from the true donor, a sampling hole manufactures an HGT signal[*Interkingdom HGT scrutiny in plants*, Frontiers in Plant Science 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2026.1789570/full). Dense clades permit dating and donor localization; sparse clades force a binary of HGT-or-unexplained-incongruence.
- **Long-read genomics** is the corrective, moving the junction inside a single read: one study reported **>50-fold more detected events after correction**, ~3,000 HGT blocks[Microbiology Spectrum](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.03602-23). This partly dissolves the sensitivity-versus-specificity tradeoff — a contaminant lacking host flanking sequence is excluded *directly* rather than filtered statistically.

The 50-fold recovery and the contamination-overestimation warning point in opposite directions on the headline count, which is why **a single frequency number is not yet defensible.**

## Scope Caps on Current Claims

- **Interkingdom claims** demand the highest contamination bar, because deep transfers produce extreme incongruence that distant contaminants also produce — and many reported plant iHGT candidates have not been examined against alternatives[*Interkingdom HGT scrutiny in plants*, Frontiers in Plant Science 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2026.1789570/full)[Nature Communications](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12865159/). Expect a documented contraction-then-consolidation of the candidate list.
- **Mechanistic confirmation** is defensible chiefly for mobilome-associated[*Genetic exchange in eukaryotes through horizontal transfer: connected by the mobilome*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5791352/) and organelle-derived[*Endosymbiotic gene transfer: organelle genomes forge eukaryotic chromosomes*, Nature Revi](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrg1271) transfers, where a vehicle is nameable; isolated single-gene transfers must rest on ecological plausibility. As detection scales faster than experimental route-reconstruction, the *proportion* of events with confirmed mechanism may fall.
- **Function-complete claims** belong to a validated minority anchored by the sweet potato, which clears Evidence Quality, Contamination Control, Mechanistic Plausibility, and Functional Integration simultaneously[*The genome of cultivated sweet potato contains Agrobacterium T-DNAs with expressed genes*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1419685112)[*The horizontal gene transfer of Agrobacterium T-DNAs into the series Batatas*, Scientific](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-48691-3) — its cultivated/wild segregation forecloses the contamination reading that still clouds the bdelloid case[*Horizontal Gene Transfer in Bdelloid Rotifers Questioned*, The Scientist](https://www.the-scientist.com/horizontal-gene-transfer-in-bdelloid-rotifers-questioned-64483).

## Time-Validity and Sampling Bias

Every frequency estimate is a snapshot of a moving denominator. Three terms shift simultaneously: rising detection sensitivity[Microbiology Spectrum](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.03602-23), tightening contamination control[Nature Communications](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12865159/), and an expanding genome census. **The direction of revision is genuinely unpredictable in aggregate.** Ancient-transfer detectability *decays permanently* as transferred genes ameliorate toward the host[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: the weak-link model*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033532/)[BMC Genomics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2164-9-136), so the ancient tail is partly dark and any total-contribution estimate is a lower bound by construction.

The catalogue is also **a sequencing-budget map**: parasites, pests, and models (whitefly, sweet potato, bdelloid) are oversampled because they matter economically, while marine eukaryotes, fungal recipients, and non-model vertebrates are undersampled. Publication bias loads the record toward positive findings — there is no equivalent of a clinical-trial registry for failed HGT candidates. These biases push in the same direction: they make the catalogue look like a collection of ecologically intimate special cases rather than a representative survey.

## A Three-Axis Falsification Frame

Any HGT claim faces three concrete falsifiers:

1. **Donor-as-contamination** (Contamination Control): re-sequence from axenic material with long reads spanning the junction; if the gene appears only as a free-floating contig lacking host flanking sequence, the claim is falsified. This already overturned the bdelloid claim[*Horizontal Gene Transfer in Bdelloid Rotifers Questioned*, The Scientist](https://www.the-scientist.com/horizontal-gene-transfer-in-bdelloid-rotifers-questioned-64483). The sweet potato survives because the T-DNA segregates with domestication[*The genome of cultivated sweet potato contains Agrobacterium T-DNAs with expressed genes*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1419685112).
2. **Gene loss / ILS** (Mechanistic Plausibility): re-analyse under a model allowing loss and duplication; if a vertical model fits as well, the claim falls[*Systematic assessment of phylogenomic approaches*, Peer Community Journal 2025](https://peercommunityjournal.org/articles/10.24072/pcjournal.579/). This bites hardest on *within-clade* cases like neochrome[*A chimeric photoreceptor gene, NEOCHROME, has arisen twice during plant evolution*, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.0504734102) — inverting the intuition that interkingdom claims are riskiest (they face contamination; within-clade claims face loss and ILS).
3. **Expression/function failure** (Functional Integration & Adaptive Benefit): a genuinely integrated but never-transcribed xenolog is a real but non-functional event. The most demanding falsifier, requiring *positive* experimental evidence. The sweet potato (expressed[*The genome of cultivated sweet potato contains Agrobacterium T-DNAs with expressed genes*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1419685112)) and whitefly (toxin neutralisation[*Whitefly hijacks a plant detoxification gene that neutralizes plant toxins*, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33770502/)) pass.

**The strength of an HGT interpretation is exactly the number of these axes it has survived.** The sweet potato clears all three; the bdelloid is contested on the first; neochrome is exposed on the second; presence-only candidates are unfalsified on the third.

One residual concern cannot be retired from current evidence: the **within-species contamination falsifier** — the hardest to detect, since a close relative confounds inference more than a distant one[*Within-species contamination of bacterial whole-genome sequence data*, Genome Biology](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s13059-019-1914-x.pdf) — may have passed silently through even the validated set, leaving an unquantifiable residual risk that only routine axenic long-read re-sequencing of every validated system would resolve.

# Conclusion: Answering the Core Questions

The central puzzle can now be answered directly. **Eukaryotic HGT is rare per lineage, better documented in plants than animals, occasionally and demonstrably adaptive, and best understood as an episodic but powerful innovation mechanism that obeys the same chance-and-selection logic as every other source of variation — it is "not a special case."**

**How rare, and why.** Documented cases exist in plants and are scarcer in animals, a contrast Keeling attributes to selection operating on variation HGT does not readily supply[Keeling, Nature Reviews Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00688-5). The overall rarity follows from the weak-link model's compounded inefficiencies[*Horizontal gene transfer in eukaryotes: the weak-link model*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033532/), and the kingdom asymmetry from germline sequestration: where parasitic plants exploit direct tissue continuity, animal cases must additionally reach a sequestered germline. The bdelloid and whitefly succeed precisely because that barrier was, by reproductive mode or ecology, unusually permeable.

**Whether transfers confer benefits.** Qualified but affirmative: a small number show a *demonstrated* fitness benefit while most reported cases remain untested. The sweet potato carries the strongest selection signature (present in all 291 cultivars, absent from wild relatives, expressed[*The genome of cultivated sweet potato contains Agrobacterium T-DNAs with expressed genes*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1419685112)); the whitefly the clearest biochemical mechanism (toxin neutralisation[*Whitefly hijacks a plant detoxification gene that neutralizes plant toxins*, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33770502/)); the nematode enzymes the clearest causal chain to a niche transition[*Horizontal gene transfer of microbial cellulases into nematode genomes*, BMC Evolutionary](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2148-11-13). **Benefit is demonstrable in a minority and presumed in many more** — a gap the functional-validation agenda is designed to close.

**The interesting reason.** Not a hidden machinery for acquiring genes — the evidence points the other way, toward an inefficient, undirected residue offset by reduced eukaryotic demand for HGT-supplied variation[Keeling, Nature Reviews Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00688-5). The genuinely interesting reason is the **structure of the filter itself**: a gauntlet strict enough to make transfer rare is also selective enough that survivors tend to matter. Frequency and impact are different axes — even a small number of transfers can generate hundreds of novel genotype combinations under stress[*HGT as an episodic driver of innovation under extinction scenarios*, Frontiers in Ecology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2025.1641717/full), and a single acquired gene can open a niche and trigger an adaptive radiation[Nature Reviews Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrg3962). **Rarity and significance, which seemed in tension, are two faces of one mechanism.**

The microbial baseline, with its abundant uncontroversial transfer[*Horizontal Gene Transfer in Eukaryotes: Not if, but How Much?*, Trends in Genetics](https://www.cell.com/trends/genetics/fulltext/S0168-9525(20), is not a different phenomenon but the same one running at higher rate under looser constraints — the tip of an iceberg whose submerged eukaryotic mass the next decade will begin to measure.

## Research Agenda Through 2030

The binding constraint has shifted from *discovery* to *demonstration*:

| Priority | Direction | Bottleneck | Payoff by 2030 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Functional validation pipelines | Documented presence ≠ demonstrated benefit | Converts candidate xenologs into tested adaptive cases |
| 2 | Long-read pangenomics | Assembly fragmentation + contamination | Cleaner catalogue; measured rate rises toward true rate[Microbiology Spectrum](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.03602-23) |
| 3 | Targeted sampling of permeable taxa | Sampling bias toward models | Expands candidate pool in under-screened lineages |

Validation ranks first because the supply of candidates has outrun the supply of demonstrated consequences[*The eukaryotic horizontal gene transfer dataset: a compendium*, PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12637581/); long-read pangenomics second because anchoring a xenolog in syntenic context resolves the ambiguity that sank the bdelloid claims[*Horizontal Gene Transfer in Bdelloid Rotifers Questioned*, The Scientist](https://www.the-scientist.com/horizontal-gene-transfer-in-bdelloid-rotifers-questioned-64483)[Microbiology Spectrum](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.03602-23); targeted sampling third, directed at phagotrophic protists, parasitic and sap-feeding invertebrates, and asexual metazoans where the germline barrier is weakest. The phytoplankton band (0.16–1.44% of gene inventories[*HGT in phytoplankton gene inventories*, Science Advances 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aba0111)) is the cleanest available genome-wide figure for microbial-eukaryote recipients, but whether the *multicellular* rate sits above or below it remains open.

## Open Questions

Three propositions remain genuinely unsettled: whether the plant–animal asymmetry survives deeper animal sampling with long-read methods[Microbiology Spectrum](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.03602-23); what fraction of documented xenologs in multicellular recipients confers a *demonstrated* benefit rather than fixing neutrally[*The eukaryotic horizontal gene transfer dataset: a compendium*, PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12637581/); and whether endogenous-viral-element insertions are empirically linked to chromosomal rearrangement and speciation[*Endogenous viral elements and chromosomal rearrangement*, arXiv 2506.04511 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.04511). The single number the question most wanted — a genome-wide fixation rate for multicellular plants and animals comparable to the phytoplankton band — **does not yet exist with confidence**, and producing it is the clearest single deliverable the long-read and functional-validation agendas are jointly positioned to deliver by the close of the decade.
