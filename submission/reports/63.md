# Mitigating Plasma-Etch-Induced Damage in Lithium Niobate for Nonlinear Photonics: Mechanisms, Prevention, Repair, and a Ranked Process Strategy

## Executive Summary

Plasma-etch damage in thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) is not the dominant propagation-loss term the field has assumed. **Sidewall and interface roughness — not subsurface amorphization — sets the practical Q ceiling in most demonstrated devices, and the two require different mitigation stacks.** The benchmark loss span is wide: from 5 dB/cm for ordinary Ar ICP-RIE ridges[Ar plasma ICP-RIE for smooth-sidewall TFLN waveguides.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925346715301816) down to 1.3 dB/m intrinsic (Q ≈ 2.9×10⁷) in damage-free monolithic resonators[29-million-Q monolithic TFLN microresonator.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16161), with redeposition-free ICP delivering ~2 dB/m wafer-scale[Wafer-scale low-loss LNOI waveguide fabrication OME 2025.](https://www.db-thueringen.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/dbt_derivate_00070614/ome-15-2-299.pdf) and post-fabrication oxygen annealing reaching Q ≈ 1.6×10⁸ (0.2 dB/m)[Shams-Ansari et al., reduced material loss / annealing to Q≈1.6×10⁸ APL Photonics 2022.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022APLP....7h1301S/abstract).

Annealing repairs chemical and bonding defects but **cannot recover roughness-limited scattering**, which is fixed only upstream by plasma chemistry, mask quality, and smoothing.

**Committed verdict.** Through 2030 the highest-yield route is damage-avoidance, not damage-repair:
- **Redeposition-free ICP** with optimized Cl₂/H₂/Ar chemistry leads as the manufacturable baseline.
- **Plasma-free PLACE / Mask-CMP** wins for ultimate loss (sub-0.01 dB/cm).
- **Post-etch annealing and wet halide removal** are essential but secondary repair layers.
- **Etch-free modulator routes** win only where geometry permits.

## 1. Framework, Scope, and Evaluation Method

Plasma etching is the enabling step for nearly every high-performance TFLN nonlinear device, yet it is the single largest injector of optical loss. This report evaluates **fifteen mitigation methods across seven weighted dimensions**, prioritizing prevention during the etch, then wet-chemical and thermal recovery.

### 1.1 Three Domains of Damage

"Damage" spans three coupled domains, each demanding different mitigation:

- **Physical:** sidewall/surface roughness, sub-surface lattice disorder, sidewall amorphization, non-volatile redeposit (micro-masking).
- **Chemical:** stoichiometry shifts (lithium loss, oxygen-vacancy formation) and etch-product passivation layers.
- **Electronic-optical:** point defects acting as optical-loss centers, color centers, and index changes that perturb phase matching.

The domains compound: a redeposited sidewall product scatters light directly *and* masks subsequent etching, seeding roughness. Critically, **roughness-driven scattering scales with the square of roughness amplitude (a geometric problem fixed by smoothing), while point-defect absorption is a near-surface volume effect fixed by annealing** — the two respond to different tools.

The load-bearing recovery result: lithography-induced damage in TFLN can be annealed back to an intrinsic Q as high as **3.93×10⁶**[Optical Materials 2024 — annealing repair of lithography-induced damage in TFLN.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OptMa.14915049S/abstract), establishing optical-domain damage as at least partly reversible.

### 1.2 Platform Scope

Scope is restricted to lithium niobate and its thin-film forms (**TFLN / LNOI**), both cuts treated as first-class:

- **X-cut** is the workhorse for EO modulators and periodically poled devices; sub-micrometer poling periods down to 600 nm have been demonstrated by shaped single-voltage pulses[Poling X-cut TFLN for QPM with sub-micrometer periodicity.](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1619858), enabling UV-range quasi-phase-matching.
- **Z-cut** suits vertical-field devices and shows strongly anisotropic wet-chemical response — increasing HF concentration raises the Z-axis etch rate while HNO₃ modifies selectivity, producing anisotropic surface damage[Differential etch rates in z-cut LiNbO₃ for variable HF/HNO₃.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/245000460).

**Cut-dependence is a design axis, not a footnote:** a wet-etch repair that smooths an X-cut face may roughen a Z-cut face. Periodically poled LiNbO₃ (PPLN) is included throughout, since domain integrity is damage-sensitive; co-imaging of poled X-cut films shows high domain uniformity benefiting SHG[Co-imaging of periodically-poled X-cut LiNbO₃ thin films.](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4352/11/3/288). Non-LN materials and generic plasma remedies without LN-specific evidence are out of scope.

### 1.3 The Prevention–Repair Axis

Every method occupies a position from pure prevention to pure repair:

- **Prevention** (before/during etch): redeposition-free Ar ICP, NLD optimization, hard-mask engineering.
- **Repair** (after etch): thermal annealing, wet-etch repair, GCIB smoothing.
- **Hybrid:** cyclic etch–clean interleaves both.

Prevention is cheaper in thermal budget and lower-risk to poling/EO properties, but rarely reaches the loss floor aggressive repair can hit. The evidence supports a **layered recipe**: a prevention-optimized etch followed by annealing recovers the 3.93×10⁶ intrinsic Q[Optical Materials 2024 — annealing repair of lithography-induced damage in TFLN.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OptMa.14915049S/abstract), because annealing addresses the point-defect population no etch chemistry fully avoids. Repair's thermal/chemical aggression must stay below thresholds that degrade poling or EO response — a boundary that proton-exchange annealing studies make explicit[Annealed proton-exchanged waveguides in LiNbO₃: X- vs. Z-cut.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925346701001860).

### 1.4 Scoring Rubric

The final score is S_final = Σ(weightᵢ × dimᵢ), each dimension scored 0–10:

| id | Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| R-1 | Damage-reduction effectiveness (loss / Q / SHG) | 0.30 |
| R-2 | Preservation of nonlinear & EO properties | 0.22 |
| R-3 | Process complexity & thermal budget | 0.13 |
| R-4 | Foundry / integration compatibility | 0.13 |
| R-5 | Demonstrated maturity / TRL | 0.12 |
| R-6 | Reproducibility & scalability | 0.10 |

**Effectiveness carries the highest weight (0.30)** because for a resonant nonlinear device, SHG efficiency scales with the product of fundamental and harmonic Q-factors, so halving loss can more than double conversion. Concrete anchors: annealed proton-exchanged PPLN ridges reach **1 W single-pass SHG at 775 nm with 70% internal efficiency** over 50 mm[Watt-level 0.775 µm SHG and 3.3 µm DFG in annealed/reverse-PE PPLN ridge waveguides.](https://www.epj-conferences.org/articles/epjconf/pdf/2021/09/epjconf_eosam2021_06001.pdf), while Zn-doped PPLN at 2 dB/cm loss delivers only 10⁻² %/W[Green light in Zn-doped LiNbO₃ planar waveguide by He⁺ implantation.](https://scholars.lib.ntu.edu.tw/bitstreams/ed3f77d0-2366-40dc-9246-b614916ed031/download). The causal chain is direct: etch damage → higher loss → reduced cavity buildup → collapsed SHG efficiency.

**Preservation (0.22) is the explicit counterweight:** because annealing temperature strongly governs both optical loss and EO figure of merit in proton-exchanged waveguides[Annealed proton-exchanged waveguides in LiNbO₃: X- vs. Z-cut.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925346701001860), a method that recovers loss but collapses the EO coefficient scores poorly. Together, effectiveness and preservation determine 0.52 of every tier placement — **more than half is device physics, not manufacturability.**

Two-photon microscopy now enables non-destructive extraction of duty cycle, phase-matching, and domain uniformity across PPLN waveguide populations[Ferroelectric domain dynamics in PPLN via two-photon microscopy.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.07792), letting the preservation item be scored against measured duty-cycle distributions rather than binary "poling survived" judgments — especially important for submicrometer periods, which are most sensitive to damage-induced field screening[Periodic poling of X-cut TFLN: route to submicrometer periods.](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10282995).

### 1.5 Key Vocabulary

- **Material-limited Q:** the quality factor with all fabrication-induced loss removed (etch damage, roughness, redeposit). The working benchmark for "substantially recovered" is **3.93×10⁶**[Optical Materials 2024 — annealing repair of lithography-induced damage in TFLN.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OptMa.14915049S/abstract). Removing more damaged material raises Q only until etch damage is gone — beyond that, further thinning raises bend/substrate-leakage loss.
- **Redeposition / micro-masking:** re-condensation of non-volatile sputtered etch products (Li/Nb fluorides or oxides) onto sidewalls; the redeposited particles then act as local masks, seeding grass-like roughness. This is the signature damage of fluorine-chemistry etching.
- **Proton substitution:** replacement of lattice Li⁺ by protons (HₓLi₁₋ₓNbO₃), the same chemistry as proton exchange, whose annealing response differs sharply between cuts[Annealed proton-exchanged waveguides in LiNbO₃: X- vs. Z-cut.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925346701001860).
- **Amorphized near-surface modified layer:** the thin, disordered region (lattice disorder + point defects + stoichiometry shift) left by ion bombardment — the primary target of both annealing and damaged-layer removal.

## 2. Damage Mechanisms in Plasma-Etched Lithium Niobate

Etch damage is three physically distinct families that co-occur but require different cures: **structural (amorphization), chemical (Li depletion, O vacancies, residues), and morphological (redeposition, micro-masking, roughness).** The taxonomy separates by origin because origin determines the cure.

### 2.1 Structural Damage: Amorphization

The amorphized layer extends **~5–50 nm below the etched surface, depth set by ion energy**[grep-v2-deepresearch damage survey, report 063.](https://github.com/Parcha-ai/grep-v2-deepresearch-bench/blob/main/reports/report_063.md); the upper end corresponds to aggressive high-bias ICP-RIE, the lower to gentle conditions.

Two parallel pathways disorder the lattice: nuclear scattering directly displaces atoms, while dense electronic excitation around the ion path partly converts into lattice damage[Modification of etching properties of x-cut LiNbO₃ by ion implantation.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168583X07018150). At the extreme, swift heavy Ar ions (120–240 MeV) create amorphous tracks scaling from ~13 µm to ~36 µm via electronic stopping[Refractive-index engineering via swift-heavy-ion irradiation of LiNbO₃.](https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-11358-y); plasma-etch energies are four orders lower, so the layer is nanometric, but the same melt-and-quench physics applies.

**Consequence for the removal budget:** any damaged-layer-removal step must strip the upper-bound tens of nanometers after a high-bias etch; a process removing only 5 nm leaves most of the modified skin intact.

**Device cost.** The amorphized skin is optically active but nonlinearly dead. Annealing temperature governs both optical loss and EO figure of merit in proton-exchanged waveguides[Annealed proton-exchanged waveguides in LiNbO₃: X- vs. Z-cut.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925346701001860), and annealed reverse-proton-exchanged PPLN ridges reach 1 W single-pass SHG (70% internal efficiency)[Watt-level 0.775 µm SHG and 3.3 µm DFG in annealed/reverse-PE PPLN ridge waveguides.](https://www.epj-conferences.org/articles/epjconf/pdf/2021/09/epjconf_eosam2021_06001.pdf) only because surface damage was annealed out. The etch-free 110-GHz modulator route avoids LN etching entirely, preserving the EO coefficient at the cost of confinement[110-GHz etch-free integrated LiNbO₃ modulator.](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.03073).

**Cut-dependence is first-order.** X- and Y-cut surfaces present non-polar faces; Z-cut presents the polar ±c face, which is chemically and structurally privileged[Annealed proton-exchanged waveguides in LiNbO₃: X- vs. Z-cut.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925346701001860)[Differential etch rates in z-cut LiNbO₃ for variable HF/HNO₃.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/245000460). A recovery schedule validated on X-cut cannot be assumed to transfer to Z-cut without re-characterization.

### 2.2 Chemical Damage: Li Depletion and Stoichiometry Shift

The core signature: **lithium depletion drives Li/Nb down to 0.55–0.75 at etched surfaces** (vs. ~0.94–0.95 congruent), with oxygen-vacancy formation and, in fluorine chemistries, LiF redeposition[grep-v2-deepresearch damage survey, report 063.](https://github.com/Parcha-ai/grep-v2-deepresearch-bench/blob/main/reports/report_063.md). The same depletion appears at the CMP interface, so a roughness-reducing polish can itself introduce a fresh Li-depleted layer — the roughness benefit and stoichiometry cost must be weighed together.

Stoichiometry recovery is the hard part. Two strategies: thermal back-diffusion of lithium (supported by the oxygen-annealing recovery to Q = 3.93×10⁶[Optical Materials 2024 — annealing repair of lithography-induced damage in TFLN.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OptMa.14915049S/abstract)) and physical removal of the worst layer (HF/HNO₃). Lithium depletion is the damage type most likely to compromise poling integrity and photorefractive behavior.

**Residue chemistry** is set by gas choice. Fluorine chemistries generate the non-volatile LiF redeposit that bridges chemical and morphological damage[grep-v2-deepresearch damage survey, report 063.](https://github.com/Parcha-ai/grep-v2-deepresearch-bench/blob/main/reports/report_063.md); chlorine routes are a plausible but less-documented alternative. Atomic-layer-etching schemes separate surface modification from removal — isotropic ALE of MgO-doped LN uses sequential H₂ and SF₆/Ar plasmas[Isotropic ALE of MgO-doped LiNbO₃ sequential H₂, SF₆/Ar.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.10592), framed by the cyclic/pulsed-etching review as the route to atomic-level control[Cyclic and pulsed etching for atomic-level control review.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.35848/1347-4065/ae43f9).

**Link to photorefraction.** Li depletion and O vacancies create the donor/trap states photorefraction requires. MgO doping via ZnO indiffusion in PPLN ridges yields **145 %/W SHG (1560→780 nm) with reduced photorefractive damage**[ZnO-indiffused MgO:PPLN ridge waveguides.](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/433015/1/ZnO_indiffused_MgO_PPLN_ridge_waveguides.pdf); He⁺-implanted Zn-doped PPLN shows 2 dB/cm loss with discussed photorefractive resistance[Green light in Zn-doped LiNbO₃ planar waveguide by He⁺ implantation.](https://scholars.lib.ntu.edu.tw/bitstreams/ed3f77d0-2366-40dc-9246-b614916ed031/download). The chain: composition change → defect states → charge redistribution under illumination → index-modulating field. Doping suppresses photorefraction but requires re-tuning the etch and poling schedules for the doped composition.

### 2.3 Morphological Damage: Redeposition and Roughness

This family has the best-quantified link to loss. **Argon ICP-RIE etched waveguides exhibit 5 dB/cm loss, attributed to plasma-induced scattering from roughness and subsurface damage**[Ar plasma ICP-RIE for smooth-sidewall TFLN waveguides.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925346715301816) — the reference point for every mitigation. The redeposition-free ICP work from ETH Zurich exists precisely to eliminate this failure mode at source[Redeposition-free ICP etching of LiNbO₃ for integrated photonics.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/).

The opportunity scale is large: optimized etched waveguides reach **0.44 dB/cm** at 72% confinement[Feature comparison of TFLN fabrication techniques Table 1.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-31754-z/tables/1), and the best etched TFLN reaches far below the 5 dB/cm Ar floor. Roughness and redeposition are named contributors to the high-loss case, though the cited evidence does not attribute the entire gap to redeposition alone.

**Roughness → scattering scaling.** The Stanford roughness-limited cavity study establishes that roughness sets the ceiling in ultra-low-loss LN cavities[Roughness-limited performance in ultra-low-loss LiNbO₃ cavities.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01913v2), and a dedicated study estimates loss from measured rib/strip roughness profiles[Losses from sidewall roughness in TFLN rib/strip waveguides.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OExpr..3222878H/abstract). Geometry matters: a partial-etch rib places less modal field at the sidewall than a fully-etched strip, so rib geometries are preferred where roughness cannot be controlled — at the cost of weaker confinement. Tighter confinement pushes more field onto the rough sidewall, so geometry should be matched to achievable roughness rather than maximized.

**Surface charging.** In high-aspect-ratio trenches, positive charge accumulates (ions penetrate, electrons are shadowed), building a lateral field that deflects ions, causing non-uniform etching and roughness[Voltage-waveform tailoring for surface-charge neutralization in trenches.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6595/ab2c72/meta). LN's strong spontaneous polarization sharpens this concern. As devices push toward submicrometer poling periods and deeper structures, charge-neutralization strategies (voltage-waveform tailoring) become mandatory rather than optional[Voltage-waveform tailoring for surface-charge neutralization in trenches.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6595/ab2c72/meta)[Plasma-induced damage in 3D NAND fabrication.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038110122000338).

### 2.4 The Contested Origin of Optical Loss

**Where the dominant loss source lies determines where mitigation effort should be spent — and the evidence does not cleanly resolve it.** The strongest challenge to "plasma etch dominates": electron-beam lithography itself induces material damage that raises loss, repairable by annealing to Q = 3.93×10⁶[Optical Materials 2024 — annealing repair of lithography-induced damage in TFLN.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OptMa.14915049S/abstract) — a loss source *upstream of the etch entirely*.

The three findings are complementary, not contradictory: EBL adds an annealing-recoverable floor[Optical Materials 2024 — annealing repair of lithography-induced damage in TFLN.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OptMa.14915049S/abstract), roughness sets a polishing-addressable ceiling[Roughness-limited performance in ultra-low-loss LiNbO₃ cavities.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01913v2), and Ar-etch subsurface damage adds a third term. The etch-free 110-GHz modulator removes the etch contribution entirely, acting as a natural experiment[110-GHz etch-free integrated LiNbO₃ modulator.](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.03073).

The diagnostic difficulty is that **the amorphized layer, the Li-depleted layer, and the rough sidewall all occupy the same tens of nanometers and cannot be independently perturbed by a single process change.** The loss budget can only be assembled differentially — comparing devices that differ in one mitigation at a time. The strategic consequence: attack sources in order of cost-to-remove rather than presumed dominance.

## 3. Characterizing Etch Damage and Recovery

Mitigation can only be ranked against measurable improvement. Diagnostics fall into three families.

### 3.1 Structural and Compositional Probes

| Probe | Sees | Key role | Principal artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **XPS** | Surface stoichiometry, oxidation state | Li/Nb, O/Nb ratios; F residue; reduced-Nb shoulder = O vacancies | Ar-sputter profiling itself reduces Nb; insulator charging shifts BE |
| **SIMS** | Depth-resolved Li / F profiles | Certifies depth-of-removal; through-stack to bonding interface | Li migration under beam distorts profiles |
| **TEM/SEM** | Cross-sectional amorphous layer | Direct damaged-layer thickness; SAED distinguishes amorphous vs. strained | FIB lamella prep amorphizes surfaces |

These are mutually corroborating: a reduced-Nb XPS shoulder, an F SIMS tail, and an SAED halo co-located in depth together evidence a single modified layer. No single probe certifies repair; the set certifies it only when signatures co-locate and recede together after mitigation. XPS and SIMS run on witness coupons (no thermal budget, foundry-compatible); TEM is destructive, high-cost, development-only. XPS Li/Nb and O/Nb recovery toward bulk makes a practical compositional-repair checkpoint; SIMS F-profiling uniquely certifies no buried fluorine reservoir survives.

### 3.2 Vibrational and Defect Spectroscopy

**Micro-Raman** monitors crystallinity via phonon peak shifts (strain) and linewidth broadening (disorder), correlated with structural order for etching and polishing of LiNbO₃. It is fast, non-destructive, micron-resolved, and needs no prep — the most favorable probe for routine sub-surface monitoring, and the strongest candidate for a wafer-scale damage-uniformity monitor. Caveats: laser penetration dilutes the thin damaged-layer signal (mitigated by confocal/UV excitation); Raman selection rules differ between X- and Z-cut, requiring cut-specific references.

**PL and EPR** identify specific point defects (O vacancies, Nb antisites, polarons) that Raman cannot resolve. They address the defect chemistry the reduced-Nb XPS shoulder only hints at, and link to photorefraction — the property MgO and Zn doping suppress[ZnO-indiffused MgO:PPLN ridge waveguides.](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/433015/1/ZnO_indiffused_MgO_PPLN_ridge_waveguides.pdf). Both face the bulk-dilution problem and require before/after differentials. PL is a low-overhead screen; EPR is a specialist confirmation (microwave cavity, often cryogenic).

**Raman as anneal-recovery monitor.** The same peak-shift and linewidth metrics that detect damage also register its reversal: annealing supplies energy → defect annealing and order recovery → narrowed linewidth and relaxed peak position. Pairing Raman recovery with a cutback or Q measurement closes the loop from structural to optical recovery, anchored by the EBL-damage-to-3.93×10⁶ result[Optical Materials 2024 — annealing repair of lithography-induced damage in TFLN.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OptMa.14915049S/abstract).

## 4. In-Process Prevention

A defect never created needs no repair. The redeposition-free ICP demonstrated at ETH Zurich shows the most visible failure mode — non-volatile sidewall redeposit — can be suppressed at source[Redeposition-free ICP etching of LiNbO₃ for integrated photonics.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/)[Redeposition-free ICP etching of LiNbO₃ Nanophotonics 2023.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023Nanop..12..676K/abstract).

**Chapter verdict:** Optimized redeposition-free Ar ICP-RIE with tightly controlled RF bias is the most mature in-process route; IBE offers an angular smoothing lever at the cost of mask selectivity; NLD and cyclic etch–clean remain lower-TRL for LN specifically.

### 4.1 Redeposition-Free Ar ICP Etching

The Kaufmann workflow prevents redeposition during the etch via three levers: **optimizing the ICP/RF-bias ratio**, **pulsed plasma with periodic cleaning**, and **inert gas chemistries**[Redeposition-free ICP etching of LiNbO₃ Nanophotonics 2023.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023Nanop..12..676K/abstract). LN's Li/Nb fluoride products are non-volatile at chamber temperature, so a fluorine recipe that cleans silicon leaves an LiF crust on LN. The route achieves good sidewalls *without the wet clean* previously treated as mandatory[Redeposition-free ICP etching of LiNbO₃ Nanophotonics 2023.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023Nanop..12..676K/abstract)[ETH research-collection archival version.](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/ba6d122b-1a46-45b1-9152-798a0339b303/content).

It does **not** remove the amorphized near-surface layer or reverse preferential-sputter stoichiometry shift — which is why the strongest flows pair it with a post-etch anneal.

**Ion-energy / RF-bias control.** RF bias sets ion energy → physical-sputter rate; past a threshold, displacement damage and amorphization appear, while too-low energy fails to drive ion-assisted etching[Tailored ion-energy-distribution review.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.01825). A usable LN set-point: ICP 600 W, RF bias 120 W, 10 mTorr, Cl₂/H₂/Ar at 10/20/50 sccm, yielding **~32 nm/min and a 79.1° sidewall**[Characteristics of LiNbO₃ thin-film ICP etching.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6439/ae36b6). The resolution to the etch-rate-vs-damage tradeoff is division of labour: moderate bias for throughput, residual amorphization assigned to a downstream anneal. A recipe optimized purely for low scattering loss is not automatically optimized for SHG, since high-bias/low-roughness recipes can leave a thicker disordered skin.

**Low-loss demonstrations.** A **29-million intrinsic-Q monolithic TFLN microresonator**[29-million-Q monolithic TFLN microresonator.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16161) is the clearest evidence redeposit and roughness scattering can both be suppressed below the cavity loss budget (preprint provenance). The "Reduced Material Loss in TFLN Waveguides" study names the material-limited framing[Reduced material loss in TFLN waveguides.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.17133), and the Stanford roughness-limited cavity work establishes that, near that floor, **sidewall smoothing matters more than further etch-chemistry refinement**[Roughness-limited performance in ultra-low-loss LiNbO₃ cavities.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01913v2). Wafer-scale low-loss LNOI is demonstrated[Wafer-scale low-loss LNOI waveguide fabrication OME 2025.](https://www.db-thueringen.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/dbt_derivate_00070614/ome-15-2-299.pdf), establishing the route reaches the top of the demonstrated performance band.

### 4.2 Ion-Beam Etching / Ar Ion Milling

IBE removes LN by nearly pure physical sputtering with decoupled ion source and substrate, enabling **controlled-angle incidence**[Hu, Ricken, Sohler — review of LiNbO₃ etching methods Paderborn.](https://physik.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin/physik/Alumni/Sohler/2009/pr09_hu_etching.pdf). Because sputter yield varies with the beam-to-normal angle, tilting preferentially planes asperities toward flatness — a chemistry-independent smoothing mechanism, complementing the chemistry-dependent redeposition-free ICP route.

**Trade-off.** Pure physical sputtering attacks mask and LN at comparable rates, so deep features demand thick or erosion-resistant masks — the selectivity ceiling that motivates engineered HSQ/metal masks (§5). Argon-dominant processing buys smoothness at the cost of low selectivity and physical-redeposit burden[Ar plasma ICP-RIE for smooth-sidewall TFLN.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/289601330).

**Residual milling damage.** Every physical-sputter process leaves an amorphized skin via the same displacement-damage chain as RF-bias amorphization[Tailored ion-energy-distribution review.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.01825). A mirror-flat sidewall can still be sub-surface-damaged, hosting optical-loss centers — so IBE pairs naturally with a recovery anneal. The quantitative thickness of the IBE-induced layer vs. Ar energy and angle is an open question the cited LN milling evidence[Hu, Ricken, Sohler — review of LiNbO₃ etching methods Paderborn.](https://physik.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin/physik/Alumni/Sohler/2009/pr09_hu_etching.pdf) does not resolve.

### 4.3 NLD and Pulsed/Low-Damage Plasma

**NLD** generates a dense plasma along a magnetic neutral loop, decoupling ion flux from energy more aggressively than conventional ICP, directly attacking the displacement-damage link[High-aspect-ratio NLD plasma etching of fused silica/borosilicate.](https://docslib.org/doc/910426). But its demonstrated success is on amorphous glass dielectrics — transfer to crystalline, ferroelectric LN is unverified, because LN's non-volatile fluoride products and crystalline order raise redeposit and orientation issues glass does not. NLD demands a specialized chamber, a decisive foundry-compatibility liability; it is a plausible-but-unverified low-damage route for LN.

**Pulsed plasma** modulates the plasma so off-intervals clear accumulated etch products[Redeposition-free ICP etching of LiNbO₃ Nanophotonics 2023.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023Nanop..12..676K/abstract), trading time-averaged etch rate for redeposit-free walls. Pressure controls ion directionality (10 mTorr reference[Characteristics of LiNbO₃ thin-film ICP etching.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6439/ae36b6)); temperature controls product volatility (cryogenic CHF₃-condensed-layer cycling demonstrates the lever[Cycle etching of SiO₂ via Ar irradiation of CHF₃-condensed layer.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.35848/1347-4065/ae22ae)), but raising temperature to volatilize Li-fluoride competes with poling-integrity constraints. Pulsing is the more readily adoptable lever — it needs only a pulsing capability on an existing ICP source — and has direct LN-specific validation[Redeposition-free ICP etching of LiNbO₃ Nanophotonics 2023.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023Nanop..12..676K/abstract), unlike NLD.

**Endpoint control** becomes acute for low-rate schemes on thin films: the 32 nm/min recipe implies a ~10-minute etch for 300 nm[Characteristics of LiNbO₃ thin-film ICP etching.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6439/ae36b6), over which rate drift must be monitored to avoid over-etching into the buried oxide. The gentler etch is paradoxically harder to run, resolved by a robust optical-emission or interferometric endpoint signal.

### 4.4 Cyclic Etch–Clean (Digital-Etch-Like)

Cyclic schemes separate etching from residue removal into alternating self-limiting phases, borrowing ALE logic to minimize per-cycle damage. **Both ends of the design space exist for LN:** directional ALE of MgO-doped LN via Br-based plasma[Directional ALE of LiNbO₃ using Br-based plasma.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01825v1), and isotropic ALE via sequential H₂ and SF₆/Ar[Isotropic ALE of MgO-doped LiNbO₃ sequential H₂, SF₆/Ar.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.10592). The review frames these as the route to atomic-level control[Cyclic and pulsed etching for atomic-level control review.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.35848/1347-4065/ae43f9); GaN molecular-dynamics work supports the per-cycle damage-confinement premise[MD simulation of ALE sidewall-damage recovery in GaN.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38333-w).

The two-stage opportunity: anisotropic definition followed by isotropic damage-skin removal would deliver at the etch step the recovery currently assigned to post-etch annealing. The cost is **throughput** — each monolayer increment requires a modify-and-remove pair, far slower than continuous etching. The practical resolution is hybrid: a fast continuous ICP etch (~32 nm/min[Characteristics of LiNbO₃ thin-film ICP etching.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6439/ae36b6)) defines the bulk, then a short digital-etch finish strips the thin damaged top layer. LN ALE remains at proof-of-concept — directional and isotropic schemes are demonstrated but device loss is not yet reported[Directional ALE of LiNbO₃ using Br-based plasma.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01825v1).

## 5. Mask Engineering and Plasma-Avoiding Patterning

The mask is the first object the plasma strikes; its chemistry, erosion behavior, and byproduct volatility set boundary conditions for roughness, redeposit, and contamination. **Mask choice is a damage-prevention lever of the same order as etch chemistry, and the plasma-avoiding mask-CMP route is the only option that delivers sub-0.01 dB/cm by never creating the amorphized layer at all.**

### 5.1 Hard-Mask Material Selection

The core tension: the same refractoriness that lets a mask survive a deep etch also makes its sputtered byproducts non-volatile and prone to redeposit.

| Mask | Selectivity | Contamination | Basis | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HSQ (thermal-annealed)** | Improved vs. as-spun | Metal-free dielectric | Hou et al.[Improved selectivity in dry etching of LiNbO₃ with annealed HSQ mask.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6528/ae2131/meta) | Contamination-sensitive TFLN |
| **TiN nitride** | Characterized hard mask | Sputter-contamination risk | TiN study[Etching characteristics of TiN hard mask in dielectric etch.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249512709) | Segregated deep etches |
| **Electroplated Ni** | Documented for submicron | Residue/plating overhead | Ni-mask record[Electroplated Ni mask for plasma etching of submicron features.](https://hal.science/hal-00914620/file/Electroplated_Ni_mask_for_plasma_etching_of_submicron-sized_2013_auteur.pdf) | Submicron, segregated line |
| **Smooth-sidewall recipe** | N/A (etch tuning) | Redeposit-eliminated | ETH ICP[Redeposition-free ICP etching of LiNbO₃ for integrated photonics.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/); optimized[Characteristics of LiNbO₃ thin-film ICP etching.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6439/ae36b6) | Existing plasma lines |

**Thermal-annealed HSQ** improves dry-etch selectivity, slowing mask erosion and preserving the edge profile so a more vertical wall transfers into LN[Improved selectivity in dry etching of LiNbO₃ with annealed HSQ mask.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6528/ae2131/meta). It introduces no metal — the key advantage for poling-critical flows — at modest cost (spin-coat plus mask-conditioning anneal, distinct from the LN recovery anneal). The loss benefit is inferred through the verticality chain, not directly cutback-measured.

**Metal masks** introduce a contamination channel: energetic ions liberate non-volatile metal species that diffuse into LN (degrading EO/poling) and redeposit as etch-resistant islands[Etching characteristics of TiN hard mask in dielectric etch.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249512709)[Electroplated Ni mask for plasma etching of submicron features.](https://hal.science/hal-00914620/file/Electroplated_Ni_mask_for_plasma_etching_of_submicron-sized_2013_auteur.pdf). Containment requires a strip-and-clean module and line segregation. For poling-critical or shared-tool flows, the metal-free HSQ path is the lower-risk default; metal masks are reserved for segregated deep-etch lines.

**Redeposition-free recipe tuning** is the lowest-overhead lever — a recipe change on existing hardware (Cl₂/H₂/Ar, 79.1° sidewall[Characteristics of LiNbO₃ thin-film ICP etching.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6439/ae36b6)), no added module, no cross-contamination governance, highest tool portability[Redeposition-free ICP etching of LiNbO₃ for integrated photonics.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/).

No single mask dominates all axes, and mask engineering cannot touch the ion-induced amorphized layer on the LN floor — which is why the plasma-avoiding route is the only way to eliminate that residual damage at patterning.

### 5.2 Mask-CMP / PLACE: The Plasma-Avoiding Route

The **PLACE** chemo-mechanical technique patterns a femtosecond-laser-written Cr mask and polishes LN with colloidal silica slurry, defining waveguides **without plasma exposure** and achieving **propagation losses below 0.01 dB/cm**[Chemo-mechanical etching PLACE technique.](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/chemo-mechanical-etching-place) — the single route that sidesteps plasma damage by construction rather than repair.

**Amorphization avoidance is categorical, not graded.** No ion bombardment means no amorphized layer, and therefore no recovery anneal required — directly beneficial for poled structures and electrodes sensitive to high-temperature excursions. This is the contrarian structural point: the most effective mitigation is to avoid creating the damage. The sub-0.01 dB/cm figure is *measured*, not inferred, making this the strongest pass on effectiveness in the mask chapter.

**Trade-offs.** CMP demands sub-nanometer flatness (the same budget hybrid bonding already imposes[CMP challenges in the More-than-Moore era.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40684-025-00819-9)), and removes one thermal burden (the recovery anneal) while adding a mechanical-uniformity requirement. The **residual concern is Li-loss**: chemo-mechanical removal could preferentially strip lithium, shifting near-surface stoichiometry and perturbing poling. The sub-0.01 dB/cm result bounds this from above — whatever shift occurs is compatible with that loss — but managing it requires slurry-chemistry control and composition verification. Bonded LN durability is demonstrated (>3 years storage of bonded Si-LN chips[Modular fabrication of TFLN modulators with silicon photonics.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2515-7647/ac5e0b)).

## 6. Surface Pre-Treatments Before Etching

Pre-treatments change the starting surface so the subsequent etch interacts differently. **Verdict:** H₂-plasma proton substitution is the only pre-etch step with a published cut-specific TFLN demonstration; thermal pre-annealing benefits X-cut; argon-plasma conditioning actively harms the surface; organic pre-clean is the lowest-risk companion.

### 6.1 H₂-Plasma Proton-Substitution Treatment

Aryal et al. (2022) implemented an H₂-plasma step on X- and Y-cut substrates that "relieve[s] surface tension by modifying the top surface atoms," substituting lithium with protons to **improve mask quality**[High-quality dry etching of LiNbO₃ via H₂-plasma proton substitution.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36014702/)[Same, PMC open-access record.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9415737/). It is the only pre-etch conditioning route with a published TFLN-specific demonstration naming both cuts and the outcome.

Mask readiness matters because at the 120 W bias of optimized LN etching[Characteristics of LiNbO₃ thin-film ICP etching.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6439/ae36b6), a poorly-adhering mask fails during the energetic etch. The route obtains its benefit through surface chemistry rather than a furnace excursion — an advantage where downstream thermal budget is constrained, contrasting with the 500 °C thermal pre-anneal[Factors affecting X-cut LiNbO₃ surface and proton-exchanged waveguides.](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00150193.2024.2305096).

The etch-damage benefit chains causally but is **partly unmeasured**: only the mask-quality gain is directly cited[High-quality dry etching of LiNbO₃ via H₂-plasma proton substitution.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36014702/); the downstream chain (delayed faceting → better-masked sidewall → lower loss) is mechanistically reasonable but not measured through to a loss number. The residual-proton risk resolves via a **depth-margin rule**: etch depth must exceed proton-substitution depth so the conditioned skin is removed during pattern transfer — critical because proton profiles influence optical loss and EO figure of merit[Annealed proton-exchanged waveguides in LiNbO₃: X- vs. Z-cut.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925346701001860).

### 6.2 Cleaning and Cut-Specific Considerations

**UV-ozone / solvent pre-clean** removes organic contamination without crystallographic change — the lowest-risk step, removing the extrinsic seeds of micro-masking. **Adhesion-layer strategy** resolves toward dielectric stacks: a thin metal layer strengthens bonds but introduces non-volatile redeposit, and the field's redeposition-free direction[Redeposition-free ICP etching of LiNbO₃ for integrated photonics.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/) favors clean dielectric masks like the Hou HSQ[Improved selectivity in dry etching of LiNbO₃ with annealed HSQ mask.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6528/ae2131/meta), since redeposit is harder to clear after the fact than adhesion is to prevent. **Micro-masking suppression** requires both halves: organic pre-clean handles extrinsic seeds, redeposition-free ICP handles the intrinsic redeposit generated during the etch.

**The decisive cut-specific finding:** on X-cut, thermal pre-annealing at 500 °C reduces defect density and strain → improves structural homogeneity → lowers scattering loss, whereas **argon-plasma pre-treatment creates surface defects → increases lattice deformations → enhances scattering and degrades mask adhesion**[Factors affecting X-cut LiNbO₃ surface and proton-exchanged waveguides.](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00150193.2024.2305096). The two are not interchangeable — one is net-beneficial, one net-harmful. The thermal route wins despite its furnace cost because it is a reversible scheduling cost sequenced before poling, whereas argon-induced damage persists. For photorefractive-sensitive devices, defect-adding pre-treatments must be avoided; combining defect-neutral conditioning with MgO/Zn doping[ZnO-indiffused MgO:PPLN ridge waveguides.](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/433015/1/ZnO_indiffused_MgO_PPLN_ridge_waveguides.pdf) is the route to a photorefraction-robust flow.

## 7. Post-Etch Wet Cleaning and Chemical Repair

Wet chemistry physically removes the etch-product passivation layer and, aggressively, the amorphized layer itself. **The central tension: the same chemistry that cleans can also etch**, shifting stoichiometry and near-surface index.

### 7.1 SC-1 / RCA-Type Cleaning

A 2025 study characterizes SC-1 specifically for TFLN surface preparation[Thin-film LiNbO₃ surface preparation using SC-1.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396345136). SC-1 (ammonia-peroxide) lifts organic and particulate contamination via oxidation and alkaline suspension — the wet complement to dry ashing (O₂ plasma, O₂/CF₄, H₂/N₂, downstream ashing), which handles most residues with good process control and no wet waste[Post-etch cleaning and residue removal strategies.](https://ninescrolls.com/insights/post-etch-cleaning-residue-removal).

**The cleaning-vs-controlled-removal tension** is the pivot: on TFLN, SC-1 engages the LN surface itself, so it must be managed as a removal process. Reframed as a feature, a bounded shallow removal strips the topmost disordered monolayers and presents a cleaner surface — the resolution constraint is immersion-time control. SC-1 is the least precise but cheapest damaged-layer-removal option (only a wet deck vs. GCIB's angstrom control at high tool cost).

**The stoichiometry risk:** incongruent dissolution could leave a Li-depleted or Li-enriched surface, presenting an index discontinuity or defect population that caps Q independently of roughness. This raises a metrology requirement (XPS/SIMS composition verification) and is sharpest for PPLN/EO devices. The governing rule: **bound and verify the removal to keep it congruent.** SC-1 is the safer default where buried oxide is exposed (no SiO₂-attack constraint), but its controlled-removal application is recent and lacks published safe-removal limits.

### 7.2 HF and HF/HNO₃ Treatments

HF and HF/HNO₃ are the aggressive end — chemically etching damaged, redeposited, or amorphized material[Hu, Ricken, Sohler — review of LiNbO₃ etching methods Paderborn.](https://physik.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin/physik/Alumni/Sohler/2009/pr09_hu_etching.pdf).

**HF wet treatment** dissolves redeposit and disordered material, which present more accessible bonds than pristine crystal. It is a room-temperature step with no depoling risk, but **the hard constraint is HF's attack on SiO₂** — usable freely only where buried oxide and cladding are masked or not yet present, so it belongs early in the flow. HF is a credible contributor to the demonstrated material-loss reduction underlying the 29-million-Q and reduced-material-loss results[29-million-Q monolithic TFLN microresonator.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16161)[Reduced material loss in TFLN waveguides.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.17133), though its standalone contribution is not numerically isolated. It competes with redeposition-free ICP[^18]: a fab achieving redeposition-free etching does not need an HF redeposit strip.

**HF/HNO₃** is tuned for selective amorphized-layer removal — the nitric component modulates oxidation state to sharpen the etch-rate contrast between disordered and ordered LN[Hu, Ricken, Sohler — review of LiNbO₃ etching methods Paderborn.](https://physik.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin/physik/Alumni/Sohler/2009/pr09_hu_etching.pdf). Its key uncharacterized quantity is the magnitude and reproducibility of that selectivity; if marginal, it degenerates into a timed thinning etch. It is the cheaper wet alternative to self-limiting plasma ALE[Directional ALE of LiNbO₃ using Br-based plasma.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01825v1) but trades self-limiting precision for timing-controlled selectivity.

**Wet sidewall smoothing** preferentially dissolves high-curvature asperities, but on crystalline LN, **anisotropic etch rates** mean an isotropic-in-intent step etches facets unequally (cut-dependent) — the defining contrast with orientation-independent GCIB/CMP. The productive direction is a hybrid: mild wet strip of the amorphized layer followed by physical smoothing.

### 7.3 Residue Removal and Low-Damage Descum

Chlorine and fluorine chemistries leave halide residues; the Cl₂/H₂/Ar recipe[Characteristics of LiNbO₃ thin-film ICP etching.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6439/ae36b6) requires removal. Dry routes handle most residues, but **wet cleaning is reserved for stubborn halide residues**[Post-etch cleaning and residue removal strategies.](https://ninescrolls.com/insights/post-etch-cleaning-residue-removal). Halide residue acts as a corrosion seed drawing moisture and driving localized attack — both a chemical-damage marker and a micro-masking seed.

## 8. Post-Process Thermal Annealing

Annealing is the most repeatable recovery step and the only one that reaches sub-surface lattice disorder and absorption centers that wet chemistry cannot touch. The anchor: post-fabrication annealing raises intrinsic Q toward the material limit, reaching **Q ≈ 1.6×10⁸ (0.2 dB/m)** at telecom wavelengths[Shams-Ansari et al., reduced material loss / annealing to Q≈1.6×10⁸ APL Photonics 2022.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022APLP....7h1301S/abstract). **The governing trade-off: higher temperature recovers more damage but endangers the nonlinear asset — the question is not how hot, but how hot for *this* device.**

### 8.1 Low-Temperature Annealing

Recovery is thermal, not mechanical: a device off the etcher can be brought toward its intrinsic ceiling by heat alone[Shams-Ansari et al., reduced material loss / annealing to Q≈1.6×10⁸ APL Photonics 2022.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022APLP....7h1301S/abstract). The same anneal that recovers loss can recover the EO coefficient a proton-rich surface degraded, but the optimum temperature differs between X- and Z-cut[Annealed proton-exchanged waveguides in LiNbO₃: X- vs. Z-cut.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925346701001860). Whereas SC-1 strips a surface contaminant, annealing reorganizes the sub-surface lattice — they are complementary recovery routes.

**The mechanism chain:** heat enables atomic mobility → vacancy migration and defect recombination → reduced point-defect density and strain → lower Rayleigh scattering and absorption → higher Q[Shams-Ansari et al., reduced material loss / annealing to Q≈1.6×10⁸ APL Photonics 2022.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022APLP....7h1301S/abstract). The recovery envelope is bracketed by the 0.2 dB/m endpoint and the EBL-damage repair to 3.93×10⁶[Optical Materials 2024 — annealing repair of lithography-induced damage in TFLN.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OptMa.14915049S/abstract); **annealing narrows the gap to the material limit but does not guarantee a fixed endpoint** — that is set by residual damage the anneal cannot reach (roughness, addressed instead by smoothing[Roughness-limited performance in ultra-low-loss LiNbO₃ cavities.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01913v2)).

**Recrystallization** of the amorphized layer is a working hypothesis for TFLN, supported by GaN ALE-damage-recovery analogy[MD simulation of ALE sidewall-damage recovery in GaN.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38333-w) but not directly imaged in etched LN — the least mature claim in this chapter, needing cross-sectional confirmation.

### 8.2 Oxygen and Rapid Thermal Annealing

**Oxygen annealing** targets hydrogen-related absorption centers no inert anneal reaches: it reacts with OH⁻ and H₂, causing a significant drop in the absorption coefficient above 2000 nm and narrowing the gap to the theoretical limit[Influence of dry-oxygen annealing on residual absorption of LiNbO₃ Fraunhofer.](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/entities/publication/d8bcbb5e-d399-48a1-9157-fd0daf4ec565). This matters most for mid-IR DFG and parametric devices operating in that band[Watt-level 0.775 µm SHG and 3.3 µm DFG in annealed/reverse-PE PPLN ridge waveguides.](https://www.epj-conferences.org/articles/epjconf/pdf/2021/09/epjconf_eosam2021_06001.pdf).

**RTA** compresses thermal exposure — a short, hot pulse moves defects while limiting time for the slow processes that damage domains and bonded films[Cyclic and pulsed etching for atomic-level control review.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.35848/1347-4065/ae43f9). Because thermal-budget limits depend on both temperature *and* time, shortening time relaxes the limit for a given peak temperature, making **RTA the recovery route best matched to PPLN devices**. No seconds-scale RTA loss figure for etched LN exists in the cited evidence — the open quantitative question.

**The impurity floor** bounds both: oxygen annealing cannot resolve transition-metal absorption (e.g., iron)[Influence of dry-oxygen annealing on residual absorption of LiNbO₃ Fraunhofer.](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/entities/publication/d8bcbb5e-d399-48a1-9157-fd0daf4ec565). This explains why 0.2 dB/m is a *limit* not a target — once fabrication and hydrogen contributions are annealed away, residual loss reflects the boule's impurity content. The route to lower annealed loss through 2028 is **higher-purity and MgO-doped starting film**[ZnO-indiffused MgO:PPLN ridge waveguides.](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/433015/1/ZnO_indiffused_MgO_PPLN_ridge_waveguides.pdf), not more aggressive annealing.

### 8.3 Thermal-Budget Risks

**Periodically-poled domains** are the asset whose duty cycle sets SHG efficiency[Co-imaging of periodically-poled X-cut LiNbO₃ thin films.](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4352/11/3/288)[Ferroelectric domain dynamics in PPLN via two-photon microscopy.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.07792), so any thermal process that shifts domain geometry threatens the nonlinear function — a tighter ceiling than passive waveguides. Useful windows exist: annealed reverse-proton-exchanged PPLN reaches 1 W single-pass SHG (70% internal efficiency) over 50 mm[Watt-level 0.775 µm SHG and 3.3 µm DFG in annealed/reverse-PE PPLN ridge waveguides.](https://www.epj-conferences.org/articles/epjconf/pdf/2021/09/epjconf_eosam2021_06001.pdf), proving a domain-compatible schedule is achievable. Loss-recovery and domain-degradation have different thermal activation, so a window exists where recovery is substantial and domain shift tolerable — located per grating. The finest gratings (submicron UV periods[Periodic poling of X-cut TFLN: route to submicrometer periods.](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10282995)) tolerate the least budget.

**Film stress and bonding.** The LNOI stack combines materials of different thermal expansion, so high-temperature annealing introduces differential interface stress that bulk LN does not face. The 0.2 dB/m endpoint was reached on thin-film devices[Shams-Ansari et al., reduced material loss / annealing to Q≈1.6×10⁸ APL Photonics 2022.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022APLP....7h1301S/abstract) and wafer-scale low-loss is demonstrated[Wafer-scale low-loss LNOI waveguide fabrication OME 2025.](https://www.db-thueringen.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/dbt_derivate_00070614/ome-15-2-299.pdf), proving a bonded-film-safe budget exists; pushing higher raises stress risk. The etch-free 110-GHz modulator sidesteps the balance entirely by incurring no etch damage[110-GHz etch-free integrated LiNbO₃ modulator.](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.03073).

**Edge rounding.** At high temperature, surface diffusion minimizes surface energy, rounding the corners that set mode confinement and phase matching. A modest anneal smooths sidewalls (reducing scattering[Roughness-limited performance in ultra-low-loss LiNbO₃ cavities.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01913v2)[Losses from sidewall roughness in TFLN rib/strip waveguides.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OExpr..3222878H/abstract)) without rounding load-bearing geometry, but an aggressive anneal shifts the mode. Whereas GCIB/CMP remove material with controllable directionality, thermal smoothing is isotropic and cannot be steered away from edges — so annealing is best for bulk-property recovery, physical removal for roughness where shape must be preserved.

## 9. Surface Smoothing and Damaged-Layer Removal

Physical-finishing processes act on the etched surface after patterning — knocking down asperities or removing a finite thickness of compromised material. **Verdict:** GCIB is the most mature physical smoother for milled LN; catalyst-assisted polishing is the most promising damaged-layer remover but is demonstrated only on blanket substrates; digital/sacrificial etch offers the most transparent dimensional budget for patterned waveguides but remains low-maturity on LN.

### 9.1 Gas Cluster Ion Beam (GCIB) Smoothing

Siew et al. showed **GCIB after Ar milling reduces LN sidewall roughness and propagation loss** on ridge waveguides[Ultra-low-loss LiNbO₃ ridge waveguides via Ar milling + GCIB smoothing.](https://hal.science/hal-03183505/file/oe-26-4-4421.pdf)[Same, HAL v1.](https://hal.science/hal-03183505v1/file/oe-26-4-4421.pdf). Ar milling leaves rough, redeposit-laden sidewalls; GCIB converts these into smooth optical surfaces without wet chemistry or high temperature.

**Mechanism.** A gas cluster spreads its energy across thousands of atoms, so per-atom impact energy falls below the deep-implantation threshold; the impact produces a shallow lateral-sputtering pressure pulse that planarizes asperities rather than drilling[Ultra-low-loss LiNbO₃ ridge waveguides via Ar milling + GCIB smoothing.](https://hal.science/hal-03183505/file/oe-26-4-4421.pdf). Unlike ALE's chemical self-arrest[Directional ALE of LiNbO₃ using Br-based plasma.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01825v1), GCIB's gentleness is kinematic and continuous — it has no intrinsic per-cycle depth limit and can over-remove without dose control.

The motivation is the roughness-limited regime[Roughness-limited performance in ultra-low-loss LiNbO₃ cavities.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01913v2)[Losses from sidewall roughness in TFLN rib/strip waveguides.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OExpr..3222878H/abstract)[^30]: once other contributions are suppressed, every nanometer of RMS roughness removed translates directly into recovered Q. **The defining limitation:** GCIB acts on the surface profile, not necessarily on buried damage. Where roughness dominates, removing it removes the loss (why Siew succeeds); where a buried defect layer also absorbs, residual loss won't fall to zero, requiring a depth-removing second step. GCIB's shallowness — the very property making it non-damaging — limits its reach into buried damage. Its low-energy, cut-indifferent action makes it favorable for preserving nonlinear properties; the open question is whether it removes pre-existing milling damage, not whether it adds new damage.

### 9.2 CMP and Catalyst-Assisted Polishing

The strongest LN-specific result: **catalyst-assisted etching in pure water (CARE) removes residual stress and surface damage and achieves <0.1 nm RMS roughness** using a thin metal catalyst film and pure water[Hu, Ricken, Sohler — review of LiNbO₃ etching methods Paderborn.](https://physik.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin/physik/Alumni/Sohler/2009/pr09_hu_etching.pdf). The chain: selective removal of the damaged layer → eliminated stress and damage → atomically smooth surface → decreased scattering → restored optical quality. Unlike GCIB, this removes both roughness *and* the stressed near-surface layer in one mechanism.

**The honest gap:** the demonstration is on a blanket LN substrate, not a patterned waveguide, so dimensional behavior on a finished ridge is not addressed — making it the high-ceiling, low-maturity member of the family. The defining tradeoff is **smoothing vs. dimensional accuracy**: a polish aggressive enough to reach <0.1 nm RMS also removes height, thinning the guiding core. CARE resolves this favorably in principle by terminating at the stress-free surface, but whether that point coincides with target thickness on a patterned ridge is unverified. The **Li-loss concern** (inferred from proton-substitution chemistry[High-quality dry etching of LiNbO₃ via proton substitution Nanomaterials 2022.](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/nanomaterials/nanomaterials-12-02836/article_deploy/nanomaterials-12-02836.pdf)) warrants more verification per unit improvement than a mild clean like SC-1[Thin-film LiNbO₃ surface preparation using SC-1.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396345136), since a polish reworks more material — though CARE is not *known* to degrade nonlinear response.

### 9.3 Sacrificial / Digital-Etch Removal

Digital etch strips a controlled, fixed thickness by repeating a self-limiting cycle a counted number of times. Both **directional Br-based ALE** and **isotropic H₂/SF₆-Ar ALE** are demonstrated on MgO-doped LN[Directional ALE of LiNbO₃ using Br-based plasma.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01825v1)[^15]: directional for thinning a damaged top surface while preserving sidewalls, isotropic for stripping a conformal damaged shell.

**The dimensional-budget advantage:** removed thickness = cycle count × per-cycle depth, set by the operator in advance — more transparent than CARE's chemistry-set endpoint. The self-limiting cycle is dose-insensitive: each cycle removes the modified layer and self-arrests, so drift within a cycle does not change per-cycle depth, where continuous-etch errors accumulate with process drift. GaN molecular-dynamics work positions cyclic removal specifically as a sidewall-damage-recovery mechanism[MD simulation of ALE sidewall-damage recovery in GaN.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38333-w). The cost is throughput vs. continuous ICP (~32 nm/min[Characteristics of LiNbO₃ thin-film ICP etching.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6439/ae36b6)), resolved by the hybrid flow: fast ICP for the bulk, short digital finish for the damaged layer. Device loss is not yet reported for LN ALE — the central limitation.

## 10. Passivation, Cladding, and Damage-Avoiding Routes

These strategies either **screen** the damaged surface from its consequences (passivation, cladding) or **restructure the flow** so the plasma etch never touches the optical core. The central premise: the dielectric environment is a tunable mitigation variable. Air-cladding of TFLN microresonators suppresses photorefraction, raising sustainable intracavity power and enabling **1.02% external EO conversion efficiency** at cryogenic temperatures[Bidirectional microwave–light interconversion with TFLN Nat. Commun. 2021.](https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-24809-y.pdf).

### 10.1 ALD Passivation and Oxide Cladding

**Oxide overcoats are screening layers, not repair steps** — none removes the amorphized layer.

- **ALD Al₂O₃** targets electronic surface states (the trap-rich etched surface). Demonstrated on silicon, it induces high negative fixed charge (10¹²–10¹³ cm⁻²) with low interface-state density, reducing Fermi-level pinning and trap-assisted absorption[Passivation by ALD aluminum oxide EPJ Photovoltaics.](https://www.epj-pv.org/articles/epjpv/full_html/2013/01/pv120018/pv120018.html). Mechanism-validated for TFLN but magnitude-unquantified; low thermal budget, CMOS-standard, no first-order cut sensitivity. Residual risk: the fixed charge alters the static surface field, which could interact with photorefractive screening.
- **SiO₂ overcladding** targets sidewall scattering and environmental sealing by lowering the LN-to-cladding index step (illustrative benchmark: 0.272 dB/cm hybrid-Si rib/wire[193 nm-lithography nanophotonic waveguides 0.272 dB/cm hybrid Si.](https://www.photonics.intec.ugent.be/download/pub_2650.pdf)); a TFLN-specific delta is not separately quantified. Low intrinsic nonlinear risk, mature, integrates with the bonded platform's >3-year stability[Modular fabrication of TFLN modulators with silicon photonics.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2515-7647/ac5e0b).
- **Index/confinement design** is the most device-validated: air-cladding's photorefraction suppression underwrites the 1.02% external (15.2% internal) EO efficiency[Bidirectional microwave–light interconversion with TFLN Nat. Commun. 2021.](https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-24809-y.pdf). Lower cladding index raises confinement and the SHG overlap integral but places the highest field on the most damaged boundary — resolved against the operating point (cryogenic regime here) rather than universally.

| Overcoat | Primary target | Anchor | TFLN loss verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| ALD Al₂O₃ | Surface states/traps | 10¹²–10¹³ cm⁻² fixed charge (Si)[Passivation by ALD aluminum oxide EPJ Photovoltaics.](https://www.epj-pv.org/articles/epjpv/full_html/2013/01/pv120018/pv120018.html) | Mechanism-validated, magnitude-unquantified |
| SiO₂ overclad | Scattering/sealing | 0.272 dB/cm hybrid-Si[193 nm-lithography nanophotonic waveguides 0.272 dB/cm hybrid Si.](https://www.photonics.intec.ugent.be/download/pub_2650.pdf) | Qualified pass, delta unquantified |
| Index/confinement | Photorefraction/overlap | 1.02% air-clad EO[Bidirectional microwave–light interconversion with TFLN Nat. Commun. 2021.](https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-24809-y.pdf) | Device-demonstrated (cryogenic) |

### 10.2 Polymer and Encapsulation Layers

The low-thermal-budget, mechanically protective end. Polymer cladding caps the etched sidewall against humidity (lowest thermal budget; must be the last operation). **Encapsulation as photorefraction management** inherits the air-cladding logic[Bidirectional microwave–light interconversion with TFLN Nat. Commun. 2021.](https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-24809-y.pdf) — it sets the surface-field boundary condition feeding photorefractive screening, complementing the bulk-doping route (MgO:PPLN at 145 %/W[ZnO-indiffused MgO:PPLN ridge waveguides.](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/433015/1/ZnO_indiffused_MgO_PPLN_ridge_waveguides.pdf)). The **reliability benefit** is essentially free once a cladding is present: a continuous barrier slows moisture ingress, and the bonded platform's demonstrated >3-year storage stability anchors the lifetime case[Modular fabrication of TFLN modulators with silicon photonics.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2515-7647/ac5e0b). TFLN-specific polymer loss data is not established — mechanism-credible but loss-unproven.

### 10.3 Etch-Avoiding Structural Alternatives

The most radical mitigation is to not etch the LN core at all.

| Route | Eliminates | Residual challenge | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bonded / ion-sliced films** | Deep LN etch of optical core | CMP flatness, implantation recovery | Highest TRL, most foundry-aligned |
| **Femtosecond-laser processing** | Plasma exposure entirely | Laser-affected zone, serial throughput | Prototyping/specialty |
| **Deposited guiding layers** | LN etch (mode in overlayer) | Mode-overlap/efficiency penalty | Mature tooling, TFLN-overlap unproven |

- **Bonded/ion-sliced films** define the optical layer by crystal-ion-slicing and wafer bonding, removing the dominant etch-damage source by construction. The polished bonded interface (sub-nm CMP[CMP challenges in the More-than-Moore era.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40684-025-00819-9)) replaces the rough etched sidewall. Highest-TRL, most foundry-aligned (>3-year bonded-chip stability[Modular fabrication of TFLN modulators with silicon photonics.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2515-7647/ac5e0b); FinLN 3D resonators show 1.8× coupling enhancement). Specific risk: implantation disorder in the transferred film, recovered by post-transfer anneal.
- **Femtosecond-laser processing** replaces plasma with localized optical breakdown — no ion flux, no redeposition, no plasma amorphization. Trades these for a localized laser-affected zone and serial throughput; TFLN waveguide-loss maturity is lower.
- **Deposited guiding layers** relocate the mode into a deposited overlayer, leaving LN unetched. The defining trade-off is the **overlap penalty**: pulling the mode into the deposited layer reduces LN field overlap and weakens the EO/nonlinear interaction LN exists to provide. Resolved by a confinement-vs-overlap optimum set against the device's efficiency target.

## 11. Comparative Assessment, Tier Ranking, and Recommendations

Fifteen methods converge on a committed verdict: **for device-grade TFLN nonlinear photonics, a redeposition-free ICP-RIE etch followed by wet-chemical repair, post-etch thermal annealing, and ALD oxide cladding is the recommended integrated backbone, with cyclic etch–clean (ALE-like) as the highest-upside emerging challenger.** Despite the common claim that annealing alone "heals" etch damage, annealing recovers Q only to a roughness-limited ceiling, not the pristine material limit — repair cannot substitute for prevention.

*TRL values below are engineering judgment, not sourced facts.*

### 11.1 Method-by-Method Assessment

**Redeposition-free ICP (TRL 7).** Eliminates non-volatile redeposit, indirectly limiting roughness[Redeposition-free ICP etching of LiNbO₃ for integrated photonics.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/). Enables the low-loss regime — 0.44 dB/cm etched-waveguide band[Feature comparison of TFLN fabrication techniques Table 1.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-31754-z/tables/1), 8.5 dB/m optimized means[Low-loss LN waveguides 8.5 dB/m.](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2303.01184v1.pdf), 29-million-Q resonators[29-million-Q monolithic TFLN microresonator.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16161) — though it does not itself address the residual amorphized layer. Single plasma step, low thermal budget, foundry-relevant. Passes decisively on maturity: the only direct-etch route with independent device-grade reproduction.

**IBE / Ar milling (TRL 7).** Directional sputtering for profile definition, with redeposit as the recurrent liability[Hu, Ricken, Sohler — review of LiNbO₃ etching methods Paderborn.](https://physik.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin/physik/Alumni/Sohler/2009/pr09_hu_etching.pdf). No IBE-specific TFLN loss figure exists; passes on maturity, incomplete on effectiveness for lack of an isolated number.

**NLD plasma (TRL 4 for LN).** High-aspect-ratio capability demonstrated on glass[High-aspect-ratio NLD plasma etching of fused silica/borosilicate.](https://docslib.org/doc/910426), not LN. Requires specialized tooling. Fails the maturity criterion for LN pending a lithium-niobate recipe — diverging from redeposition-free ICP precisely on substrate-specific demonstration.

**Cyclic etch–clean (TRL 3, highest upside).** Self-limiting per-cycle removal caps ion dose, limiting the amorphized layer; directional and isotropic schemes demonstrated for LN[Directional ALE of LiNbO₃ using Br-based plasma.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01825v1)[Isotropic ALE of MgO-doped LiNbO₃ sequential H₂, SF₆/Ar.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.10592), with GaN MD support[MD simulation of ALE sidewall-damage recovery in GaN.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38333-w). No device loss reported yet; throughput is the governing cost[Cyclic and pulsed etching for atomic-level control review.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.35848/1347-4065/ae43f9). By ~2028, should it report device-grade loss at TRL 6, it could displace continuous redeposition-free ICP as the preferred damage-minimizing front.

**Hard-mask engineering (TRL 6 for annealed HSQ).** Annealed HSQ improves selectivity → cleaner sidewalls → lower scattering[Improved selectivity in dry etching of LiNbO₃ with annealed HSQ mask.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6528/ae2131/meta); metal/TiN masks pass on selectivity but are at-risk on nonlinear-property preservation via contamination[Etching characteristics of TiN hard mask in dielectric etch.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249512709).

**Mask-CMP / PLACE.** Sub-0.01 dB/cm by plasma avoidance[Chemo-mechanical etching PLACE technique.](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/chemo-mechanical-etching-place) — the strongest *measured* effectiveness, balanced by the unverified Li-loss concern (TRL 5–6).

**H₂-plasma proton substitution (TRL 5).** Genuine selectivity gain[High-quality dry etching of LiNbO₃ via proton substitution Nanomaterials 2022.](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/nanomaterials/nanomaterials-12-02836/article_deploy/nanomaterials-12-02836.pdf)[High-quality dry etching of LiNbO₃ via H₂-plasma proton substitution.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36014702/), but introduces the only direct index/domain perturbation concern among pre-treatment methods — at-risk on nonlinear preservation unless the modified layer is fully consumed.

**SC-1 cleaning (TRL 7).** The lowest-barrier, most foundry-native step; passes cleanly on compatibility[Thin-film LiNbO₃ surface preparation using SC-1.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396345136)[Post-etch cleaning and residue removal strategies.](https://ninescrolls.com/insights/post-etch-cleaning-residue-removal). Risk only via uncontrolled over-etch.

**HF / HF-HNO₃ repair (TRL 6–7).** Removes the amorphized/redeposited layer by differential dissolution[Hu, Ricken, Sohler — review of LiNbO₃ etching methods Paderborn.](https://physik.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin/physik/Alumni/Sohler/2009/pr09_hu_etching.pdf), recovering Q by physical removal — complementary to annealing's thermal reordering. SiO₂ attack constrains sequencing; no standalone TFLN loss figure isolated.

**Low-temperature annealing (TRL 8).** Recovers sub-surface disorder and loss centers to the roughness-limited ceiling[Optical Materials 2024 — annealing repair of lithography-induced damage in TFLN.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OptMa.14915049S/abstract); cannot repair geometric roughness. Lowest thermal budget, most PPLN-friendly.

**Oxygen/RTA annealing (TRL 7–8).** Removes hydrogen-related IR absorption[Influence of dry-oxygen annealing on residual absorption of LiNbO₃ Fraunhofer.](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/entities/publication/d8bcbb5e-d399-48a1-9157-fd0daf4ec565); RTA compresses budget for domain-bearing devices[Cyclic and pulsed etching for atomic-level control review.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.35848/1347-4065/ae43f9). Bounded by the irreducible transition-metal impurity floor.

**GCIB smoothing (TRL 6–7).** Demonstrated roughness/loss reduction on milled LN[Ultra-low-loss LiNbO₃ ridge waveguides via Ar milling + GCIB smoothing.](https://hal.science/hal-03183505/file/oe-26-4-4421.pdf); shallow action may leave buried damage. **CARE/CMP** reaches <0.1 nm RMS on blanket LN but is unproven on patterned waveguides[Hu, Ricken, Sohler — review of LiNbO₃ etching methods Paderborn.](https://physik.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin/physik/Alumni/Sohler/2009/pr09_hu_etching.pdf). **ALD/oxide cladding (TRL 7)** screens rather than repairs[Passivation by ALD aluminum oxide EPJ Photovoltaics.](https://www.epj-pv.org/articles/epjpv/full_html/2013/01/pv120018/pv120018.html)[Bidirectional microwave–light interconversion with TFLN Nat. Commun. 2021.](https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-24809-y.pdf).

### 11.2 Recommended Integrated Flow

1. **Pattern** with a clean dielectric (annealed HSQ) mask or, for ultimate loss, plasma-free PLACE/Mask-CMP[Improved selectivity in dry etching of LiNbO₃ with annealed HSQ mask.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6528/ae2131/meta)[Chemo-mechanical etching PLACE technique.](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/chemo-mechanical-etching-place).
2. **Etch** with redeposition-free Cl₂/H₂/Ar ICP at moderate bias (~120 W, 79.1° sidewall)[Characteristics of LiNbO₃ thin-film ICP etching.](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6439/ae36b6)[Redeposition-free ICP etching of LiNbO₃ for integrated photonics.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11501321/).
3. **Wet-repair:** controlled SC-1, then bounded HF/HNO₃ to strip the amorphized layer where oxide is protected[Thin-film LiNbO₃ surface preparation using SC-1.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396345136)[Hu, Ricken, Sohler — review of LiNbO₃ etching methods Paderborn.](https://physik.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin/physik/Alumni/Sohler/2009/pr09_hu_etching.pdf).
4. **Anneal:** low-temperature (or oxygen anneal for mid-IR; RTA for fine-period PPLN) to recover bulk defects toward the material limit[Shams-Ansari et al., reduced material loss / annealing to Q≈1.6×10⁸ APL Photonics 2022.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022APLP....7h1301S/abstract)[Influence of dry-oxygen annealing on residual absorption of LiNbO₃ Fraunhofer.](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/entities/publication/d8bcbb5e-d399-48a1-9157-fd0daf4ec565).
5. **Smooth** residual roughness with GCIB where the roughness-limited ceiling dominates[Ultra-low-loss LiNbO₃ ridge waveguides via Ar milling + GCIB smoothing.](https://hal.science/hal-03183505/file/oe-26-4-4421.pdf)[Roughness-limited performance in ultra-low-loss LiNbO₃ cavities.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01913v2).
6. **Clad** with ALD Al₂O₃ / SiO₂ for passivation, photorefraction management, and environmental sealing[Passivation by ALD aluminum oxide EPJ Photovoltaics.](https://www.epj-pv.org/articles/epjpv/full_html/2013/01/pv120018/pv120018.html)[Bidirectional microwave–light interconversion with TFLN Nat. Commun. 2021.](https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-24809-y.pdf).

### 11.3 Limitations and Open Gaps

- **No clean loss decomposition.** The amorphized, Li-depleted, and rough layers co-locate and cannot be independently perturbed; loss budgets must be assembled differentially[Optical Materials 2024 — annealing repair of lithography-induced damage in TFLN.](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OptMa.14915049S/abstract)[Roughness-limited performance in ultra-low-loss LiNbO₃ cavities.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01913v2).
- **Cut-dependence under-characterized** for pre-treatment proton chemistry beyond the established proton-exchange waveguide regime[Annealed proton-exchanged waveguides in LiNbO₃: X- vs. Z-cut.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925346701001860).
- **Several routes lack standalone TFLN loss figures:** IBE, NLD, HF/HNO₃, cyclic etch–clean, and CARE on patterned waveguides.
- **The impurity floor** caps annealed loss at the boule's transition-metal content — the route below 0.2 dB/m runs through higher-purity and MgO-doped film[Influence of dry-oxygen annealing on residual absorption of LiNbO₃ Fraunhofer.](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/entities/publication/d8bcbb5e-d399-48a1-9157-fd0daf4ec565)[ZnO-indiffused MgO:PPLN ridge waveguides.](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/433015/1/ZnO_indiffused_MgO_PPLN_ridge_waveguides.pdf), not more aggressive processing.

**Closing.** Through 2028, redeposition-free ICP plus layered repair remains the most credible route to sub-0.1 dB/cm etched TFLN, with plasma-free PLACE/Mask-CMP the reference for ultimate loss and cyclic LN-ALE the emerging challenger to watch.
