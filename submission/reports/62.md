# Scaling Trapped-Ion Quantum Computers: A Comparative Assessment of Architectural Strategies Toward Fault-Tolerant Utility

## Executive Summary

**A modular QCCD core networked by photonic interconnects is the only trapped-ion architecture with a credible route from today's ~100-qubit processors to the ~1 million physical qubits a useful fault-tolerant machine demands.** As of November 2025, the field's largest device is Quantinuum's 98-qubit Helios, while surface-code accounting requires roughly 1,000 physical qubits per logical qubit — leaving a three-to-four-order-of-magnitude gap to the ~1,200 logical qubits real-world cryptographic problems need.

The decisive bottleneck through 2030 is no longer gate fidelity — single-qubit errors reach the 10⁻⁷ level and SPAM the 99.9993% level — but **interconnect throughput**, which today runs at kHz–MHz Bell-pair rates against MHz local gates. This report's verdict, computed against a six-criterion weighted rubric: **distributed QCCD-plus-photonic architectures lead, monolithic single-trap scaling is dead beyond ~50 qubits, and chip-to-chip matter-link modules rank a near-term bridge. Useful fault tolerance arrives in the late 2030s, not before.**

Scaling trapped ions is fundamentally an *architectural* problem, not a qubit-physics one: per-operation fidelities are already near fault-tolerant quality. This report evaluates seven scaling strategies against a uniform rubric and argues that — despite the common claim that monolithic shuttling and photonic modularity are interchangeable routes — they separate sharply once error-correction compatibility and engineering complexity are weighted properly.

| Strategy | Mechanism | Maturity anchor |
|---|---|---|
| QCCD Shuttling | Ions transported between storage and gate zones on a multi-zone chip | 98-qubit Helios |
| Photonic Modular Interconnect | Trap modules linked by interfering emitted photons | IonQ two-system link (2026) |
| Quantum Matter-Link | Ions moved across a junction between adjacent chips | High-fidelity link demo |
| 2D Surface Trap Arrays | Lithographic planar electrode arrays | Surface-trap NISQ studies |
| Integrated On-Chip Photonics | Waveguides delivering laser light on-chip | Photonic mesh demos |
| Cryogenic / CMOS Control | Co-integrated classical control electronics | FT engineering assessments |
| Hybrid / Hierarchical | Intra-module shuttling + inter-module links | Walking Cat blueprint |

## 1. Framing: Targets, Metrics, and Rubric

### 1.1 Three Qubit Metrics That Must Not Be Conflated

The word "scale" carries multiple meanings that distort progress assessments. This report fixes three distinct metrics:

- **Physical qubit** — one trapped ion serving as a two-level system; the figure in product data sheets.
- **Logical qubit** — an error-corrected qubit assembled from many physical qubits via a QEC code, with effective error rate suppressed below the physical rate once the code runs above threshold. Utility-scale algorithms are denominated in logical qubits.
- **Algorithmic Qubits (#AQ)** — an application-oriented benchmark folding qubit count, gate quality, and connectivity into one number reflecting the largest *useful* circuit a machine can run.

**The single most important framing fact: physical-qubit growth does not translate linearly into logical-qubit capability.** A strategy that adds physical qubits cheaply but degrades connectivity or fidelity scales physical count while *losing* ground on logical count. QCCD preserves the all-to-all connectivity codes exploit; a naive surface-array tiling that sacrifices connectivity for density would add qubits while making the logical conversion harder. Headline physical-qubit counts — the figure press coverage fixates on — may be the *least* informative of the three for predicting utility.

### 1.2 The Target Vector

**Threatening RSA-2048 — the canonical "real-world problem" — requires roughly 1,200–1,400 logical qubits, translating at realistic code distances into on the order of one million physical qubits.** Google Quantum AI estimates factoring a 2048-bit RSA integer in under a week needs fewer than one million noisy qubits at a 0.1% gate error rate, a 1 µs surface-code cycle, and 10 µs reaction time. A more aggressive architecture, Iceberg's Pinnacle, claims the same with fewer than 100,000 physical qubits at a 10⁻³ error rate — demonstrating that the physical-qubit target is a function of architectural efficiency, not a fixed constant.

The committed target vector: **thousands of logical qubits, ~1 million physical qubits, logical error below 10⁻⁹, two-qubit fidelity ≥99.9%, rich connectivity, runtimes on the order of days.** By 2030, the resource-estimate trajectory implies the *first* utility-scale application will be early-fault-tolerant **chemistry rather than cryptanalysis**, because the chemistry logical-qubit floor sits below the cryptographic threshold and tolerates partial-randomization phase-estimation methods.

### 1.3 The Six-Criterion Weighted Rubric

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Scalability Ceiling | 0.25 |
| Technical Maturity (TRL & milestones) | 0.20 |
| QEC / Fault-Tolerance Compatibility | 0.20 |
| Engineering & Implementation Complexity | 0.15 |
| Near- vs Long-Term Viability | 0.10 |

**The weight vector is itself a thesis: 0.45 of total weight rests on Scalability Ceiling plus QEC compatibility,** encoding the claim that the demo-to-utility transition is won or lost on whether a strategy's growth path preserves error-correctability — not on present qubit count. Six criteria sit in the best-practice band of five-to-seven; keeping the application-oriented weights low (0.10 each) avoids double-counting signal already captured by ceiling and QEC.

Scores run 1–10 per criterion; the final score is the weighted sum. **Tier 1** (lead) ≥ 7.00; **Tier 2** (enabling/conditional) 5.00–6.99; **Tier 3** (standalone-infeasible) < 5.00. Because different normalization choices can shift 20–40% of MCDM rankings, every score is stress-tested under ±10-percentage-point weight perturbation, reporting which placements are robust and which are knife-edge.

## 2. Foundations and Scaling Bottlenecks

**The dominant bottlenecks — motional-mode crowding, surface heating, control wiring, and fault-tolerance overhead — are not independent nuisances but a coupled chain, each feeding the next, so a single-chain monolithic processor cannot reach utility scale by incremental improvement alone.**

### 2.1 Strong Qubits, Hard Scaling

Trapped ions combine the highest per-operation quality with the most severe per-qubit scaling penalty. Qubits are identical atomic systems — Helios uses ¹³⁷Ba⁺ ions — so they avoid the device-to-device spread of fabricated solid-state qubits. They share a common motional bus delivering native all-to-all connectivity, which avoids the swap-gate overhead nearest-neighbor architectures incur. Single-qubit gates reach errors at the 10⁻⁷ level.

**Both headline strengths are double-edged: the shared motion that enables any-pair entanglement is precisely what crowds as ion number grows.** A linear chain of N ions has 3N collective vibrational modes; as N grows, the spectrum densens until a gate laser can no longer address one mode without exciting neighbors. This forces gates slower to preserve selectivity, lengthening decoherence exposure and **capping usable chain length at a few tens of ions**. Every serious roadmap abandons the single chain past a few tens of ions, preserving the bus within small registers (~10–30 ions) and connecting them by shuttling or photonic links.

The 2025 monolithic baseline: Helios at N ≥ 98 (QCCD, ¹³⁷Ba⁺), roughly fivefold above the H1 baseline of N ≥ 20 across about five years. IonQ frames its baseline through #AQ rather than raw count.

### 2.2 Error Mechanisms That Degrade With Scale

These obey *distinct* scaling laws, so solving one (SPAM) does not solve the others:

- **Anomalous heating** scales with surface area and inversely with ion-electrode distance. Microfabrication brings ions within tens of microns of noisy electrodes, raising heating that injects energy into the gate bus. Cryogenic operation suppresses it by one to two orders of magnitude; sympathetic cooling (a co-trapped coolant species) drains residual energy. Helios depends on this class of motional management.
- **Optical crosstalk and spontaneous emission** scale with ion density and parallel-gate count. A benchmark measured on an isolated pair systematically overstates fidelity inside a dense register. Microwave/electronic-driven gates sidestep spontaneous emission entirely — Oxford Ionics' approach.
- **SPAM** scales weakly with N and is the least binding: Oxford Ionics demonstrated 99.9993%, a ~13× error reduction.
- **Ion loss and leakage** scale with N × runtime. A background-gas collision ejects one ion, disrupting its register's mode structure and corrupting subsequent gates until detection and reload. Useful machines need fast loss detection and automated reloading — irrelevant at 30 qubits, essential at 30,000.

| Mechanism | Scales with | Best demonstrated | Dominant at scale? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anomalous heating | Surface area, 1/distance | ~10–100× cryo suppression | Yes |
| Optical crosstalk | Ion density, beam waist | Pair-isolated benchmarks | Yes |
| Spontaneous emission | Parallel gate count | Negligible per gate | Yes (parallel) |
| SPAM | Weakly with N | 99.9993% | No |
| Ion loss/leakage | N × runtime | Low per-ion rate | Yes (aggregate) |

### 2.3 The Control and Wiring Problem

**The scaling limit is increasingly a classical-engineering limit, not a quantum-physics one.** Under strict 1:1 control, a million-qubit machine needs ~1 million signal lines crossing the cryogenic boundary — geometrically and thermally untenable. The resolution is **multiplexing**: sharing control lines so signal-line count grows sublinearly. But quantum control is analog and noise-sensitive, so multiplexing depth is bounded by analog precision rather than the digital margins classical CMOS memory enjoys.

The same constraint applies to optics and readout. Parallel gates require many independently steered beams whose free-space optics do not shrink with ion count; the resolution is integrated on-chip photonics. Error correction requires fast, repeated mid-circuit measurement of syndrome qubits, requiring parallel readout channels that scale with register size — which free-space imaging cannot supply at thousand-ion scale. Wiring, optics, and readout must be solved *together*.

### 2.4 The Gap to Utility

The gap is measured along two axes:

- **Error rate (the more severe):** two-qubit physical errors of ~10⁻³–10⁻⁴ versus the <10⁻⁹ logical rate useful algorithms need — five to six orders of magnitude. This is closed not by better gates but by **encoding**: a physical rate below the code threshold is exponentially suppressed as code distance grows. Trapped ions have already demonstrated real-time fault-tolerant error correction, fault-tolerant universal gates, and logical qubits with better-than-physical error rates — at the cost of a physical-to-logical overhead of hundreds-to-thousands to one.
- **Physical qubit count:** ~98 demonstrated versus 10⁵–10⁶ required — three to four orders of magnitude.

| Metric | 2025 baseline | Utility requirement | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical qubits | ~98 (Helios) | 10⁵–10⁶ | 3–4 orders |
| Single-qubit error | ~10⁻⁷ | Below threshold | Met |
| Two-qubit error | ~10⁻³–10⁻⁴ | Below threshold | ~1 order |
| Logical error | Better-than-physical | <10⁻⁹ | 5–6 orders |
| SPAM | 99.9993% | Below threshold | Met |
| Connectivity | All-to-all | All-to-all/modular | Met (intra-module) |

**No monolithic single-chain or single-chip processor will reach the millions-of-physical-qubit regime, so the credible path runs through modular and networked architectures** — the analytical hinge of the report. A well-connected 100-qubit machine with 10⁻⁴ gates can outperform a poorly connected 1,000-qubit machine with 10⁻² gates, which is why architectures must be scored on multiple dimensions rather than ranked by qubit count.

## 3. The QCCD Shuttling Architecture

QCCD is the most commercially mature trapped-ion strategy and the only one reduced to a documented multi-zone product. **It is the strategy that has already won the near-term race and simultaneously the one whose intrinsic ceiling guarantees it cannot win the long-term one alone.**

### 3.1 Mechanism and Maturity

By analogy to a charge-coupled-device camera moving charge packets, QCCD shuttles ion packets between storage, gate, and measurement zones. The 2021 Nature demonstration converted the blueprint into a working universal machine, validating that transport can be interleaved with high-fidelity gates without destroying quantum information. The mechanistic core: **zone separation keeps the shared vibrational spectrum sparse enough to address cleanly, preserving gate fidelity** that a crowded single register would degrade — exactly what the static linear trap cannot do.

The commercial line runs H1 (N ≥ 20, all-to-all, laser gates, QCCD) → H2 (race-track geometry, 2023) → Helios (N ≥ 98, ¹³⁷Ba⁺, 2025). **All-to-all connectivity is the QCCD's durable moat against nearest-neighbor superconducting machines:** any two qubits can be co-located on demand, paying only transport cost rather than compounding SWAP overhead.

Topology splits by purpose: the **race-track** (closed oval) optimizes routing flexibility for connectivity-hungry workloads; the **grid** imposes the regular 2D structure surface-code stabilizer measurement rewards. A fault-tolerant QCCD will likely migrate toward grid regularity as code distance grows.

**Performance.** Helios is the single most important maturity datapoint in this report — a running ~100-qubit QCCD machine no other strategy has matched as a documented product. Combined with the 10⁻⁷ single-qubit errors and Oxford Ionics' 99.9993% SPAM, the platform enters the QEC era with the two ingredients a surface code most demands — high gate fidelity and high-fidelity readout — already in hand. QCCD's TRL leads decisively: it is the only trapped-ion strategy documented as a commercial product, while photonic and matter-link strategies remain at the few-module stage.

### 3.2 Bottlenecks and QEC Codesign

Three intrinsic bottlenecks bound the single chip:

1. **Parallelism vs. shuttling fidelity.** Instantiating more gate zones raises throughput but also the aggregate shuttling burden that degrades fidelity, because feeding more zones means moving more ions more often. The resolution is a *finite optimized* zone count where parallel-gating benefit balances recooling overhead.
2. **Junction errors.** A junction crossing imparts more motional excitation than straight transport, making junction count a dominant driver of total shuttling cost. Any QCCD targeting grid topologies must first solve junction errors, because a grid places a junction at every vertex.
3. **Single-chip area — the binding ceiling.** Every added qubit needs more electrodes and wiring, saturating chip perimeter and interconnect density. **This is why QCCD, however mature, cannot reach million-qubit scale on a monolithic device.** The named falsifier — a monolithic chip holding tens of thousands of qubits without a modular interconnect — does not exist, and wiring physics argues against it.

On QEC codesign, the topology debate pits grid layouts (surface-code-native, well-validated) against flexible non-2D designs (e.g. Cyclone) that exploit shuttling freedom for lower-overhead codes. Syndrome extraction is the operation that most stresses the shuttling primitive: it runs continuously, and higher code distance means more stabilizer measurements, more junction traversals, more recooling. **The QCCD's fault-tolerant clock speed will be gated by syndrome-extraction routing time rather than gate speed** — so the decisive engineering target is faster, lower-heating transport, not faster gates.

QCCD has already crossed from physical into the logical regime: real-time fault-tolerant error correction, fault-tolerant universal gates, and better-than-physical logical error rates. These prove operation below threshold, making the path to many logical qubits a matter of qubit-count growth rather than crossing a physics barrier — but the single-chip ceiling means reaching hundreds-to-thousands of logical qubits requires modular composition.

**Verdict.** Highest maturity and strongest near-term QEC of any trapped-ion strategy; fails the utility-scale ceiling as a monolithic device. Its long-term role is the high-quality node *inside* a modular machine.

## 4. Photonic Modular Interconnect (Distributed QC)

The photonic interconnect reframes scaling entirely: rather than fighting to place more ions in one vacuum envelope, it connects many small traps with light. **It is the highest-ceiling scaling strategy on offer, and its single rate-limiting bottleneck — the remote-entanglement rate — moved from a decade-long stall into active commercial attack in 2025–2026.**

### 4.1 Mechanism: Remote Entanglement and Gate Teleportation

Two ions in different vacuum systems, never sharing a motional mode, are entangled via emitted photons interfering on a beamsplitter; a coincident detector click *heralds* entanglement. That heralded Bell pair is consumed by gate teleportation to execute a two-qubit gate between modules that never physically meet. Because the connecting medium is fiber, not a shared electrode, the architecture sidesteps the single-chip wiring problem.

The architecture is a layered stack: a heralded ion-photon primitive, a switchable photonic mesh, and gate teleportation delivering **module-level all-to-all connectivity**. The Monroe-style modular blueprint trades a hard physics ceiling for a softer engineering ceiling. Critically, modularity is not stylistic preference but structural necessity: large-scale fault-tolerant machines will be modular "by necessity or by design," because no single substrate supports the required qubit count.

**The efficiency chain governs everything:** photon collection solid angle × fiber coupling × detector efficiency sets the per-attempt success probability. The mixed-species register answers a genuine tradeoff — the communication qubit must couple strongly to light, the memory qubit must be insensitive to it — by separating roles onto two species, so off-resonant communication light leaves the memory undisturbed.

### 4.2 The Entanglement-Rate Bottleneck — Now Under Active Attack

Historically, the per-attempt success probability was small, so mean time-to-link was long relative to microsecond local gates. A low collection probability → long time-to-link → a machine whose duty cycle is dominated by link establishment rather than computation. Photon loss in the link is *irreversible qubit loss*, distinguishing quantum from classical optical networking.

Two 2025–2026 advances changed the calculus:

- **IonQ (April 2026)** photonically interconnected two independent commercial trapped-ion systems, generating entanglement at a distance — the first networked commercial quantum computers. This proves the photonic link can be packaged as a *product feature*, the prerequisite for any roadmap to commit to it.
- **Main et al. (Oxford/Oxford Ionics)** demonstrated multipartite mixed-species entanglement spanning more than two nodes, establishing the primitive scales past a single pairwise link.

### 4.3 Scalability and QEC

**The distributed architecture's Scalability Ceiling is the highest of any strategy, because it is set by network bandwidth and link fidelity rather than any intrinsic limit on module count.** By 2030 the binding constraint is likely the aggregate entanglement-generation bandwidth of the switching fabric, not module count.

The error-correction payoff is concrete. The modular-QEC roadmap of Benito et al. distributes logical qubits across modules joined by **teleported lattice surgery** — merging surface-code patches via a consumed inter-module Bell pair rather than physical adjacency. The within-module operations this assumes are already real: real-time FT error correction and better-than-physical logical qubits. The all-to-all module-level connectivity also makes the architecture a natural host for high-distance and high-rate codes, relaxing the geometric locality surface codes impose on a planar chip.

The price is **communication latency**: inter-module gates are orders of magnitude slower than local gates. The resolution is not eliminating the slow link but *compiling around it* — partitioning so most gates stay local and only sparse interactions cross the photonic link, exploiting the locality structure most useful circuits have.

### 4.4 Verdict

The distributed architecture sits one readiness tier below QCCD: it passes the primitive-demonstration bar but fails the complete-machine bar QCCD already clears. Its feasibility is bound to its weakest hardware enabler — the integrated matter-photon interface — not the qubit itself. **It is the most credible long-term route to utility scale precisely because its ceiling is set by network engineering rather than trap physics**, with high confidence on the long-term call and moderate confidence on timing. The contrarian reading: QCCD shuttling and photonic interconnect are not competitors but sequential layers of the same machine.

## 5. Chip-to-Chip Matter-Link and Surface-Trap Arrays

Two demonstrated mechanisms join separate chips: moving the ion across a gap, or entangling ions through light. This section covers the first — the **matter-link** — and the **surface-trap substrate** both it and QCCD run on, then confronts a 2025 critique on whether planar geometry can reach utility scale.

### 5.1 The Quantum Matter-Link

A matter-link physically transports an ion across a junction to an adjacent chip, keeping the qubit as matter throughout. The canonical 2022 Nature Communications demonstration reports a **transfer rate of 2,424 s⁻¹ with loss infidelity below 7×10⁻⁸** — fewer than one ion in fourteen million lost at the junction. This is the highest inter-chip transfer fidelity in the literature.

**The architectural distinction is clean: photonic links fail by not generating a photon this attempt; matter-links fail only if the chips' potential wells are misaligned at handoff.** Matter-links win on per-junction fidelity and lose on reach (chips must nearly touch); photonic links invert both. This is exactly why hybrid architectures are attractive: tile matter-linked chips into dense clusters, then network clusters photonically.

Maturity is a strong single-junction lab demonstration, not a multi-module fabric. The central integration risk: misalignment → motional heating → degraded gate fidelity → recooling overhead. **The verdict is split — pass at the single-junction level with a peer-reviewed fidelity record, fail the multi-module-fabric bar a shipping QCCD already clears within one chip.**

### 5.2 Microfabricated 2D Surface Trap Arrays

The surface-electrode trap confines ions above a chip using lithographically patterned electrodes, traced to NIST work circa 2006. **Its value proposition was never trap performance in isolation — it was making the trap a *repeatable fabricated module*.** A lithographic pattern reproduced across a wafer makes variability far smaller than hand-assembled devices, and supports chiplet-style yield economics: defective modules are discarded, only known-good copies assembled.

The fabrication route is documented in vendor products — IonQ's compact "deck of cards" Aria package and Quantinuum's H-series data sheets. Materials and electrode discipline have matured: Oxford Ionics' 99.9993% SPAM indicates readout is no longer the limiting error, and Helios at N ≥ 98 establishes that populating a near-hundred-qubit register is an *achieved milestone, not a projection*.

### 5.3 The Trap-Geometry Feasibility Debate

A 2025 critique (Nguyen/Bowers/Mouradian) argues purely planar 2D surface traps suffer intrinsic limits in **trap depth and field harmonicity**, attacking the substrate not on manufacturability but on the physics of what a single-sided electrode plane can produce. **The contrarian thrust: the property that makes surface traps manufacturable — all electrodes on one plane — is the same property that limits the depth and symmetry of the well.**

The resolution is not that one side is wrong but that **manufacturability and trap quality pull in opposite directions.** A shallower, less harmonic well raises ion loss and noisier motional modes → higher per-operation error → larger physical overhead per logical qubit under the Beverland resource accounting, compounding exactly where qubit count is largest. The tension between "planar traps work at 98 qubits" and "planar traps fail at utility scale" resolves cleanly: both are true in different regimes. **The planar trap is a working module but not a working monolith.**

If the depth-and-harmonicity limits are hard walls, modularity is not optional but mandatory — and the matter-link and photonic links become necessities the geometry forces, not conveniences. The surface-trap substrate should be scored as an **enabling module technology, not a standalone path to utility scale.**

| Dimension | Matter-link | Surface trap |
|---|---|---|
| Demonstrated | 2,424 s⁻¹, loss <7×10⁻⁸ | 98-qubit Helios; 99.9993% SPAM |
| Ceiling | Bounded by alignment reach | Bounded by well depth/harmonicity |
| Bottleneck | Multi-junction alignment | Single-sided field shape |
| Verdict | Near-term at cluster scale | Strong as module, not monolith |

## 6. Hardware Enablers: Photonics, Cryogenics, Infrastructure

**The binding constraint on moving from ~100 to tens of thousands of qubits is no longer the physics of the qubit but the engineering of the support stack.** Three enabler clusters share the same tension: demonstrated physics, unproven integration density.

### 6.1 Integrated On-Chip Photonics

Light is routed through lithographic waveguides into grating couplers that steer beams up to ions, converting a recurring alignment problem (which scales with beam count) into a one-time fabrication problem. The crossover where this becomes necessary is the thousand-ion regime.

The stability gain is **categorical, not incremental**: a coupler etched into the chip cannot drift relative to its electrode, eliminating an entire class of time-dependent recalibration that consumes a material share of operating time in free-space systems. Oxford Ionics' 99.9993% SPAM on a manufacturable platform shows the electrode-and-readout side has cleared the fault-tolerance bar, shifting the burden of proof onto optics.

**The materials mismatch is the most underappreciated obstacle:** the alkaline-earth ions (¹³⁷Ba⁺ in Helios) need visible/UV light, far harder to guide on-chip than the telecom-infrared wavelengths the silicon-photonics industry was built around. So inter-module photonic links can borrow a mature telecom supply chain, but *intra-module* delivery must develop its own short-wavelength process.

The maturity gap is **port count**: demonstrations cluster at a few couplers; surface-code architectures presume thousands of parallel optical ports. Integrated photonics is therefore not a convenience but a *structural requirement* of the surface-code route — if operations are delivered serially, cycle time lengthens with code distance and logical error suffers. **Integrated photonics has moved the binding constraint from physics to yield: nothing in the record contradicts the approach, but nothing yet proves it at the thousand-port scale.**

### 6.2 Cryogenic Operation and CMOS Control

**"Cryogenics" is not one enabler but two, at opposite ends of the maturity spectrum.**

*Cold trap operation* is established and standard on high-fidelity QCCD machines, delivering two independent gains at once: **cryopumping** drives background-gas pressure down (longer ion lifetime), and cooling the electrode suppresses **anomalous heating** by one-to-two orders of magnitude. The chain — cold electrode → lower heating → higher two-qubit gate fidelity → tractable logical overhead — is the core reason cryogenics matters for fault tolerance.

*Cryo-CMOS control* co-locates signal generation on the cold stage, collapsing analog fan-out: only a few digital lines carrying multiplexed commands cross the cryostat wall, rather than thousands of analog lines. Co-location also shortens the controller-to-qubit path, reducing the latency of the measure-decode-correct loop that real-time error correction depends on — moving cryo-CMOS from convenience to requirement as code distances grow.

But cryo-CMOS is thermally and characterizationally immature. **The thermal budget, not transistor count, is the binding constraint:** every milliwatt dissipated on the 4 K stage must be removed by a cooler with severely limited capacity there. The apparent paradox — co-location relieves fan-out heat yet adds dissipation heat — resolves because a single multiplexed digital line conducts far less heat than the hundreds of analog lines it replaces; co-location is a net win *only if* per-channel cold-electronics dissipation stays below the conductive load it displaces. Transistor models also shift at 4 K, so designs cannot reuse room-temperature libraries. Any roadmap conflating cold operation with cold control will overstate near-term readiness.

### 6.3 Vacuum, Lasers, and System Infrastructure

The vacuum-packaging choice is downstream of the cryogenic choice. IonQ's compact room-temperature package optimizes portability at the cost of vacuum quality; the QCCD cryogenic route accepts a larger enclosure for cryopumped vacuum and heating suppression. As machines move from hours-long demos to months-long commercial services, **mean-time-between-failures of vacuum and cooling components becomes a first-order determinant of application suitability**, not an engineering detail.

The **laser subsystem** is a shared bottleneck integrated photonics *relocates but does not eliminate*: on-chip waveguides route light, but the stabilized sources generating it remain off-chip and must scale. Beam steering governs parallel gate delivery, which directly sets logical-clock speed — a surface code cycling at fixed rate needs many physical gates per cycle, and serializing them through one steered beam throttles the machine. QCCD splits the difference: it brings ions to a modest number of parallel gate zones, using transport rather than steering for flexibility.

Finally, **calibration overhead** increasingly governs useful throughput more than raw gate speed; automating it is a precondition for scale.

## 7. Architectural Codesign with Quantum Error Correction

**Error-correction codesign, not raw device performance, is the axis that orders the architectures** — the same 10⁻³ physical error rate maps to wildly different logical scales depending on which code the connectivity permits.

### 7.1 From Physical to Logical: Threshold and Overhead

The surface code tolerates a ~1% physical error before correction stops helping. The decisive fact: **trapped-ion 10⁻³ gate errors sit *below*, not above, that threshold**. The platform is not fighting to reach the threshold — it is already past it, and the entire six-order-of-magnitude gap to 10⁻⁹ is bridgeable by *encoding* rather than raw-gate heroics. Every incremental reduction in physical error below threshold compounds super-linearly into reduced code distance and reduced qubit overhead.

Overhead — physical qubits per logical qubit at a target rate — is set by how far below threshold the hardware sits and how many logical operations the algorithm demands. For a 10⁻⁹ target from a 10⁻³ physical rate, the surface code's ~2d² scaling pushes overhead into the hundreds-to-thousands per logical qubit. The spread between Google's sub-million and Iceberg's sub-100,000 RSA-2048 estimates, both at comparable physical error, is the clearest illustration that **overhead is an architectural choice, not a physical constant.**

Small logical demonstrations anchor these claims: measurement-free universal computation with three logical qubits in eight physical qubits running Grover's algorithm (measurement-free matters because mid-circuit measurement risks disturbing neighbors via scattered light); fault-tolerant code switching between 7- and 10-qubit codes; and a logical three-qubit QFT on H2-1 using the [[7,1,3]] Steane code — a logical rehearsal of a Shor's-algorithm subroutine. The demonstrations operate at three logical qubits versus the 1,200–1,400 RSA-2048 needs: a ~400-fold gap the architecture exists to close. **The threshold is won; the overhead is the battlefield.**

### 7.2 Connectivity as the Decisive Lever

Ion traps are under no obligation to take the surface-code route. The **Bravyi-Poulin-Terhal bound** says a stabilizer code constrained to 2D locality must satisfy kd² ≤ O(n). Local connectivity limits how many parity checks each qubit joins, forcing n to grow dramatically for low logical error. **All-to-all coupling lifts the restriction entirely, admitting high-rate qLDPC codes with constant-overhead scaling** — the structural mechanism behind Iceberg's sub-100,000-qubit estimate against Google's sub-million surface-code figure.

IonQ supplied experimental confirmation: logical error rates up to nine times lower than the only prior demonstration of the same qLDPC code class, with logical-qubit lifetimes matching or exceeding the underlying physical qubits on a 40-ion device. **A logical qubit outliving its constituents is, by definition, doing useful error correction** — and it ran the very code family BPT says requires non-local connectivity, on hardware whose all-to-all coupling supplies exactly that.

The **Cyclone** proposal takes this to architectural scale, designing highly parallel QCCD codesigns for fault-tolerant memory. Its insight: QCCD shuttling supplies the rich connectivity high-rate codes need but is slow relative to gates, so a serialized syndrome cycle would be transport-dominated; Cyclone *parallelizes* shuttling across independent zones to amortize transport cost. The cost is many independently controllable zones, raising the wiring burden.

Universal computation also needs **magic-state distillation** routing — the dominant resource cost in many algorithms. The surface code accepts a large spatial distillation footprint for simple lattice surgery; the qLDPC route accepts more complex routing in exchange for constant-overhead memory. The codesign question is which transport burden the QCCD or photonic fabric carries more cheaply.

### 7.3 Modular vs Monolithic QEC

The connectivity advantage holds within one trap, but the resource estimates demand more logical qubits than any single trap holds. Three routes:

- **Inter-module operations over photonic links.** Mixed-species networks distribute high-quality entanglement; **lattice-surgery teleportation** performs logical operations across modules by consuming Bell pairs rather than requiring physical proximity. IonQ's 2026 two-system link is the commercial proof the primitive has left the lab. This is the mechanism that lifts the platform past the single-trap ceiling, and the structural reason the modular route dominates long-horizon roadmaps.
- **Lattice surgery across QCCD zones (monolithic).** The surface code's locality, a liability under BPT, becomes an asset mapping onto a 2D zone layout. Resource-assignment and design-toolflow studies automate the layout-and-scheduling search that becomes intractable by hand at hundreds of ions. **The same lattice surgery is realized through deterministic ion shuttling, trading the photonic link's entanglement-generation cost for the shuttling fabric's transport-and-recooling cost.** Monolithic parallelism is ultimately bounded by single-chip zone count — exactly where the modular route gains its unbounded advantage.

| Architecture | QEC code fit | Ceiling | Logical milestone | Long-term feasibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| QCCD Shuttling | Surface (clean 2D map) | Single-chip zones | 3 logical, universal | High near, capped long |
| Photonic Modular | Surface + qLDPC, teleported | Unbounded modules | Inter-machine entanglement (2026) | Highest long-term |
| Quantum Matter-Link | Surface across chips | Gap-shuttling overhead | High-fidelity inter-chip link | Strong medium-scale |
| 2D Surface Trap | Surface (geometric host) | Wiring + yield | Hosts QCCD demos | High near-term fabric |
| Integrated Photonics | Enabler for routing | Optical loss budget | Entanglement mesh | Essential enabler |
| Cryogenic CMOS | Enabler for zone counts | Cryo heat budget | Control multiplexing | Essential enabler |
| Hybrid/Hierarchical | Surface within, qLDPC/teleported between | Photonic-unbounded | Components done, integration pending | Highest combined |

**No single architecture wins on every axis:** QCCD and the 2D surface trap lead on demonstrated logical maturity, the photonic interconnect leads on long-term ceiling, and the hybrid hierarchy leads on combined feasibility by inheriting each route's strength while delegating around its weakness.

## 8. Industrial Roadmaps and Demonstrated Milestones

Three programs dominate — **IonQ** (photonic-interconnect thesis), **Quantinuum** (QCCD lineage, peer-reviewed 98-qubit processor), and **Oxford Ionics** (fidelity-and-manufacturability thesis) — but only one has published peer-reviewable specifications at the ~100-qubit scale. The recurring tension: marketing-facing scale metrics and utility-facing performance metrics diverge, and the divergence widens where roadmaps reach furthest.

### 8.1 IonQ: Photonic Interconnects, #AQ, and the 2030 Vision

IonQ reports through **Algorithmic Qubits (#AQ)**, an application-level benchmark folding gate fidelity and connectivity into one number, corroborated by independent benchmarking of the platform's high-fidelity operations. #AQ aligns IonQ's marketing metric with the utility-facing criterion this report uses — but carries a known hazard: a compressed number can mask the demonstrated-versus-required gaps that decide fault-tolerant capability.

IonQ's roadmap escalates physical-qubit count toward a multi-million-physical / tens-of-thousands-logical register by 2030. **From a ~100-qubit-class baseline to multi-million inside ~five years implies a compound growth far steeper than the register-doubling cadence trapped-ion vendors have historically shown** — the most aggressive escalation in the field, carrying the heaviest burden of proof. The falsifiable test is the *2027–2028* interim register milestone, not the 2030 endpoint: a roadmap missing the early checkpoint cannot recover the curvature needed.

The load-bearing demonstration is the April 2026 photonic interconnection of two commercial systems — establishing feasibility at N=2 modules, not that the entanglement rate scales to a multi-module fabric. The causal chain: a successful two-module demo → credible tiling claim → the binding constraint shifts from "can we link" to "can we link fast enough" → the inter-module entanglement-generation rate becomes the single parameter governing whether the 2030 target is reachable, because lattice-surgery throughput scales with that rate.

### 8.2 Quantinuum: QCCD, Race-Track, and Apollo

**Helios is the strongest single anchor the trapped-ion field has at the ~100-qubit scale** — a 98-qubit QCCD processor with a peer-reviewable specification (N ≥ 98 ¹³⁷Ba⁺ qubits), the empirical baseline against which every larger roadmap claim should be measured. The shift to ¹³⁷Ba⁺ co-evolved qubit species with architecture. Unlike IonQ's #AQ-led documentation, Helios is documented through a device-physics specification independent reviewers can scrutinize — the decisive maturity contrast, giving Quantinuum more demonstrated credibility per claimed qubit.

The **race-track** closed-loop geometry is Quantinuum's answer to the wiring problem: a fixed set of control zones is reused as ions circulate, rather than dedicating a new fully-wired zone per ion. It postpones the wiring problem rather than eliminating it — the constraint reasserts once the register outgrows a single loop.

**Apollo** targets thousands of physical and hundreds of logical qubits around 2029 — roughly **2–3× annual register growth** from the Helios anchor, aggressive but far gentler than IonQ's curve. Its logical side rests on a *demonstrated* foundation: real-time FT error correction (the feed-forward primitive), the better-than-physical logical-error result, and fault-tolerant universal gates. The chain runs from the 2021 primitive through the better-than-physical demonstration as its load-bearing link. The 2029 falsifiable test: demonstrating tens-to-hundreds of logical qubits operating together with maintained better-than-physical error rates. **Apollo is the most credible logical-scaling roadmap because its target is *extrapolated* from demonstrated error correction rather than projected from first principles.**

### 8.3 Oxford Ionics: Fidelity and Manufacturability

Oxford Ionics introduces a third axis: not how many qubits but how well each performs and how repeatably the hardware is built. The **99.9993% SPAM record (13× error reduction)** locates the differentiator on fidelity; higher per-operation fidelity means lower physical error → fewer physical qubits per logical qubit. Research relaxing the sympathetic-cooling burden targets the total time-to-solution that end-users actually care about.

**Foundry fabrication** attacks a bottleneck distinct from both the wiring problem and the entanglement-rate problem: manufacturing yield and repeatability. Building one record-fidelity trap solves a physics problem; building a thousand identical ones on a wafer begins to solve the manufacturing problem utility scale requires. Fidelity and manufacturability reinforce each other — higher fidelity lowers the trap count needed, making the foundry target more tractable.

Academic and national-lab groups frequently set the records commercial roadmaps later industrialize — the 10⁻⁷ single-qubit result defining the platform's fidelity ceiling, the multipartite networking primitive feeding distributed strategies, and the quantum-interconnect roadmap. **A record is necessary but not sufficient for a roadmap;** the gap between record-setting and system-building is exactly where credibility concentrates.

### 8.4 Roadmap Credibility Check

When vendor timelines are read against resource-estimation targets, **the gap is not primarily in qubit count — it is in the demonstrated physical error rate sustained *at scale*, the parameter governing physical-to-logical overhead, which no vendor has demonstrated at its roadmap's register size.** A vendor reaching thousands of qubits with an error rate that degrades at scale would hit its marketing milestone while missing its utility milestone entirely.

| Metric | Demonstrated | Required for FT utility | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical qubits (single proc.) | 98 (Helios) | Thousands–millions | 10×–10,000× |
| SPAM fidelity | 99.9993% | Below threshold | Met; gate-at-scale open |
| Single-qubit error | 10⁻⁷ | Below threshold | Met; throughput cost open |
| Logical error correction | Real-time FTQEC; better-than-physical | Hundreds logical sustained | Small-scale demo; scale-up unproven |
| Inter-module entanglement | Two-node link | Multi-module at lattice-surgery throughput | N=2; rate-at-scale open |

**The load-bearing finding: the platform has already demonstrated the per-operation fidelities fault-tolerance requires — but not at the register scale a utility system needs. The gap is scale-at-fidelity, not fidelity itself.** A physical qubit count and #AQ are *scale* metrics — easy to announce. The *utility* metrics — demonstrated error rate at full register, inter-module throughput, sustained logical-qubit count — grow only when the hard engineering is done. The silent substitution of the former for the latter makes roadmaps read more optimistic than the record warrants. The credibility filter: utility metric over scale metric, demonstrated over announced.

### 9.4 System-Level Tradeoff Synthesis

The five canonical bottlenecks — motional crowding, ion heating, the wiring problem, link inefficiency, and fault-tolerance overhead — form a **coupled causal scaffold** where mitigating one aggravates another:

- **Wiring → heating → overhead:** packing more electrodes raises control-line density → on-chip dissipation → motional decoherence → higher QEC overhead → larger physical qubit count. The cryo-CMOS enabler breaks this chain by removing dissipated heat.
- **Crowding → shuttling → link:** spreading ions across more zones lengthens shuttling paths → pushes toward modular partitioning → reintroduces link inefficiency at boundaries. Lattice-surgery teleportation manages this conversion.

**No strategy eliminates the bottlenecks — each chooses which coupling path to absorb and which to export.** The winning strategies export complexity to *independently mitigable* layers (interconnect, photonics, cryo-control), where dedicated enablers can attack it directly, rather than concentrating it in the coupled wiring-heating-overhead chain that caps the monolithic strategies. The standalone Surface Trap concentrates every coupling in one chip and inherits the worst of the scaffold (Tier 3); the Hybrid distributes them across mitigable layers (Tier 1).

Modularity compensates for slow interconnects precisely when the **communication-to-computation ratio is low** — most operations local, few crossing boundaries. For utility-scale fault tolerance, the monolithic ceiling binds before interconnect latency dominates, so the trade favors modularity. The unresolved decision-relevant number: the exact crossover problem size at which the hybrid's routing complexity overtakes the monolithic QCCD's wiring complexity — which depends on still-maturing interconnect rates.

## 10. Recommendation, Outlook, and Limitations

### 10.1 The Decisive Recommendation

**The top-ranked architecture is a QCCD-plus-photonic hybrid:** QCCD shuttling inside each module for dense, high-fidelity local computation, and a photonic (or matter-link) interconnect to compose modules into a machine large enough for utility scale. The structural argument: no single primitive scales cleanly from tens to hundreds of thousands of qubits. QCCD delivers connectivity and fidelity but caps node size on the wiring problem; the photonic route relaxes that cap by networking nodes, at the cost of a slower, less mature link.

**The hybrid wins not because it dominates any one criterion but because it is the only candidate that fails none catastrophically.** A standalone QCCD chip concentrates all pressure onto the wiring problem and shuttling congestion; a pure-photonic fabric concentrates pressure onto the entanglement rate. The hybrid splits the burden: the QCCD core need only reach moderate per-module size, the photonic link need only *compose* modules rather than carry the full computational load. The TITAN distributed proposal treats large-scale trapped-ion computation as inherently distributed across cooperating nodes, and the resource-assignment literature shows QCCD scaling is a tractable compiler-level problem.

**Two pathways, different risk profiles:**

- **Near-term feasible:** the QCCD core, scaled by better compilation and tighter control integration. Requires *no new physical primitive* — only engineering along tool-supported directions already in active development.
- **Long-term high-reward:** the photonic fabric networking those cores. Far higher module-count ceiling, but its binding risk — the immature inter-module entanglement rate — is correspondingly larger.

These are sequential phases, not competitors: the near-term builds the node, the long-term composes nodes. A program perfecting the QCCD core now while maturing the photonic link against the fault-tolerant cycle-time target captures near-term feasibility without forfeiting the long-term ceiling.

### 10.2 Confidence Levels

Subjective scenario judgments built on the §9 scoring and demonstrated milestones:

| Recommendation | Confidence | Dominant discount |
|---|---|---|
| QCCD dominates near-term qubit-count leaderboard | 80–90% | Competing-platform upset |
| QCCD+photonic hybrid is utility-scale winner | 55–70% | Interconnect-rate immaturity |
| Pure-photonic reaches utility scale alone | 30–45% | Entanglement-rate ceiling |
| Matter-link reaches utility scale alone | 30–45% | Topology constraint |

The 55–70% band is, on current evidence, **the ceiling of confidence for reaching fault-tolerant utility scale by any route on the trapped-ion platform**, because every credible route still passes through an inter-module link demonstrated only at minimal module count. A roughly one-in-three chance for either standalone alternative is still a rational risk-adjusted hedge given the prize size — but a hedge, not the core bet.

### 10.3 What Must Go Right, and Falsifiers

Four hardware enablers gate the recommendation:

1. **Integrated photonics** must raise collection efficiency and routing fidelity enough to lift the entanglement-generation rate toward fault-tolerant cycle times.
2. **Cryo-CMOS control** must integrate densely enough that adding zones does not reintroduce the wiring problem at the control layer.
3. **The ion-photon interface** success probability and collection efficiency — identified via the cavity-mediated figure of merit as **the single parameter on which the entire photonic outlook turns**.
4. **Foundry fabrication** must deliver repeatable high-fidelity modules at the volume a multi-thousand-qubit machine needs.

**Key falsifiers, datable to 2027–2029:** if IonQ misses its interim register milestone, the 2030 multi-million target is unrecoverable. If Apollo cannot demonstrate tens-to-hundreds of logical qubits at maintained better-than-physical rates by ~2029, the logical-scaling extrapolation breaks. If the inter-module entanglement rate stays far below fault-tolerant cycle time even after integrated-photonics maturation, the photonic and hybrid ceilings cannot be realized on a useful timescale. The single most decision-relevant unknown: the crossover problem size at which hybrid routing complexity overtakes monolithic wiring complexity — which only a full hierarchical logical demonstration can measure.

**The committed verdict stands: a QCCD core networked by photonic interconnects is the architecture most likely to host the first problem-relevant logical computation on a trapped-ion platform, with useful fault tolerance arriving in the late 2030s — and the inter-module entanglement rate is the one parameter that gates whether the timeline holds.**
