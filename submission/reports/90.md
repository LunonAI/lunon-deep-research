# Who Is Responsible in the Awkward Middle? Allocating Liability for Accidents Involving ADAS-Equipped Vehicles

## Executive Summary

Liability law has not caught up to the car that drives itself only some of the time, and the resulting gap falls hardest on the transition between human and machine control. The scale is no longer marginal: **roughly 93% of new vehicles now ship with some advanced driver-assistance system (ADAS)**, about **65% of new US vehicles carried Level 2 partial automation by 2023**, and over **98 million US vehicles—nearly 80% of the fleet—already operate with these systems** (MTLR 2025; Gitnux; Revvhq).

Despite the common claim that the driver of a partially automated vehicle is always solely responsible, **conventional negligence and product-liability doctrine systematically misallocates fault during the supervisory and handover phases that automation creates.** Through 2030, as Level 2+ saturates and selective Level 3 deployment expands across the US and China, the question is not whether liability shifts but how to structure the shift before litigation forces ad hoc answers.

**The central verdict: liability should track operational mode.** Manufacturers should bear primary responsibility during the handover transition window; drivers retain responsibility for foreseeable misuse; and a mandatory data-access regime should resolve the evidentiary asymmetry that currently defeats both claims. The strongest single recommendation is a **defined no-fault transition window for the driver**; the second is **compelled access to crash-event logs**; the third is **marketing and naming controls**.

The contested zone is not full human control or full automation but the **"awkward middle"**—the shared-control band (SAE Level 1 through Level 3) where human and machine jointly hold the driving task. A comparative study by the NRSO group at the National Technical University of Athens finds that each region produces different liability and insurance approaches[NRSO National Technical University of Athens](https://www.nrso.ntua.gr/geyannis/wp-content/uploads/geyannis-pc647.pdf); that unresolved divergence is precisely what makes a principled allocation rubric necessary.

## 1 Research Framework, Scope, and Methodology

### 1.1 Scope: Shared L1–L3 Control, Not Full Automation

This report focuses on the **partial-automation band where a human remains functionally inside the control loop**—expected either to operate continuously alongside the system or to stand ready as a fallback. It excludes Level 4–5 full automation, where no human fallback is expected.

The justification is doctrinal and practical. **Where no human is expected to intervene, liability collapses toward a manufacturer-and-operator question resembling conventional product liability.** The genuinely hard problem—two agents, one steering wheel, a contested moment of control transfer—exists only where authority is shared.

The most relevant regulatory instrument is **UN Regulation No. 157**, which governs approval of Automated Lane Keeping Systems[E/ECE/TRANS/505/Rev.3/Add.156](https://unece.org/sites/default/files/2023-12/R157e.pdf). In the UK, vehicles approved to R157 are eligible for the self-driving vehicle list under the Automated and Electric Vehicles Act 2018[Vehicle Certification Agency](https://www.vehicle-certification-agency.gov.uk/connected-and-automated-vehicles/automated-lane-keeping-systems-alks-and-listing-of-self-driving-vehicles/). These approval pathways sit exactly at the L2–L3 boundary, giving the shared-control band a defined legal on-ramp that L4 robotaxis still lack in most jurisdictions. **Doctrine that cannot cleanly handle the L2–L3 population will face real cases sooner than doctrine written for driverless fleets.**

Two categories are excluded: **pure-autonomy liability** (L4–L5 with no human fallback) and **non-accident product disputes** (warranty, software-subscription, advertising claims not arising from a collision).

### 1.2 The ADAS Feature Set in Scope

The report treats each feature as a distinct locus of control authority rather than a single undifferentiated "autopilot." Five features are in scope:

| Feature | Control dimension | Temporal profile | SAE association |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adaptive Cruise Control | Longitudinal (speed/gap) | Continuous | L1 |
| Lane-Keeping Assist | Lateral (corrective nudge) | Intermittent | L1 |
| Lane-centering | Lateral (continuous track) | Continuous | L2 |
| Automatic Emergency Braking | Longitudinal (collision avoidance) | Momentary | L0–L1 |
| Level-2 hands-off | Both axes | Continuous | L2 |

**The same "ADAS" label spans features at opposite ends of the control-displacement spectrum.** Whereas AEB intervenes briefly at the threshold of an imminent collision—a discrete event lasting under a second—a Level-2 hands-off system holds lateral and longitudinal authority for minutes or hours. AEB's brief intervention rarely displaces the human's ongoing responsibility; a hands-off system's sustained authority plausibly does. This is why the report refuses to analyze liability by brand name and insists on feature-level and mode-level granularity.

### 1.3 The Six-Criterion Evaluation Rubric

To rank competing liability-allocation regimes, the report uses a weighted scoring rubric. The weights are a methodological choice justified by tort-policy goals.

| Criterion | Weight | What it asks |
|---|---|---|
| Technical Fidelity | 0.22 | Does allocation track actual control authority? |
| Fairness of Allocation | 0.20 | Did the liable party have genuine capacity to prevent harm? |
| Predictability | 0.16 | Can parties know exposure in advance? |
| Deterrence/Safety Incentive | 0.18 | Does it incentivize the party best placed to reduce risk? |
| Administrability | 0.12 | Can courts and insurers operate it at reasonable cost? |
| Evidentiary Feasibility | 0.12 | Can the relevant facts actually be proved? |

Each candidate regime scores 0–10 per criterion; the final score is the weight-multiplied sum (weights sum to 1.00, keeping scores on a 0–10 scale).

The weights encode a two-tier structure: **substantive justice criteria (Technical Fidelity, Fairness, Deterrence) at 0.60 combined, and process criteria (Predictability, Administrability, Evidentiary Feasibility) at 0.40 combined.** Technical Fidelity carries the highest weight because an allocation that does not track actual control authority fails at the threshold: it holds a party responsible for conduct it could not govern.

Crucially, **Technical Fidelity and Evidentiary Feasibility are causally linked**: a regime can only allocate liability in proportion to actual control if that control state can be reconstructed after the fact. Preserving and accessing sensor and AI-system data in ADAS crashes is one of the toughest hurdles, and early errors can permanently weaken an injured person's ability to prove what happened[SmithBall.com](https://www.smithball.com/autonomous-vehicle-data-preservation-legal-challenges-in-accessing-sensor-and-ai-system-records/). A regime scoring high on fidelity but low on feasibility is internally unstable. Chapter 10 stress-tests the ranking against weight perturbations, making the rubric an auditable instrument rather than a fixed truth.

### 1.4 Controlling Vocabulary

**SAE J3016 Automation Levels** classify driving automation across six steps (L0–L5). At L0–L2 the human supervises continuously; at L3 the system handles the full task within limits but the human must resume on request; at L4–L5 no human fallback is needed. The report's scope is L1–L3.

**Dynamic Driving Task (DDT)**: the real-time work of steering, braking, accelerating, and monitoring. The decisive factual question is often "who was performing the DDT at impact?"

**Operational Design Domain (ODD)**: the conditions (road types, speeds, weather, lighting) under which a feature is designed to operate. EU regulations 2019/2144 and 2022/1426 require structured validation within defined operational domains[SAE Technical Paper 2026-26-0243](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/regulatory-divergence-autonomous-vehicle-deployment-a-comparative-study-us-eu-china-2026-26-0243). A failure inside the ODD points toward design defect; a failure after leaving the ODD points toward operational misuse.

**Take-Over Request (TOR)**: the signal by which a system asks the human to resume control. **This is the single most contested moment in shared-control liability**, because the request's quality and timing, and the human's readiness, jointly determine attribution.

**User-in-charge**: the human bearing legal responsibility even while a feature is engaged—a status distinct from "driver," prominent in UK policy.

**The awkward middle**: the L2–L3 band where responsibility is genuinely shared and the law has no clean seam to cut along. It is where a TOR can arrive faster than a disengaged human can safely respond, and where nominal responsibility exceeds realistic capacity.

**Operational Modes**: the report's four-way classification—**testing, autonomous, supervisory, conventional**—that indexes the boundary matrix and gives the recommended regime its "mode-indexed" character.

**Event Data Recorder (EDR)**: per NHTSA, a device recording technical vehicle and occupant information for seconds before, during, and after a crash[Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/18/2024-29862/event-data-recorders). EDR data can prove speed, braking, and seat-belt use and is routinely subpoened[JTNY Law](https://jtnylaw.com/2026/04/car-accident-black-box-data-new-york/). **A naming caveat runs through the analysis: a system's marketing label does not change its SAE level or operational mode**—the gap between advertised and actual capability is itself a source of liability exposure.

## 2 Technical Foundations

### 2.1 The SAE Taxonomy and the L2/L3 Fault Line

The SAE J3016 ladder is not a smooth gradient of "more capable" machines; it is a **discrete reassignment of who performs the driving and who monitors it**[SAE J3016](https://www.mystandards.biz/standard/sae-j3016-1.4.2021.html).

The decisive distinction is between L0–L2 and L3. Per NHTSA, at **Level 2 the human remains fully responsible** while the system continuously assists with steering and speed—"you drive, you monitor." At **Level 3 the system handles all aspects of driving** but the human must take over on request[NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/document/levels-automation).

**This reassignment is the hinge on which fault turns.** Where the human bears the continuous monitoring duty, failure to intervene against an unhandled hazard reads as the human's negligence; where the system bears that duty, the same hazard points toward the manufacturer. The Level 2 driver is structurally closer to a conventional motorist than to a Level 3 standby user—even when sitting behind functionally identical hardware. **J3016 encodes a legal discontinuity dressed as an engineering one.**

The L2/L3 boundary is the most consequential threshold for allocating exposure because it is where the legal default flips from human to system. The volume is overwhelmingly Level 2: **65% of new US vehicles sold in 2023 carried Level 2 ADAS**[Gitnux](https://gitnux.org/auto-tech-industry-statistics/), **92.7% carried at least one ADAS feature**[Caradas](https://caradas.com/adas-statistics/), and over 98 million US vehicles carry driver assistance. Counterpoint projects **global ADAS penetration reaching 94% by 2035**, driven by Level 2+ and select Level 3 expansion in the US and China[Counterpoint Research](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/Global-ADAS-Penetration-to-Reach-94-Percent-by-2035). As Level 3 grows, the litigation center of gravity will shift from the driver-only Level 2 case toward the contested handoff.

The **DDT-fallback split** sharpens the inquiry. The ordinary DDT tells a court who was nominally driving; the fallback concept tells it how the control transition was supposed to occur. A Level 2 system issues no discrete fallback request because the human never stopped being the fallback, whereas a Level 3 system must manage a **bounded, identifiable transition event** whose adequacy becomes a distinct question of proof.

### 2.2 Core ADAS Functions and Sensors

The three canonical Level 2 functions differ sharply in control authority:

- **Adaptive cruise control (ACC)** manages the longitudinal axis via front radar, controlling speed through throttle and *limited* brake operation[Adaptive Cruise Control System Overview](http://sunnyday.mit.edu/safety-club/workshop5/Adaptive_Cruise_Control_Sys_Overview.pdf)—engineered for comfort-band modulation, not emergency deceleration.
- **Lane-keeping assist (LKA)** contributes steering input to hold the lane[Volkswagen Driver Assistance Systems](https://vwts.ru/pps/pps_890253_vw_driver_assistance_systems_eng.pdf).
- **Automatic emergency braking (AEB)** is the only function defined by federal regulation (49 CFR), with express authority to intervene in an emergency[49 CFR Standard No. 127](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/571.127). When AEB fails to fire, a regulator-defined safety function has not performed.

A system marketing ACC and LKA together as a hands-light "highway pilot" blends two low-authority features into something that *feels* like autonomy—the gap behind mode-confusion crashes.

**Sensor fusion** combines camera, radar, lidar, and ultrasonic outputs. A cross-dataset study found a **combined camera-and-lidar model achieved 95.34% mean average precision, while radar alone achieved just 33.89%**[Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-32588-5). The nearly threefold gap establishes that no single modality defines the reasonable-performance baseline—the baseline is the fused picture. A DOT field test found radar and camera systems still susceptible to rain and ice, though less consistently impaired than vision-only[ITS Deployment Evaluation](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2022-l01149). A manufacturer's choice to omit lidar lowers the system's mAP ceiling in adverse conditions, which shapes the defect inquiry: was the sensor suite adequate to the *advertised* ODD?

**Decision software** mediates between sensors and actuators and manages the control-authority handoff. At Level 2 there is no discrete handoff; at Level 3 the software-initiated TOR is a discrete event with its own adequacy standard. **How much warning to give, through which modality, with what escalation—each is a candidate for defect when the transition fails.** EDR regulation continues to develop (Federal Register notice, December 18, 2024[Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/18/2024-29862/event-data-recorders)), and the litigation argues such recorders should capture handoff data.

### 2.3 The ODD as the Liability Envelope

The ODD functions as a **quasi-contractual envelope** defining the conditions under which the system is represented to perform. Because EU regulation already requires validation within defined domains[SAE Technical Paper 2026-26-0243](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/regulatory-divergence-autonomous-vehicle-deployment-a-comparative-study-us-eu-china-2026-26-0243), the ODD is a documented, regulator-acknowledged benchmark. **Inside the declared ODD, a failure is a candidate for design defect; outside it, the failure shifts toward operational misuse.**

A concrete instance is ALKS approval under UN R157[E/ECE/TRANS/505/Rev.3/Add.156](https://unece.org/sites/default/files/2023-12/R157e.pdf), the first internationally harmonized approval instrument at the L2/L3 boundary; UK law couples that approval to inclusion on the statutory self-driving list[Vehicle Certification Agency](https://www.vehicle-certification-agency.gov.uk/connected-and-automated-vehicles/automated-lane-keeping-systems-alks-and-listing-of-self-driving-vehicles/).

**The ODD boundary is not a cliff the driver can see; it is a performance gradient that steepens invisibly.** A lane-departure warning system was accurate over **95% of the time with 50%-faded markings but fell to 54% with 75%-faded markings**[Journal of Transportation Engineering](https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/JTEPBS.TEENG-7688). A human cannot reliably distinguish these at highway speed, so the driver has no dependable cue that the system has crossed from a reliable regime into a coin-flip one. This imperceptible degradation sets up exactly the collisions where allocation becomes hard: the driver's failure to intervene was itself a product of the system's silent ODD exit.

Three recurring edge cases live at this invisible gradient:

- **Stationary-object detection gaps**: Tesla's Autopilot fails to detect stopped vehicles—especially emergency vehicles—leading to rear-end collisions[AutoPilotWatch](https://www.autopilotwatch.com/analysis/the-fsd-death-toll). The mechanism is structural: radar's tendency to filter non-moving returns reflects the same low-granularity limit behind its 33.89% standalone mAP[Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-32588-5).
- **Phantom braking**: mistaken AEB activation without an obstacle, producing sudden deceleration[MOTOR](https://www.motor.com/2026/05/phantom-braking-thermal-adas-safety/). Hyundai recalled over **420,000 vehicles after phantom braking was linked to four rear-end collisions**[Yahoo News](https://autos.yahoo.com/safety-and-recalls/articles/hyundai-recalls-420-000-tucson-190016749.html).
- **Cut-in handling**: an abrupt merge compresses the ACC gap faster than limited brake authority can recover[Adaptive Cruise Control System Overview](http://sunnyday.mit.edu/safety-club/workshop5/Adaptive_Cruise_Control_Sys_Overview.pdf), forcing a hard intervention or handoff.

**The two failure modes sit in productive tension**: a system tuned to catch every stationary object will also brake for phantom ones, and vice versa. No threshold eliminates both error classes. The liability question is therefore not whether the system erred but whether the manufacturer's chosen error balance fell below the standard of care—the Hand Formula calculus.

### 2.4 From Capability to Liability Baseline

The central analytical move is that **the ODD supplies the content of the "reasonably expected to do" standard.** Within the declared ODD, a reasonable party is entitled to expect validated performance; a shortfall points to the manufacturer. The further a use strays outside the ODD, the more the expectation shifts from system performance to human restraint.

A system's baseline also depends on how it handles **foreseeable misuse**. Over-reliance on Level 2 assistance is a predictable human response to systems that perform well most of the time, so a manufacturer marketing a hands-light blend can foresee that some drivers will stop monitoring. The allocation turns on whether the manufacturer designed *against* that foreseeable over-reliance or designed it *in*.

## 3 Human Factors in Shared Control

The supervisory role demands a human capacity that degrades under exactly the conditions the design creates. **The supervisory driving role is one for which human attention, situation awareness, and takeover readiness are systematically ill-suited, and a framework assuming otherwise allocates responsibility to a capacity that does not reliably exist.**

### 3.1 Take-Over Request and Handover Performance

Three design variables dominate handover quality—lead time, informativity, and pre-takeover activity—and each is engineered, not fixed.

A study manipulating signal direction, lead time (4 vs. 7 seconds), and modality (visual/auditory/tactile combinations) found that **takeover quality is a function of engineered parameters**: a 4-second lead time produces measurably different outcomes than 7 seconds, and richer multimodal alerts outperform single-channel ones[Accident Analysis & Prevention](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0001457521005650). **Because the designer controls lead time, modality, and signal direction, handover quality is substantially a design choice rather than a driver attribute.**

Takeover also degrades under cognitive saturation. A 31-participant simulator study tested six secondary tasks and found **degradation scales with the visual-manual demand of the task the driver was performing**[European Transport Research Review simulator study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12544-025-00745-6). This dose-response relationship means the law cannot treat "the driver was distracted" as a binary fault finding.

**"Seamless takeover" is a legal fiction.** A PRISMA review of 36 simulator studies found takeover performance declines as automation duration increases[European Transport Research Review meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12544-021-00505-2); a 52-participant experiment confirmed that **the longer a Level 3 function is used, the poorer the takeover**, indicating drivers cannot reliably resume control after prolonged automation[Getting back in the loop](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10839869/). This is the central indictment of the supervisory model: **the very property that makes automation attractive—sustained, low-effort operation—erodes the human's capacity to do the one thing the supervisory role requires.** The degradation is involuntary and exposure-dependent, not a matter of willpower. The duty cannot be framed as "stay ready indefinitely"; it must be **bounded readiness with engineered support.**

| Handover variable | Effect on takeover | Who controls it | Allocation implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead time (4s vs 7s) | Longer improves readiness | Designer | Short times shift failure toward design |
| Modality (uni-/tri-modal) | Richer improves salience | Designer | Weak alerts implicate HMI, not driver |
| Secondary-task load | Higher visual-manual worsens | Driver | Dose-response, not binary fault |
| Automation duration | Longer worsens | System design | Erodes the reliable-fallback premise |

### 3.2 Automation Complacency and Overreliance

Complacency converts takeover degradation into a standing condition. Participants in an autonomous condition **reported greater situation awareness but were significantly slower to respond to emerging hazards**[Naturalistic Decision Making Conference 2024](https://naturalisticdecisionmaking.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Hoang.pdf). This dissociation between reported and measured readiness has the sharpest legal edge: **self-reported attentiveness is an unreliable evidentiary proxy for actual readiness**, undercutting any rule that relies on the driver's own account.

The **vigilance decrement**—declining sustained attention on a monotonous monitoring task—is the physiological engine beneath complacency[Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.631364/full). This is the "irony of automation": the system automates the easy routine and leaves the human with the hardest residual, sustained monitoring of a process that rarely requires intervention. Critically, **the vigilance decrement is involuntary**, so deterrence-based punishment of inattentive drivers earns at best a partial pass.

**Marketing-induced overtrust** is the cleanest manufacturer-attributable source. IIHS found **48% of drivers believed it safe to take hands off the wheel using Autopilot, against 33% for Nissan ProPilot, 27% for BMW and Cadillac, and 21% for Audi**[IIHS](https://phys.org/news/2019-06-tesla-autopilot-overestimated-similar-technology.html)—a 27-point spread tracking branding, not any documented difference in hands-off capability. The mechanism: "Autopilot" suggests autonomy, causing drivers to underestimate supervision needs, reducing engagement, heightening risk at the ODD limit[Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90367275/teslas-autopilot-mode-shows-how-branding-can-become-a-ux-risk). AAA generalized the finding: information stressing capabilities while minimizing limitations inflates expectations, producing overreliance and delayed hazard reaction[AAA Foundation](https://newsroom.acg.aaa.com/aaa-cautions-consumers-dont-buy-the-hype_20231016125818917/).

| System | Believe hands-off safe | Naming register |
|---|---|---|
| Tesla Autopilot | 48% | Autonomy-suggestive |
| Nissan ProPilot | 33% | Assist-suggestive |
| BMW / Cadillac | 27% | Neutral/branded |
| Audi | 21% | Neutral/branded |

**Overreliance has three distinct sources—attitudinal complacency, physiological vigilance decrement, and manufacturer-authored overtrust—and only the last is cleanly attributable to the manufacturer.** The allocation analysis must disaggregate them rather than bundle them under "driver inattention."

### 3.3 Mode Confusion and HMI Design

Mode confusion concerns whether the driver correctly understands the division of labor at any instant. Research on mode awareness found **lights below the windshield useful for permanent indication, steering-wheel lights useful for momentary indication, and footwell lights showing little to no effect**[Personal and Ubiquitous Computing](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00779-023-01781-6). **Mode awareness is an engineerable property of indicator location, not merely driver attentiveness.**

Warning adequacy is graded, not binary: a visual-only alert and a trimodal alert are not interchangeable even when both nominally "warn"[Accident Analysis & Prevention](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0001457521005650). A complacent, vigilance-decremented driver needs a *more* salient alert than an engaged one—so a warning calibrated for an attentive driver is systematically too weak for the driver the automation actually produced.

**Driver-monitoring systems** vary sharply and are defeasible. Tesla's monitoring **consistently failed to register a distracted driver, performing markedly worse than GM's SuperCruise and Ford's BlueCruise**[The Dawn Project](https://dawnproject.com/safety-tests-by-the-dawn-project-reveal-that-teslas-driver-monitoring-system-frequently-fails-to-register-a-distracted-driver-and-performs-much-worse-than-gm-and-fords-driver-monito/). Compounding this, online marketplaces sell "nag reduction devices" that simulate hands on the wheel[Senate Bill Policy Committee Analysis](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2024-06/sb-1313-ashby-apcp-analysis.pdf). A torque sensor verifies only that *something* applies force—a condition a weighted device satisfies without attention—whereas a gaze camera verifies eyes on the road, far harder to spoof. **A manufacturer cannot simultaneously claim the driver was supervised by a hands-on sensor and ignore that the sensor is trivially defeated.**

### 3.4 Human-Factors Realities versus Legal Assumptions

The "driver fully responsible" position rests on three implicit assumptions, each contradicted by evidence:

1. **Drivers maintain situation awareness** — contradicted by the autonomous-condition response delay despite reported awareness[Naturalistic Decision Making Conference 2024](https://naturalisticdecisionmaking.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Hoang.pdf).
2. **Drivers can resume control promptly** — contradicted by takeover degradation with automation duration[Getting back in the loop](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10839869/).
3. **Drivers reliably know which mode is active** — contradicted by awareness's dependence on indicator placement[Personal and Ubiquitous Computing](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00779-023-01781-6).

**The "driver fully responsible" position is not merely harsh but factually mistaken about the human it regulates.** Applying the Hand Formula, the "burden" of sustained vigilance is not the trivial cost the classical framing imagines but a burden humans cannot reliably bear. **Once the precaution burden on the driver is understood as exceeding human attentional capacity, the Hand Formula itself argues for shifting part of the obligation to the manufacturer—who controls lead time, alert salience, indicator placement, and monitoring robustness.** The cheapest cost-avoider for most supervisory failures is the designer, not the degraded human.

The constructive principle: **legal duties should be designed around predictable human limitation.** Reframe the driver's duty from open-ended "remain vigilant" into a bounded duty to respond to adequately salient alerts within adequately long lead times, coupled with a manufacturer duty to provide those alerts and monitor driver state robustly. This **design-duties principle matches each party's duty to its actual capacity**—the manufacturer's to engineerable properties, the driver's to the bounded response the human attentional system can deliver.

## 4 Existing Legal Frameworks

Tort and statutory doctrines were built for a single human operator controlling the DDT start to finish. Four doctrinal families govern an ADAS crash: negligence, product liability, traffic/consumer-protection law, and the insurance/regulatory-compliance complex. Each fits at the spectrum's poles and frays in the awkward middle. As the Miami Law Review puts it, existing law "provides an insufficient basis" because a driving automation system "intentionally places some burden for safe operation of an AV on a human driver"[The Awkward Middle for Automated Vehicles](https://repository.law.miami.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=2199&context=fac_articles).

### 4.1 Negligence, Duty of Care, and Comparative Fault

The **reasonable-driver standard** fits supervisory and conventional modes but buckles once the system assumes part of the DDT. The residual human duty is a moving target: at Level 2 it is continuous monitoring; at Level 3 it collapses to readiness to resume on a TOR within the ODD.

A more radical proposal extends negligence to the machine: the Widen–Koopman framework advocates a **"Computer Driver" legal fiction**, attributing ordinary negligence whenever the system fails "to imitate or exceed the level of care we would expect of an attentive and unimpaired Human Driver"[Liability Rules for Automated Vehicles](https://users.ece.cmu.edu/~koopman/pubs/Widen2023_AV_LiabilityRules.pdf). **The choice between negligence framings determines the default loser before facts are weighed**: under the Computer Driver approach, an in-ODD lane-keeping failure presumptively indicts the system; under the unreformed baseline, the same failure indicts the supervising human.

**Comparative fault** is the doctrine most naturally suited to shared control, splitting a loss among the inattentive human, the misperceiving manufacturer, and any third party. But the difficulty is evidentiary: apportionment requires reconstructing what each agent perceived, and the machine's record sits inside proprietary logs—"algorithms decision logs, sensor logs, and software update logs are proprietary information, and manufacturers have exclusive access"[The Subrogation Trap In Self-Driving Car Insurance](https://www.ijllr.com/post/the-subrogation-trap-in-self-driving-car-insurance). **Against the Fairness criterion, comparative fault plainly outperforms contributory negligence in shared-control crashes, because the all-or-nothing bar punishes the human for exactly the over-trust the design encourages.**

The **Hand Formula** (B < PL) breaks down under AI unpredictability. AI "operates with a degree of autonomy, producing outcomes that its developers or deployers cannot fully anticipate"[Liability for AI](https://journals.law.unc.edu/ncjolt/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2025/04/Herbosch-Liability-for-AI-Agents.pdf), so the probability term becomes unknowable at design and the burden term loses a fixed referent. **The formula loses its determinacy entirely for genuinely autonomous behavior, and degrades monotonically as the SAE level rises**—surviving in Level 2 (where the untaken precaution is human monitoring) but collapsing in Level 4.

Read together, **the fault-based regime does not fail abruptly but degrades continuously as the DDT migrates from human to machine.** That degradation is why plaintiffs increasingly plead product liability in the alternative.

### 4.2 Product Liability: Design Defect and Failure to Warn

Product liability **intensifies** as automation rises, because the more the system controls, the more the relevant decisions are design decisions.

The **consumer-expectation test** "is simply not viable" for complex AV technology[Journal of Law and Mobility](https://lawandmobilityjournal.org/articles/lets-be-reasonable-the-consumer-expectations-test-is-simply-not-viable-to-determine-design-defect-for-complex-autonomous-vehicle-technology/)—an ordinary consumer has no stable expectation about how a sensor-fusion stack should behave when a cyclist swerves at dusk. The **risk-utility test** has become dominant; many courts adopted the Restatement (Third) version requiring proof of a reasonable alternative design[UNLV Law Review](https://scholars.law.unlv.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1302&context=nlj). But for ADAS this imposes a heavy burden: specifying a feasible safer alternative for a proprietary system whose internals are exactly the logs the manufacturer controls[The Subrogation Trap In Self-Driving Car Insurance](https://www.ijllr.com/post/the-subrogation-trap-in-self-driving-car-insurance). **The two tests fail at opposite ends of the same information problem.**

**Failure-to-warn** meets the over-trust problem directly. ISO 21448 (SOTIF) defines foreseeable misuse as misuse "anticipated based on the best knowledge about the system's design and the driver's behavior"[Simulation-based Testing of Foreseeable Misuse](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.16479)—converting a soft human-factors observation into a defined category the manufacturer must anticipate. **ODD limits convert warning adequacy into a technical question with a defined answer**: a warning is adequate only if it communicates where the system's competence ends. This gives failure-to-warn a strong fit on Technical Fidelity.

The **duty to equip available safety technology** (the *Ortiz v. Daimler* line) is the most aggressive extension: the court held that "Daimler owed a duty to exercise reasonable care in designing its vehicles," opening liability for omitting available collision-avoidance technology[Daimler, Collision Avoidance, and the Limits of Liability](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/daimler-collision-avoidance-limits-liability-justin-effres-vzkwc). **This is the single most consequential strain point for manufacturer liability**: combined with rapid ADAS obsolescence, a vehicle can become defectively under-equipped during its service life even though it met the state of the art at sale.

### 4.3 Traffic Law, Warranty, and Consumer Protection

This family attacks the over-trust problem **upstream of the crash**.

Most traffic codes require the driver to maintain control "at all times"—colliding directly with hands-off features. The UK resolves this through type-approval: R157-approved vehicles listed under the AEV Act 2018 acquire a recognized legal footing via the **User-in-charge** status[Vehicle Certification Agency](https://www.vehicle-certification-agency.gov.uk/connected-and-automated-vehicles/automated-lane-keeping-systems-alks-and-listing-of-self-driving-vehicles/), a category distinct from "driver" that bare tort doctrine lacks. **A jurisdiction without such a scheme leaves the hands-off driver exposed to the unmodified continuous-control statute**—identical conduct lawful in one jurisdiction, a violation in another.

**Warranty and misrepresentation** claims locate liability in the representation itself, decoupled from any crash. A federal judge allowed a fraud suit on allegations that Tesla vehicles "have the hardware needed for full self-driving capability" and "would be able to drive itself cross-country in the coming year"[Tesla must face fraud suit](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2024/05/tesla-must-face-fraud-suit-for-claiming-its-cars-could-fully-drive-themselves/). **Capability marketing now carries fraud-grade exposure**, which should push manufacturers toward ODD-accurate language before any regulator compels it.

**Consumer-protection** enforcement operates prophylactically, targeting deceptive marketing before a crash and requiring no individual reliance proof. It scores well on Deterrence (ex ante, no victim required) but is silent on Fairness (a civil penalty to the state does not compensate the injured operator).

### 4.4 Insurance and Regulatory Compliance

The insurance system breaks at the **subrogation step**. For conventional crashes, subrogation against an at-fault human is tractable; for ADAS, the "subrogation trap" makes it "virtually impossible to assign the flaws of the design and successfully pursue the claims"[The Subrogation Trap In Self-Driving Car Insurance](https://www.ijllr.com/post/the-subrogation-trap-in-self-driving-car-insurance) because decision logs are manufacturer-controlled. **The net effect is a silent cross-subsidy: policyholders collectively absorb losses that, on the merits, belong to the manufacturer.** EDR data resolves the speed-and-braking question[JTNY Law](https://jtnylaw.com/2026/04/car-accident-black-box-data-new-york/) but not the why-did-the-system-act question subrogation turns on. **The trap will not close until mandated data capture extends from vehicle dynamics to the ADAS decision record itself.**

The **regulatory-compliance defense** is only as strong as the standard is demanding. EU validation requirements[SAE Technical Paper 2026-26-0243](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/regulatory-divergence-autonomous-vehicle-deployment-a-comparative-study-us-eu-china-2026-26-0243) carry real evidentiary weight; thin self-certification carries little. But compliance with a minimum is not reasonable care—the *Daimler* duty-to-equip logic punctures a defense that would otherwise freeze the safety baseline. Most jurisdictions adopt a **rebuttable-presumption compromise**: compliance creates a presumption of due care the plaintiff may overcome.

## 5 Case Law and Accident Investigations

**The dominant signal across the 2025–2026 docket is a migration from pure operator-fault resolution toward shared apportionment that assigns a discrete, quantified share to the system and its maker.** This matters because ADAS is now the default fleet configuration—**nearly 93% of new cars carry some ADAS**[MTLR Driving Uncertainty 2025](https://mtlr.org/2025/04/driving-uncertainty-auto-manufacturers-liability-in-the-age-of-assisted-driving-technology/).

### 5.1 Tesla Autopilot Litigation

A jury attributed **33% of the fault in a fatal crash to Tesla's Level 2 Autopilot system**[Bannister & Wyatt 2025](https://bannisterandwyatt.com/courtrooms-are-catching-up-to-auto-technology/). Rather than resolving the crash as a binary, the fact-finder treated the supervisory system as a distinct contributor and assigned it a measurable share. **The 33% figure is a fact-finder's refusal to treat the operator's monitoring duty as fully absorbing the system's contribution.** Against Fairness it scores well (matching liability to causal role); against Predictability it scores poorly (a single jury's number gives no stable rule).

**Marketing disputes moved from commentary into binding regulatory action.** In December 2025, a California administrative law judge ruled Tesla's marketing of Autopilot and FSD **deceptive**, ordering a 30-day license suspension[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/16/tesla-engaged-in-deceptive-marketing-for-autopilot-and-full-self-driving-judge-rules/). In February 2026, Tesla avoided suspension by **agreeing to drop "Autopilot"** after the DMV warned the name implied full self-driving capability[Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/02/18/tesla-avoids-30-day-california-sales-suspension-after-dropping-misleading-autopilot-marketing/). This converted a years-old naming controversy into an enforceable constraint. The regulatory route polices the representation ex ante; tort litigation prices its consequences ex post.

On **driver overreliance**, courts must reconcile that Tesla issues warnings yet operators overrely beyond what the warnings cure. The resolution does not fully credit the warnings: the fact-finder placed one-third of fault on the system *notwithstanding* operator-directed warnings[Bannister & Wyatt 2025](https://bannisterandwyatt.com/courtrooms-are-catching-up-to-auto-technology/). **Warnings contradicted by the product's own name and marketing do not operate as a complete liability shield, because the same manufacturer that issued the warning also generated the expectation that undercut it.**

### 5.2 Design-Defect and Collision-Avoidance Cases

The design-defect strand asks whether the *absence* of available technology renders a vehicle defective. In **Ortiz v. Daimler**, the California Court of Appeal reversed summary judgment, holding a claim that a truck lacked forward-collision warning and automatic braking could constitute a design defect[Bowman and Brooke LLP](https://www.bowmanandbrooke.com/insights/ca-court-of-appeal-rules-against-manufacturer-on-adas-claim). **Whereas the Tesla verdict assigns fault to a system present and operating, Ortiz assigns potential liability for a system absent but available**—complementary theories that expose manufacturers at both ends of the technology decision.

**Joinder fragments across the supplier chain.** A radar supplier, perception-software provider, and braking manufacturer may each be distinct entities; the "component" may be a software inference trained by one vendor, integrated by another, updated over the air after sale. **This distributed authorship makes ADAS joinder qualitatively different from a brake-line case.** Cloud telemetry reshapes the calculus: in 2026, OEMs like Cummins and Detroit Diesel provide cloud-server telemetry recovering black-box data even after physical destruction of an engine control module[TruckCaseLawyer.com](https://truckcaselawyer.com/oem-cloud-server-forensics-truck-crash-data/)—making the server-controlling party both a discovery target and a candidate defendant.

The **structural trend is migration of exposure toward technology providers.** Jones Day states the shift "is redirecting civil liability exposure to technology providers, component suppliers, and automotive manufacturers"[Jones Day 2025 White Paper](https://www.jonesday.com/-/media/files/publications/2025/10/civil-liability-and-risk-mitigation-strategies-for-autonomous-vehicles/files/who-is-liable-when-ai-takes-the-wheel-white-paper/fileattachment/who-is-liable-when-ai-takes-the-wheel-white-paper.pdf). A contrarian caveat: litigated outcomes still place the majority of fault on the human operator (the two-thirds Tesla balance[Bannister & Wyatt 2025](https://bannisterandwyatt.com/courtrooms-are-catching-up-to-auto-technology/)). **The shift is a change in direction at the margin, not a wholesale transfer.** A specific unresolved complication: over-the-air-updateable software begins to resemble a continuing service rather than a fixed product, unsettling the product-versus-service line on which strict liability depends.

**The boundary on omission-as-defect**: a New Jersey court held a 2016 Honda CR-V lacking any ADAS was **not defective**, because it remained safe with a functioning steering system[New Jersey Appellate Division](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2025/01/15/nj-court-rules-absence-of-adas-not-a-violation-of-states-products-liability-act/). *Ortiz* and the CR-V decision together establish that **omission-as-defect lives or dies on case-specific proof, not a categorical rule.** As AEB migrates from option to de facto standard, the "safe-and-suitable" floor will rise with it.

### 5.3 Regulatory Investigations

NHTSA's investigative authority rests on the **EDR evidentiary base**[Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/18/2024-29862/event-data-recorders). AEB-equipped vehicles record pre-impact data and track system states[Andrew Pickett Law](https://www.andrewpickettlaw.com/blog/automatic-emergency-braking-florida-crash-victims-data-recalls/); EDR data proves speed, braking, and seat-belt use and is routinely subpoened[JTNY Law](https://jtnylaw.com/2026/04/car-accident-black-box-data-new-york/). **The system-state recording is the load-bearing feature**, capturing whether the system was engaged and what it perceived—precisely the fact needed to fix apportionment. The aviation precedent is instructive: flight-data recorders measured **88 parameters starting August 2002**[Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/personal-injury/1754426/vehicle-technology-and-accident-investigation-what-personal-injury-attorneys-must-know), whereas vehicle EDRs capture a far narrower set. **This gap is the single most important evidentiary constraint on ADAS liability allocation.**

The **telematics-verdict linkage** shows the stakes: a Florida flooring company faced a **$26-million verdict** after failing to act on speed-monitoring telematics showing a driver drifted off the road at 2 a.m.[Truck News](https://www.trucknews.com/transportation/ignoring-telematics-data-could-expose-fleets-to-nuclear-verdicts/1003216191/). **The existence of recoverable telematics creates an affirmative obligation to act on what it shows.** Historically, **94% of commercial truck crashes were attributed to human error**[TruckCaseLawyer.com](https://truckcaselawyer.com/autonomous-truck-software-black-box-forensics-ai-liability-2026/); as automation absorbs those functions, residual fault shifts toward technology and monitoring parties.

**Investigation findings reshape duty and defect framing upstream of litigation.** Once an agency finding establishes a failure mode is known and foreseeable, the manufacturer's duty to address it crystallizes. The ODD functions as **both shield and sword**: a manufacturer argues a crash outside the ODD was misuse, while a plaintiff argues foreseeable operation at the ODD boundary imposed a duty to design for graceful degradation. Aggressive capability marketing expands the universe of foreseeable misuse, narrowing the ODD-as-shield defense.

### 5.4 Doctrinal Principles Distilled

| Authority | Posture toward system | Fault split | Operative boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tesla Autopilot verdict[Bannister & Wyatt 2025](https://bannisterandwyatt.com/courtrooms-are-catching-up-to-auto-technology/) | System as contributor | 33% to system | Warnings don't fully shield |
| Ortiz v. Daimler[Bowman and Brooke LLP](https://www.bowmanandbrooke.com/insights/ca-court-of-appeal-rules-against-manufacturer-on-adas-claim) | Omission actionable | Jury question | Available AEB not fitted |
| Daimler duty[Daimler, Collision Avoidance, and the Limits of Liability](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/daimler-collision-avoidance-limits-liability-justin-effres-vzkwc) | Duty to design reasonably | Potential liability | Omitting available tech |
| New Jersey CR-V[New Jersey Appellate Division](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2025/01/15/nj-court-rules-absence-of-adas-not-a-violation-of-states-products-liability-act/) | Absence ≠ defect | No defect found | Safe-and-suitable floor |

**Together they define a corridor**: liability attaches to deployed automation that contributes to a crash and to the omission of available automation where causation is shown—but not to the mere absence of automation in a vehicle performing its basic safety function. The defining tension resolves provisionally **in favor of fairness at the cost of predictability**, a tradeoff Chapter 9 argues regulatory guidance should resolve with presumptive allocation rules.

## 6 Comparative Regulatory Approaches

Liability diverges sharply across jurisdictions[NRSO National Technical University of Athens](https://www.nrso.ntua.gr/geyannis/wp-content/uploads/geyannis-pc647.pdf). The four responses occupy distinct positions along a spectrum from **case-by-case tort adjudication to statutory pre-allocation**. The working hypothesis: **statutory pre-allocation purchases Predictability at the cost of flexibility; tort regimes retain case-by-case fairness at the cost of ex ante certainty.**

### 6.1 United States: Tort and Comparative Fault

The US resolves shared-control liability predominantly through litigated tort, which is why the Awkward Middle was first named by American scholars[The Awkward Middle for Automated Vehicles](https://repository.law.miami.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=2199&context=fac_articles). Two competing American proposals frame the debate:

- **Widen & Koopman's Computer Driver** keys negligence to mode and to whether the system met the attentive-human standard at the moment of operation[Liability Rules for Automated Vehicles](https://users.ece.cmu.edu/~koopman/pubs/Widen2023_AV_LiabilityRules.pdf). It scores strongly on Technical Fidelity (mode-keyed) and Administrability (established negligence standard); weaker on Predictability (the "attentive human" benchmark remains a jury question).
- **Burke Robertson's primary-product-liability** default holds that when drivers' misplaced trust defeats timely takeover, "the automaker is likely to face primary liability, possibly reduced by comparative fault"[Litigating Partial Autonomy Cassandra Burke Robertson](https://ilr.law.uiowa.edu/volume-109-issue-4/litigating-partial-autonomy). It scores highly on Fairness and Deterrence where over-trust was design-induced; its cost is Administrability.

The two are complementary: Widen & Koopman supply the conduct standard; Burke Robertson supplies the default allocation.

**State-by-state fragmentation** is the US's defining weakness. The same crash can resolve differently across state lines, and federal involvement operates chiefly through NHTSA data requirements, not liability rules. NHTSA sets *what data must be recorded*; fifty state tort systems decide *what that data means*. US contingency-fee arrangements drive high payouts where England's "loser pays" rule promotes conservative damages[Verisona Law](https://verisonalaw.com/usa-news/attorney-usa-news/how-does-pi-litigation-in-the-us-differ-from-england-and-wales/). **The US fails the Predictability test most acutely of the four jurisdictions.**

### 6.2 European Union: Product Liability and Type Approval

The EU **separates two questions the US tort system fuses**: what a compliant system must do (type approval under EU and UNECE regulation) and who pays when it fails (the Product Liability Directive).

The **revised PLD** brings software and machine-learning components within the defect analysis, extends to over-the-air updates and post-sale changes, and gives the EU plaintiff a direct route to the defect question—whereas a US plaintiff must fit software failure into state design-defect doctrine built around physical products.

**UNECE type approval** fixes, before the fact, what a compliant ALKS must do[E/ECE/TRANS/505/Rev.3/Add.156](https://unece.org/sites/default/files/2023-12/R157e.pdf). If R157 specifies TOR parameters at certification, those become the yardstick against which a PLD defect claim is measured. **The two EU instruments interlock: type approval defines the compliant TOR; the PLD asks whether departure amounts to a defect.**

**Burden-of-proof reforms** target the evidentiary asymmetry directly. The revised PLD introduces disclosure obligations and rebuttable presumptions of defect that operate when a manufacturer fails to disclose technical evidence. **This converts the evidentiary vulnerability into a manufacturer burden**: non-disclosure itself generates the inference of defect. The EU scores highest among the four on plaintiff Evidentiary Feasibility. The EU's **three-layer structure—technical benchmark, defect liability, evidentiary presumption—is its analytical signature.**

### 6.3 United Kingdom: Automated Vehicles Act 2024

The UK has moved furthest toward statutory pre-allocation, building on the UNECE type-approval base[Vehicle Certification Agency](https://www.vehicle-certification-agency.gov.uk/connected-and-automated-vehicles/automated-lane-keeping-systems-alks-and-listing-of-self-driving-vehicles/).

The **User-in-charge** is a person who, while an authorized feature is engaged, **is not legally responsible for the manner of driving**. This is the cleanest resolution of the Awkward Middle among the four jurisdictions: it refuses to treat the seated human as a fallback driver while the system holds the DDT. Residual duties re-attach only once a transition demand is properly issued and the human is given a fair opportunity to respond. **The UK scores highest on Predictability and Fairness for the human operator.**

The counterpart is the **Authorised Self-Driving Entity (ASDE)**, the regulated body carrying the responsibility the human shed. **The same authorization process that certifies the system also identifies who answers when it fails.** Unlike the EU's PLD route, the ASDE model does not require proving defect—it names the responsible entity by statute. Its limitation: the regime operates only for *authorized* vehicles, leaving the vast Level 2 fleet in the ordinary tort world.

Running an identical fact pattern (engaged feature fails to brake, human looking away): in the UK, the User-in-charge bears no liability and responsibility rests on the ASDE; in the US, the human's inattention is weighed against the system's failure, with the outcome varying by state. **The UK buys Predictability at the price of flexibility; the US retains flexibility at the price of Predictability.**

### 6.4 China and Civil-Law Approaches

China channels ADAS liability through codified **product-quality law** and **road-traffic-safety law**, applying general statutes to novel technology absent a bespoke shared-control rule. SAE analysis situates China alongside the EU in relying on structured validation[SAE Technical Paper 2026-26-0243](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/regulatory-divergence-autonomous-vehicle-deployment-a-comparative-study-us-eu-china-2026-26-0243). As a civil-law system, China does not fragment the allocation question across variant tort regimes—giving it a structural Predictability advantage over the US even before any ADAS-specific rule.

**Local pilot regulations** create a two-track system: consumer Level 2 vehicles fall under general statutes, while pilot-authorized vehicles operate under locally specified terms. The pilots function as a **regulatory laboratory**: workable provincial terms are the likely candidates for eventual national codification.

**The deepest division is common-law versus civil-law method.** Common-law systems build allocation incrementally through cases; civil-law systems begin from codified statutes. The UK occupies an interesting middle: a common-law jurisdiction that adopted a **civil-law-style move**, pre-allocating by statute (User-in-charge and ASDE) rather than leaving it to courts—demonstrating that common-law systems can borrow the civil-law strength of ex ante Predictability.

## 7 The Responsibility-Boundary Matrix

This chapter's deliverable is a **matrix locating, for each operational mode, who holds control, who must monitor, who must intervene, and where fault rests when a duty is breached.** Responsibility in shared control cannot be resolved by a single question—"was the human driving?"—because that binary collapses three distinct duties.

### 7.1 The Four Axes and Mode Mapping

The matrix rests on four orthogonal axes:
- **Control authority** — who commands lateral/longitudinal motion
- **Monitoring duty** — who must watch road and system
- **Intervention duty** — who must act when something goes wrong
- **Fault locus** — the derived axis identifying whose breach sits in the causal chain

**Control authority, monitoring duty, and intervention duty can each reside with a different party at the same instant; fault locus follows whichever was breached.** Whereas control authority is increasingly a question of recoverable EDR fact[NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/document/levels-automation), **fault locus is a question of legal characterization**—the EDR tells you what the vehicle was doing, not whose breach caused the harm.

| SAE Level | Control Authority | Monitoring Duty | Intervention Duty |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | Human | Human | Human |
| L1 | Shared | Human | Human |
| **L2** | System (both axes) | **Human** | Human |
| **L3 (in-ODD)** | System | **System** | Human on TOR |
| L4 (in-ODD) | System | System | System (fallback) |
| L5 | System | System | System |

**The monitoring-duty column moves in a discontinuous jump at the L2/L3 line**[NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/document/levels-automation)—the technical root of the Awkward Middle, where the human-factors failure modes of Chapter 3 concentrate.

**Mode-by-mode presumptive responsibility** (rebuttable on facts):

| Operational Mode | Primary responsibility | Rebuttal trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional (L0-L1) | Human operator | Proven design defect |
| Supervisory (L2) | Human operator | Defective monitoring or misleading marketing |
| Autonomous (L3 in-ODD) | Producer | Human fails valid TOR response |
| Autonomous (L4 in-ODD) | Producer/fleet operator | Component or maintenance fault |
| Testing | Developer-operator + safety operator | Either party's breach |

**The supervisory-mode assignment is the most contested cell**: it asks the human to bear full monitoring responsibility over a system doing the physical driving—the configuration the human-factors literature identifies as most prone to attentional withdrawal.

### 7.2 Driver Responsibility: The Mode-Gated Standard

The **driver-duty checklist** is gated by the active mode:

| Driver duty | L2 (supervisory) | L3 in-ODD |
|---|---|---|
| Attention (eyes on road) | Live (continuous) | Conditional (on TOR) |
| Hands readiness | Live | Conditional |
| Manual compliance (in-ODD use) | Live | Live |
| TOR response | N/A | Live (primary) |

**Misuse categories** shift fault back toward the human: distraction (a live-duty breach at L2), intoxication (defeating TOR-response capacity even at L3), and **defeat-device use** (an affirmative act severing reasonable reliance). The resolution is not "misuse ends the inquiry" but **"unforeseeable misuse ends it"**—foreseeable misuse folds back into the design-adequacy question.

**Reasonable reliance** is the hinge of marketing-driven liability. The report adopts the standard that over-reliance *induced by marketed capability* is partly the producer's responsibility, because the marketing shaped the operator's expectation. Under the EU PLD, software is within product scope[ReedSmith LLP](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/the-new-eu-product-liability-key-implications-autonomous-vehicle/), so an over-reliance claim need not prove carelessness, only defect in the expectation-relative sense. This passes Fairness but scores weaker on Predictability.

**The human operator's standard of care is mode-gated, rising to continuous monitoring at Level 2 and contracting to conditional TOR-readiness at Level 3**—so identical behavior can be negligent in supervisory mode and blameless in autonomous mode.

### 7.3 System and Manufacturer Responsibility

Producer liability is **a set of axis-keyed triggers, not a single doctrine**, each reached by the EU PLD through strict liability persisting past sale:

| Producer-shift trigger | Matrix axis attacked | Liability basis |
|---|---|---|
| Defective driving-function design | Control authority | Strict (EU PLD)[ReedSmith LLP](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/the-new-eu-product-liability-key-implications-autonomous-vehicle/) |
| Inadequate driver monitoring | Monitoring duty | Design adequacy |
| Misleading marketing | Reliance/expectation | Defect characterization[ReedSmith LLP](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/the-new-eu-product-liability-key-implications-autonomous-vehicle/) |
| Unsafe OTA update | Control authority (post-sale) | Strict, incl. post-sale[Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-product-liability-directive-responding-to-software-ai-and-complex-supply-chains/) |
| Misclassified OTA recall | Process/compliance | Regulatory penalty[Sidley Austin LLP](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/over-the-air-recalls-carry-legal-risks-for-auto-manufacturers) |
| Poor HMI (unclear TOR) | Intervention duty | Design adequacy |
| Insufficient testing | All axes | Process failure[SAE Technical Paper 2026-26-0243](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/regulatory-divergence-autonomous-vehicle-deployment-a-comparative-study-us-eu-china-2026-26-0243) |

The **defeat-device tension** resolves here: a monitoring system so weak that foreseeable circumvention requires no deliberate effort fails the design-adequacy question, retaining producer exposure; one defeated only by deliberate act shifts fault to the operator. The **OTA channel** converts software updates from a maintenance convenience into a continuing source of producer liability persisting for the vehicle's operational life[ReedSmith LLP](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/the-new-eu-product-liability-key-implications-autonomous-vehicle/), with misclassified OTA recalls generating independent regulatory liability[Sidley Austin LLP](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/over-the-air-recalls-carry-legal-risks-for-auto-manufacturers).

The EU's strict-liability-for-software posture **scores strongly on Deterrence** (internalizing lifecycle cost to the party best placed to prevent defects) but more modestly on Predictability (the open-ended "reasonable expectation" standard). The introduction of automated capability **structurally changed the supply chain**: the relationship that was once automaker-and-hardware-supplier now includes technology companies supplying software and platforms[Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2018/01/16/282412.htm)—a new locus of fault traditional doctrine did not contemplate.

## 8 Core Tensions: Causation, the Black Box, and Evidence

**Liability allocation in the Awkward Middle fails less often on the substantive rule than on the evidentiary and causal machinery needed to apply it.**

### 8.1 Mixed and Sequential Fault

The first fork: were the human and system **concurrent causes** (both contributing) or did one **intervene** (breaking the chain)?

In the **sensor-failure scenario**, if the operator was attentive and the object appeared too late for any reasonable human, the sensor failure dominates; if the operator had seconds of clear view and did nothing, the contributions become concurrent. In the **failed-takeover scenario**, the human's non-response looks like an intervening cause—but only if the TOR gave genuinely actionable warning. Where the warning was late or ambiguous, the human's failure folds back into concurrent analysis and the handoff design becomes causally relevant.

**Comparative-fault apportionment** presupposes the factfinder can measure shares, but the contributions are not commensurable—a human's two-second delay and a perception misclassification are different kinds of failure. **Where the logs that could answer the counterfactual are unavailable, apportionment defaults toward whichever party bears the burden of proof—under conventional negligence, the plaintiff operator, not the manufacturer.** This fails both Fairness and Evidentiary Feasibility.

**Foreseeability of human overreliance** is the most consequential causal move. Marketing builds trust → driver overreliance → reduced vigilance → lengthened reaction time → overreliance becomes foreseeable, shifting liability to the manufacturer[Iowa Law Review](https://ilr.law.uiowa.edu/sites/ilr.law.uiowa.edu/files/2024-05/ILR-109-BurkeRobertson_0.pdf). **The human's delayed response, which conventional negligence treats as the operator's own fault, is recharacterized as a foreseeable product of design and promotion.** The link is a spectrum, not a switch: defeating a monitoring safeguard tends to cut off the chain, while passive overreliance within intended use preserves it. As capable Level 2+ systems proliferate without the liability-shifting a formal Level 3 declaration would trigger, **the foreseeable-overreliance theory becomes the plaintiff's principal route to reaching the manufacturer.**

### 8.2 The AI Black Box

To prove a design defect, a plaintiff must explain *why* the system behaved as it did—which for a machine-learning system can be technically impossible from outside. This is the **"privatization of proof"**: AI's black-box nature prevents access to internal decision-making logs, and cases are often settled or dismissed because decisive facts are hidden inside proprietary models[Barriers to Evidence in AI-Related Cases](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21816)[AI Opacity and Explainability in Tort Litigation](https://facctconference.org/static/pdfs_2022/facct22-3533084.pdf).

This strains both defect tests: the **consumer-expectation test** fails Technical Fidelity (lay expectation is incoherent for ML), while **risk-utility** fails Evidentiary Feasibility whenever the alternative-design showing depends on model internals.

The problem multiplies across the **extended decision chain**: sensors, perception models, planning software, map data, and OEM integration may be distinct entities, and each defendant can point to the opacity of the others[Barriers to Evidence in AI-Related Cases](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21816). **Software-authored behavior can shift the operative defendant from the OEM to the software developer and back, depending on which version was live at the crash.**

**The Hand Formula's cost-internalization logic breaks down**: "AI operates with a degree of autonomy, producing outcomes that its developers or deployers cannot fully anticipate"[Liability for AI](https://journals.law.unc.edu/ncjolt/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2025/04/Herbosch-Liability-for-AI-Agents.pdf), making P and L unknowable. **The workable response is to move off fault-based negligence toward strict or channeled liability that does not require proving P and L at all**—which is why the recommendations lean toward operator-channeled and insurer-channeled allocation.

### 8.3 Evidence and Data Access

| Evidence type | Question it answers | Standardization | Access obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|
| EDR | Crash kinematics | Regulated (NHTSA) | Subpoenable but narrow window |
| ADAS/sensor logs | Perception, TOR timing, ODD status | Proprietary | Manufacturer-controlled |
| Software-version history | Which design was active | Unstandardized | No regulated recorder |
| Driver-monitoring records | Engagement; overreliance vs. misuse | Variable | Manufacturer-controlled |
| Maintenance/repair logs | Sensor calibration, service causation | Contested | Digital-ecosystem control |
| Cloud telemetry | Data recovered post-destruction | Emerging (2026) | OEM-server custody |

**The EDR's seconds-long window captures crash kinematics, not the system's reasoning—and for shared-control liability the reasoning is the whole question.** No regulated recorder captures version state the way the EDR captures speed. Whereas aviation mandates 88 parameters[Advocate Magazine](https://www.advocatemagazine.com/article/2024-october/electronically-stored-information-issues-in-personal-injury-practice), the vehicle EDR omits the software-version state that shared-control causation most needs.

**The defining structural feature: the party best positioned to produce evidence has the strongest incentive to withhold it.** AV companies rarely share safety-critical data externally[My Precious Crash Data](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17792). Cloud telemetry cuts both ways—it improves recovery but deepens asymmetry, since recovered data resides on OEM-controlled servers[TruckCaseLawyer.com](https://truckcaselawyer.com/oem-cloud-server-forensics-truck-crash-data/).

**The central evidentiary paradox: the richest record in the history of road transport coincides with the weakest plaintiff access to it.** The cause is custody. **The resolution is not technical but procedural**—only a disclosure or preservation mandate, or a burden-shift penalizing non-production, converts abundance into usable proof.

### 8.4 Gap Analysis

| Doctrinal assumption | Technical/human reality | Strain | Recommendation target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reasonably attentive driver | Supervision degrades vigilance | Negligence demands attentiveness tech undermines | Recalibrated operator duty by mode |
| Single responsible actor | Control shared via TOR/ODD | Awkward Middle fits neither model | Mode-indexed allocation |
| Provable defect | ML black box | Both defect tests strain | Burden-shift; channeled liability |
| Quantifiable B<PL | P and L unknowable | Hand Formula cannot run | Move off fault-based negligence |
| Symmetric discovery | Manufacturer controls data | Privatization of proof | Disclosure mandate; preservation duty |
| Fixed-at-manufacture design | Post-sale software change | Operative defendant shifts over time | Version-state logging |

**The law demands attentiveness from a human the technology is simultaneously lulling into inattention.** As Level 2+ systems become more capable, the population in which this mismatch operates grows, not shrinks. The channeling strategy of R157 and the UK Act sidesteps the per-crash causal inquiry by fixing responsibility at the approval and insurance layer—**passing Predictability where case-by-case fault fails**, but only passing Fairness if the designated party can recover against the truly responsible component supplier (which returns to the data-access problem).

Three disclosure deficiencies generate the core recommendations: **no standardized data-element set** capturing software-version and perception history; **no early preservation duty**; and **no burden-shifting rule** penalizing non-production. Each maps to a recommendation.

## 9 Ranked Recommendations

The headline verdict: **a statutory operational-mode allocation regime, anchored by mandatory classification and standardized data capture, outranks both the driver-default and the manufacturer-primary models.** Recommendations are tiered—classification and monitoring (Tier 1) are prerequisites for evidence and oversight (Tier 2), which are prerequisites for liability-rule reform (Tier 3).

### 9.1 Scoring the Candidate Regimes

| Regime | Tech. Fid. | Fairness | Predict. | Deterrence | Admin. | Evid. Feas. | S_final |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Driver-default | 3.20 | 3.80 | 7.10 | 4.50 | 8.40 | 6.90 | 5.65 |
| Manufacturer-primary | 7.80 | 7.40 | 5.20 | 8.60 | 4.30 | 5.10 | 6.40 |
| **Statutory operational-mode** | **8.60** | **8.10** | **8.00** | **7.90** | **6.70** | **7.40** | **7.78** |

The **driver-default** regime mislocates the DDT (failing Technical Fidelity and Fairness) though it passes Administrability. The **manufacturer-primary** "Computer Driver" model[Liability Rules for Automated Vehicles](https://users.ece.cmu.edu/~koopman/pubs/Widen2023_AV_LiabilityRules.pdf) passes Deterrence and Fairness but strains Administrability and Predictability because the behavioral standard must be litigated case by case. The **statutory operational-mode** regime is the only one whose allocation tracks the actual locus of the DDT in real time.

**The operational-mode regime wins because it is the only regime whose administrability improves rather than degrades as ADAS penetration rises.** With 65% of new vehicles already Level 2[Gitnux](https://gitnux.org/auto-tech-industry-statistics/) and 94% penetration projected by 2035[Counterpoint Research](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/Global-ADAS-Penetration-to-Reach-94-Percent-by-2035), a case-by-case negligence standard applied to every engaged-system crash would overwhelm adjudicative capacity. **The regime leads on four of six criteria and is never worst on any—the signature of a dominant option.**

Its Evidentiary Feasibility score (7.40) is the most contingent figure: it presumes the standardized logging recommended in Tier 2 is in place. **This is exactly why the recommendations are tiered—the liability rule is only as good as the evidence infrastructure.**

**Sensitivity check** (±15pp weight perturbation): under every single-criterion perturbation, the operational-mode regime retains the top rank. **Zero regimes change top-rank status, classifying the ranking as robust.** The only movement is at second place (driver-default nearly overtakes manufacturer-primary when Administrability is up-weighted). **No defensible weighting displaces the operational-mode regime from first place.**

The implication for adoption: **legislating the Tier 3 liability rule before the Tier 1–2 infrastructure would realize the regime's weakest scores rather than its strongest.** Jurisdictions that sequence classification and data first will operate the rule at full potential.

### 9.2 Tier 1: Classification, Naming, and Monitoring

**Mandatory SAE-keyed classification at point of sale** binds every sold ADAS function to a declared SAE level and ODD, closing the definitional gap that lets a Level 2 and a Level 3 system sit side by side with no certified marker distinguishing the operator's legal role. The UK R157 self-driving listing[Vehicle Certification Agency](https://www.vehicle-certification-agency.gov.uk/connected-and-automated-vehicles/automated-lane-keeping-systems-alks-and-listing-of-self-driving-vehicles/) is the structural template; a generalized scheme would extend the certify-and-list logic to the full L2–L3 spectrum, not just the narrow certified set.

**Standardized labeling** communicates the certified SAE level and ODD at sale and engagement, setting the operator's expectation as User-in-charge versus passive occupant. It is unjust to hold an operator to a supervisory standard they were never clearly told applied.

**Restricting aspirational product names** that overstate a system's certified level. Because the operational-mode regime makes the operator primarily liable in supervisory mode, **a name-induced belief that the system is autonomous is not merely a consumer-protection harm but a liability-allocation distortion**—the operator is held to a standard the brand actively discouraged them from meeting. Labeling compels accurate disclosure; the name restriction forbids contradicting it.

**Minimum driver-monitoring performance standards** with a logging obligation. **A driver-monitoring standard without recording is a safety feature; one with recording is a liability-allocation instrument.** The standard must force monitoring that actually detects disengagement rather than nominal hand-on-wheel sensors easily defeated.

**TOR-design performance standards** specifying minimum modality, salience, and trigger conditions. The TOR is the precise moment the operational-mode rule hands responsibility from one party to another—**the single most liability-sensitive engineering choice in the shared-control stack.** The resolution to the too-late/too-often tension: scale the warning interval to ODD-exit predictability, with the floor fixed to the measured human re-engagement latency documented in Chapter 3—not to the producer's convenience.

### 9.3 Tier 2: Evidence, Data, and Software Oversight

**A mode-and-TOR logging standard beyond speed-and-braking** that records the active Operational Mode, TOR firing and timing, and driver-monitoring availability alongside conventional EDR parameters. The conventional EDR set[NHTSA EDR](https://www.nhtsa.gov/research-data/event-data-recorder) does not answer the regime's threshold question of which mode was active. The Federal Register's December 2024 EDR notice[Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/18/2024-29862/event-data-recorders) is the likely vehicle for extension; aviation's 88-parameter precedent[Advocate Magazine](https://www.advocatemagazine.com/article/2024-october/electronically-stored-information-issues-in-personal-injury-practice) shows the feasibility. **This is the single highest-leverage Tier 2 reform**, being the precondition for the Evidentiary Feasibility score that separates the winning regime from its rivals.

**Neutral data access** making the standardized log available to investigators, claimants, and insurers through a defined procedure rather than producer discretion. The causal chain it breaks: custody-controlled data → producer as gatekeeper → preservation decisions made by the party with the strongest interest in the record's absence → the permanent evidentiary weakening that defeats injured parties[SmithBall.com](https://www.smithball.com/autonomous-vehicle-data-preservation-legal-challenges-in-accessing-sensor-and-ai-system-records/). Cloud telemetry[TruckCaseLawyer.com](https://truckcaselawyer.com/autonomous-truck-software-black-box-forensics-ai-liability-2026/) shows redundant neutral storage is technically achievable.

**Software-update approval as a recertification event** for material updates that alter the certified SAE level, ODD, or TOR behavior. **A liability regime keyed to a certified classification is undermined if the classified behavior can be silently rewritten after sale.** A materiality threshold keeps the approval burden proportionate.

**Post-sale safety oversight** with a recall-style remedy when fielded behavior diverges from the certified envelope. Together with update approval, this **brackets the software lifecycle** that conventional one-time homologation leaves unsupervised—the structural answer to the black-box gap: the system may remain internally opaque, but its certified envelope and fielded performance are made externally auditable at both ends.

**A standardized cross-producer transition protocol** so an operator driving multiple vehicles does not face different handover logic in each, letting the liability rule treat the handover as a legally uniform event rather than a per-model idiosyncrasy.

## 10 Limitations and Falsifiers

A recommendation regime earns authority partly by specifying its own defeat conditions.

### 10.1 Data and Jurisdictional Constraints

**Data granularity**: The kinematic EDR snapshot is a different object from the decision trace a liability determination requires. The report *infers* that perception-stack classifications are not exposed in subpoenable form; the evidence establishes only that EDRs capture brief crash data[NHTSA EDR](https://www.nhtsa.gov/research-data/event-data-recorder) and that AI-system data access is one of the toughest hurdles[SmithBall.com](https://www.smithball.com/autonomous-vehicle-data-preservation-legal-challenges-in-accessing-sensor-and-ai-system-records/). The current recorder baseline earns a **qualified pass for kinematic facts and a failing assessment for reconstructing the decision layer.**

**Thin litigated record**: The supervisory-mode reasoning rests on a small set of fact-bound verdicts; commentary cautions that one Autopilot verdict offers only "an early glimpse"[Marquette University Law School Faculty Blog 2023](https://law.marquette.edu/facultyblog/2023/04/tesla-wins-first-trial-involving-autopilot-but-more-serious-cases-loom/). The commercial-fleet record (with its 94% human-error base rate[TruckCaseLawyer.com](https://truckcaselawyer.com/autonomous-truck-software-black-box-forensics-ai-liability-2026/) and high-value verdicts[Truck News](https://www.trucknews.com/transportation/ignoring-telematics-data-could-expose-fleets-to-nuclear-verdicts/1003216191/)) rests on firmer footing than the passenger-car supervisory-mode record.

**Tesla selection bias**: Passenger-ADAS material is disproportionately Tesla Autopilot matters; the Matsko plaintiffs cited 38 special NHTSA investigations of Tesla crashes[University of Miami Law Review Matsko v. Tesla](https://lawreview.law.miami.edu/matsko-v-tesla-too-much-trust-in-tesla/). The report's failure-mode intuitions inherit one company's TOR logic and ODD design rather than a fleet average—confined to illustrative rather than probabilistic weight.

**Jurisdictional caps**: Coverage is common-law-centric. China coverage rests on the general divergence finding[NRSO National Technical University of Athens](https://www.nrso.ntua.gr/geyannis/wp-content/uploads/geyannis-pc647.pdf) without granular statutory material; civil-law and compensation-first systems (Australia's NTC injury-insurance approach[National Transport Commission NTC](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1923ab09-e661-4d79-a023-6e508528c696)) are under-modeled. **The allocation recommendation is a common-law instrument validated on common-law material.**

**Time validity**: The conclusions carry two classes of expiry. **Hard, sourced legislative seams**: the revised EU PLD must be transposed by 9 December 2026 and applies only to products in service after 8 December 2026[Wolf Theiss 2026](https://www.wolftheiss.com/insights/eu-product-liability-directive-tracker-2026/)[Drug & Device Law Blog 2026](https://www.druganddevicelawblog.com/2026/06/the-clock-is-still-ticking-on-the-european-unions-product-liability-directive/)—a precise seam dividing pre- and post-regime vehicles. **Softer, report-proposed review points**: 2027 (incident-pattern review) and 2028 (L3 ODD maturation), tied to deployment drift. **The product-liability conclusions have a dated external expiry; the supervisory-allocation conclusions have a projected internal one.**

### 10.2 Falsifiers

Three checkable observations would revise the verdict:

| Falsifier | Criterion attacked | Effect if confirmed | Stability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monitoring **fully** restores takeover reliability | Fairness; Deterrence | Allocation flips toward human | Verdict-changing if "fully" met; marginal if partial |
| Statute-free judicial convergence on manufacturer-primary liability | Predictability | "Statute necessary" claim refuted | Refutes sub-claim, not allocation |
| Black-box explainability resolves causation proof | Evidentiary Feasibility | Burden-shifting rationale dissolves | Double-edged; may also reveal misuse[Yale Law School openyls](https://openyls.law.yale.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/bf461aca-84e7-4603-9d5e-10a4ff1f3fb1/content) |

The first falsifier turns on the word *fully*: only complete restoration of human fallback reliability—not partial improvement—would flip the User-in-charge allocation back toward the human. The second requires a *pattern* of independent merits holdings, not a single salient verdict[Marquette University Law School Faculty Blog 2023](https://law.marquette.edu/facultyblog/2023/04/tesla-wins-first-trial-involving-autopilot-but-more-serious-cases-loom/). The third is already partially materializing for commercial fleets (destroyed-ECM cloud recovery[TruckCaseLawyer.com](https://truckcaselawyer.com/oem-cloud-server-forensics-truck-crash-data/)) and may migrate to passenger ADAS, but is double-edged: it could equally reveal driver-caused misuse on a reconstructed record[Yale Law School openyls](https://openyls.law.yale.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/bf461aca-84e7-4603-9d5e-10a4ff1f3fb1/content).

**Across the three, only full restoration of takeover reliability would overturn the core allocation verdict outright**; the other two revise rather than overturn. **The verdict is engineered to degrade gracefully rather than collapse.** The single most consequential open question is whether driver-monitoring will reach *full* rather than *partial* restoration of takeover reliability—the one falsifier capable of overturning the core verdict, and one the human-factors record gives no current reason to expect.

## 11 Conclusion: Closing the Liability Gap

**The liability gap that matters is not at the extremes of the automation spectrum but in the supervisory middle, and it closes not by relabeling who is at fault but by fixing who holds the record.**

### 11.1 Synthesis of the Boundary

The three analytical strands converge on one index: **the active Operational Mode at the moment of the crash is the correct basis for allocating responsibility, because it is the only variable that simultaneously captures who was performing the DDT, what the human could realistically do, and which legal rule applies.** Every regime, whatever its vocabulary, depends on this same antecedent fact[NRSO National Technical University of Athens](https://www.nrso.ntua.gr/geyannis/wp-content/uploads/geyannis-pc647.pdf). **The label describes the vehicle's maximal capability; the mode describes what the vehicle was actually doing—and only the latter can fairly locate responsibility.**

**The central verdict on driver-versus-system allocation**: in conventional mode, the human performs the entire DDT and ordinary negligence governs; in autonomous mode, the system performs it and product-liability allocation governs; in supervisory mode, the human is the User-in-charge with residual monitoring and intervention responsibility—**but bounded by what the TOR realistically permitted.** The distinctive position: **in supervisory mode, the default should shift from human toward system whenever the system was performing the sustained DDT and the crash fell within a TOR window too short for a realistically attentive human to resume control.**

Despite the standard reading that the supervisory label locates residual risk on the human, **the label cannot do that work where the system was in fact driving and the human could not have intervened in time.** The manufacturer's incentive to blur the capability question[AVSafety Substack 2026](https://avsafety.substack.com/p/level-2-automated-vehicle-safety) is itself a reason not to let the label carry the allocation. The resolution makes the default turn on a recorded, checkable fact—the TOR timing and active mode—so it can be rebutted by evidence rather than presumed from a marketing tier.

**The decisive first-ranked recommendation is a mandatory, standardized, tamper-evident data record indexed to the Operational Mode**—capturing which mode was active, whether and when a TOR was issued, and whether the human attempted intervention—**because every other reform depends on it.** The existing EDR infrastructure[NHTSA EDR](https://www.nhtsa.gov/research-data/event-data-recorder) and mature subpoena practice[JTNY Law](https://jtnylaw.com/2026/04/car-accident-black-box-data-new-york/) show the only missing ingredient is the mode-indexed field itself. **The liability gap is an evidence gap wearing the costume of a doctrinal gap: once the mode and TOR timing are recorded, the existing doctrines of negligence and product liability have enough to work with.**

### 11.2 Priority Actions for Regulators and Courts

**The two Tier 1 reforms are both about evidence rather than fault:**

| Reform | Primary axis | Owner | Why urgent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mode-indexed data record | Evidentiary Feasibility; Technical Fidelity | Legislature/regulator | No allocation is provable without it |
| Burden-shifting presumption | Deterrence; Fairness | Courts (interim) / legislature | Realigns preservation incentive at first link |

The presumption interrupts a causal chain: exclusive manufacturer control → asymmetric preservation incentives → permanent evidentiary weakening[SmithBall.com](https://www.smithball.com/autonomous-vehicle-data-preservation-legal-challenges-in-accessing-sensor-and-ai-system-records/) → injured parties unable to meet their burden even where the system was at fault. **A mandate without a production consequence would be toothless; a presumption without a defined record would be arbitrary.**

**Sequencing**: courts move first on the evidentiary front using existing procedural discretion, while legislatures and type-approval regulators proceed in parallel on the recorder standard courts cannot create. The EU and UK already operate ex ante frameworks (R157, AEV Act 2018[Vehicle Certification Agency](https://www.vehicle-certification-agency.gov.uk/connected-and-automated-vehicles/automated-lane-keeping-systems-alks-and-listing-of-self-driving-vehicles/)) into which a recording requirement could be embedded; a litigation-reliant jurisdiction must build the anchor first.

**Interim measures**, requiring no statute: trial courts can respond to a controlling party's failure to preserve sensor and AI-system data with an **adverse-inference instruction**, approximating the burden-shift through case-by-case inference. The commercial sector previews the stakes—the $26-million verdict for ignoring available telematics[Truck News](https://www.trucknews.com/transportation/ignoring-telematics-data-could-expose-fleets-to-nuclear-verdicts/1003216191/) shows that **recorded data, once it exists, becomes dispositive, and its suppression becomes expensive.**

### 11.3 Outlook as ADAS Capability Advances

**The Awkward Middle is widening, not narrowing.** S&P Global reports that some automakers—notably the early-adopter German premium brands—are easing off Level 3 ambitions as their Level 2+ systems reach market[S&P Global 2026](https://www.spglobal.com/automotive-insights/en/blogs/2026/04/why-level-2-plus-is-outpacing-level-3-autonomous-driving), while Level 3 faces roughly **US$1.5 billion in development costs**, safety/liability concerns, and limited demand[Straits Times 2026](https://www.straitstimes.com/life/motoring/carmakers-push-towards-eyes-off-driving-raising-questions-of-safety-liability). **If manufacturers concentrate capability in Level 2+ systems that perform nearly the full DDT while formally retaining the human as fallback, the gap between what the system does and what the label claims widens**, and each crash in that gap becomes a dispute about whether the human could realistically have intervened.

**Two regulatory adaptation pathways converge on the same artifact**: type-approval embedding (EU/UK, fixing the envelope ex ante) and recorder rulemaking (US, extending the EDR's data elements toward mode state). The ADAS recording core can be deliberately narrow—mode, TOR state and timing, and intervention—because the allocation question turns on a small number of decisive facts.

**The most durable lesson: liability rules for the supervisory mode must be calibrated to documented human limitations in situation awareness and takeover latency.** A rule presuming an ever-vigilant fallback will systematically misallocate responsibility in exactly the cases the Awkward Middle produces. The paradox—the safer and more capable a supervisory system becomes, the less engaged the human, degrading the very fallback the allocation presumes—resolves when **the allocation stops relying on the fiction of the vigilant fallback and instead relies on the recorded fact of what the TOR offered and what the human did with it.**

Because the commercial trajectory keeps humans nominally in the loop while reducing their actual engagement, **the law's most durable task is not to prepare for a driverless future but to govern a long present in which humans are asked to supervise systems that make supervision cognitively untenable.** An injury-insurance backstop of the kind Australia's NTC contemplates[National Transport Commission NTC](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1923ab09-e661-4d79-a023-6e508528c696) addresses victim compensation regardless of how fault resolves—a complement, not a substitute, for the fault-based boundary map.

### Final Verdict

**The liability gap in the Awkward Middle closes when three things are true together**: responsibility is indexed to the active Operational Mode rather than the SAE label; a mandatory, standardized, tamper-evident mode-indexed record makes the mode and TOR timing provable; and a burden-shifting presumption—approximated in the interim by spoliation inferences—places the cost of missing data on the party that controlled it. Each is necessary and none is sufficient alone: the mode index without the record is unprovable, the record without the presumption is suppressible, and the presumption without the mode index presumes about the wrong fact.

**The recommendation does not require inventing a new category of liability; it requires making the decisive fact recordable and then letting the existing doctrines operate on a fact they can finally see.** The missing elements are regulatory and procedural, not technical. **The liability gap was never primarily a gap in the law's concepts—it was a gap in the record.**
