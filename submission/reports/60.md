# Comprehensive and Accurate Cislunar Space Situational Awareness: A Ranked Recommendation of Sensing Architectures

## Executive Verdict

No single sensing modality can deliver comprehensive cislunar situational awareness. The decisive architecture is a **hybrid that anchors space-based optical custody at Earth–Moon libration points and fuses it with ground-based optical, passive RF, and selective radar.** The problem is not marginal: the cislunar volume is roughly **an order of magnitude larger than the entire near-Earth and GEO regime**, and as of 2024–2025 ground-based surveillance alone leaves blind spots that no telescope-siting strategy closes.

This report scores nine candidate architectures against six weighted criteria. The ranked result, after sensitivity testing, is unambiguous:

- **Hybrid Distributed Multi-Modal Architecture leads (S_final 7.56)**, on coverage, accuracy, and short-term responsiveness, held below the top tier only by near-term cost and maturity.
- **Ground-Based Radar (6.23) and Space-Based Optical Observers (6.20)** follow as the strongest single modalities.
- The remaining six options land in Tier 3, each disqualified by one binding constraint.

The cislunar SSA radar-and-optical sensing market was valued at **$2.8 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $8.6 billion by 2034 at a 13.2% CAGR**, with the broader SSA market projected to reach $61 billion cumulatively over the same period. Demand through 2030 is driven by congestion at the lunar South Pole and Gateway-class orbits.

## 1. Framework, Scope, and Evaluation Rubric

### 1.1 Defining the Cislunar Problem

The 2023 National Cislunar Science & Technology Strategy defines cislunar space as the volume **beyond geosynchronous orbit under the gravitational influence of Earth and/or the Moon**, including the Earth–Moon Lagrange regions and the lunar surface. This report adopts an operational boundary of the **xGEO-to-lunar volume, roughly ten times the geosynchronous radius**, parameterized by libration-point and manifold structure rather than spherical shells — because the dynamics are non-Keplerian. No universally accepted boundary exists across the community; Artemis I planning, for instance, postulated collision-avoidance maneuvers at 100,000 km from Earth and again at lunar orbit insertion.

**Target classes** span active spacecraft, spent stages, debris, transfer vehicles, and non-cooperative or maneuvering objects, each imposing distinct sensing demands. Disposal status governs whether an object needs **custody maintenance** (an active crew orbiter) or one-time **characterization** (an impacted lander). Crucially, the modalities fail on different axes: electro-optical/infrared sensors offer **confirmation without persistence**, RF sensing reveals **intent without location certainty**, and radar detects **objects without intent**. This complementarity is the analytical seed of the hybrid recommendation.

**SSA decomposes into six functions** — initial acquisition, custody maintenance, characterization, prediction, conjunction awareness, and anomaly recognition. A **short-term tracking task** is a time-bounded campaign (hours to days) triggered by a launch, maneuver, or predicted conjunction, demanding fast retasking and geometric agility — traits that trade against the broad steady coverage cataloging rewards. This report deliberately weights toward that short-term mission.

### 1.2 The Scoring Rubric

Nine architectures are scored 0–10 on six weighted criteria summing to 1.00:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Tracking Accuracy & Uncertainty Control | 0.22 |
| Short-Term Tasking Responsiveness | 0.22 |
| Maturity, Cost & Near-Term Feasibility | 0.16 |

| Robustness to Constraints & Adversarial Behavior | 0.13 |

**Accuracy and responsiveness jointly command 44% of the score**, mapping directly to the prompt's emphasis on *accurate* awareness supporting *short-term* tasks. Tier thresholds: **Tier 1 (Recommended) ≥ 8.0; Tier 2 (Supporting) ≥ 6.0; Tier 3 (Limited) < 6.0.** Integration carries explicit weight because the fusion literature treats source-and-schema discipline as make-or-break — insufficient discipline causes a fusion pipeline to fail within six months.

### 1.3 Why No Single Modality Closes the Gap

Earth-based sensors are **already overwhelmed by LEO traffic and structurally ill-suited to cislunar distances**. The GAO documents "gaps in the geographical distribution of global sensors and limited sensor capability for objects in deep space". Because each modality fails on a different axis, combining an optical sensor's angular precision with radar range data yields a fused estimate tighter than either alone — but only if a disciplined schema layer preserves each modality's distinct semantics through fusion. That governed complementarity is the backbone of every recommendation that follows.

## 2. The Cislunar Dynamical Environment

Cislunar SSA is not a scaled-up version of near-Earth tracking; **the fundamental difference in orbital behavior has substantial implications for debris mitigation, safety, and disposal**. Three properties make the domain distinct.

### 2.1 Multi-Body, Non-Keplerian Dynamics

Dynamical modeling forms a fidelity hierarchy with the **Circular Restricted Three-Body Problem (CR3BP)** as the autonomous base, extended by the **Bicircular Restricted Four-Body Problem (BCR4BP)**, which adds solar gravity, and by ephemeris models layering additional perturbing frequencies. Solar perturbation is **a structuring force, not a negligible correction** — a CR3BP prediction propagated over days diverges from truth by exactly the solar terms it discards.

The practical inference: operational pipelines need a **model-transition layer that selects fidelity by task horizon** — BCR4BP for rapid retasking and manifold reasoning, the ephemeris tier for week-scale custody.

**Weak stability boundaries** create chaotic transfer regions where trajectories diverge exponentially, so small state uncertainty becomes large within days. The transfer corridors most valuable to monitor are therefore the hardest to predict. The resolution is doctrinal, not algorithmic: chaotic arcs demand sustained distributed revisit, and the observation budget must absorb the chaos that cannot be removed.

**Keplerian assumptions fail outright.** Gauss's classical two-body method has no valid conic in cislunar space. The literature responds with nonlinear methods: sparse-grid collocation, multi-order single-shooting, ephemeris-model IOD via nonlinear programming, and tailored element sets such as the Modified Generalized Equinoctial Orbital Elements for uncertainty propagation. Any filter ported directly from a near-Earth catalog will mis-propagate covariance and must be replaced before it can hold custody.

### 2.2 Priority Orbit Families

The custody burden concentrates on three families:

- **The 9:2 NRHO** hosts NASA's Lunar Gateway. Its resonance gives a predictable, repeating geometry that tasking can pre-plan, but custody requires both visibility *and* observability — an object can be in view yet contribute little to reducing uncertainty if the geometry is poorly conditioned.
- **L1/L2 halo and Lyapunov orbits** serve a dual role: mission destinations *and* candidate observer stations. A single libration-point observer cannot resolve both the Earth-facing volume and the **L2 dark cone**, a structural blind spot driving the multi-sensor conclusion.
- **Earth–Moon transfer corridors**, especially ballistic lunar transfers, inherit the chaotic sensitivity of weak-stability regions. The corridor cannot be covered from any single vantage; physics forces a handoff between departure-phase and approach-phase sensors.

| Orbit family | Dynamical character | Custody driver |
|---|---|---|
| 9:2 NRHO (Gateway) | Resonant, elongated | Fast near-pole arc; visibility ≠ observability |
| L1/L2 halo | Periodic, stationkept | Dual target/observer role |
| Lyapunov | Planar periodic | Corridor-aligned |
| Transfer / BLT | Chaotic, weak-stability | Covariance growth over multi-week arc |

### 2.3 Observability and Illumination Geometry

Three compounding gates have no strong near-Earth analog:

- **Long-range faintness.** Reflected flux scales roughly as the inverse fourth power of range; at ~400,000 km an object is **thousands of times fainter than at GEO**. Artemis I flight data — the Orion camera imaging the ICPS at long range — grounds the reflectance models empirically. Conventional single-frame detection cannot meet the requirement; **long integration or image stacking is a structural necessity**.
- **Solar phase angle and lunar background.** Detectability depends on Sun–Earth–Moon geometry at the observation epoch, not on the object's intrinsic brightness. A non-cooperative object can exploit this, loitering in the L2 dark cone or against the lunar limb — defeating any single-vantage optical architecture.
- **Intermittent access.** From any Earth-surface site, visibility is gated by day/night, weather, and exclusion angles; **intermittent access is the default, and persistence is an engineered exception**. A libration-point observer partly escapes this, holding a quasi-fixed position in the rotating frame so targets stay within a bounded angular region. **This access-window advantage — not merely range — is the decisive argument for space-based stationing.**

## 3. Sensing Architectures: The Modality Trade Study

### 3.1 Ground-Based Assets: The Proven but Bounded Floor

**Los Alamos demonstrated the empirical baseline.** Using 36 cm f/2.2 telescopes with CMOS detectors, the team detected CAPSTONE at magnitude **19.57 from 4.4 × 10⁵ km** and Orion at magnitude **15.53 from 3.2 × 10⁵ km** during the 2022 lunar eclipse. This is the most deployable, lowest-cost cislunar capability available — but the eclipse provided an unusually dark background, so the result does not yet generalize to the full illumination calendar.

**Ground radar hits a physics wall.** The link budget scales as the inverse fourth power of range, so the GEO-to-lunar step costs four orders of magnitude in returned energy. The most powerful planetary radars detect objects as small as ~10 m only **within one lunar distance**; at 233 MHz only the Moon yields sufficient SNR for high-resolution mapping. Ground radar is a precision characterization tool on a cued target, not a custody instrument.

**Shared duty-cycle constraints** — weather, day/night, solar/lunar exclusion — leave portions of the cislunar volume permanently or intermittently unobservable. Zimmer's conclusion is the consensus: **no single sensor or system of sensors is sufficient; a multilayered approach is necessary**. Longitude diversity mitigates diurnal gaps but the fundamental blind spots persist regardless of site count. The ground layer **passes on maturity and cost, fails on coverage**, and will serve as a validation-and-cueing tier rather than a primary custody tier.

### 3.2 Space-Based Optical, Infrared, and Patrol Observers

**The ~36 dB space-based advantage is the trade's most consequential number.** The CisLunarSense analysis derives an orbit-phase-dependent Cramér–Rao bound and quantifies a **36 dB sensing advantage** (a factor of ~4,000 in SNR) over a ground-based Ka-band reference for its Gateway debris-detection geometry. Placing the sensor inside the volume shortens range dramatically; the same fourth-power law that punishes ground radar rewards the in-volume sensor. A higher per-sensor floor lets a given coverage requirement be met with the observers positioned by the Georgia Tech p-Median formulation.

**Patrol-orbit placement delivers continuous custody.** Penn State shows that L1/L2 halo and NRHO placement yields **continuous custody of cislunar objects**, overcoming ground blind spots. L1 and L2 are complementary, not interchangeable — an L2 observer covers the far-side volume an L1 vantage misses. Operational programs are now flying this concept: **Quantum Space's Ranger Prime targets a June 2026 launch from EML-1**, and **AFRL's Oracle** operates near Lagrange points to track objects invisible to ground sensors. First operational L1 patrol data is plausible in the 2026–2027 window.

**Infrared attacks the cold-target class** that defeats optical reflectance. A two-component grey-body thermal model separates temperature and emissivity to characterize unresolved objects, and Dempster–Shafer evidential fusion detects dim infrared targets more accurately than single-feature methods. Infrared uniquely detects the self-emitting, sun-shadowed, or low-albedo target the optical and radar modalities miss — but its near-term feasibility trails optical given cryogenic focal-plane readiness challenges.

The space-based layer **wins on sensitivity and coverage; its binding constraint is maturity and cost** — the exact reverse of the ground layer's profile.

### 3.3 Passive RF, ISAC, and Lunar-Surface Nodes

- **ISAC (Integrated Sensing and Communication)** reuses the Gateway's Ka-band relay as an opportunistic radar illuminator, contributing range and Doppler observables at near-zero marginal transmitter cost. The tension is opportunism versus dependence: the sensing is nearly free but hostage to the relay's deployment schedule and duty cycle.
- **Passive RF** detects emitting spacecraft without radiating. Space-based TDOA/FDOA receivers achieve **faster filter convergence and lower state error than ground-only systems**, contributing intent-bearing signal for cooperative targets — but nothing against an adversary practicing emission control.
- **Lunar-surface and lunar-orbit nodes** deliver the strongest near-Moon custody case: a moon-based architecture maintains **100% track custody with sub-1-km position RSS error, and adding a single space-based angles-only observer cuts that error fivefold**. The fivefold gain is a geometric-diversity result — a second independent line of sight sharpens the full 3D state. The cost is the highest deployment burden and longest timeline in the trade.

The structural claim across these modalities: **no single one is unconditionally superior; the measurement-type diversity is itself the asset.**

## 4. Detecting Faint, Dim, and Non-Cooperative Targets

Detecting a non-cooperative object at lunar distance is the limiting case that governs whether any short-term task can begin. **Faint-target detection is solved not by aperture alone but by a chain linking motion-matched integration, clutter-aware discrimination, and maneuver-robust orbit determination.**

### 4.1 Image Stacking and Synthetic Tracking

**Quadratic Shift-and-Stack (QSS)** generalizes linear stacking by allowing the frame-to-frame shift to vary, following the curved apparent paths that slow cislunar motion produces over long integrations. Li et al. (2026) show QSS **significantly improves SNR** over a linear model and verify a criterion for when the quadratic term becomes necessary. The cost is an enlarged hypothesis search — more compute and more false alarms — so QSS is selected per orbit geometry, not switched on by default.

Under shot-noise-dominated conditions, **SNR grows as √N** in the number of coherently stacked frames; recovering ~2.5 magnitudes of depth requires ~100 frames. The binding constraint is coherence: field rotation, tracking residuals, and finite background coherence eventually break √N scaling, motivating a coherent block followed by incoherent combination.

A second sensitivity lever is **scheduling against the illumination cycle.** Reflected brightness swings predictably over an orbit, so a stack timed to the favorable phase exploits illumination geometry independently of integration depth — turning scheduling into a sensitivity tool. **Two telescopes of equal aperture can differ by several magnitudes in effective cislunar depth** depending on whether they run curvature-aware stacking and phase-aware scheduling.

### 4.2 Deep-Learning and Streak Detection

The discrimination problem — separating real dim signatures from lunar and stellar clutter at a tolerable false-alarm rate — is the second lever. **Dempster–Shafer feature fusion** combines weak, individually ambiguous cues (marginal intensity, point-source shape, cislunar-consistent motion) into a confidence that exceeds any single cue, recovering detections at lower per-feature SNR than intensity thresholding, with the thermal-separation feature supplying an independent axis.

False-alarm management is the binding constraint because QSS's enlarged search admits more noise peaks. **Cross-stack confirmation** suppresses false alarms geometrically (three independent stacks at 10⁻³ each drive toward 10⁻⁹) but rejects genuine faint targets that fluctuate below threshold. The resolution is to raise per-candidate confidence with feature fusion *before* confirmation, so the same false-alarm target is met with shallower confirmation and less completeness loss.

**Detection-to-track initiation** runs detection → cross-stack confirmation → angles-only IOD → track initiation. The hard step is angles-only IOD in the non-Keplerian regime, enabled by sparse-grid collocation that handles nonlinear dynamics without a good linear seed. The pipeline passes the responsiveness criterion when detection-to-initiation latency fits inside a single visibility window.

### 4.3 Maneuvering and Ambiguous Objects

**Maneuver detection versus natural uncertainty** is the central interpretive challenge, because in the chaotic CR3BP regime a predicted state diverges even with no thrust. An **adaptive Gaussian-mixture filter maintains custody through both ballistic and maneuvering phases** of an Artemis I-like trajectory: rising residuals broaden the mixture and spawn hypothesis components that absorb the unmodeled burn. Better-conditioned element sets reduce false-maneuver rates by tightening the propagated covariance.

**Maneuvers are reconstructable after the fact.** Collocation plus nonlinear programming recovers a burn from the post-maneuver arc without prior knowledge, so the limiting factor is **observational adequacy and re-acquisition latency, not the burn's concealment.** This converts a characterization problem into a tasking-and-coverage problem. Against deception, a fused optical-plus-infrared posture is materially harder to spoof than either alone, since thermal signature is less under the object's control. The most severe structural blind spot — the L2 dark cone — is closed by sensor placement, not estimation.

**Fragmentation** produces the hardest ambiguous case: many faint objects on near-identical trajectories. Probabilistic IOD represents each detection as a state distribution, separating fragments as their arcs diverge, aided by catalog priors on where break-ups are plausible. The dense-cloud limit — whether separation holds when dozens of fragments occupy overlapping uncertainty volumes — remains the open research question.

The chapter's core takeaway: **a maneuvering or deceptive object cannot permanently hide from a well-tasked architecture, but every detection capability is conditional on arc geometry and re-acquisition latency that only the tasking and placement layers supply.**

## 5. Orbit Determination, Data Association, and Fusion

Cislunar OD converts sparse, geometry-starved tracks into trustworthy state estimates. **Accuracy is won not by better sensors alone but by matching the estimator's uncertainty representation to the non-Gaussian, three-body reality.**

### 5.1 Angles-Only OD in Nonlinear Regimes

Across most of the volume, **angles-only OD is the primary mode** — only two angles per epoch, with the state reconstructed from the curvature of the angular track against known dynamics. This imposes a structural accuracy ceiling that passes only when arc length and geometric diversity are engineered into the tasking plan. A **moving observer buys range observability** through self-generated parallax that a stationary ground site cannot — the estimation-theoretic argument under the space-based preference.

**Initial orbit determination** must contend with three-body dynamics having no closed-form conic. Sparse-collocation formulations handle the non-Keplerian regime directly; collocation-plus-nonlinear-programming reconstructs maneuvers as part of the cold-start solution; single-shooting offers an alternative. The analytically important contrast: collocation distributes constraints across nodes (more seed-robust), while single-shooting iterates from one integrated trajectory (cheaper with a good seed). In a chaotic regime where nearby seeds diverge exponentially, **seed-robustness dominates method selection** as catalogs grow — an IOD pipeline requiring hand-tuned seeds per target does not scale.

### 5.2 Nonlinear Filtering and Uncertainty Propagation

The governing difficulty is that uncertainty refuses to stay Gaussian under three-body propagation. **LeGrand's Bayesian angles-only filter significantly outperforms a central-moment filter across L2 halo OD problems** — and the filter families diverge most exactly where the dynamics are most nonlinear and custody most acute. The defensible policy is regime-conditional selection, not a single default.

A complementary strand attacks **representation**: the Modified Generalized Equinoctial elements express uncertainty more benignly than Cartesian coordinates, keeping sigma points in a regime where the unscented transform stays accurate. These are composable layers — coordinate choice reduces non-Gaussianity at the source, while the mixture or Bayesian density handles what remains. The **adaptive Gaussian-mixture filter holds custody through Artemis I-like maneuvering phases** for both cooperative and non-cooperative objects.

| Method | Demonstration | Strongest where |
|---|---|---|
| Bayesian angles-only (LeGrand) | Outperforms central-moment on L2 halo | Highly nonlinear L2 halo |
| Central-moment filter | Baseline, outperformed | Near-Gaussian regimes |
| Adaptive Gaussian-mixture | Holds custody through maneuvers | Ballistic + maneuvering |
| Modified equinoctial propagation | State/uncertainty propagation | Long-horizon propagation |

### 5.3 Track Correlation, MHT, and Heterogeneous Fusion

With large, geometry-shaped covariances, **multiple tracks fall inside one another's association gates**, making correlation genuinely ambiguous. The NASA cislunar SSA Technical Steering Group names **ephemeris, interpolation/propagation, and covariance as key exchanged message fields** — covariance is first-class because without a shared realistic covariance two centers cannot compute a consistent track-to-track distance, and the interoperability criterion fails at the schema layer.

**Multi-Hypothesis Tracking (MHT)** defers ambiguous decisions by carrying parallel association hypotheses until later observations disambiguate. Because cislunar revisit cadences are long, the disambiguating observation may be hours or days away, so **cislunar MHT must carry hypotheses longer and needs more aggressive lifecycle discipline than near-Earth analogues** to bound the combinatorial explosion.

The governing fusion principle, imported from defense practice: **heterogeneous sensors must not be treated as interchangeable observations** — the most common architectural failure. The complementary failure profiles are what make fusion valuable rather than redundant. The architecture is a staged pipeline: sources/schemas → correlation and lifecycle → multi-source fusion/classification → operationalization. **Governance and schema discipline matter more than fusion-algorithm sophistication** — a standardized message set governed against silent drift is the load-bearing requirement.

| Modality | Supplies | Lacks | Fusion role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Space-based optical | Geometry, parallax | Range, intent | Primary state track |
| Ground optical | Long-arc angles | Range, persistence | Catalog maintenance |
| Radar / bistatic | Range (near-Earth) | Cislunar reach, intent | Near-Earth handoff |
| Passive RF / ISAC | Intent, identity | Location certainty | Identity/behavior |
| Infrared | Thermal signature | Fine geometry | Confirmation |

### 5.4 Custody and Uncertainty-Driven Re-Tasking

Custody depends jointly on **visibility and observability** — and can be lost in two distinct ways, the object becoming invisible or remaining visible but unobservable. **Covariance realism is the single quantity on which the short-term custody decision pivots:** an over-optimistic covariance leads the scheduler to defer a revisit, letting true uncertainty grow past the reacquisition gate, producing a loss the filter never flagged. Only filters demonstrated to maintain covariance consistency under nonlinear propagation support trustworthy custody.

The estimator generates a **prioritized demand signal** — which objects need observation, how urgently, at what geometry. **The uncertainty gradient across the catalog is the tasking scheduler's objective function.** Because cislunar windows are sparse and geometry-gated, an uncertainty-driven policy is a necessity, not an efficiency refinement — there are too few windows to spend on well-determined objects. As catalogs grow, this cueing loop must run faster than human operators can adjudicate, pointing toward autonomous closed-loop tasking.

## 6. Sensor Tasking and Short-Term Task Planning

Sensor tasking is the control layer that converts a heterogeneous inventory into timely custody, and it is the single most consequential software decision in the chain. A short-term task succeeds on three coupled decisions: *when* to look, *which* sensor on *which* target, and *how* to hand off.

### 6.1 Visibility-Window and Epoch Optimization

The visibility window is the fundamental currency. A **visibility-informed covariance analysis** scores candidate epochs by expected covariance reduction rather than raw visibility, so a brief high-parallax pass can outrank a long low-information one. Windows must be computed in **BCR4BP geometry** because optical visibility depends on illumination and solar exclusion, which the Sun-free CR3BP cannot reproduce; reflectance modeling sets the instant a target crosses limiting magnitude mid-pass.

When windows are scarcer than targets, the operational rule is to **rank tasks by the product of mission value and uncertainty-growth rate**, partitioning the catalog by revisit class. SDA change-detection requires **hours-scale revisit**; traffic management tolerates days-scale. A flat round-robin wastes scarce hours-scale windows on days-scale targets and cannot react to an unpredicted maneuver.

### 6.2 Adaptive Multi-Sensor Tasking

Three algorithmic families structure the design space:

- **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)** plans over a horizon rather than greedily, sacrificing a mediocre immediate look to preserve a high-information later window. Its cost is per-rollout dynamics propagation; pairing MCTS with a fast surrogate propagator (e.g., modified equinoctial elements) keeps replanning inside the hours-scale loop. MCTS passes on decision quality, conditionally on responsiveness.
- **Distributed tasking** is forced by structural resource limits. The resolution is a **two-timescale hybrid**: a slow central layer sets information-maximal coverage via concurrent phasing-and-tasking optimization, while a fast reactive layer retasks locally when a custody alert fires. This hybrid passes interoperability where pure centralization (too slow) and pure distribution (persistent blind spots) fail.
- **Information-theoretic objectives** score each look by expected covariance reduction. In angles-only geometry, range is recovered as parallax accumulates, so **a tasker values a geometrically diverse second look far above a redundant one**. As catalogs grow, covariance-reduction tasking is the only scalable way to spend a fixed budget; a coverage-maximizing objective would fail the accuracy criterion.

### 6.3 Cueing, Handoff, and Detection-to-Track Timelines

A cue is the **atomic custody-preserving unit**, carrying ephemeris, propagation rule, and covariance. **Handoff latency is the binding constraint**, because the handed-off error volume grows while the cue is in transit — aggravated by cislunar light-time. A well-typed cue carrying the propagation rule lets the receiver propagate the aged state to its own epoch rather than chasing a stale point. An ad hoc, pairwise scheme collapses as sensor count grows; a common message passes the interoperability criterion.

The **detection-to-track timeline** must close well inside the hours-scale revisit interval, or a maneuver can occur in the gap. Fast IOD sized to the receiving sensor's field of view keeps the chain fast end to end. The **ground-to-space handoff** is the highest-stakes transfer, existing precisely because the L2 dark cone and low lunar orbit are Earth-invisible; a cue carrying covariance lets the space observer size its search volume principally, and consistent dynamical models on both sides prevent uncaptured bias.

**Operational recommendation — a three-layer stack for hours-scale custody:** a concurrent phasing-and-tasking coverage plan underneath, an MCTS or covariance-reduction planner in the middle, and a distributed reactive retasking layer on top, all bound by the NASA ephemeris-propagation-covariance message. Custody is not a property of any one sensor but of the message discipline that binds them into a chain.

## 7. Sensor Placement and Architecture Optimization

Placement converts algorithmic advances into deliverable custody. The governing tension is between **exact methods that guarantee optimality on a linearized model and heuristics that tolerate full nonlinear dynamics without any optimality certificate.** For short-term campaigns, the question is not "what is the optimal permanent constellation" but "what is the minimal asset set that achieves the task in its time bound."

### 7.1 Exact and Heuristic Methods

The Georgia Tech **time-expanded p-Median formulation** places *p* observers into discretized slots along libration-point orbits, with time-expansion replicating decision variables across epochs to capture coverage over a full orbital period. It buys an optimality certificate at the price of linearizing coverage — a bounded, acceptable trade for a short-term campaign whose geometry is fixed and known. Sweeping *p* recovers the cost-minimization posture; the steep marginal return on the second observer (the **fivefold accuracy gain**) means the coverage-per-asset curve is highly convex at small fleet sizes, exactly the short-term regime.

**Geometric diversity, not asset count, drives OD accuracy.** A second spatially separated observer resolves the range ambiguity a single angles-only sensor cannot; the visibility-informed covariance framework is therefore a placement tool, not merely a tracking diagnostic. Coverage and accuracy objectives can conflict — a long collinear viewing geometry adds visibility without adding diversity — so an optimizer scoring only coverage systematically under-serves accuracy.

Where linearization is too lossy, **heuristics take over.** The AFIT genetic algorithm scores candidate architectures against the full nonlinear observability landscape, forgoing the certificate for fidelity. **Nested evolutionary/MILP** schemes dissolve the binary — an outer evolutionary loop proposes configurations, an inner MILP scores each exactly — and **multi-objective MCTS** offers an anytime property well-suited to time-bounded campaigns. No head-to-head benchmark on a common instance exists, so the defensible recommendation is a **division of labor: heuristic search for exploration, exact methods for certification.**

| Method | Optimality | Nonlinear objective | Anytime |
|---|---|---|---|
| p-Median MILP | Certified | No (linearized) | No |
| Genetic algorithm | Near-optimal | Yes | Partial |
| Multi-objective MCTS | Near-optimal | Yes | Yes |
| Nested evol./MILP | Inner certified | Outer yes | Partial |

### 7.2 Right-Sizing for Short-Term Campaigns

A short-term task needs custody of a known target over a bounded interval, not continuous all-region custody — and that relaxation changes which architecture is cost-effective. The Raytheon result (100% custody, sub-1-km error, fivefold gain from one added observer) and the AFIT passive-RF result (existing SSN sites plus **one GEO satellite yielding nearly an order-of-magnitude accuracy gain**) agree in direction: **a single added space node delivers an order-of-magnitude-class improvement** over a ground-only baseline.

The central tension — leverage existing Earth-orbit assets (available now, poorly matched to geometry) versus build dedicated cislunar infrastructure (well-matched, nonexistent) — **resolves along a timeline, not a verdict.** For near-term execution, leveraging existing assets plus one incremental space node is the only feasible posture; dedicated infrastructure is reserved for the standing architecture Artemis-era traffic will justify.

A **reconfigurable distributed pool** generalizes the minimal-asset finding: each short-term task draws the minimal geometrically-and-modally diverse subset it needs. The interoperability substrate is already emerging — the NASA message-field definitions satisfy the integration criterion at the standards layer before the hardware layer, and source-and-schema discipline is the binding constraint.

## 8. Effectiveness Metrics, Validation, and Constraints

**Cislunar effectiveness cannot be scored on a single aggregate; it requires a vector spanning detection, custody, accuracy, and timeliness, validated under sparse-observation stress.**

### 8.1 Performance Metrics

Three families matter. **Detection probability paired with a false-alarm ceiling** — the operationally meaningful figure is P_d at the maximum FAR the correlation stage can tolerate before false tracks swamp adjudication. **Custody fraction** (proportion of the horizon held in continuous custody) is often the single most decisive short-term metric. **Covariance realism** — whether reported covariance actually brackets true error — most often separates a trustworthy track from a dangerously overconfident one, and the NASA message set already standardizes covariance as an exchanged field. Timeliness is bounded by downlink: where a satellite generates ~two orders of magnitude more data than it can downlink per orbit, on-board triage delays observations that would have tightened the covariance.

**Catalog completeness and task-specific metrics are orthogonal.** An architecture could report 90% NRHO completeness yet fail a specific task against a newly maneuvering object. The resolution is a **two-tier scorecard** — a persistent tier (completeness, steady-state P_d/FAR) and a task tier (custody fraction, covariance realism, timeliness).

| Metric | Tier | Rubric criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Detection probability P_d | Both | Coverage |
| False alarm rate | Both | Robustness |
| Custody fraction | Task | Responsiveness |
| Covariance realism | Both | Accuracy |
| Revisit latency | Task | Responsiveness |
| Catalog completeness | Persistent | Coverage |

### 8.2 Validation Under Sparse-Observation Stress

The cislunar operating point lives in the sparse tail, so validation must starve the pipeline of measurements and ask not whether accuracy degrades but **whether covariance degrades honestly** — a sparse-arc covariance-consistency test. A defensible program demonstrates the normalized estimation error stays within chi-squared bounds across roughly **three to six short angles-only arcs per multi-day horizon**, with process noise tuned to CR3BP/BCR4BP dynamics rather than a two-body fallback.

Validation proceeds on a three-rung ladder, each covering the others' blind spot: **simulation for breadth, hardware-in-loop testbed for detector physics, on-orbit/on-sky for ground truth.** The Sun et al. (2026) on-sky image-stacking survey is a concrete existence proof that faint cislunar detection can be exercised on real sky. Robustness must be scored as the **worst-case** metric value across the illumination and geometry sweep — an architecture validated only at favorable illumination fails, because that is precisely the phase an adversary would exploit.

### 8.3 Operational Constraints and Mitigations

Cislunar constraints are a coupled system, not independent nuisances. **Exclusion angles** (22°/35° lunar, 70°–110° solar) carve time-varying blind cones; **coverage gaps** are the most intractable because they are fixed by where observers physically sit. The **downlink bottleneck converts a communications constraint into an accuracy constraint**, and **sensor saturation** drives false correlations that corrupt the common operating picture and erode operator trust until the system is abandoned within months.

Four mitigation levers deploy as a coordinated set: **sensor diversity** (complementary failure modes let one modality cover another's blind spot), **adaptive/uncertainty-aware tasking** (steers scarce looks to fastest-growing covariance), **redundancy** (no single exclusion cone drops a target), and **uncertainty-aware planning**.

| Constraint | Metric impacted | Primary mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage gaps | Custody fraction | Placement + redundancy |
| Dim targets | P_d | Image stacking |
| Maneuver uncertainty | Covariance realism | Adaptive tasking |
| Exclusion angles | Custody fraction | Geometric diversity |
| Comms latency | Timeliness | On-board processing |
| False correlations | Covariance realism, trust | Source-schema discipline |

### 8.4 Data Infrastructure and Coordination

The ingestion layer normalizes heterogeneous observations onto a common time and coordinate frame — **time synchronization is non-negotiable**, since an angles-only arc mistimed by milliseconds maps to a large position error at cislunar range. Carrying covariance and provenance metadata as first-class fields is what makes downstream covariance-realism scoring possible. Automation follows the four-phase defense fusion pipeline, with **graduated releasability** resolving the civil-commercial-military tension: a track is fused internally at full fidelity and released externally at reduced fidelity, protecting sensitive methods while still contributing position and covariance.

## 9. Integrated Framework and Maturity Stratification

**Cislunar SSA is a closed-loop control problem, not a pipeline of independent stages.**

### 9.1 The Coupling

The difficulties compound along a directed chain: **long range → faintness → sparse arcs → rapid uncertainty growth → custody loss**. Naming the intervening links is what distinguishes the cislunar case from near-Earth, where dense radar short-circuits the chain at its second link.

This chain is circular in operation. **Tasking → detection → orbit determination → next tasking** forms a feedback loop that is virtuous on one side (each detection tightens covariance, shrinking the next search box) and vicious on the other (a miss inflates covariance, growing the box, lowering the next detection probability). A tasker greedily maximizing immediate detection can drive the loop toward its unstable side; **the resolution is to task for covariance control across the full horizon.** The highest-leverage interventions act on the loop's gain — **reachability pruning** (a Poincaré-map method shrinks the ground-based SDA search domain by up to **85.3%, from 4π to 0.587π steradian**) and **observer-geometry selection**.

### 9.2 Maturity Tiers

The capability set is **bimodal, split by a hard launch discontinuity:**

| Tier | Capability | State |
|---|---|---|
| Mature | Ground optical + image stacking | Demonstrated |
| Mature | EKF/UKF OD + IOD | Methods inherited |
| Partial | Space-based optical observers | Simulation-demonstrated |
| Partial | Libration-orbit placement | Optimization mature, unflown |
| Open | ISAC sensing | Analysis-stage CRLB |
| Open | Fully autonomous tasking | Formulation-stage LP |

The near-term-feasible set needs **only software on existing assets** (image stacking, reachability pruning, the angles-only/IOD stack). The research-stage set is **gated by unflown hardware** — the ground path advances by writing algorithms, the space path only by executing a spacecraft program. **The near-Earth analogy holds for the estimation and mission layers but breaks on dynamics, sensor geometry, and hidden volumes** — telling the architect to spend on geometry- and dynamics-aware tasking, not on re-inventing the filter stack.

### 9.3 The Short-Term Verdict

A **ground-optical network, tasked by a reachability-pruned scheduler and an angles-only filter, delivers short-term custody for bright, well-placed targets today** — all three ingredients are demonstrated and none requires a launch. It passes responsiveness for in-window targets and maturity decisively, but only partially passes coverage (the L2 dark cone and low lunar orbit lie beyond reach).

The near-term ceiling is set by **geometry-limited single-vantage coverage and the faint-target floor of ground optics**, not by the algorithms — constraints only the launch-gated space and open tiers can lift. Demand is outrunning supply: the market triples to $8.6 billion by 2034, ESA notes dozens of missions building permanent infrastructure, lunar traffic management is judged an immediate need, and the U.S. Space Force has stood up a dedicated cislunar office. One programmatic risk: NASA's redirection of ~$20 billion over seven years from Gateway to a lunar base shows the orbital infrastructure space-based concepts assume as host can shift — a planning risk the mature ground path, depending on no single program, avoids.

### 10.2 The Recommended Architecture and Stack

**A three-layer hybrid:** a ground layer (existing optical and radar) for the near-Earth leg, an Earth-orbit wide-field layer for mid-volume handoff, and a space layer of patrol optical observers on libration-point orbits for the lunar-vicinity volume. On top sits the **decisive software stack**, where most near-term gain is available without new launches:

- **OD:** angles-only IOD via sparse-grid collocation, MGEOE state/uncertainty propagation, nonlinear-programming IOD for maneuvering targets, probabilistic Gauss-method IOD for sparse arcs.
- **Tasking:** concurrent phasing-and-tasking optimization via the LP co-design formulation, narrowed by Poincaré-map reachability pruning (**up to 85.3% search-area reduction**).

### 10.3 Phased Implementation Roadmap

**Phase 1 (~12 months) — Existing ground and Earth-orbit assets for immediate custody.** Extract maximum value from installed infrastructure by adding software, not hardware: reachability pruning (85.3% search-area reduction) and image-stacking detection. Teide Observatory's TTT3 already imaged the Artemis II outbound trajectory. Establish the passive-RF baseline on existing SSN sites — deliberately under-powered, sized against the Phase 2 satellite geometry it will pair with. *Verdict:* maintains near-Earth-leg custody but cannot hold an hours-cadence track through the full transfer.

**Phase 2 — Space-based patrol observers and adaptive tasking.** Deploy patrol optical observers on libration-point orbits positioned by the p-Median formulation, driven by concurrent phasing-and-tasking optimization to shorten revisit toward the hours-cadence SDA demands. This phase is already underway: **AFRL Oracle** and **Quantum Space's Ranger Prime (June 2026 launch from EML-1)**. Adding the single space node unlocks the Phase 1 RF baseline's order-of-magnitude latent accuracy, and begins cross-platform space-based OD. *Verdict:* extends coverage into the lunar-vicinity volume and begins closing the L2 dark-cone blind spot.

**Phase 3 — ISAC, autonomy, and data-sharing standards.** Mature the integration criterion the hybrid scored only 7.00 on. **CisLunarSense's 36 dB advantage and velocity-adaptive processor detect Gateway-operational debris within 700 km with over 30 minutes of warning** — enough to cue a conjunction response. Onboard autonomy escapes the cislunar light-time ground loop, and a shared data standard plus common covariance representation turns fielded sensors into a single fused system. The economics now favor completion: SSA spending reaches $61 billion by 2034, the Space Force raised its Andromeda ceiling past $6.2 billion, and Observable Space raised $90 million while winning a Space Force optical contract.

## Conclusion

Comprehensive and accurate cislunar SSA supporting short-term tracking is achievable, but only as a **governed composition rather than a single sensor or algorithm.** The dynamics make the domain categorically harder than near-Earth tracking; the observability gates make any single vantage structurally blind; the closed-loop coupling means the highest-leverage interventions act on geometry and pruning, not on any one estimation stage.

The ranked verdict is robust: **the hybrid distributed multi-modal architecture leads decisively, anchored by space-based optical custody at libration points and bound by a schema-governed fusion and cueing discipline.** Short-term custody for bright, well-placed targets is deliverable today with mature ground assets and reachability-pruned, covariance-driven tasking; the binding near-term constraint is geometric coverage and faint-target sensitivity, which only the launch-gated space tiers can lift — on a timeline that surging demand is already outrunning.
