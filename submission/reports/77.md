# The Role of Need for Cognitive Closure in Misinformation Acceptance: Mechanisms, Evidence, and Boundary Conditions

Need for cognitive closure shapes misinformation acceptance not as a uniform amplifier but as a **conditional motivational lever** whose direction reverses depending on what answer is locally available, what source is trusted, and how threatened the perceiver feels. A 2024 meta-analysis aggregating 31 studies and 256,337 participants finds a positive but moderate association between closure motivation and misinformation susceptibility—real but bounded, accounting for far less variance than partisanship or analytic ability in most paradigms.

The published record is genuinely mixed. Despite the common claim—advanced most visibly by Marchlewska and colleagues (2017)—that high closure reliably breeds conspiracy and false-belief endorsement, **the De keersmaecker et al. (2020) seven-study illusory-truth program (combined N=2,196) found no moderation whatsoever**, and the Boot et al. (2021) COVID longitudinal data found closure associated with *better* knowledge and conspiracy *rejection*. This report reconciles that contradiction by treating closure as a process that seizes whatever explanation is salient and freezes it, so that its informational consequences hinge on the epistemic environment. The operative question shifts from *whether* closure raises acceptance to *when*, *for whom*, and *under which informational defaults* it does so.

---

# 1. Framing the Question

**Need for cognitive closure (NFC)** is defined across the measurement literature as a preference for clarity, order, structure, and a desire for firm, stable knowledge["Need for Cognitive Closure Modulates How Perceptual Decisions Are Affected by Task Diffic](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0146002). It is one of the most frequently invoked individual-difference explanations for why some people accept misinformation that others reject.

The central tension this report resolves is empirical. The De keersmaecker program found no evidence that NFC moderates the illusory truth effect across 2,196 participants[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844), while a COVID-19 credibility study (n=265) found high-NFC individuals measurably less aligned with experts when judging pandemic tweets["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820). From this divergence, any defensible verdict must be **conditional rather than categorical**.

## Scope: Acceptance, Not the Whole Phenomenon

The report targets a narrow link: the association between NFC and **misinformation acceptance**—belief endorsement, perceived accuracy, and continued influence. This deliberately excludes three neighboring outcomes that obey different psychological rules:

- **Exposure** is mere encounter, which carries its own belief consequence: prior exposure increases the perceived accuracy of fake news[Pennycook, G., Cannon, T. D., & Rand, D. G. 2018, "Prior exposure increases perceived accu](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279465/). Repetition raises perceived accuracy for everyone, so the question for NFC is whether it *amplifies* that universal effect.
- **Sharing** is a behavioral outcome partly independent of belief. The accuracy-attention work in *Nature* showed shifting attention to accuracy reduces sharing[Pennycook, G., et al. 2021, "Shifting attention to accuracy can reduce misinformation onli](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03344-2)—but a person may circulate a claim they have not evaluated, so sharing evidence is not belief evidence.
- **Production** lies outside scope entirely, concerning the motivations of a small set of actors rather than the credence of the general population.

The report admits sharing studies only when they also report a credence or perceived-accuracy measure, accepting a smaller evidence base in exchange for outcome validity.

## The Evidentiary Standard

Because the field's central liability is that NFC travels with its correlates, this report weights causal and mechanistic evidence above raw significance. The COVID-19 study found that **political conservatism was also associated with reduced expert alignment**["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820)—the trait and the ideology are confounded, so the bivariate NFC association cannot be read as closure-specific without statistical separation. Throughout, every reported effect is flagged as correlational or causal, and as replicated or not.

This discipline matters because a well-powered correlational study—however large—cannot on its own establish that NFC *causes* acceptance. The cross-sectional COVID-19 association establishes association, not causation["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820); the illusory-truth program's null, drawn from seven studies with an experimental repetition manipulation, has a stronger causal footing on the moderation question[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844). The two answer different causal questions at different levels of rigor.

## Core Vocabulary

**Seizing** is the rapid uptake of early-available information—the mind grasping at the first plausible answer to end the discomfort of not-knowing—governed by the **urgency tendency** (the press to reach any answer quickly).

**Freezing** is the premature crystallization of that answer and subsequent resistance to revising it, governed by the **permanence tendency** (the press to maintain closure once attained).

These two phases imply opposite intervention windows: **pre-exposure prebunking for seizing, post-exposure debunking for freezing.** Three related processes name what closure may exploit:

- **Illusory truth effect**: the increase in perceived accuracy following repetition[Pennycook, G., Cannon, T. D., & Rand, D. G. 2018, "Prior exposure increases perceived accu](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279465/)—the baseline mechanism against which the De keersmaecker program tested and failed to find NFC moderation.
- **Motivated reasoning**: biased processing in service of a preferred conclusion.
- **Belief perseverance**: the survival of a belief after its evidential basis is discredited—the conceptual cousin of freezing.

## Discrimination Ability vs. Response Bias

The report's single most consequential analytic distinction:

- **Discrimination ability** is the capacity to tell true from false—the gap between credence assigned to accurate versus inaccurate items.
- **Response bias** is a blanket tendency to rate claims as true (or false) regardless of accuracy.

A trait can affect one without the other. When the COVID-19 study reports high-NFC respondents are less aligned with experts["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820), the misalignment could reflect a genuine **discrimination deficit** or merely a **response bias** toward rating confident claims as true—and the two interpretations imply entirely different countermeasures. A finding framed as "elevated acceptance" is ambiguous until resolved into a discrimination story or a bias story.

# 2. Defining Need for Cognitive Closure

NFC is a **motivational construct, not a cognitive deficit**: it names the degree to which a person is driven to reach and hold a definite answer rather than tolerate confusion. The construct's content-indifference is precisely what makes it relevant to misinformation.

## The Canonical Definition

Kruglanski's 2004 monograph defines NFC as "the individual's desire for a firm answer to a question, any firm answer, as opposed to confusion and/or ambiguity"[Kruglanski, A. W. 2004, "The Psychology of Closed Mindedness," Psychology Press.](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2004-21682-000). **The load-bearing phrase is "any firm answer":** the motivation is indifferent to veridicality. If the definition read "correct answer," high NFC would predict greater accuracy and the misinformation question would be moot. It is the content-blindness that opens the door to false claims—provided they arrive with the structural property the motivation craves: firmness.

Webster and Kruglanski (1994) established that NFC is a **motivational continuum**, not a binary type, running from high need for closure to a need to *avoid* closure[Webster, D. M., & Kruglanski, A. W. 1994, "Individual Differences in Need for Cognitive Cl](https://psycnet.apa.org/doiLanding?doi=10.1037/0022-3514.67.6.1049). This dictates that NFC should be modeled as a continuous predictor, preserving the full range of individual variation.

## Dimensional Structure

The Need for Closure Scale operationalizes the construct through **five facets**: desire for predictability, preference for order and structure, discomfort with ambiguity, decisiveness, and closed-mindedness[Webster, D. M., & Kruglanski, A. W. 1994, "Individual Differences in Need for Cognitive Cl](https://psycnet.apa.org/doiLanding?doi=10.1037/0022-3514.67.6.1049). These are conceptually distinct enough that a person can score high on some and low on others—so total-score analyses can obscure facet-level relationships that drive misinformation outcomes. The predictability, order, and ambiguity-discomfort facets drive **seizing**; the closed-mindedness facet drives **freezing**. Decisiveness sits awkwardly between—and, as shown below, is the facet contaminated by ability.

Beneath the facets, Kruglanski and Webster (1996) identified two temporal tendencies: **"the urgency tendency represents an individual's inclination to attain closure as soon as possible, and the permanence tendency represents an individual's inclination to maintain it for as long as possible"**[Kruglanski, A. W., & Webster, D. M. 1996, "Motivated Closing of the Mind: 'Seizing' and 'F](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/Psyc590Readings/Kruglanski1996.pdf). This split is the chapter's most consequential tool:

- **Urgency → seizing**: rapid uptake of early-available information biases belief toward first-encountered claims.
- **Permanence → freezing**: resistance to subsequent information produces belief perseverance even when corrections arrive.

The two compound: urgency determines that a false claim is adopted, permanence that it persists. This combination is the worst-case profile for misinformation resistance, and it distinguishes NFC from impulsivity (speed without subsequent rigidity).

| Sub-drive | Temporal locus | Processing stage | Misinformation consequence | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Urgency | Adoption | Seizing | Adoption of early-arriving false claim | Accuracy prompt *before* judgment |
| Permanence | Maintenance | Freezing | Resistance to correction; belief perseverance | Retraction must overcome crystallization |

## Trait vs. Situationally-Induced Closure

NFC exists in two distinct forms, and the distinction governs causal interpretation:

- **Trait/chronic NFC** is measured by the dispositional NFCS as a stable individual difference[Webster, D. M., & Kruglanski, A. W. 1994, "Individual Differences in Need for Cognitive Cl](https://psycnet.apa.org/doiLanding?doi=10.1037/0022-3514.67.6.1049). Most misinformation evidence uses this form, meaning the dominant design is **correlational** and cannot establish causation. Both the illusory-truth program[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844) and the COVID-19 tweet study["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820) used dispositional measures.
- **Situationally-induced NFC** is the state form, raised experimentally through time pressure, ambient noise, fatigue, or mortality salience—administered by **random assignment**, the property the dispositional measure lacks.

A dispositional finding that high-NFC individuals accept more misinformation is compatible with three causal stories: reverse causation (accepting firm false claims reinforces a preference for firm answers), a third-variable confound (ideology drives both), or the hypothesized forward pathway. The third-variable worry is not hypothetical: the COVID-19 study found both high NFC and conservative leaning independently associated with lower expert alignment["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820). Random assignment breaks this confound—which is why the situational form is causally informative where the dispositional form is not. **The misinformation literature has leaned heavily on the dispositional form, so the causal verdict rests on a thin experimental base.**

| Property | Trait/chronic NFC | Situational NFC |
|---|---|---|
| Measurement | Dispositional NFCS | Manipulation (time pressure, noise, fatigue) |
| Assignment | Self-selected | Random |
| Dominant design | Correlational | Experimental |
| Confound vulnerability | High (ideology, reverse causation) | Low |
| Causal license | Association only | Causal, in principle |
| Use in misinformation corpus | Dominant | Sparse |

## The Ability/Motivation Confound

Roets and Van Hiel (2007) identified and corrected a structural flaw: the original decisiveness facet contained items tapping the *ability* to produce quick answers rather than the *desire* for them. They developed six revised items defining the facet as "the urgent desire for quick, unambiguous answers"[Roets, A., & Van Hiel, A. 2007, "Separating Ability From Need," PSPB 332.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167206294744). The confound matters because cognitive fluency tends to predict *better* discrimination of true from false—so the contaminated scale mixes two subpopulations with opposite expected relationships to the outcome, attenuating and destabilizing the observed correlation.

This creates a comparability problem. The original Webster-Kruglanski NFCS, the Roets-Van Hiel revision, and various short forms are not interchangeable, because short forms weighted toward some facets measure a differently weighted version of the construct. The report's recommendation: **any credible synthesis should code scale version as a study-level moderator.** Some apparent inconsistency in the literature—including the contrast between the null illusory-truth result and the positive COVID association—may resolve into instrument-driven heterogeneity rather than substantive contradiction. This is falsifiable: if future syntheses code scale version and find it accounts for little variance, the inconsistency is substantive.

# 3. Defining and Measuring Misinformation Acceptance

Until "acceptance" is disaggregated, any claim about NFC's role is under-identified. The outcome is **a family of outcomes** tapped through overlapping but non-identical indicators.

## Facets of Acceptance

Operationalized as **belief endorsement or perceived accuracy of false information, typically through truth-rating tasks**[Authors 2026, "Prior beliefs & automated fact checking," PLOS ONE.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0342332), acceptance contains separable facets that form a rough ordering from transient to entrenched:

- **Perceived accuracy** and **belief endorsement** sit at the exposure moment.
- **Internalization** reflects integration into a wider belief system over time (conspiracy-endorsement scaling comes closest to tapping this).
- **Resistance to correction**—indexed by post-correction endorsement[Authors 2026, "Prior beliefs & automated fact checking," PLOS ONE.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0342332)—reflects the stability of belief against contradictory input.

The ordering matters because **seizing loads on the exposure-moment facets while freezing loads on resistance to correction.** A study measuring only perceived accuracy taps a different NFC pathway than one measuring post-correction endorsement.

Acceptance must be separated from **recall** (a memory outcome that can be high while acceptance is zero), **sharing** (governed partly by partisan signaling and novelty), and **production**. The accuracy-nudge research built its intervention on exactly the belief/sharing gap[Pennycook, G., et al. 2021, "Shifting attention to accuracy can reduce misinformation onli](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03344-2). Because NFC's sub-processes describe how a person *arrives at and holds* a belief, a study using sharing as its proxy risks attributing to NFC a null that actually reflects the sharing decision's independence from belief.

## Operational Paradigms

Three paradigms dominate, and **each instantiates a different region of the facet space, with different sensitivity to NFC:**

**Headline accuracy-rating** presents true and false headlines for accuracy judgments[Pennycook, G., et al. 2021, "Shifting attention to accuracy can reduce misinformation onli](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03344-2). Its central property is that the true/false mix permits, in principle, a signal-detection decomposition into discrimination ability and response bias. But because most applications report the false-headline rating alone, the mechanism behind any NFC effect is left under-identified—a high-NFC respondent might rate false headlines as accurate because they seize on the first plausible reading (discrimination deficit) or because they have a general bias toward treating assertions as true.

**Illusory truth repetition** measures whether repeated claims are rated more accurate than novel ones, isolating processing fluency[Pennycook, G., Cannon, T. D., & Rand, D. G. 2018, "Prior exposure increases perceived accu](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279465/). This is where the clearest NFC test was run: De keersmaecker and colleagues found **every study showed a significant illusory truth effect, but none showed moderation by NFC** across N=2,196[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844). The fluency-only paradigm washes out the NFC signal—a contrarian result, since one might expect closure-craving individuals to be especially susceptible to repetition-driven familiarity.

**Conspiracy-endorsement scaling** taps a more stable, worldview-level acceptance behaving like a trait—the internalization facet where **freezing** is most likely implicated. This is the theoretical place to look for NFC signal, though the head-to-head comparison across paradigms within a single sample has not been run.

| Paradigm | Facet tapped | Mechanism isolated | NFC sensitivity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline accuracy-rating | Perceived accuracy | Seizing (if decomposed) | Under-identified in raw form[Pennycook, G., et al. 2021, "Shifting attention to accuracy can reduce misinformation onli](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03344-2) |
| Illusory truth repetition | Accuracy via fluency | Processing fluency | **Null** — no moderation, N=2,196[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844) |
| Conspiracy-endorsement | Internalization | Freezing (predicted) | Higher — predicted, untested |

## Distinctions That Do and Do Not Matter

The **misinformation/disinformation distinction** (intent to deceive) is foundational for policy but **irrelevant to acceptance measurement**: the WHO defines them by sender intent[World Health Organization 2024, "Q&A on Disinformation and public health."](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/disinformation-and-public-health), but the recipient cannot observe intent, so the seizing and freezing processes proceed identically. The productive distinction is not sender intent but the **dispositional/situational axis of NFC itself**: rumor and crisis-information contexts are ambiguity-rich by definition and therefore the natural setting for studying situationally-induced closure—the field's richest untested opportunity.

## Acceptance Across Domains

Domains vary in the **commitment level** they engage, which structures where NFC shows an effect:

- **Health/COVID** supplies the most direct dispositional evidence: high-NFC respondents and conservatives were less aligned with experts on COVID-19 tweets (n=265)["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820). A companion finding—the most effective detectors of health disinformation had either high need for cognition or liberal affiliation["Political affiliation or need for cognition? It depends on the post," PLOS One.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0315259)—shows the two dispositions point in **opposite directions on the same outcome**.
- **Climate** supplies worldview-level placement[Orr, M., Stewart, A., & Grundstein, A. 2020, "Investigating Connections between Need for C](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/17/15/5619)[Ejaz, W., Altay, S., Fletcher, R., & Nielsen, R. K. 2024, "Trust Is Key: Determinants of F](http://users.ox.ac.uk/~polf0572/publication/trust-is-key/), though causal direction is open; trust emerges as the key determinant of false climate beliefs across eight countries[Ejaz, W., Altay, S., Fletcher, R., & Nielsen, R. K. 2024, "Trust Is Key: Determinants of F](http://users.ox.ac.uk/~polf0572/publication/trust-is-key/).
- **Crisis-rumor** supplies the untested situational-induction opportunity.
- **Eyewitness memory** supplies a memory-based analogue: incorporation of false post-event detail isolates the freezing-relevant resistance-to-correction facet.

**Because the facet, paradigm, and domain jointly determine whether an NFC signal can appear, the mixed empirical record is not a contradiction to explain away but a predictable consequence of measurement heterogeneity**—fluency-only designs null the signal[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844), raw-accuracy designs blur it, worldview-level designs sharpen it.

# 4. Theoretical Mechanisms: Seizing, Freezing, and the Closure Cascade

## Seizing: Urgency and Truncated Search

The defining feature of seizing is **indifference to the content of the answer relative to the speed of obtaining one**[Kruglanski, A. W., & Webster, D. M. 1996, "Motivated Closing of the Mind: 'Seizing' and 'F](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/Psyc590Readings/Kruglanski1996.pdf). The high-NFC person is not incapable of evaluating evidence; the motivation reprioritizes the goal so that resolution outranks verification. High NFC is associated with simplified and accelerated processing[Strojny et al. 2016, R Psychol.](https://ojs.tnkul.pl/index.php/rpsych/article/download/615/625/).

The urgency tendency does its damage through truncated hypothesis testing: high NFC raises the cost of remaining unresolved → shortened search through possible explanations → fewer competing hypotheses held in mind → acceptance of the first claim clearing a low plausibility threshold[Strojny et al. 2016, R Psychol.](https://ojs.tnkul.pl/index.php/rpsych/article/download/615/625/). **The load-bearing insight is that acceptance errors arise not because the accepted claim beat its rivals, but because the rivals were never generated.** When a fabricated explanation is the early-available one, the truncated search removes the safeguard of alternative-generation.

## Freezing: Permanence and Resistance to Updating

Freezing is an active motivational posture: information consistent with the held answer is welcomed; information that would reopen the question is treated as a threat. Kruglanski and colleagues (1993) showed high-NFC individuals were **more resistant to persuasion** by their low-NFC counterparts than vice versa[Kruglanski et al. 1993, "Motivated resistance and openness to persuasion," JPSP.](https://scispace.com/papers/motivated-resistance-and-openness-to-persuasion-in-the-2gveqaqe9s)—they are *differentially* hard to move once a position is in place.

The route runs: high NFC → resistance to new information → belief perseverance → resistance to correction of accepted misinformation[Kruglanski et al. 1993, "Motivated resistance and openness to persuasion," JPSP.](https://scispace.com/papers/motivated-resistance-and-openness-to-persuasion-in-the-2gveqaqe9s). **The same trait that made the misinformation easy to accept makes it hard to retract**—freezing double-counts against the reasoner.

This resolves an apparent paradox: why would closure-seekers reject the closure a correction offers? Because a correction first demands surrender of the existing answer, reopening the question in the interim. The permanence tendency assigns a high price to that momentary reopening—favoring corrections that **supply a replacement answer** rather than merely negating the old one. (Note: Kruglanski et al. tested resistance to *persuasion* generally; the extension to *correction of accepted misinformation* is inference, not direct test.)

## Heuristic Processing and Cue Reliance

Closure motivation shifts processing toward fast heuristic shortcuts—but conditionally. Kossowska and Bar-Tal (2013) found NFC related to heuristic processing **only when participants believed the shortcut would deliver closure**[Kossowska & Bar-Tal 2013, British Journal of Psychology.](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjop.12001). This is a *motivated* rather than *lazy* deployment, with a counterintuitive implication: raising stakes may *intensify* urgency and thereby heuristic reliance.

Three heuristics form the attack surface misinformation exploits:

- **Consensus**: Yousif and colleagues (2019) found people insensitive to consensus *quality*—equally confident in conclusions from a "true" and a "false" consensus[Yousif et al. 2019, Psychological Science.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797619856844). The heuristic reads the *signal* of agreement, not its validity, so manufactured agreement receives the same evidentiary weight as genuine.
- **Fluency**: prior exposure increases perceived accuracy of fake news[Pennycook, G., Cannon, T. D., & Rand, D. G. 2018, "Prior exposure increases perceived accu](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279465/); ease of reprocessing is misread as truth.
- **Source**: perceived credibility substitutes for evaluation, exploited by mimicking authoritative formats.

Across all three, the heuristic reads a **surface feature that can be simulated independently of truth**. As manufactured agreement becomes cheaper to stage at scale, the consensus vulnerability grows most consequential.

The behavioral signature confirms this: high-NFC respondents showed reduced **discrimination ability** (alignment with experts), not merely a response bias toward believing["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820); and high need for cognition—NFC's motivational mirror—predicts *better* detection["Political affiliation or need for cognition? It depends on the post," PLOS One.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0315259). Convergence across COVID-19, health, and climate domains elevates the detection-asymmetry from curiosity to reproducible signature.

## Preference for Simple, Certainty-Restoring Explanations

Marchlewska and colleagues (2017) found NFC positively predicted conspiracy endorsement, **particularly when conspiratorial explanations were salient for events lacking clear official explanations**[Marchlewska et al. 2017, European Journal of Social Psychology.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.2308). The mechanism: conspiracy theories "offer simple answers to complex problems." The conditional is crucial—the effect is triggered by the very ambiguity closure is built to resolve.

This grounds a **targeted** claim: NFC raises susceptibility to simple, certainty-restoring false explanations *when ambiguity is high*—not a blanket claim that NFC raises acceptance of all misinformation. An accurate account of a complex event names multiple causes, assigns uncertain weights, and resists a single verdict; a conspiratorial account names one hidden cause and delivers finality. For a reasoner whose goal is to dispel not-knowing, **the false claim wins the closure competition even when it loses the truth competition**—but only for the subset of false claims that are *simpler* than the accurate alternative. Where the accurate account is the simpler one, the closure mechanism should *favor* truth.

This is the **double-edged property of freezing**: the permanence tendency entrenches whatever answer was seized, so NFC predicts entrenchment of the *prevailing* belief in a reasoner's environment, which may be accurate or inaccurate. The sign of the NFC-accuracy relationship flips with the accuracy of the locally dominant narrative.

## The Integrated Cascade

The full chain proceeds through six stages, urgency driving the front half and permanence the back:

```
Ambiguity/threat → NFC activation → Seizing (truncated search)
→ Heuristic acceptance → Freezing (maintain answer) → Entrenchment (resist correction)
```

**Grading the links by empirical support:**

- **Best-supported**: the NFC→heuristic-reliance step, with two independent lines converging (Kossowska & Bar-Tal[Kossowska & Bar-Tal 2013, British Journal of Psychology.](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjop.12001); Yousif et al.[Yousif et al. 2019, Psychological Science.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797619856844)).
- **Well-supported in the abstract**: freezing→resistance, though the evidence tests persuasion generally rather than misinformation correction specifically[Kruglanski et al. 1993, "Motivated resistance and openness to persuasion," JPSP.](https://scispace.com/papers/motivated-resistance-and-openness-to-persuasion-in-the-2gveqaqe9s).
- **Solid**: seizing→truncation[Strojny et al. 2016, R Psychol.](https://ojs.tnkul.pl/index.php/rpsych/article/download/615/625/), though the final step to "first plausible claim accepted" is more often modeled than measured.
- **The most consequential disconfirmation**: the De keersmaecker null[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844). The heuristic-acceptance link does *not* hold for the fluency-based illusory truth effect, even though the effect robustly exists. The reconciliation: illusory truth runs through fluency mechanisms that operate *below* the level closure influences—repetition raises perceived accuracy for everyone because misattributing ease to truth is a general cognitive process, not a motivated one. **NFC moderates the motivated uptake of a coherent account, not the fluency that drives repetition-based belief.**

# 5. Distinguishing NFC From Neighboring Constructs

NFC's relationship to misinformation cannot be collapsed into its neighbors. A trait that merely correlates with NFC and predicts the same outcome through the same mechanism buys no distinctness; one that predicts a *different facet* or through a *different pathway* earns its place as a separate vulnerability channel.

## NFC vs. Need for Cognition and Analytic Thinking

These point in **opposite motivational directions**: need for cognition is the enjoyment of effortful elaboration; NFC is the desire for a firm answer that *ends* elaboration[Kruglanski, A. W., & Webster, D. M. 1996, "Motivated Closing of the Mind: 'Seizing' and 'F](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/Psyc590Readings/Kruglanski1996.pdf). They predict different halves of the misinformation problem:

- **Need for cognition maps onto detection (discrimination ability)**: the most effective health-disinformation detectors had high need for cognition["Political affiliation or need for cognition? It depends on the post," PLOS One.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0315259).
- **NFC maps onto a shifted readiness (response bias)**: lower expert alignment in a contested task["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820).

A sharper dissociation: **fluid intelligence, but not need for cognition, was associated with attitude change after correction**["Fluid intelligence but not need for cognition is associated with attitude change after co](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41235-024-00595-1). Even within analytic traits, the capacity to update tracks fluid intelligence rather than the disposition to enjoy thinking. Read against the permanence tendency: fluid intelligence governs the *capacity* to integrate a correction; the NFC permanence tendency governs the *motivation* to hold the prior fixed. The two can diverge within one person—correction responsiveness is jointly determined, with capacity setting the ceiling and motivation determining whether it is reached.

## NFC vs. Intolerance of Uncertainty

Both name discomfort with not-knowing, but differ in framing: **IU is anchored in anxiety, NFC in epistemic motivation**["Intolerance of uncertainty and perceived threat," ScienceDirect.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S000579670700201X). IU's nomological home is the worry/generalized-anxiety literature; NFC's is epistemic motivation. The motivation framing gives NFC **broader reach**—because misinformation is not confined to threat-relevant content, NFC predicts premature settling across any domain, while a threat-anchored IU would predict acceptance specifically of threat-relevant misinformation.

A dimensionality study placed IU and NFC in one nomological net (n=239)["Intolerance of uncertainty: Exploring its dimensionality and associations."](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17349774/), treating them as distinguishable enough to warrant separate measurement but without quantifying overlap. The verdict is the chapter's most qualified: a **conditional pass**—theoretically distinct in framing, but their measured association means discriminant validity for any specific finding must be demonstrated, not assumed. A study measuring NFC alone cannot rule out that some effect is carried by unmeasured IU.

## NFC vs. Dogmatism and Authoritarianism

NFC names a cognitive **form** (the drive to reach and hold an answer); authoritarianism names ideological **content**. The mediation literature shows NFC related to conservative and racist belief endorsement *through* authoritarianism-related mediators (submission and dominance)["The Impact of Need for Closure on Conservative Beliefs and Racism."](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167204264333), positioning NFC **upstream** of authoritarianism in mediational models. NFC's internal urgency/permanence structure survives being embedded in authoritarianism's net["Need for Closure Relations with Authoritarianism, Conservative Beliefs and Racism."](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224961593)—the signature of a distinct construct.

Because NFC specifies form not content, it predicts **valence-neutral seizing and freezing** that can attach to misinformation of any ideological valence, whereas authoritarianism predicts congruent-content bias. This is a cleaner separation than NFC earns against IU: a predictor and its mediator are distinguishable by construction.

## NFC vs. Conspiracy Mentality and Epistemic Trust

These name a worldview and a trust orientation rather than an epistemic style—**separate vulnerability channels that compound with closure rather than substitute for it.** Conspiracy mentality appears as its own predictor alongside social trust in a four-country study[Gundersen et al., "Predicting Misinformation Beliefs."](https://jspp.psychopen.eu/index.php/jspp/article/download/13385/13385.html), and Pierre's two-component model frames conspiracy belief as the joint product of mistrust *and* misinformation[Pierre, "Mistrust and Misinformation: A Two-Component Socio-Epistemic Model."](https://jspp.psychopen.eu/index.php/jspp/article/view/5273)—a different generative structure than NFC's single closure-drive.

**Epistemic trust is the channel most clearly orthogonal to closure.** Where NFC governs *how* quickly and firmly a person settles, trust governs *which sources* they treat as credible. "Trust is key" across eight countries on false climate beliefs[Ejaz, W., Altay, S., Fletcher, R., & Nielsen, R. K. 2024, "Trust Is Key: Determinants of F](http://users.ox.ac.uk/~polf0572/publication/trust-is-key/). The three channels each hold a different part of belief-formation fixed:

- **NFC** holds epistemic style fixed, lets content vary.
- **Conspiracy mentality** holds a worldview fixed, lets theories vary.
- **Epistemic trust** holds a source-credibility map fixed, lets claims vary by source.

The intervention consequence: a countermeasure aimed at one channel will not touch the others; an accuracy prompt does not repair a broken source-credibility map. Multi-channel vulnerability requires multi-channel defense.

# 6. Empirical Evidence: Direction, Strength, and Paradigms

The evidence distributes across four paradigms that do not speak with one voice. **The central claim: the NFC-acceptance association is positive in direction under specifiable conditions but is neither uniform across paradigms nor demonstrated to be large in any standardized sense the studies report.**

## Correlational Survey Evidence

Hejniak and colleagues (2019) replicated the misinformation effect and established a **positive correlation between NFC and susceptibility** to misleading post-event information[Hejniak et al. 2019, "The Need for Cognitive Closure as a Determinant of Susceptibility to](https://arch.ies.gov.pl/index.php/2019)—the empirical anchor for the seizing half of the mechanism. A high-closure respondent seizes the most recently supplied, coherent-seeming version (the misleading narrative) and freezes on it.

But the survey layer's defining feature is **heterogeneity of sign**. The 2019 Hungarian elections study found NFC *negatively* associated with fake-news detection accuracy["Social Psychological Predictors of Belief in Fake News in the 2019 Hungarian Elections" 2](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.790848/full); the Boot COVID-19 survey (Wave 1 N=501, Wave 2 N=326) found NFC associated with *greater* knowledge and conspiracy *rejection*[Boot et al. 2021, "Gathering, processing, and interpreting information about COVID-19," Sc](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-86088-3). Two rigorous surveys produced opposite-signed associations—the signature of a genuinely moderated effect. The Hungarian context: partisan election headlines where false content may be the closure-satisfying option. The Boot context: an early-pandemic environment where the *official* consensus was the closure-satisfying option, and conspiracies were the unsettling alternative high-closure respondents rejected. **The sign of the closure effect depends on whether the closure-satisfying answer in a given environment is the false one or the true one.** Any sign-blind meta-analytic pooling would average positive and negative signs toward zero.

## NFC and Conspiracy Beliefs

Marchlewska and colleagues found NFC positively predicted conspiracy endorsement, **especially when conspiratorial explanations were salient for events lacking a clear official explanation**[Marchlewska et al. 2017, European Journal of Social Psychology.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.2308). The salient conspiratorial account is the seizing target; the explanatory vacuum makes it the most readily seizable option. This reconciles with Boot's conspiracy-*rejection* result: where a clear official explanation is the default, high closure cements it; where the official explanation is absent and a conspiratorial account is salient, high closure seizes the latter.

The Jedinger & Masch study (2025) brings **political trust** into the same frame[Jedinger & Masch 2025, "Need for cognitive closure, political trust, and belief in conspir](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/social-psychology/articles/10.3389/frsps.2024.1447313/full). The hypothesis the design implies: trust determines the direction. When institutional trust is high, the official account is seized and frozen and high closure resists conspiracy; when trust is low, the official account loses its grip and the conspiratorial account becomes the closure-satisfying option. (The study establishes that the three constructs are examined jointly; it does not by itself report the interaction's simple slopes.) The contrarian implication: the often-presented "main effect" of NFC on conspiracy belief may be the wrong level of description—an average of two opposite-signed conditional effects.

## NFC and Fake-News Susceptibility

When entered directly, NFC shows the expected effect: the 2019 Hungarian study found NFC negatively associated with **detection accuracy**["Social Psychological Predictors of Belief in Fake News in the 2019 Hungarian Elections" 2](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.790848/full)—a *discrimination* claim, not merely a response bias—within a competitive field of predictors (social dominance, RWA, system justification, openness, conspiracy mentality).

The large PNAS meta-analytic program provides the quantitative anchor: across 31 experiments, 256,337 choices by 11,561 US participants, discrimination ran above chance for both true news (68.51%) and false news (67.24%), with **older age, higher analytical thinking, and Democratic affiliation** linked to greater accuracy[PNAS 2024.](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/d35db420-eecd-4464-b998-eefb9cf7358a)["Susceptibility to online misinformation: A systematic meta-analysis," PNAS.](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2409329121). NFC is **not foregrounded** among these top predictors. This is a statement about emphasis, not a demonstrated null. The reconciliation: closure's effect concentrates where a salient narrative exists to be seized, so it dilutes toward a small average across a heterogeneous headline pool where many items supply no ready narrative—reconciling the strong Hungarian partisan-headline effect with closure's absence from the foregrounded roster. A separate Cambridge study found susceptibility better explained by **myside bias and partisanship than by analytical thinking**["Susceptibility to misinformation is consistent across question framings," Judgment and De](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/1121D46E918815B2CD7A9C6C237464A2), consistent with motivated reasoning as a process through which closure operates.

## Memory-Based Paradigms

The cleanest division. **Closure amplifies the eyewitness misinformation effect but does not moderate illusory truth.**

Pica and colleagues, across three experiments, showed NFC **amplifies retrieval-induced forgetting, which in turn increases susceptibility to post-event misinformation**[Pica et al., "The Role of Need for Cognitive Closure in Retrieval-Induced Forgetting and M](https://www.academia.edu/84707704). The chain: high NFC intensifies forgetting → forgetting creates a memory gap → the gap is filled by false detail. This is the most mechanistically specified study, supplying the encoding route that the Hejniak correlation was consistent with but could not itself demonstrate.

De keersmaecker and colleagues found, across seven studies (N=2,196), that **NFC does not moderate the illusory truth effect**[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844). Every study produced a significant illusory truth effect; none showed NFC moderation. The contrast with Pica is the heart of the matter: **closure operates on acceptance that requires actively resolving ambiguity by taking up an account (eyewitness), not on acceptance that accrues from mere repetition (illusory truth).** The well-powered null shows closure is not a generic credulity factor—it ties specifically to ambiguity-resolving uptake.

The conditionality *is* the finding. On magnitude, the studies report directions and significance rather than a pooled standardized coefficient, so no single magnitude is committed—except the PNAS discrimination baseline (68.51%/67.24%) bounding the headroom within which any single trait can move headline accuracy.

# 7. Moderators and Boundary Conditions

NFC behaves **less like a fixed vector pointing toward falsehood and more like a conditional amplifier** whose output is set by four families of moderator.

## Situational Ambiguity, Threat, and Time Pressure

Molinario and colleagues (2024) reported that in a 2022 study (1,668 respondents), both perceived COVID-19 threat and perceived Russia-Ukraine threat were associated with elevated NFC, which in turn was associated with greater misinformation susceptibility[Molinario et al. 2024, "Need for cognitive closure, perceived threat, and misinformation s](https://rosa.uniroma1.it/rosa04/psychology_hub/article/view/18443)—a **threat → NFC → susceptibility** mediation. This is the situational analogue of the trait pathway: a collective crisis elevates the closure motive across a broad population. The two entry points—trait vs. state—converge on seizing but diverge in what they recommend: targeting a stable subpopulation vs. targeting the threat-charged moment.

Time pressure and cognitive load function as state inducers because urgency raises the subjective cost of extended search, pushing average users toward the high-NFC information profile["Need for Cognitive Closure Modulates How Perceptual Decisions Are Affected by Task Diffic](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0146002)["Influence of Need for Cognition and Need for Cognitive Closure on Information Behaviour,"](https://journals.library.ualberta.ca/ojs.cais-acsi.ca/index.php/cais-asci/article/download/637/587). (The direct time-pressure-on-acceptance manipulation is not in evidence; what the evidence warrants is the mechanistic expectation, not the demonstrated downstream effect.)

## Political Trust and Ideology

**The single most analytically important boundary condition.** Jedinger & Masch study NFC and political trust as *joint* correlates of conspiracy belief rather than treating NFC as isolated[Jedinger & Masch 2025, "Need for cognitive closure, political trust, and belief in conspir](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/social-psychology/articles/10.3389/frsps.2024.1447313/full). The hypothesized mechanism: where institutional trust is low, the official account is less attractive for seizing and a conspiracy narrative becomes the more available object for closure; where trust is high, the institutional account itself supplies the firm explanation. **Closure selects for firmness, not content; trust determines which available account carries the firmness.** This makes trust a boundary condition rather than a competing covariate—and recommends that programs reduce conspiracy belief in high-NFC populations by *rebuilding institutional trust* rather than attacking the closure disposition.

**Ideological congruence** moderates by determining which claims the motive finds congenial. Jost and colleagues (2018) found conservatives' stronger desire for shared reality interacts with NFC to increase acceptance of ideology-congruent misinformation[Jost et al. 2018, "Ideological Asymmetries in Conformity, Desire for Shared Reality, and t](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322420871). Congruence must be modeled as a property of the **claim-by-believer pairing**, not a fixed trait—which is why detection of health disinformation "depends on the post"["Political affiliation or need for cognition? It depends on the post," PLOS One.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0315259), and why studies using a single fixed item set risk mis-estimating the effect.

## The Education Paradox

Cosgrove (2026) found that **education's normally protective effect is offset by narcissism, need for uniqueness, and NFC, which redirect reasoning away from accuracy goals**[Cosgrove 2026, "Education, epistemically unwarranted beliefs, and the offsetting role of n](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191886925005306). The paradox resolves through **goal substitution**: more education means more reasoning capacity that, when closure is high, is deployed in service of a firm identity-confirming conclusion rather than an accurate one. The cause of the reversal is *what the reasoning is for*, not how much capacity exists.

This connects to the need-for-cognition/NFC distinction: need for cognition seeks the *process* of thinking, NFC seeks its *termination* in a firm answer. Higher numeracy can *widen* rather than narrow group gaps when the goal is defense rather than accuracy["Motivated reasoning about climate change and the influence of Numeracy, Need for Cognitio](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10920913/). The implication: sophistication-raising interventions may underperform in precisely the high-closure subpopulations where they are most needed, unless paired with accuracy-goal priming.

## Source Cues and Information Environment

The closure motive has a **constructive use**. Lewandowsky and colleagues (2023) showed corrections from high-credibility sources reduce belief in misinformation more effectively[Lewandowsky et al. 2023, "Source reliability and belief updating after correction," Cognit](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0010027725000307); Ecker and colleagues (2022) showed high-credibility corrections reduce continued influence by altering source perceptions[Ecker et al. 2022, "The psychological drivers of misinformation belief and its resistance](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-021-00006-y). For a high-NFC recipient, **the credible source supplies the definitive answer the closure motive demands, so the correction is accepted**; an uncredible source supplies no firm answer, so closure holds to the already-seized misinformation through freezing. This makes source credibility a more powerful lever for high-NFC than low-NFC recipients.

Conversely, **engagement-ranking algorithms and repeated exposure** raise acceptance. Engagement-optimized feeds increase repetition["Ranking for Engagement: How Social Media Algorithms Fuel Misinformation and Polarization.](https://www.ifo.de/en/cesifo/publications/2026/working-paper/ranking-engagement), which raises familiarity, which raises perceived accuracy via the illusory truth effect[Pennycook, G., Cannon, T. D., & Rand, D. G. 2018, "Prior exposure increases perceived accu](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279465/). The tension is built into engagement ranking: the same weighting of likes/shares that maximizes engagement raises misinformation["Ranking for Engagement: How Social Media Algorithms Fuel Misinformation and Polarization.](https://www.ifo.de/en/cesifo/publications/2026/working-paper/ranking-engagement)—a revenue-vs-accuracy tradeoff no technical fix dissolves. A partial remedy: accuracy prompts make the accuracy goal momentarily salient, redirecting reasoning back toward a firm *accurate* answer[Pennycook, G., et al. 2021, "Shifting attention to accuracy can reduce misinformation onli](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03344-2).

**One bounding null.** The De keersmaecker result (N=2,196) found no NFC moderation of the illusory truth effect[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844). This forbids the lazy generalization that NFC moderates all routes to false belief. The apparent tension with the algorithmic argument resolves: that argument requires only that repetition raise familiarity for *everyone*, not that high-NFC individuals show a *larger* illusory truth effect. The synthesis: **magnitude is set by several moderators; direction is conditioned by trust; and at least one route (familiarity) is left entirely unmoderated.**

# 8. Mediating Processes

Three candidate routes carry NFC's influence, supported unevenly: one is formally estimated, one proxied, one proposed.

## Reduced Analytic Processing (Formally Mediated)

The most directly supported route. Nurses et al. (2021) documented a chain: **high NFC → lower analytic thinking → reduced detection → increased susceptibility**[Nurses et al. 2021, "Reduced analytic thinking and susceptibility to COVID-19 misinformati](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13421-021-01219-5.pdf). Participants with higher analytic thinking rated COVID-19 misinformation as less accurate and were less willing to share it. This is seizing at the level of processing depth.

But what the design licenses is narrower than often read: the downstream outcome is measured on *false* content only, so whether this reflects improved **discrimination** or a shifted **response bias** cannot be resolved—the central unsettled measurement question for this route. The companion finding (best detectors had high need for cognition["Political affiliation or need for cognition? It depends on the post," PLOS One.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0315259)) is consistent but doesn't itself distinguish capacity from motivation.

## Confirmation Bias and Motivated Reasoning (Mechanism Proxy)

Where deliberation concerns *how much* a person thinks, this route concerns *which direction*. Ask (2005) found high-NFC participants **more resistant to persuasion**—the behavioral expression of freezing[Ask, K. 2005, "Need for cognitive closure, confirmation bias, and resistance to persuasion](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jip.19). The route makes a distinct prediction: while reduced processing predicts *indiscriminate* susceptibility, motivated reasoning predicts *directional* susceptibility (preferentially accepting worldview-congenial falsehoods). **The two routes are empirically separable by whether susceptibility is uniform or belief-congruent.** (Ask establishes the NFC→resistance link as a *proxy* for freezing, not a formal estimate of confirmation bias as a statistical mediator.)

The **epistemic-social framework** separates the epistemic goal (wanting a stable answer—NFC) from the social goal (wanting a group-aligned answer—partisanship). In politically charged domains they converge: NFC and conservative lean *independently* predicted COVID-19 misalignment["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820). The most important qualification: **NFC increases acceptance of *whatever the closure-providing answer happens to be*, which in a consensus-affirming environment can be protective**[Orr, M., Stewart, A., & Grundstein, A. 2020, "Investigating Connections between Need for C](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/17/15/5619)—a directional flexibility yielding a falsifiable prediction: in a domain where the closure-providing answer is the *accurate* one, high NFC should *reduce* misinformation acceptance.

## Lowered Verification Behavior (Theoretically Proposed)

The most intuitive and least directly measured. Seizing compresses the search (urgency pushes toward the first answer); freezing seals it (permanence discourages reopening). **High NFC reduces verification at two points—uptake (seizing) and revision (freezing).** But direct behavioral measurement of verification effort against NFC is absent from the evidence; the route rests on the broad information-behaviour link["Influence of Need for Cognition and Need for Cognitive Closure on Information Behaviour,"](https://journals.library.ualberta.ca/ojs.cais-acsi.ca/index.php/cais-asci/article/download/637/587) and adjacent findings (trust[Ejaz, W., Altay, S., Fletcher, R., & Nielsen, R. K. 2024, "Trust Is Key: Determinants of F](http://users.ox.ac.uk/~polf0572/publication/trust-is-key/), cognitive skill[Ahmed, S., Madrid-Morales, D., & Tully, M. 2023, "Online political engagement, cognitive s](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/193828/1/AAV_UoSDeposit.pdf)). Verification resists clean measurement because it is often a *non-event*—the search that was not conducted leaves no trace.

For high-NFC populations, **pre-exposure defenses like inoculation are better matched than post-exposure verification prompts**, because they don't depend on reopening a question the person has already frozen["Psychological inoculation strategies to fight climate disinformation across 12 countries,](https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01736-0).

## Toward a Mediation Model

```
NFC --seizing--> Reduced analytic processing --> Acceptance
NFC --freezing--> Confirmation bias/motivated reasoning --> Acceptance
NFC --urgency+permanence--> Lowered verification --> Acceptance
NFC --residual direct path--> Acceptance
```

The facet-to-route mapping is the model's most testable prediction: a seizing measure should predict the analytic-processing mediator more strongly than the confirmation-bias mediator, and a freezing measure the reverse.

**A contrarian reading:** the De keersmaecker null suggests at least one major acceptance channel—fluency-driven illusory truth—bypasses the NFC mediators entirely[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844). If a substantial fraction of real-world acceptance runs on repetition-driven fluency rather than closure-driven processing, the NFC mediation model explains a bounded slice rather than the whole. What remains open: whether a residual *direct* NFC→acceptance path survives once all three mediators are entered jointly—a model no study in the evidence has run.

# 9. Causality vs. Correlation: Reconciling Mixed and Null Findings

**The evidence for a causal NFC-to-acceptance link is real but narrow in scope, while the broad correlational base is too confounded to license a general causal claim.**

## Experimental Evidence

The strongest causal demonstration is the Pica eyewitness paradigm: NFC manipulation amplified retrieval-induced forgetting, which increased misinformation susceptibility[Pica et al., "The Role of Need for Cognitive Closure in Retrieval-Induced Forgetting and M](https://www.academia.edu/84707704). Because the design is experimental, the arrow runs from the closure manipulation to the memory outcome. But the outcome is a *memory* distortion—narrower than the belief endorsement at stake in conspiracy studies. By contrast, Pennycook et al. (2018) manipulated *exposure* and found an effect on perceived accuracy[Pennycook, G., Cannon, T. D., & Rand, D. G. 2018, "Prior exposure increases perceived accu](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279465/)—**a closure manipulation and an exposure manipulation are not interchangeable tests**, which is the most common source of apparent contradiction.

Two limits bound the experimental verdict. **Manipulation strength**: situational inductions engage the *urgency* tendency for minutes but cannot reproduce the *permanence* tendency that accumulates over a lifetime[Kruglanski, A. W., & Webster, D. M. 1996, "Motivated Closing of the Mind: 'Seizing' and 'F](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/Psyc590Readings/Kruglanski1996.pdf)—so the most rigorous designs test the least consequential half of the mechanism. **Generalizability**: where the outcome is a laboratory memory distortion, the leap to real-world belief endorsement requires an inferential bridge the design doesn't supply. The highest-value next experiment would cross a situational closure induction with a repetition manipulation on *politically relevant* misinformation.

## Correlational and Longitudinal Constraints

**Reverse causation** is mechanistically plausible: a firm conspiratorial worldview makes the world feel more resolved, which could *raise* measured NFC. **Third-variable threats** are serious and named: narcissism, conspiracy mentality, social trust, unsafe-neighborhood perceptions[Gundersen et al., "Predicting Misinformation Beliefs."](https://jspp.psychopen.eu/index.php/jspp/article/download/13385/13385.html); "trust is the key determinant" of false climate beliefs[Ejaz, W., Altay, S., Fletcher, R., & Nielsen, R. K. 2024, "Trust Is Key: Determinants of F](http://users.ox.ac.uk/~polf0572/publication/trust-is-key/); Pierre's model places mistrust upstream[Pierre, "Mistrust and Misinformation: A Two-Component Socio-Epistemic Model."](https://jspp.psychopen.eu/index.php/jspp/article/view/5273). A regression controlling for trust as a single covariate understates the problem, because trust sits *on the causal path* rather than beside it.

The evidence base contains essentially **one longitudinal design**, and its scarcity is itself the finding. Boot et al. (Wave 1 N=501, Wave 2 N=326) found NFC related to greater COVID-19 knowledge, conspiracy *rejection*, and change in knowledge over time[Boot et al. 2021, "Gathering, processing, and interpreting information about COVID-19," Sc](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-86088-3)—**a protective rather than harmful association**, plausibly because the survey was fielded when the official public-health account was the salient, trusted default a high-closure mind would seize and freeze on. Temporal precedence weakens the reverse-causation threat but doesn't touch the third-variable threat. The field needs a three-wave-minimum cross-lagged panel that introduces a correction event between waves.

## Reconciling Positive vs. Null Results

The apparent contradiction dissolves once outcomes are distinguished. The positive side: a Polish study found COVID-19 conspiracy belief mediated NFC's relationship to fear["Need for Cognitive Closure, Conspiracy Beliefs, and Coronavirus Fear" 2022, IJERPH 1922,](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/19/22/14789)—a motivationally loaded, identity-relevant belief. The null side: De keersmaecker found no NFC moderation of illusory truth[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844)—a familiarity-driven accuracy shift. **If closure shapes which answer a person commits to and defends, it should moderate motivated belief endorsement but need not moderate a familiarity-driven shift, because repetition raises familiarity regardless of closure disposition.**

The contrarian point worth stating plainly: **the De keersmaecker null is not evidence against the closure-acceptance hypothesis—it is evidence that closure does not moderate one particular low-level mechanism, which the seizing-freezing theory never predicted it should.** The discrimination/bias distinction reinforces this: illusory truth is primarily a *response-bias* phenomenon, so a test built around a discrimination-neutral repetition manipulation is structurally insensitive to a closure effect running through bias.

The broader pattern: the *same* pandemic domain produces a positive conspiracy association[Jedinger & Masch 2025, "Need for cognitive closure, political trust, and belief in conspir](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/social-psychology/articles/10.3389/frsps.2024.1447313/full)["Need for Cognitive Closure, Conspiracy Beliefs, and Coronavirus Fear" 2022, IJERPH 1922,](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/19/22/14789) and a protective conspiracy-rejection result[Boot et al. 2021, "Gathering, processing, and interpreting information about COVID-19," Sc](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-86088-3), depending on timing and salience. **Closure determines the strength of commitment to whichever answer is seized, not its content.** Any future meta-analysis must stratify by outcome type before pooling.

## Closure as Risk vs. Protective Factor

**The single most important conceptual move: closure is a commitment amplifier, not a falsehood amplifier.** Freezing acts on *whatever answer currently occupies the closure slot*. When the held answer is the accurate official one, freezing entrenches *rejection* of incoming misinformation. Kruglanski et al. (1993) documented the resistance half of this chain[Kruglanski et al. 1993, "Motivated resistance and openness to persuasion," JPSP.](https://scispace.com/papers/motivated-resistance-and-openness-to-persuasion-in-the-2gveqaqe9s); the sign of the outcome depends entirely on what the persuasion attempt pushes toward.

This bidirectionality is uncomfortable for intervention design: lowering a population's NFC would *loosen* the grip on correct beliefs where the official answer is accurate and salient. **The target should not be the closure disposition but the answer that occupies the closure slot**—ensure the accurate account is the first and most available at the moment of seizing.

Three conditions flip the sign from risk to protection: (1) the accurate answer is *already held* when misinformation arrives; (2) it is *salient and available* at seizing; (3) the belief is not so identity-relevant that an ingroup answer outcompetes the official one. The PNAS data give this a quantitative frame: with ~68% accuracy on both true and false items[PNAS 2024.](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/d35db420-eecd-4464-b998-eefb9cf7358a), the population already discriminates well above chance, so for the *majority* of items the accurate answer is the one a well-calibrated person would seize. **The net population-level NFC effect is a weighted average dominated by the base rate of accurate seizures**—which can easily be protective on net.

The complicating evidence: high-NFC respondents were less aligned with experts on COVID-19 tweets["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820)—harmful in the same domain where Boot found protection. The reconciliation returns to salience: the tweet task asks respondents to adjudicate individual items with *no single official answer pre-attached*, whereas Boot measured standing endorsement against a salient consensus. **The protective case requires a pre-attached accurate answer; remove it, and the closure effect reverts to harmful.**

| Condition | Protective | Harmful | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answer already held | Accurate consensus first | Misinformation first | Boot[Boot et al. 2021, "Gathering, processing, and interpreting information about COVID-19," Sc](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-86088-3) |
| Salience at seizing | Official answer salient | No pre-attached answer | Alignment study["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820) |
| Identity relevance | Low | High; ingroup falsehood available | [Borah, P. 2022, "The Moderating Role of Political Ideology," International Journal of Comm](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/download/18261/3845) |
| Outcome measured | Standing endorsement | On-the-spot judgment | De keersmaecker[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844) |

**The synthesis:** NFC is a causally efficacious but sign-neutral commitment amplifier whose net effect is determined by the answer it amplifies, not by the disposition in isolation. High confidence in a narrow experimental causal effect; moderate confidence in a conditional real-world effect stratified by domain and salience; low confidence in any unconditional "closure causes misinformation" claim.

# 10. Meta-Causal Synthesis: The Integrated Verdict

**NFC operates not as a uniform direct cause but as a conditional, indirect amplifier whose component mechanisms couple to one another and whose evidentiary support varies sharply by link.**

## Coupling Among Mechanisms

The four mechanisms form a reinforcing cascade, not parallel predictors. Urgency → seizing → heuristic processing → permanence/freezing[Kruglanski, A. W., & Webster, D. M. 1996, "Motivated Closing of the Mind: 'Seizing' and 'F](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/Psyc590Readings/Kruglanski1996.pdf); the faster the seizing, the thinner the evidentiary base at freezing, the more the frozen belief must be defended by motivated reasoning rather than re-examined. Cosgrove's education-offset finding[Cosgrove 2026, "Education, epistemically unwarranted beliefs, and the offsetting role of n](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191886925005306) marks where NFC stops being a passive preference and becomes an active distorter: once accuracy is no longer the governing goal, heuristic shortcuts go uncorrected. **NFC's contribution is multiplicative across its own sub-processes, not additive.**

The most consequential feature is the **feedback loop**: freezing closes back onto future search. Once a belief crystallizes, the permanence tendency suppresses the search that would reopen it. Pica's eyewitness chain makes this concrete—the frozen account leaves a vacancy that misinformation fills, and the filled vacancy reduces motivation to search for the true detail[Pica et al., "The Role of Need for Cognitive Closure in Retrieval-Induced Forgetting and M](https://www.academia.edu/84707704). **But the loop is content-blind**: Boot's data show NFC related to greater knowledge in a high-quality environment[Boot et al. 2021, "Gathering, processing, and interpreting information about COVID-19," Sc](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-86088-3), because freezing reinforces reduced search around whatever was seized first. The loop amplifies the epistemic quality of whatever the environment delivered at seizing—the single most important qualification on any direct-cause reading.

The illusory-truth link, central to the fluency story, carries the weakest warrant of any link (a high-confidence *null* across 2,196 participants), while the seizing link, peripheral in public discussion, is best evidenced. **Theoretical centrality and evidentiary strength are not aligned.**

## External-Domain Calibration

NFC must be judged against rival predictors. The analytic-thinking family is far better-powered: the systematic meta-analysis covers 256,337 participants["Susceptibility to online misinformation: A systematic meta-analysis," PNAS.](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2409329121)—more than a hundredfold the 2,196 anchoring the NFC null. The antagonistic-trait family (narcissism, conspiracy mentality) replicates cross-nationally[Gundersen et al., "Predicting Misinformation Beliefs."](https://jspp.psychopen.eu/index.php/jspp/article/download/13385/13385.html)["The role of narcissism and motivated reasoning on misinformation propagation" 2024, Front](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2024.1472631/full). Both out-cover NFC on breadth.

**What closure uniquely adds is not predictive power but a specified processing mechanism.** Narcissism tells you *who* accepts misinformation; the seizing-freezing cascade tells you *through what cognitive steps*. The Cosgrove finding crystallizes this: NFC is not a redundant copy of narcissism but a *distinct route* by which the accuracy goal is suppressed[Cosgrove 2026, "Education, epistemically unwarranted beliefs, and the offsetting role of n](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191886925005306). **NFC's incremental value is mechanistic and conditional, not main-effect: it names the process that turns off the accuracy goal—exactly what an accuracy-prompt intervention would need to turn back on.** NFC also aligns with the myside-bias/partisanship cluster that *out-predicts* analytic thinking["Susceptibility to misinformation is consistent across question framings," Judgment and De](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/1121D46E918815B2CD7A9C6C237464A2), repositioning it as a directional-motivation variable rather than a cognitive-capacity one.

The direct effect is contradicted where it should be strongest (the illusory truth null[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844)) and flips sign with context (Boot's conspiracy rejection[Boot et al. 2021, "Gathering, processing, and interpreting information about COVID-19," Sc](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-86088-3))—a variable that flips sign cannot be a uniform direct cause. The indirect path has the only experimental warrant. The conditional effect determines whether the indirect path raises or lowers acceptance.

**The mixed record—positive (eyewitness), null (illusory truth), protective (conspiracy rejection)—is not contradictory but the expected output of a conditional amplifier**, each finding corresponding to a different setting of the information-environment and goal moderators. The constraint: the model predicts only that NFC will *amplify*, not in which direction, unless the moderators are measured. Future studies reporting a main effect without measuring environmental trust or active goal should be expected to produce the same sign-flipping record the existing literature shows—**an unmeasured conditional amplifier looks exactly like an unreliable direct cause.**

What remains unresolved: NFC's incremental validity over the broad HEXACO dimensions, which no study models jointly—the single most direct test of construct distinctness the field has not run.

# 11. Intervention Design

Counter-misinformation design becomes tractable when corrective tools are matched to the psychology that made misinformation attractive. Every lever below works by **redirecting** the seizing-freezing sequence toward accuracy rather than suppressing it—because a tendency this deeply motivated cannot be eliminated, only pointed at a better target.

## Designing Corrections That Satisfy Closure

A correction competes with misinformation precisely on the dimension high-NFC readers most value: which account supplies firmer certainty. Lewandowsky and colleagues (2023) showed **corrections from high-credibility sources reduce belief more effectively, with the mechanism running through closure-satisfaction**: a credible source feels more definitive, satisfying the need for closure and reducing reliance on the alternative[Lewandowsky et al. 2023, "Source reliability and belief updating after correction," Cognit](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0010027725000307). Ecker and colleagues (2022) converged from the continued-influence literature[Ecker et al. 2022, "The psychological drivers of misinformation belief and its resistance](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-021-00006-y). For a high-NFC reader, freezing can be *redirected*: if the official correction crystallizes, freezing protects accuracy.

Three properties follow: corrections should be **clear** (an unambiguous statement of what is true), **authoritative** (attributed to an audience-trusted source), and **timely** (delivered before competing accounts consolidate).

**The accuracy/honesty tension:** the high-NFC reader wants firm certainty, but the truth is often itself uncertain. The resolution: a 2023 study (N=6,489) found that communicating inherent scientific uncertainty **can maintain public trust without oversimplifying**["The effects of communicating scientific uncertainty on trust and decision making" 2023, J](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/721727C5DA7F0438804568657808D951). What high-NFC readers seek is a *usable, authoritative* answer, not a *complete* one. The design target is **calibrated confidence**—clarity about the action to take paired with honesty about residual uncertainty. (The study measured population-level trust, not NFC-stratified response; the NFC-specific replication remains the natural next test.)

## Timing: Prebunking Before Freezing

The seizing-and-freezing sequence creates a window where prevention is far cheaper than cure. **Inoculation occupies the seizing window before freezing closes it.** The 2023 study of psychological inoculation against climate disinformation tested it across 12 countries and found it effective["Psychological inoculation strategies to fight climate disinformation across 12 countries,](https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01736-0). For high-NFC readers, the mechanism prediction: the inoculation, arriving during the seizing window, supplies the firm framework the reader then freezes around—**the same tendency that makes high-NFC readers vulnerable to first-mover misinformation makes them responsive to first-mover inoculation.** (This NFC-specific prediction is mechanism-derived; the inoculation studies did not stratify by NFC.)

The mirror image is why post-freezing correction is hard: once a belief is frozen, the permanence tendency actively resists revision, producing the continued-influence effect[Ecker et al. 2022, "The psychological drivers of misinformation belief and its resistance](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-021-00006-y). The total resistance a post-freezing correction faces is the *product* of seizing speed and freezing depth. **On the mechanism account, anticipatory inoculation should outperform reactive correction for high-NFC audiences**—a falsifiable prediction pointing to a clean experiment: deliver identical content as pre-exposure inoculation vs. post-exposure correction to a high-NFC sample.

## Audience Tailoring

Messaging is more effective when segmented by closure-relevant characteristics. The 2026 vaccine-prebunking study argues for **strategic segmentation tailored to psychological vulnerabilities**["Increasing prebunking effectiveness to aid disinformation-susceptible publics" 2026, Vacc](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0264410X2600558X). Messaging optimized for high-NFC audiences (clear, authoritative, early) and for low-NFC audiences (deliberative, complexity-acknowledging) are *structurally different messages*, not the same message at different volumes.

**A crucial boundary:** the De keersmaecker null bounds what segmentation can promise[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844). Where the misinformation effect runs through repetition-based familiarity rather than seizing, NFC-based tailoring offers no leverage. Segmentation by NFC is indicated for seizing-and-freezing-driven acceptance, not familiarity-driven acceptance. The next-generation design should route audiences through a two-variable screen—dispositional NFC and current situational ambiguity—reserving closure-congruent firm messaging for the high-on-both segment.

**The deepest leverage point is trust.** Trust determines which source a high-NFC reader freezes around—and freezing around a trusted official source is the protective outcome the intervention seeks[Ejaz, W., Altay, S., Fletcher, R., & Nielsen, R. K. 2024, "Trust Is Key: Determinants of F](http://users.ox.ac.uk/~polf0572/publication/trust-is-key/). This is the bidirectional reading of freezing: the same permanence tendency that entrenches misinformation entrenches accuracy when the reader has frozen around a trusted official account. A correction from a distrusted source asks the high-NFC reader to revise a frozen belief in favor of an account they have no closure-based reason to prefer—a near-impossible request. **Trust-building, which works *with* the closure motivation, is the more promising lever for precisely the population education fails to reach**[Cosgrove 2026, "Education, epistemically unwarranted beliefs, and the offsetting role of n](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191886925005306).

## Information-Environment Design

The environment manufactures situational closure. Engagement-optimized feeds (prioritizing likes/shares) increase the rapid, time-pressured flow that triggers situational closure["Ranking for Engagement: How Social Media Algorithms Fuel Misinformation and Polarization.](https://www.ifo.de/en/cesifo/publications/2026/working-paper/ranking-engagement)—the platform's commercial objective and its misinformation-resistance objective are **structurally opposed**, because the same arousing, identity-confirming content drives both engagement and falsehood-sharing. Any genuine reduction requires accepting some engagement cost. The accuracy nudge interrupts the fast closure-driven impulse[Pennycook, G., et al. 2021, "Shifting attention to accuracy can reduce misinformation onli](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03344-2), working even for dispositionally high-NFC readers by momentarily shifting the *situational* closure level.

**Legitimate closure cues**—verified labels, expert-consensus signals—work *with* the seizing tendency. A high-NFC reader will seek and freeze around *some* source of certainty regardless, so the platform's job is to ensure the most easily-seized cue is an accurate one. A verified label is functionally a closure shortcut[Lewandowsky et al. 2023, "Source reliability and belief updating after correction," Cognit](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0010027725000307); a consensus signal converts diffuse expert opinion into a single firm marker the high-NFC reader can seize. **But the cue only supplies legitimate closure if the reader trusts the verifier**[Ejaz, W., Altay, S., Fletcher, R., & Nielsen, R. K. 2024, "Trust Is Key: Determinants of F](http://users.ox.ac.uk/~polf0572/publication/trust-is-key/)—so platform-cue design and audience trust-building are complementary, not substitutable.

# 12. Limitations and Falsifiers

## Measurement Constraints

**The ability/motivation confound** is the most consequential. A closure-flavored explanation and a resource-depletion explanation predict the same cross-sectional correlation—Oktar and Lombrozo (2025) name beliefs persisting because people "fail to deploy cognitive resources to update"[Oktar & Lombrozo 2025, "How Beliefs Persist Amid Controversy: The Paths to Persistence Mod](https://cognition.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf3386/files/documents/OktarLombrozo-PsychRev-2025.pdf). The confound dissolves only in studies that jointly model a capacity measure with the closure disposition.

**Short-form scales** report total-score properties rather than recovering the separable urgency/permanence facets the mechanism requires[Roets & Van Hiel 2011, "Item selection and validation of a brief, 15-item Need for Closure](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224961598). Cross-national comparability is supported—the NFC scale shows configural, metric, and scalar invariance across Polish, Flemish, Korean, and American samples[Psychologica Belgica NFC measurement invariance.](https://psychologicabelgica.com/articles/998/files/submission/proof/998-1-4257-1-10-20200826.pdf)—but mean closure scores differ significantly (higher in American and Korean samples), so meta-analytic pooling should model national means as moderators.

**Outcome conflation:** "acceptance" bundles belief, recall, and sharing[Pennycook, G., et al. 2021, "Shifting attention to accuracy can reduce misinformation onli](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03344-2); a closure-sharing association cannot claim to test the freezing mechanism, defined over belief revision. And **a raw count of false items endorsed conflates discrimination ability with response bias**—the closure account predicts a response-bias shift, but a raw count cannot distinguish it from a discrimination deficit. The fix: signal-detection scoring (d-prime vs. criterion) as default.

## Domain-Transfer Limits

The illusory truth effect anchoring the seizing mechanism was established in **truth-rating tasks**—isolated statements stripped of the source cues, social endorsements, and scroll dynamics of real feeds[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844). A live feed supplies far more early-available cues, so the lab effect may be amplified or swamped in situ. The large feed-adjacent studies (16-country discernment, N=33,480["Understanding and Reducing Online Misinformation Across 16 Countries on Six Continents."](https://static.poder360.com.br/2022/06/pesquisa-desinformacao-online-16paises-6continentes-mit-universidadedaregina.pdf)) measure analytic style—the *opposite* pole from NFC—licensing inference about closure only by assuming low analytic style and high closure are interchangeable, which the construct-distinctness chapter warned against.

The evidence concentrates in **climate and COVID**—domains entangled with political identity in ways that confound any closure effect with identity-protective cognition. The account needs testing on **low-identity, low-trust-relevant misinformation**, where a persisting effect would isolate the motivational mechanism.

## Time Validity

**The freezing mechanism is inherently temporal, yet the evidence is overwhelmingly cross-sectional.** No design tracks the same respondents' acceptance before correction, immediately after, and at delay—the design needed to distinguish a belief that never updated from one that updated and reverted. The closure account separates three quantities a static count folds into one: initial acceptance (seizing), persistence (freezing), and updating rate (permanence). **The account's most distinctive claim—that the permanence tendency specifically retards updating—is the one the static base is least equipped to test, because updating is a rate requiring two time points.**

The misinformation environment evolves faster than the research cycle, imposing a shelf-life: findings indexed to 2020-era platforms describe an environment that may no longer exist. The trait side ages well (invariance is durable[Psychologica Belgica NFC measurement invariance.](https://psychologicabelgica.com/articles/998/files/submission/proof/998-1-4257-1-10-20200826.pdf)); the environment side ages quickly. **This report's own conditional verdict is a mechanism-level claim that should survive platform change, but any quantitative effect-size estimate carries a validity horizon.**

## Sampling

The base concentrates in **WEIRD populations**[HKS Misinformation Review 2023, "What do we study when we study misinformation? A scoping](https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/what-do-we-study-when-we-study-misinformation-a-scoping-review-of-experimental-research-2016-2022/), oversampling the high-closure American end of the global distribution[Psychologica Belgica NFC measurement invariance.](https://psychologicabelgica.com/articles/998/files/submission/proof/998-1-4257-1-10-20200826.pdf)—so the estimated slope may be a high-closure local approximation, not the global slope. Within that band, it narrows further to students and online panels, plausibly under-sampling the high-closure, low-education respondents for whom Cosgrove's finding suggests the effect is most consequential[Cosgrove 2026, "Education, epistemically unwarranted beliefs, and the offsetting role of n](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191886925005306).

# 13. Conclusion: The Conditional Verdict

NFC is best characterized as a **conditional and mechanism-specific amplifier of misinformation acceptance, not a general one.** The evidence does not license the simpler claim that high-NFC individuals believe more falsehoods across the board.

## The Committed Answer

**NFC amplifies misinformation acceptance selectively, and the selection rule is whether the false claim is attitude-congenial and an ambiguity-resolving answer is available to be seized.** In the attitude-laden COVID-19 context, high-NFC respondents were less aligned with experts["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820); in the attitude-neutral illusory truth paradigm, NFC showed no moderation across N=2,196[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844). The contrast *is* the finding.

The named comparison sharpens it: need for cognition behaves like a **stable protective main effect**["Political affiliation or need for cognition? It depends on the post," PLOS One.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0315259), while NFC is **situationally contingent**—an association in one attitude-laden paradigm, a null in the repetition paradigm.

The **weakest** claim is the positive COVID-19 result—not implausible but fragile: a single sample of 265 where conservatism and NFC travel together without statistical separation["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820). The positive evidence cannot yet be called causal because of this partisanship overlap and because the mechanistic backbone (the seizing-to-freezing transition) is theoretically reconstructed at its middle links and observed only at its endpoints.

## The Cautionary Coda

The education literature qualifies any optimism: education's protection is offset by NFC, which redirects reasoning away from accuracy goals[Cosgrove 2026, "Education, epistemically unwarranted beliefs, and the offsetting role of n](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191886925005306). **If the same closure motive that offsets education also operates on literacy training, a high-NFC recipient might convert added competence into better-defended rather than more-accurate beliefs**—a testable prediction, not an established result.

## The Highest-Value Studies

Two flagship designs would promote the moderate and low confidence tiers:

1. **A longitudinal design** that manipulates or tracks NFC and measures acceptance at multiple time points—the only architecture that can distinguish whether closure precedes and drives acceptance or merely co-occurs. It should include an education covariate to avoid misattributing the education-by-closure interaction to NFC.

2. **A domain-comparative design** holding NFC measurement constant while varying content congeniality across attitude-laden and attitude-neutral materials within the same participants—converting the report's two-study contrast into a directly tested moderation. Given the N=2,196 null detected nothing, this design should budget for several thousand participants.

## The Open Frontier

The most consequential open question is the **temporal locus**: seizing predicts an effect on initial acceptance, freezing on persistence and updating-resistance, and the cross-sectional evidence measures neither cleanly. **The secure null constrains the acceptance-via-repetition story more than the updating story**[De keersmaecker, J., et al. 2020, "Investigating the Robustness of the Illusory Truth Effe](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167219853844)—if closure does not amplify the repetition route yet a positive association appears in attitude-laden judgment, the residual variance most naturally attaches to the **freezing-driven updating stage that no study has measured.** Future work should aim at the correction-resistance paradigm, not another acceptance snapshot.

The **crisis-context question** is urgent: the one positive association comes from a pandemic—itself a crisis that elevates situationally-induced closure["Who's in the Crowd Matters: Cognitive Factors and Beliefs Predict Misinformation Assessme](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10447820). Whether it reflects chronic NFC, situational NFC, or their interaction is unresolved. Inoculation strategies tested across 12 countries["Psychological inoculation strategies to fight climate disinformation across 12 countries,](https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01736-0) may be especially suited to high-closure conditions because they act *before* the cascade crystallizes a belief—sidestepping the freezing that makes post-hoc correction fail[Oktar & Lombrozo 2025, "How Beliefs Persist Amid Controversy: The Paths to Persistence Mod](https://cognition.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf3386/files/documents/OktarLombrozo-PsychRev-2025.pdf).

**The final structural claim:** need for cognitive closure is a conditional, congeniality-gated, and as-yet-non-causally-established contributor to misinformation acceptance, whose genuine effect—if it survives proper causal and temporal testing—most likely lives at the **updating-resistance stage in attitude-laden, crisis-inflected, high-closure contexts.** The disposition is neither the general villain some framings imply nor an irrelevance; it is a domain-specific amplifier whose boundary this report has mapped and whose causal core the field has yet to establish. The frontier is longitudinal, domain-comparative, and cross-cultural—and crossing it yields a counter-misinformation strategy that targets closure where it bites and accuracy-attention where repetition rules.
