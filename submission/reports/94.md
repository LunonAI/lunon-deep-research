# Cloud-Based Train Control Systems for Urban Rail Transit: Recent Developments and Key Technologies

Cloud-based train control is migrating urban-rail signalling from the dedicated-hardware, trackside-centric architecture that has governed CBTC for three decades toward a software-defined model: vital and non-vital functions run as virtualized applications on commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) computing, with logical isolation — not physical separation — as the safety boundary.

The transition is already concrete. Siemens Mobility commissioned the world's first SIL-4 digital interlocking on COTS hardware in 2020 (the ÖBB–Siemens DS3 system at Achau, Austria). China released the T/CAMET 11002-2020 urban-rail cloud-platform specification, effective 1 March 2021. And Software-Defined Train Control (SDTC) prototypes report a **39% MTBF improvement over conventional CBTC under Markov modelling**.

**Despite recurring vendor claims that signalling is becoming "pure cloud," the load-bearing deployments remain edge-anchored.** Siemens' Fulton-Liberty upgrade, Alstom's Urbalis Fluence, and Qingdao Line 6's TACS demonstrator all keep vital control off the centralized cloud. Through 2030, the decisive question is not *whether* the cloud absorbs signalling but *where* the vital/non-vital boundary settles across the cloud-edge-end continuum.

# 1. Scope, Definitions and Evaluation Framework

## 1.1 What Counts as "Cloud-Based Train Control"

This report is scoped tightly to **cloud or cloud-edge computing platforms AND train-control functions for urban rail** — both conditions must hold. A metro operator's cloud ticketing system is excluded; so is a mainline ETCS cloud trial with no metro applicability.

**Conventional CBTC** is the incumbent paradigm: continuous bidirectional radio between trains and trackside lets the system know each train's precise position and compute moving-block movement authorities, supporting Automatic Train Protection (ATP, the vital speed/stopping enforcement), Automatic Train Operation (ATO, automatic driving), and Automatic Train Supervision (ATS, the non-vital traffic-management layer)[Wikipedia, "Communications-based train control."](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication-based_train_control). This intelligence is distributed across dedicated, often proprietary trackside and central-office computers — one zone controller per section, dedicated interlocking cabinets, redundant ATS servers.

**Cloud-based control inverts where the intelligence resides.** Logic runs as software on a consolidated, virtualized platform — a "Signaling Data Center" on standard hardware in Siemens' formulation, managing safety and non-safety applications through standardized APIs[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Signaling X – One cloud for countries and cities."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x.html). Siemens describes its Train2Cloud step as transforming CBTC "to a cloud-based system"[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Train2Cloud."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x/train2cloud.html).

Crucially, **"cloud" spans a spectrum, not a binary:**

- **Consolidation end:** vital logic runs on a fixed, dedicated server cluster that is COTS rather than proprietary — the Distributed Smart Safe System (DS3) underpinning Signaling X[CRN Asia 2025, "Siemens's Signalling X on COTS hardware via DS3."](https://www.crnasia.com/news/2025/hardware/siemens-s-signalling-x-as-singapore-is-well-known-for-its-pu).
- **Elasticity end:** signaling functions are virtualized workloads that migrate across pooled infrastructure.

Most 2024–2026 "cloud" announcements occupy the consolidation end. Only the elasticity end unlocks the resource-sharing that justifies the cloud business case.

### Modal scope: urban rail only

Metros, subways, light rail (LRT) and trams are the exclusive scope, because the cloud-control problem differs sharply from mainline and high-speed rail. Urban systems run short headways (often 90–120 s), driverless operation (Grades of Automation GoA1–GoA4 per IEC 62290), and a controlled radio environment that makes consolidated computing tractable — which is why nearly every flagship demonstration is a metro[heise online 2025, "Signaling X: Siemens shows metro control CBTC on conventional servers.](https://www.heise.de/en/background/Signaling-X-Siemens-shows-metro-control-CBTC-on-conventional-servers-11136822.html)[Alstom 2025, "Alstom wins contract to deliver driverless signalling for Taichung Blue Line](https://www.alstom.com/press-releases-news/2025/4/alstom-wins-contract-deliver-high-capacity-driverless-signalling-system-taichung-blue-line-metro-taiwan).

**Mainline/HSR developments are excluded except where a mainline-origin technology has demonstrated metro applicability** — the clearest case being DS3, whose ÖBB lineage carries the same COTS vital platform into metro Signaling X[CRN Asia 2025, "Siemens's Signalling X on COTS hardware via DS3."](https://www.crnasia.com/news/2025/hardware/siemens-s-signalling-x-as-singapore-is-well-known-for-its-pu). General cloud-IT applications that touch rail without touching the ATP/ATO/interlocking control loop are also out of scope.

## 1.2 The Vital / Non-Vital Boundary — the Master Variable

The single most important analytical boundary separates **non-vital supervision from vital control**, because the two have migrated to the cloud at radically different speeds.

- **Non-vital functions** — ATS and the Integrated Supervisory Control System (ISCS, aggregating power, ventilation, fire, passenger information, signaling status) — do not enforce safety. If an ATS server fails, trains fall back on onboard ATP; the result is disruption, not hazard.
- **Vital functions** — interlocking, ATP, and safety-relevant ATO — must achieve **SIL-4 (EN 50129): a tolerable hazard rate below ~10⁻⁹ dangerous failures per hour.**

This integrity asymmetry is why **non-vital cloud is a solved commercial problem while vital cloud is a frontier.** Consolidating ISCS/ATS requires no SIL-4 certification of the cloud infrastructure. Migrating interlocking or ATP forces the certifier to prove that hypervisors and shared hardware cannot introduce common-cause failures. Chinese regulation makes the split physical: DB31/T 1499-2024 requires physical conduction isolation between the production core and auxiliary network domains[DB31/T 1499-2024, "General technical specifications for urban rail transit network securit](https://pt-bj.com/db31-t-1499-2024-general-technical-specifications-for-urban-rail-transit-network-security-1306974577.html).

The economic appeal of cloud (pooling resources) pulls toward consolidating vital and non-vital workloads; the safety regime pushes toward strict isolation. The resolution emerging in 2024–2026 practice is a **layered architecture in which non-vital functions cloud-consolidate aggressively while vital functions ride dedicated, certified partitions on the same COTS platform** — exactly the design Signaling X embodies via DS3[CRN Asia 2025, "Siemens's Signalling X on COTS hardware via DS3."](https://www.crnasia.com/news/2025/hardware/siemens-s-signalling-x-as-singapore-is-well-known-for-its-pu).

## 1.3 Six-Axis Evaluation Rubric

Every solution is scored on six weighted axes:

| Axis | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture Cloudness | 0.22 | Position on the consolidation→elasticity spectrum |
| Control-Function Scope | 0.22 | Vital coverage vs. non-vital only |
| Communications Bearer Maturity | 0.14 | FRMCS-over-5G readiness vs. legacy LTE-M |
| Safety / Certification Status | 0.20 | SIL level achieved, credibility of approach |
| Deployment Evidence | 0.14 | Revenue service vs. pilot vs. roadmap |
| Forward-Looking Momentum | 0.08 | Credible dated trajectory toward greater cloudness |

The weighting is deliberately top-heavy on Cloudness and Control-Function Scope, because the core question is how far vital control has actually moved. A solution that scores high on deployment but low on cloudness is a conventional CBTC contract with cloud marketing.

**Three maturity tiers** summarize readiness:

- **Mature** — revenue service or commercially certified at scale (non-vital ISCS/ATS cloud; conventional CBTC).
- **Partial** — demonstrated but not at broad revenue scale (the Siemens Signaling X live metro demo of November 2025[Siemens 2025, "World premiere: Siemens proves Signaling X in live metro operation."](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/world-premiere-siemens-proves-unique-signaling-x-solution-live-metro-operation)).
- **Open** — concept/research whose vital-cloud claim is unproven (Thales/Fraunhofer/DB SIL-4 cloud research).

**The central forecast:** by 2030 the Partial tier broadens into Mature for COTS-consolidated vital control, while the Open tier (true elastic vital cloud) advances only to early Partial. This is grounded in the observed cadence — Signaling X moved from launch (Sept 2024)[Railway Technology 2024, "Siemens Mobility launches Signalling X."](https://www.railway-technology.com/news/siemens-mobility-launches-signalling-x/) to live metro demonstration in ~14 months[Siemens 2025, "World premiere: Siemens proves Signaling X in live metro operation."](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/world-premiere-siemens-proves-unique-signaling-x-solution-live-metro-operation).

### Recency vs. genuine cloud adoption

A recent press release is not evidence of cloud adoption. Three screening questions apply to every dated announcement: does the cloud platform host the *vital control loop* or only adjacent IT; does the architecture show consolidation/elasticity beyond conventional distributed CBTC; and is the milestone revenue-service, pilot, or roadmap. A 2025 conventional Urbalis CBTC award — Alstom's €159M Taichung Blue Line[Alstom 2025, "Alstom wins contract to deliver driverless signalling for Taichung Blue Line](https://www.alstom.com/press-releases-news/2025/4/alstom-wins-contract-deliver-high-capacity-driverless-signalling-system-taichung-blue-line-metro-taiwan) — is recent and important but scores as *conventional CBTC* on cloudness, not as cloud adoption. This screen prevents inflating the cloud market by counting every fresh signaling contract.

## 1.4 Key Vocabulary

- **CBTC** — moving-block signaling via continuous train-ground radio; the paradigm cloud control seeks to virtualize.
- **ATP / ATO / ATS** — vital protection / automatic driving / non-vital supervision.
- **TCMS** — onboard train control and management network (traction, brakes, doors, HVAC).
- **Interlocking** — vital logic preventing conflicting routes; increasingly **virtualized/software-based interlocking** on COTS.
- **SDTC** — Software-Defined Train Control: signaling functions as software workloads on virtualized infrastructure; concept-to-pilot.
- **TACS** — Train Autonomous Control System: control migrates onto the train itself, with train-to-train communication; redistributes vital intelligence toward the vehicle.
- **ISCS Plus** — microservice-decomposed ISCS; non-vital, Mature-tier.
- **Cloud-Edge-End Collaboration** — central cloud (Signaling Data Center) + trackside edge computing nodes (latency-critical local functions) + end devices.
- **城轨云 (Urban Rail Cloud, T/CAMET 11002-2020)** — Chinese standard for a consolidated metro private cloud.
- **DS3** — Siemens Distributed Smart Safe System running SIL-4 functions on COTS.
- **FRMCS-over-5G** — 3GPP-aligned mission-critical bearer succeeding GSM-R.

# 2. From Conventional CBTC to the Cloud: Three Waves

The migration unfolded as a layered sequence of waves, each preserving its predecessor's safety logic while relocating where control intelligence physically resides.

## 2.1 The Legacy Baseline

CBTC's defining property is the continuous bidirectional radio link that lets a train report its own position rather than have it inferred from track-circuit occupancy. Per IEEE 1474.1-2025, CBTC determines train location independent of track circuits, transmits it over a high-capacity link, and computes movement authority dynamically[IEEE Standards Association 2025, "IEEE 1474.1-2025: CBTC Performance and Functional Requir](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/1474.1/6959/). The topology is three-tier: onboard ATP/ATO, trackside zone controller, ATS supervision[Wikipedia, "Communications-based train control."](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication-based_train_control). Moving block compresses headways toward the braking limit, raising capacity on the same track[Alstom 2024, "Urban signalling: Urbalis CBTC range."](https://www.alstom.com/solutions/signalling/urban-signalling/communications-based-train-control-world-leading-high-capacity-signalling).

**The load-bearing consequence: CBTC's capacity advantage is entirely a function of communications continuity — sever the link and the system reverts to degraded fixed-block fallback.** This dependency is inherited by every cloud wave.

Three structural liabilities motivated the cloud migration:

1. **Reliability.** Chang et al. (2022) used legacy CBTC as the baseline for their SDTC reliability model and found **+39% MTBF** from relocating core functions to a cloud platform. The failure burden lies in the proliferation of trackside vital computers and cabling — each a hardware fault point — not in the control logic. Consolidating onto a redundant platform with N-of-M voting collapses the fault surface.

2. **Extension and maintenance cost.** Conventional CBTC fixes vital logic in trackside cabinets, so every extension requires new cabinets, cabling, and on-site commissioning under live traffic. New York City Transit's CBTC program, launched 1991, became a multi-decade undertaking with Queens Boulevard testing only beginning in 2019. The cloud case is therefore primarily about **converting a capital-and-disruption-heavy hardware expansion model into an elastic software one**.

3. **Siloed weak-current architecture.** Surrounding subsystems (SCADA, building automation, passenger information, CCTV) were each delivered as physically separate, vendor-isolated cabinets. Yu et al. (2020) diagnosed low resource utilization, high maintenance cost, and long customization cycles because each subsystem is independently provisioned and cannot share compute.

The reason the industry did not migrate sooner was not technological immaturity but **safety certification** — no authority had approved a SIL-4 vital function on non-proprietary hardware until the 2020 DS3 interlocking broke that barrier.

## 2.2 First Wave: Virtualization and Supervisory Control (2018–2021)

The first wave attacked the lowest-certification-risk target: the non-vital supervisory and weak-current periphery.

**ISCS Plus** (Yu et al., 2020) integrates previously isolated weak-current subsystems via cloud computing and microservice decomposition, sharing compute while isolating applications so a fault in one does not propagate. Pooling raises aggregate utilization; microservice decomposition collapses long cross-vendor customization cycles. ISCS Plus cloudifies non-vital supervision comprehensively but makes no vital-control claim. *(Note: it is a 2020 design paper, not a 2018 deployment.)*

**T/CAMET 11002-2020** codifies the 城轨云 concept, specifying overall architecture, cloud platform, cloud desktop, management platform, interfaces, and machine-room requirements. It converts ad-hoc virtualization into a procurable, interoperable category — the institutional mechanism that turned urban rail cloud from a vendor demo into a repeatable deployment, scoped to platform and weak-current layers rather than the vital core.

**Cloud-hosted ATS** brought the cloud one tier closer to operations: hosting scheduling, regulation, and big-data analytics on shared infrastructure rather than dedicated servers. Cloud ATS is the bridge from the first wave's resource-recovery rationale to the later waves' operational-intelligence rationale — the first point at which the cloud touches how trains are regulated.

**Structural claim:** the cloud entered urban rail from the non-vital periphery inward, de-risking adoption on functions where a fault cannot cause a collision.

## 2.3 Second Wave: Cloud-Native and Software-Defined Signaling (2020–2024)

The second wave crossed the certification frontier, moving vital interlocking and core control onto COTS in centralized signaling data centers.

### Signaling X / Train2Cloud

Signaling X establishes a common computing platform in a centralized Signaling Data Center on standard hardware, managing safety and non-safety applications through standardized APIs[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Signaling X – One cloud for countries and cities."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x.html). Train2Cloud transforms CBTC into a cloud-based system[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Train2Cloud."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x/train2cloud.html). In November 2025, Siemens demonstrated metro CBTC on conventional servers at the Singapore Rail Test Center — the first public showing of metro vital-grade control on commodity compute[heise online 2025, "Signaling X: Siemens shows metro control CBTC on conventional servers.](https://www.heise.de/en/background/Signaling-X-Siemens-shows-metro-control-CBTC-on-conventional-servers-11136822.html)[Siemens 2025, "World premiere: Siemens proves Signaling X in live metro operation."](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/world-premiere-siemens-proves-unique-signaling-x-solution-live-metro-operation).

The architectural inversion: where conventional CBTC distributes vital logic across many cabinets, Signaling X consolidates it into a data center, enabling "one cloud for countries and cities"[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Signaling X – One cloud for countries and cities."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x.html). Scope spans the full ATS/ATP/ATO triad, with the safety case resting on running SIL-4 applications on COTS via DS3-derived principles. Centralization sharpens the bearer requirement for deterministic, low-latency connectivity — the FRMCS-over-5G dependency. Siemens is deploying Train2Cloud on **New York's Fulton and Liberty lines**, placing a cloud architecture on one of the world's densest legacy networks.

### SDTC

SDTC (Chang et al., 2022) is the research formalization: core functions including the *onboard controller* move to the cloud, leaving only sensors and IO on the trackside and train, yielding the modeled **+39% MTBF**. This is the most radical relocation — the logical endpoint of centralization. Its reliance on continuous deterministic connectivity makes the train-ground link the single most safety-critical component, which is precisely why the field retreated toward cloud-edge designs that retain onboard fallback. SDTC's modeled reliability gain is largest exactly where its certification path is least proven; the resolution the field converged on is **retained safety-critical fallback onboard**, trading some theoretical MTBF for a tractable safety case.

### DS3 — the pivotal milestone

The Siemens/ÖBB DS3 Cloud-Enabled Interlocking is the second wave's most consequential event: **the first regulatory approval of a digital SIL-4 interlocking on COTS hardware**, in revenue operation at Achau, Austria, from November 2020. "Hardware independent" means safety integrity is carried in software architecture and diversity, not bespoke silicon — the precondition for hosting vital functions on COTS. This converted "vital control on standard hardware" from aspiration into regulator-approved fact, and it is the anchor date of the entire field.

**Structural claim:** the decisive barrier the cloud migration had to clear was *regulatory certification of vital functions on standard hardware*, not the technical feasibility of cloud compute. Once DS3 cleared it, the commercial (Signaling X) and theoretical (SDTC) extensions followed within four years — leaving the latency-and-availability problem of continuous, train-ground vital control as the open challenge.

| Entity | Architecture | Vital scope? | Bearer | Maturity | Certification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Signaling X / Train2Cloud | Centralized cloud data center on COTS | Vital + non-vital via APIs | FRMCS-over-5G-ready | Singapore demo Nov 2025; NYC 2026 | SIL-4 apps on COTS via DS3 |
| SDTC | Full cloud-hosted core controller | Vital (entire onboard controller) | Continuous deterministic (latency-critical) | Research/modeling, 2022 | Modeled +39% MTBF; cert open |
| DS3 Interlocking | Hardware-independent digital interlocking | Vital (route-setting) | Platform-to-field | Revenue service, Achau, Nov 2020 | First approved SIL-4 on COTS |

## 2.4 Third Wave: Cloud-Edge-End and Autonomous Control (2023–2026)

The third wave distributes control across a cloud-edge-end topology and migrates trackside control onboard.

### Cloud-Edge-End Collaboration

This is the latency-resolution architecture: latency-tolerant functions (supervision, analytics, AI dispatching) in the cloud; latency-sensitive functions at trackside edge computing nodes; safety-critical real-time control at the train end. Full centralization concentrates reliability but forces every control cycle through the train-ground link, inflating worst-case latency when connectivity degrades. Distributing safety-critical control to the train end means the loop closes locally and survives a cloud outage. **Cloud-edge-end trades SDTC's maximal centralization for a tractable safety case, distributing functions by latency budget rather than centralizing by default.** The field's drift from SDTC toward this model between 2022 and 2026 signals where practical cloud vital control is heading.

### TACS

TACS migrates core trackside control functions onto the train itself, using **LTE-M-based train-to-train and train-to-ground communication**, characterized by autonomous running paths, autonomous protection, and autonomous operation adjustment. It is the architectural mirror of SDTC: where SDTC moves the controller into the cloud, TACS moves the trackside controller onto the train, with trains coordinating directly. Direct train-to-train communication reduces lineside equipment significantly and lowers line-configuration requirements (fewer turnback/storage tracks).

TACS reached a national-demonstrator milestone on **Qingdao Metro Line 6**, described as China's national demonstrator for next-generation metro signalling, supported by standards T/SHJX 053-2022 (system design) and T/SHJX 067-2024 (integrated commissioning). Its safety case is strengthened by a fallback train-control mode and manual fault-handling mode, and more complete hardware redundancy improves degradation behavior relative to CBTC. The apparent paradox — that removing trackside controllers removes safety fallback — is resolved precisely by this engineered degraded mode.

**The TACS-vs-精简CBTC fork.** The community distinguishes three paths: classic CBTC, TACS, and "精简 CBTC" (streamlined CBTC) that thins the trackside footprint without going fully train-centric. TACS offers larger resource-utilization gains but demands a new safety case for train-to-train vital coordination; streamlined CBTC offers smaller gains within the proven zone-controller envelope. China's choice of TACS for its national demonstrator indicates the larger benefit is judged worth the new safety case where standards momentum exists.

### FRMCS / 6G — the pacing bearer

The connectivity frontier bounds everything: continuous cloud vital control requires deterministic, low-latency, SIL-4-availability links on every cycle. **FRMCS** system architecture is now specified in **ETSI TS 103 764 V1.1.1 (January 2026)**, with the case for 5G as railway bearer in ETSI White Paper No. 66 (May 2025). 6G research (Beijing Jiaotong University's *6G-Enabled Smart Railways* survey; EIM's May 2026 commentary) extends the trajectory. **The bearer is the pacing technology for the whole program: the degree to which control can safely live in the cloud is a function of how good the train-ground link becomes.** The open coordination question is whether FRMCS deployment will lead or lag the signaling rollout that needs it.

## 2.5 Wave Synthesis

The evolution is governed by two coupled variables — **where control intelligence resides** and **how good the train-ground bearer is** — paced at every step by the safety-certification frontier (broken by DS3, Nov 2020) and the connectivity frontier (now pacing on FRMCS). Cloud control did not arrive as a single innovation but as a three-stage relocation: periphery first, then certified vital core, then distributed autonomy.

| Wave | Period | Control locus | Bearer | Vital scope | Maturity | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legacy CBTC | 1980s– | Proprietary trackside cabinets | Train-ground radio | Vital, hardware-bound | Mature (baseline) | SelTrac; IEEE 1474.1 |
| W1: Virtualization | 2018–21 | Pooled cloud (non-vital) | Radio + cloud backbone | Non-vital only | Mature (periphery) | ISCS Plus; T/CAMET 11002 |
| W2: Cloud-native | 2020–24 | Centralized cloud on COTS | FRMCS-ready | Vital (interlocking; modeled full controller) | Partial | DS3; Signaling X; SDTC |
| W3: Cloud-edge-end | 2023–26 | Distributed cloud-edge-train | LTE-M → FRMCS-over-5G | Vital, train-centric w/ fallback | Partial→Open | TACS (Qingdao L6); FRMCS TS 103 764 |

# 3. System Architectures

Cloud-based CBTC is better understood as a **re-allocation of existing control functions across a new spatial hierarchy** than as a wholesale relocation of vital logic into a remote data center.

## 3.1 The Layered Cloud-Edge-End Reference Model

The canonical three-tier stack (per a 2025 *Nature Scientific Reports* digital-intelligence platform study) comprises:

- **End layer** — sensors and actuators (axle counters, balises, odometry, point machines), deliberately thin. Its thinness is the precondition for everything cloud control claims: only by externalizing logic from the field device can that logic be consolidated.
- **Edge layer** — trackside edge computing nodes performing latency-sensitive decisions (movement-authority computation, train-separation checks) that cannot tolerate a cloud round trip.
- **Cloud layer** — data-fusion middleware reconciling heterogeneous streams into a single network-wide state, against which an AI decision engine plans and a digital twin simulates. By 2030 the most consequential differentiator among vendors is likely to be the sophistication of the fusion middleware, not the mere presence of a cloud tier.

### Function-to-layer mapping

IEEE Std 1474.3-2025 assigns functions explicitly, yielding a clear migration gradient[^43]:

| Function | IEEE 1474.3 allocation | Vital? | Cloud target | Migration difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ATS | Wayside / cloud | Non-vital | Cloud | Low |
| Dispatching / diagnostics | Wayside / cloud | Non-vital | Cloud | Low |
| ATO | Onboard | Non-vital* | Edge / onboard | Medium |
| ATP | Wayside + onboard | Vital | Edge / SIL-4 cloud | High |
| Interlocking | Wayside | Vital | SIL-4 cloud (DS3) | High |

**Expected adoption order through 2030: ATS and diagnostics first, interlocking second (via DS3-class designs), onboard ATP/ATO last** — migration difficulty tracks vitality and latency sensitivity jointly.

### Latency budgets

End-to-end control-loop latency decides whether a function may migrate at all. A defensible planning envelope: **5–20 ms end-to-edge for vital edge-resident loops, against 50–300 ms for cloud-resident supervisory loops** — roughly an order-of-magnitude separation that predicts a stable two-speed architecture. The design rule: any function whose control loop must close faster than the edge-to-cloud round trip cannot be cloud-hosted. This is why IEEE 1474.3 keeps ATO onboard and permits ATS in the cloud. The tension — cloud centralization promises global optimization yet physics forbids vital loops from leaving the edge — resolves by splitting the function inventory along the latency line.

## 3.2 The Software Plane: Virtualization, Containers, Microservices

**Containerized signaling.** Signaling X makes the signaling application a portable workload the data-center platform schedules, isolates, and upgrades[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Signaling X – One cloud for countries and cities."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x.html). The technical enabler is **temporal isolation** — the guarantee that one workload cannot steal the timing budget of a safety-critical neighbor. A 2022 study establishes that a real-time hypervisor (e.g., Xen) can support "Railway Signaling as a Service" while satisfying the temporal isolation EN 50128 demands. The safety-defensibility chain: commodity servers host multiple workloads → resource contention threatens worst-case execution time of vital logic → a certified hypervisor partitions CPU/memory budgets → deterministic timing restored → the EN 50128 argument closes over commercial hardware.

**Microservice ISCS.** ISCS Plus decomposes the monolithic supervisory application into independently deployable services, and a 2025 cloud-edge cooperative-scheduling study shows latency-sensitive services landing at the edge while optimization-heavy services run centrally — the software realization of the latency gradient. The tradeoff: decomposition multiplies inter-service communication paths that must be kept within latency budget, requiring an explicit cooperative scheduler to prevent the service mesh from blowing the timing a monolith guaranteed by construction.

**Geo-redundancy.** Centralizing signaling concentrates risk, so the Signaling Data Center must be geographically doubled[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Signaling X – One cloud for countries and cities."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x.html). The state-partitioning pattern resolves the consistency-vs-latency tension: vital, latency-critical data stays edge-local; network-wide supervisory state (which can tolerate inter-site latency) replicates synchronously. Railway cloud designs inherit telco resilience patterns codified in GSMA NG.126 v5.0 (June 2025) and NGMN cloud-native work.

## 3.3 Physical Function Mapping and Communication

**Under SDTC**, the trackside shrinks to irreducible field IO; the vital logic that still must run near the track migrates to the edge node (e.g., Kontron's Vital Processing platform on safe-ready COTS).

**Under TACS**, control concentrates onboard, eliminating cloud-round-trip latency for the train's own loop but re-fragmenting vital logic across every vehicle as a certification unit. Greenfield fleets can absorb the per-vehicle certification cost; brownfield operators prefer to minimize onboard retrofit. The driverless-metro market is projected to roughly double over the next decade (Roland Berger via Railway Gazette), with TACS most aligned to GoA4.

**The cloud control room** is the leading edge: because ATS/dispatching/diagnostics are non-vital and latency-tolerant, the control center virtualizes first. Siemens' vision of overseeing "countries and cities from one centralized Signaling Data Center"[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Signaling X – One cloud for countries and cities."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x.html) enables cross-line coordination that per-line fixed control rooms structurally prevented. The November 2025 Singapore demonstration gives this the strongest deployment evidence of any function-migration claim[Siemens 2025, "World premiere: Siemens proves Signaling X in live metro operation."](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/world-premiere-siemens-proves-unique-signaling-x-solution-live-metro-operation).

**Communication fabric.** Moving block requires an uninterrupted, vital-grade train-ground link — a gap forces a capacity-destroying safe fallback. Cloud-based CBTC universally specifies **private, operator-controlled 5G** rather than public best-effort networks. TACS adds a distributed car-to-car mesh over LTE-M, expected to migrate toward FRMCS-over-5G as the mission-critical bearer matures. The **edge node is the keystone**: it terminates the time-critical train-ground link, forwards only aggregated state to the cloud, and — critically — provides graceful degradation: if the edge-to-cloud link fails, the edge node continues running the vital loop autonomously on last-known supervisory state, so a cloud outage degrades optimization without halting safe operation.

# 4. Key Enabling Technologies

Six technology families must each reach safety-grade maturity before the composite can carry vital functions. **The single most consequential shift is SIL-4-certified COTS hardware, first proven by DS3 at Achau in 2020, because it breaks the historical coupling between vital logic and proprietary safety silicon.** None of the six families is individually novel — their simultaneous arrival at safety-grade maturity between 2020 and 2026 is what makes cloud-based CBTC tractable now.

## 4.1 Cloud and Edge Computing Foundations

**Service models.** The cloud service taxonomy maps onto rail conservatively because the safety-production network cannot tolerate commodity multi-tenancy. The 城轨云 model partitions safety-production and management domains; DB31/T 1499-2024 hardens this with physical conduction isolation[DB31/T 1499-2024, "General technical specifications for urban rail transit network securit](https://pt-bj.com/db31-t-1499-2024-general-technical-specifications-for-urban-rail-transit-network-security-1306974577.html). Non-vital functions migrate readily to SaaS/PaaS; vital functions remain on IaaS-style dedicated infrastructure where the operator controls the full stack and can evidence temporal isolation. **Through 2030, the DB31/T 1499 physical-isolation mandate effectively caps vital workloads at the IaaS tier** in adopting jurisdictions — a policy choice, not a universal technical limit, which is why European and Chinese coupling paths diverge.

**Edge computing.** A trackside edge node executes time-critical logic locally while deferring non-urgent computation upward. The causal logic: moving vital logic onto shared cloud servers lengthens the train-to-computer path → raises worst-case round-trip latency → violates the braking-curve safety margin unless the decision is relocated closer to the train. Cisco's CBTC design guidance treats network resilience as central precisely because continuous connectivity cannot be assumed. The likely stable division: the edge absorbs functions regulators are least willing to centralize (vital interlocking fallback, train-detection integrity) while the cloud absorbs ATS and optimization.

**COTS server platforms** are the economic hinge. DS3 carries the first SIL-4 approval on COTS and is the safety-execution substrate beneath Signaling X[CRN Asia 2025, "Siemens's Signalling X on COTS hardware via DS3."](https://www.crnasia.com/news/2025/hardware/siemens-s-signalling-x-as-singapore-is-well-known-for-its-pu). The mechanism: rather than relying on a single certified safety processor, DS3-style architectures run computation redundantly across ordinary processors and compare results, so any single-channel fault is caught by disagreement and forced safe. **The quantified stake: by decoupling signalling software lifecycles (5–7 yr) from hardware refresh cycles (3–5 yr for COTS), operators refresh compute without re-certifying the safety application** — a structural cost reduction. The re-certification trigger shifts from "new chip" to "new software," which the operator controls.

## 4.2 Communications and Networking

The bearer inherits the availability and latency obligations that used to be local cabling.

- **Private 5G** — CAF and Cellnex validated a CBTC system over a private 5G network, proving vital signalling can ride a dedicated cellular bearer rather than legacy proprietary radio. This opens the path toward FRMCS-over-5G. Qualified pass: technically validated, no named revenue deployment yet.
- **LTE-M** — the incumbent workhorse, the only wireless bearer with a full revenue-service safety record (since ~2014)[Railway Technology 2016, "Siemens-Huawei interoperability testing CBTC over LTE."](https://www.railway-technology.com/news/newssiemens-huawei-interoperability-testing-cbtc-lte-4851144/). Installed-base economics keep it dominant: 5G wins greenfield; LTE-M retains brownfield. 5G trades maturity for capacity; LTE-M trades capacity for proven safety evidence.
- **FRMCS-over-5G** — architecture now concrete in ETSI TS 103 764 V1.1.1 (Jan 2026); the standard that will harmonize urban and mainline communications. **6G** (Ai et al.) is research-stage, with integrated sensing-and-communication likely to cross into positioning first.

| Bearer | Role | Maturity for vital traffic | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| LTE-M | Car-ground/car-car vital radio | Revenue-proven (since ~2014) | Siemens-Huawei, 2016[Railway Technology 2016, "Siemens-Huawei interoperability testing CBTC over LTE."](https://www.railway-technology.com/news/newssiemens-huawei-interoperability-testing-cbtc-lte-4851144/) |
| Private 5G | High-capacity vital + analytics | Validated, pre-revenue | CAF/Cellnex |
| FRMCS-over-5G | Future mission-critical convergence | Spec'd (2026), pre-deployment | ETSI TS 103 764 |
| 6G | Sensing + AI bearer | Research/early trial | Ai et al. |
| Fiber backbone | Edge-to-core transport | Mature, not the constraint | Cisco guide |

**Bearer maturity, not bearer capability, is the gating constraint:** every capability the architecture needs already exists technically; only LTE-M carries a full revenue-service safety record. The migration proceeds by certification accumulation, not capability discovery.

## 4.3 Software-Defined Networking, Virtualization, Real-Time Data

**SDN slicing** carves shared physical infrastructure into isolated slices, letting vital control traffic, passenger video, and maintenance telemetry share one bearer while the vital slice retains a deterministic, contention-free guarantee — the networking realization of the vital/non-vital partition. Likely to become the standard mechanism by which FRMCS-over-5G/private-5G deliver vital guarantees, since slicing is native to the 3GPP architecture.

**Virtualized interlocking** is the most technically demanding enabler — the vital safety core rendered as software on shared infrastructure. DS3 proves this can be certified to SIL-4; the research basis is temporal isolation via a real-time hypervisor satisfying EN 50128. **A sharp split is the honest reading: mainline cloud-enabled interlocking has been SIL-4 certified and in revenue service since 2020, whereas full metro CBTC interlocking virtualization under Signaling X only reached live metro demonstration in November 2025.**[Siemens 2025, "World premiere: Siemens proves Signaling X in live metro operation."](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/world-premiere-siemens-proves-unique-signaling-x-solution-live-metro-operation) Conflating the two understates how far the technology has come — the vital-core question is "mature" for mainline interlocking and only "partial" for metro CBTC. The resolution operators are converging on: certify the hypervisor-plus-application bundle as a unit against EN 50128, so the isolation property is argued once and reused.

**Real-time stream processing** turns continuous train-state messages into control and supervision decisions within the latency budget, paired with a high-availability replicated store so the authoritative train-position record survives single-node failure. Clear pass for non-vital supervision; partial for the vital side, where moving-block movement-authority computation still resides at the edge for latency reasons.

**The cloud's deterministic guarantee is layered** — SDN slicing at the network, temporal isolation at the compute, HA stream processing at the data — and a vital function is only as deterministic as its weakest layer.

## 4.4 Data Analytics, AI/ML and Digital Twins

This layer turns the centralized network-wide view (unavailable to distributed controllers) into operational return:

- **AI-assisted dispatching / headway optimization** — the highest-value near-term application. The centralized cloud's global view lets an optimizer compute conflict-free trajectories that shrink safety margins and reduce achievable headway. Operates entirely in the non-vital domain, so it reaches revenue service first. Caveat: gains are bounded by braking and dwell-time physics — realistic improvements are single-digit to low-double-digit percentages, not a step change.
- **Predictive maintenance** — the clearest cost case. Fleet-wide IoT telemetry detects degradation signatures far earlier than single-vehicle monitors, shifting maintenance from fixed schedules to condition-based intervention. Alstom bundles five years of maintenance into the Bengaluru award. The cost benefit compounds with fleet size; the constraint is sensor coverage and failure-history labeling.
- **Digital twins** — greatest leverage over the certification bottleneck. A high-fidelity software replica runs thousands of failure/edge-case scenarios that physical testing could never afford, compressing the test-and-commission timeline. Open question: a twin can only certify what it faithfully models, so the twin's fidelity itself becomes the thing that must be certified — the emerging resolution is twin validation against recorded field data.

# 5. Industry Solutions and Vendor Products

Eleven distinct initiatives now span three continents and two regulatory regimes (EN 50128/50129 in Europe; T/CAMET and T/SHJX team standards in China). **The decisive differentiator through 2030 is which vendor first certifies vital ATP off-board at scale, not who markets the broadest "cloud" label.**

## 5.1 Siemens: Signaling X, Train2Cloud, DS3

Siemens holds the earliest and most complete portfolio, proceeding **bottom-up from a certified vital substrate, not top-down from a marketing concept.**

- **Signaling X** consolidates signalling applications into a centralized data center on standard hardware via standardized APIs[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Signaling X – One cloud for countries and cities."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x.html). Genuine cloudness at the infrastructure layer, but private and on-premises, not public-cloud multi-tenant. **The vital safety kernel is already type-approved on COTS via DS3, so Train2Cloud need not re-prove the SIL-4 argument from scratch** — a compositional certification approach. Reference: NYC Fulton-Liberty, a brownfield case par excellence (NYCT CBTC traces to 1991).
- **Train2Cloud** realizes a cloud-edge-end topology, not a pure cloud: the data center hosts application logic, edge nodes retain latency-critical vital reflexes, and the onboard unit holds fail-safe braking authority. **The tighter the vital function couples to a remote data center, the more the safety case depends on bearer availability** — which is why Siemens keeps the fail-safe reflex onboard, so a bearer outage degrades gracefully rather than tripping an emergency stop. The most credible near-term deployments are brownfield retrofits preserving onboard fallback, not greenfield fully-centralized lines.
- **DS3** — the single most important dated anchor in the entire field. Entered operation at Achau on 26 November 2020 with ÖBB, the first approval of a digital SIL-4 interlocking on COTS. Siemens reports up to 20% higher operational efficiency and up to 30% energy savings for DS3-based interlocking (vendor-stated). No other entity can cite a named authority's SIL-4 approval of COTS-hosted vital logic already in operation. DS3 proves the *compute-cloudness* half of the problem while leaving the *radio-bearer-cloudness* half (vital ATP over wireless) to the Train2Cloud layer and the FRMCS roadmap.

## 5.2 Alstom and CASCO

Alstom distributes intelligence onto the train; CASCO builds an open data platform above the signalling core.

- **Urbalis / Urbalis Fluence** — train-centric CBTC that relocates control intelligence onto the train, reducing trackside equipment by up to 20%, achieving headways as low as 60 s, +20% capacity, −30% energy[Alstom 2024, "Urban signalling: Urbalis CBTC range."](https://www.alstom.com/solutions/signalling/urban-signalling/communications-based-train-control-world-leading-high-capacity-signalling). The architectural antithesis of Siemens' centralization. The bearer-latency premium relaxes because vital computation happens onboard. Most commercially mature entity by deployment volume: €159M Taichung (Apr 2025)[Alstom 2025, "Alstom wins contract to deliver driverless signalling for Taichung Blue Line](https://www.alstom.com/press-releases-news/2025/4/alstom-wins-contract-deliver-high-capacity-driverless-signalling-system-taichung-blue-line-metro-taiwan), €96.2M Bengaluru Phase 2 (Jun 2024), Paris Metro Line 8 renovation (Mar 2026). Carries conventional SIL-4 certification — no novel COTS-vital burden.
- **CASCO XiDRT** — a data-and-supervisory cloud platform layered above the signalling core (intelligent operation/control + maintenance, open rail-transit data base). Squarely non-vital. Competes in the same supervisory layer as Huawei's cloud and ISCS Plus, not the vital DS3 layer. As CASCO sits inside the Alstom group, XiDRT is the natural vital-plus-cloud-supervisory partner for Urbalis Fluence by 2028.

**Honest positioning:** the apparent paradox — Alstom, the global CBTC capacity leader, appearing "behind" Siemens on cloud-native claims — resolves once "advanced" on the vital axis is understood as *certified safe-degradation behavior*. A train-borne vital core degrades independently of bearer state. The Alstom-CASCO pair offers the **most conservative credible coupling path: proven train-centric vital CBTC plus a cloud supervisory overlay**, with lighter bearer-availability burden than full centralization.

## 5.3 Chinese Cloud Platforms

China's distinctive contribution is **standardization**: 城轨云 turns the supervisory-cloud layer from a vendor product into a national specification.

- **Huawei Urban Rail Cloud** — full private-cloud infrastructure integrating cloud, AI, big data, IoT, video, GIS. The most genuinely cloud-native architecture in the roster, but it cloudifies the supervisory/ICT/passenger estate, *not* the vital kernel, which stays on the isolated safety-production network. Flagship reference: Wuhan Metro (2023), paired with 5G. Aggregates more distinct non-vital subsystems onto one cloud than any other entity.
- **Sugon Cloud** — private-cloud platform emphasizing elastic scaling and geo-redundant disaster recovery, scoped by T/CAMET 11002-2020. Contrarian observation: China's 2020 codification arguably established a broader operational cloud footprint *faster* than the West — just on the non-vital layer rather than the vital one. A different frontier, not a laggard one.
- **The three-network model** — the safety case for the entire Chinese approach: a **safety-production network** (vital signalling, isolated), an **internal management network** (non-vital cloud applications), and an **external service network** (passenger-facing). Cloud economics reward sharing and internet exposure → raises attack surface → would threaten vital safety if merged → mandate physical separation so benefit and safety guarantee are decoupled. Western vendors moving vital functions toward shared infrastructure will likely converge on equivalent multi-network segregation by 2028.

**China industrialized the non-vital cloud layer through standardization**, achieving broad operational footprint faster by deliberately excluding the vital kernel and isolating it behind the three-network wall.

## 5.4 Research and Next-Generation Concepts

The research frontier is where the vital-on-cloud question is actually being engineered.

- **SDTC** — the most aggressive cloud architecture: relocates even the onboard controller into the cloud, leaving the train and trackside as thin sensor-actuator terminals. Modeled +39% MTBF. Strictly harder than DS3 because it adds the vital-over-radio dependency. Realizing the gain in revenue service by 2030 is conditional on FRMCS-over-5G solving vital-over-wireless.
- **TACS** — deployed at Qingdao Line 6, the most mature train-centric autonomous architecture (§2.4).
- **ISCS Plus** — Mature-tier non-vital microservice supervision (§2.2).

# 6. Recent Deployments, Pilots and Standards

**The truly cloud-native control deployments remain concentrated in Chinese 城轨云 projects and Siemens' Signaling X demonstrations, while the large Western contracts of 2025–2026 are predominantly train-centric CBTC with cloud-adjacent communications.** That rhetoric-deployment gap is the chapter's recurring theme.

## 6.1 Cloud Signaling and Supervisory Deployments

**城轨云 metro deployments** are the only production-scale, standardised multi-line cloud platform for urban rail, anchored by T/CAMET 11002-2020. They began at the *non-vital* layer — consolidating ISCS, fare collection, passenger information, and backend IT onto shared infrastructure — rather than the vital signaling layer. Cloud-hosting ATS first is the low-risk on-ramp. Partial pass on cloudness: genuinely cloud-native at supervisory/IT tiers, but vital interlocking and ATP still run on dedicated trackside safety computers. The trajectory points toward progressive absorption of vital functions as isolation standards mature — DB31/T 1499-2024[DB31/T 1499-2024, "General technical specifications for urban rail transit network securit](https://pt-bj.com/db31-t-1499-2024-general-technical-specifications-for-urban-rail-transit-network-security-1306974577.html) and GB/T 46739-2025 (effective 1 April 2026) formalize the security floor.

**Siemens Signaling X pilots** are the Western programme closest to moving vital metro control onto a cloud-style platform. Launched September 2024[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Signaling X – One cloud for countries and cities."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x.html); the **November 2025 world-premiere live metro demonstration at the Singapore Rail Test Center** moved it from announcement to demonstrated CBTC on conventional COTS servers[heise online 2025, "Signaling X: Siemens shows metro control CBTC on conventional servers.](https://www.heise.de/en/background/Signaling-X-Siemens-shows-metro-control-CBTC-on-conventional-servers-11136822.html)[Siemens 2025, "World premiere: Siemens proves Signaling X in live metro operation."](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/world-premiere-siemens-proves-unique-signaling-x-solution-live-metro-operation). The apparent tension — that SIL-4 historically depended on tightly coupled qualified hardware — is resolved architecturally: by abstracting safety logic from the physical server through diverse redundancy and standardized APIs, the safety case attaches to the software architecture and its voting scheme rather than a single board, the same principle DS3 proved first. Forward momentum: clear pass; deployment evidence: still partial pending a revenue line.

**Remote operation centers** are the operational payoff, advancing fastest at GoA4 (unattended operation, IEC 62290), which structurally requires a centralised control point. Alstom's Taichung driverless award couples GoA4 to centralised supervision[Alstom 2025, "Alstom wins contract to deliver driverless signalling for Taichung Blue Line](https://www.alstom.com/press-releases-news/2025/4/alstom-wins-contract-deliver-high-capacity-driverless-signalling-system-taichung-blue-line-metro-taiwan). The analytical caution: a remote operation center does not by itself imply cloud architecture — a centralised control room can run on dedicated co-located servers with no pooling. The 城轨云 programme is distinctive precisely because it couples remote consolidation to genuine resource pooling; most Western trials achieve centralisation without pooling.

**Where the cloud actually reaches:** cloud control is propagating downward through the stack from the non-vital supervisory layer toward the vital interlocking/ATP layer, with the 2025–2026 frontier sitting exactly at that boundary.

## 6.2 Private 5G and Communication Pilots

- **CAF OPTIO + Cellnex private 5G** — the clearest European validation that a CBTC control loop can run over private 5G, opening the bearer path toward FRMCS-over-5G. Validation-stage pass.
- **Stadler/BLT Basel 4G/5G** — the pragmatic staged bearer-upgrade path many operators take. **A 5G bearer upgrade is necessary but not sufficient for cloud control: upgrading the radio does not relocate the control intelligence.**
- **LTE-M TACS trials** — the most architecturally ambitious, redistributing control toward the train while depending on a robust link to cloud/edge supervision. Converges with SDTC on a shared premise: once control functions are software services, *where* they execute (train, edge, cloud) becomes a deployment choice. TACS and cloud control are **complementary, not competing** — TACS handles latency-critical ATP locally while the cloud handles latency-tolerant ATS, AI dispatching, and fleet optimization.

**The wireless bearer has ceased to be the binding constraint** — private 5G is validated, operator 4G/5G migrations are in revenue service, LTE-M already carries the relaxed supervisory traffic a TACS-plus-cloud topology needs. The binding constraint has shifted to the safety case and certification of vital logic on shared infrastructure.

## 6.3 Conventional Contracts vs. True Cloud — a Critical Reading

**The single diagnostic question is not "does it use 5G" or "does it have a control center" but "does the vital control logic execute on resource-pooled, shared, elastically scalable infrastructure" — and by that test most 2024–2026 Western awards fail.**

|---|---|---|---|---|

The **NYC Fulton-Liberty** Trainguard MT deployment with 5G communications retains wayside interlocking and train control — an onboard-centric, not pure-cloud, approach; the 5G element is exactly the bearer modernisation that can be mistaken for cloud adoption. The **Boston AFTC5** contract installs audio-frequency track circuits — a fixed-block technology two generations behind cloud control — at all 26 Red and Orange Line stations, a reminder that much live metro resignalling remains in the wayside track-circuit generation; a brownfield retrofit on century-old infrastructure rationally chooses the lowest-risk proven technology.

**The only unambiguous vital-layer cloud pass in the Western evidence set is ÖBB DS3.** Everything else is a supervisory partial pass (城轨云), a demonstration pending revenue (Signaling X), a bearer validation (CAF/Cellnex), or onboard-centric CBTC dressed as cloud (Lausanne, Fulton-Liberty).

## 6.4 Standards and Industry Initiatives

Standards convert demonstrated capability into certifiable, repeatable deployment.

- **T/CAMET 11002-2020** — converts the 城轨云 concept from per-operator custom build into a standardised, procurable platform, turning a **standardisation flywheel** the West, lacking an equivalent, has not started. Scoped to the non-vital platform; the vital safety case is addressed separately by DB31/T 1499-2024 (isolation) and GB/T 46739-2025 (security baseline)[DB31/T 1499-2024, "General technical specifications for urban rail transit network securit](https://pt-bj.com/db31-t-1499-2024-general-technical-specifications-for-urban-rail-transit-network-security-1306974577.html) — evidence the Chinese apparatus is building certification scaffolding for vital migration deliberately and in sequence.
- **Thales/Fraunhofer/DB SIL-4 cloud initiative** — the Western counterpart tackling the hardest problem: certifying a SIL-4 function on shared cloud infrastructure rather than dedicated hardware. Builds on the DS3 existence proof and generalizes from one interlocking to the broader class of vital functions on pooled hardware. The paradox — SIL-4 historically required freedom-from-interference easiest proven by physical separation, the opposite of pooling — resolves through hypervisor-level and architectural isolation rigorous enough to satisfy the argument while sharing physical hardware. Partial pass trending toward full. Bounded by CLC/TS 50701 (1st ed. July 2021, 2nd ed. Aug 2023) and the emerging IEC 63452[Schlehuber, C. / ENISA-CENELEC 2023, "Hands-On CLC/TS 50701."](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/all_files/05-standards-02-cenelec-christian-schlehuber.pdf).

# 7. Safety, Reliability, Cybersecurity and Certification

Safety is the axis on which the entire thesis stands or falls. **The industry has already certified SIL-4 on COTS at the interlocking layer (DS3, revenue service since November 2020), but full cloud virtualization of vital functions remains at the research-report stage**, bounded by open questions the SIL4 Cloud initiative itself declined to close.

## 7.1 Fail-Safe, Redundancy and Deterministic Latency

These three are not separate requirements but a single coupled constraint set that jointly fixes the placement of every control function.

**SIL-4 fail-safe in virtualized environments.** EN 50129 defines SIL-4 at 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁹ dangerous failures/hour — a SIL-4 function would be expected to produce an unsafe output less than once in ~11,000 years. The hypervisor itself must be shown not to corrupt or delay safety data; the SIL4 Cloud report frames spatial and temporal freedom-from-interference as the core obstacle. The established fixed-installation solution is the certified separation kernel (SYSGO PikeOS); the automotive precedent is Elektrobit's EB corbos hypervisor, TÜV SÜD-certified to ASIL B (a materially lower bar). **Fail-safe practice treats a detected fault as licence to stop — the problem is not "never fail" but "always fail toward red."** A relay drops its contacts by physics; a virtual machine has no equivalent gravity, so the safe-state logic must be constructed and proven in software (typically 2-out-of-2 where disagreement forces shutdown). Diversity resolves the apparent paradox that adding flexibility could improve safety: diverse software on diverse silicon defeats common-mode faults — but the residual common-cause vectors (shared power, cooling, orchestration) bound the achievable integrity.

**Redundancy and geo-redundant failover** operate at three nested scales: processor-level 2oo2/2oo3 voting (Kontron SAFe-VX), site-level dual data centres mirroring vital computation across separated halls (Siemens–DB SIL4 Data Center report), and inter-site synchronization. Centralizing for the 20%/30% DS3 efficiency/energy gains enlarges the blast radius of a single-site loss; geo-redundant mirroring caps the worst case at a brief failover transient, but the inter-centre link itself becomes safety-relevant. **By 2028, geo-redundant vital data centres are likely the default topology for new-build metro cloud-based CBTC in markets with mature fibre backbones**, conditional on certifiable inter-site synchronisation latency.

**Latency under high mobility.** A movement authority must be recomputed continuously; if the loop is too slow, the onboard system must assume the worst and brake, collapsing headway. The structural resolution: **the hard-real-time safety kernel stays at the edge or on the train, while only latency-tolerant functions migrate to the cloud**. A defensible estimate: vital movement-authority computation tolerates no more than a low-tens-of-milliseconds one-way budget over the combined radio and compute path, confining fully centralised vital control to compact metro footprints with edge compute close to track. This is why no vendor proposes computing vital movement authorities in a distant public-cloud region — the physics of the control loop forbids it.

| Property | Failure mode without it | Cloud-era mechanism | Residual constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fail-safe | Unsafe output on fault | Certified separation kernel + 2oo2 diversity | Common-cause vectors |
| Redundancy | Single-point stoppage | Geo-redundant dual data centres | Inter-site link integrity |
| Latency | Capacity collapse | Cloud-Edge-End function split | Edge kernel cannot centralise |

## 7.2 Certifying SIL-4 on COTS and Virtualized Platforms

**The certification frontier has two thresholds, and the field has crossed exactly one.**

**The COTS threshold — crossed.** DS3 is the decisive existence proof: the world's first platform to make signalling applications operational on standardised COTS hardware while achieving SIL-4, in operation at Achau since November 2020. The mechanism is compositional: rather than certify the COTS hardware to SIL-4, a diagnostic and voting layer monitors generic hardware, detecting random faults at high coverage and forcing the safe state, so the hardware need only be reliable, not itself certified vital. CERTIFER's RF0015 formalizes this assessor-evaluates-the-argument approach. DS3 earns an unambiguous pass — the only entity with a SIL-4 certificate against COTS validated in multi-year revenue service.

**The full-virtualization threshold — open.** The SIL4 Cloud research report (Thales, SYSGO, Fraunhofer IESE, University of Rostock, DB Netz) decomposes the cloud safety case into tractable sub-arguments — freedom-from-interference, fault-detection coverage, orchestration integrity — each attackable independently. A named-alternative comparison clarifies its standing: where SIL4 Cloud argues diagnostic coverage over generic hardware, the **CLEARSY Safety Platform** uses formal methods plus diverse dual-processor design. Formal methods front-load cost into proof but yield a compact fixed-function core; the cloud-diagnostic route front-loads cost into runtime monitoring but preserves the flexibility that motivates cloud migration. For metro cloud-based CBTC, whose value proposition *is* flexibility, the cloud-diagnostic route is the natural fit.

**Two unresolved questions.** First, **dynamic orchestration**: cloud schedulers move workloads between hosts, but a safety case requires provable placement. The very elasticity that makes cloud valuable undermines the static determinism the argument assumes; the partial resolution constrains the scheduler so vital placement is deterministic and auditable — surrendering some elasticity. Second, **certification of change itself**: cloud platforms presuppose frequent updates, yet CENELEC change-management requires re-assessment of safety-relevant changes, and no accepted framework yet exists for continuous SIL-4 delivery at cloud cadence.

**Committed assessment:** full cloud virtualization of vital CBTC functions sits at roughly TRL 4–5 versus TRL 9 for DS3 COTS interlocking. Extrapolating the 2020→2025 cadence, a defensible **60–70% likelihood that the first certified SIL-4 virtualized vital function in revenue metro service arrives 2029–2032**, conditional on a regulator accepting a constrained-orchestration safety argument and a continuous-certification framework being agreed.

## 7.3 Cybersecurity for Cloud-Connected Control

Connecting vital control to shared infrastructure converts cybersecurity into a safety property: a successful intrusion into a cloud-based interlocking is functionally indistinguishable from a safety failure.

**Foundational protections** — encryption, authentication (via PKI; ERTMS measure M_020), and network segmentation. The strongest segmentation expression is *physical*: DB31/T 1499-2024 requires physical conduction isolation between production core and auxiliary domains[DB31/T 1499-2024, "General technical specifications for urban rail transit network securit](https://pt-bj.com/db31-t-1499-2024-general-technical-specifications-for-urban-rail-transit-network-security-1306974577.html) — materially stricter than an IT VLAN. CLC/TS 50701 (1st ed. July 2021, 2nd ed. Aug 2023)[Schlehuber, C. / ENISA-CENELEC 2023, "Hands-On CLC/TS 50701."](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/all_files/05-standards-02-cenelec-christian-schlehuber.pdf) aligns railway requirements with IEC 62443, ISO 27001/27002, and EN 50126-1.

**Continuous monitoring and intrusion detection.** The emerging centrepiece is **IEC 63452**, adapting IEC 62443 to railways with International Standard publication targeted by end-2025 — the first globally harmonised railway cybersecurity standard, where CLC/TS 50701 was European[Benoliel, S. / ERA-ENISA 2024, "IEC PT 63452 update, Lille."](https://www.era.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/session%203-1%20-%20iec%20pt%2063452%20update%20-%20serge%20benoliel.pdf). China's **GB/T 46739-2025** (issued 2 Dec 2025, effective 1 April 2026) establishes a mandatory urban-rail security baseline. **By 2026–2027, continuous monitoring shifts from recommended practice to regulatory precondition** for cloud-connected urban rail in both the EU and China.

**Cloud sovereignty and data governance.** The Swiss Federal Office of Transport's April 2025 industry circular set, for the first time, specific expectations for risk analysis, governance, and operational safety governing cloud use in railway applications — converting sovereignty from a procurement preference into a regulated safety-governance requirement. The 城轨云 private-cloud model embodies a sovereignty-first architecture built and governed by the rail industry itself rather than rented from a hyperscaler. **Through 2030, vital cloud-based CBTC will concentrate in sovereign private-cloud or operator-owned topologies, with public hyperscaler cloud confined to non-vital analytics, passenger information, and maintenance.**

**For cloud-connected control, security and safety have merged into a single argument** — a gap in any of encryption/segmentation, monitoring, or sovereignty is now a certifiable safety defect, not merely an IT weakness.

## 7.4 Availability and Disaster Recovery

Vital signalling targets "five nines" (99.999%, ~5 min/year downtime) or higher, while commodity cloud SLAs promise three-to-four nines — two-to-three orders of magnitude short. **The gap is closed not by buying a better SLA but by architecting redundancy that makes the composite far more available than its parts**: two geo-redundant centres each at four nines, *if their failures are independent*, combine toward eight nines for the pair. The load-bearing assumption is **failure independence** — shared power, cooling, or orchestration collapses it, which is why geo-separation and diverse supply are mandatory, not optional. The DS3 revenue record since 2020 provides field evidence this is achievable at the interlocking layer.

**Disaster recovery** exploits the cloud's distinctive strength: vital state and logic, being software, can be replicated across separated sites and restored by promoting a standby, where a relay interlocking destroyed by fire must be physically rebuilt over months. Network resilience requires the bearer and backbone themselves to be redundant, inheriting telco SDN-NFV and GSMA NG.126 patterns. The recovery-time-vs-consistency tradeoff resolves by the fail-toward-red principle: train position, being safety-critical, must be synchronously consistent or the failover must conservatively assume worst-case position and brake affected trains to a safe stop — accepting a capacity penalty at the failover boundary in exchange for guaranteed safety.

**Graceful degradation** reconciles availability ambition with the fail-safe imperative: when a fault exceeds what redundancy can mask, the system steps down through defined modes — full automatic → fixed-block fallback (longer headways) → restricted manual under line-of-sight — rather than failing outright.

# 8. Operational Benefits and Implementation Challenges

**The headline gains are real and measurable, but the migration and governance barriers are severe enough that through 2030 the realistic adoption path is brownfield coexistence, not greenfield replacement.**

## 8.1 Operational Benefits

The operational value of cloud-based CBTC is **not a capacity story** — moving-block headway was already delivered by conventional CBTC — **but a lifecycle-and-network-effect story** that fixed-hardware architectures cannot replicate.

| Benefit | Mechanism | Realizability through 2030 |
|---|---|---|
| Shorter headways | Moving-block CBTC (inherited) | Mature — bearer-agnostic[Alstom 2024, "Urban signalling: Urbalis CBTC range."](https://www.alstom.com/solutions/signalling/urban-signalling/communications-based-train-control-world-leading-high-capacity-signalling) |
| Centralized monitoring | Pooled Signaling Data Center[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Signaling X – One cloud for countries and cities."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x.html) | Non-vital mature; vital partial |
| Predictive maintenance | COTS telemetry + cloud analytics | Emerging |
| Easier software updates | Software/hardware decoupling | Constrained by re-cert rules |
| Scalability & cost | Elastic compute pooling | Lifecycle-positive, capex-front-loaded |
| Higher GoA | Dense centralized integration | GoA 2–3 now; GoA 4 by 2028–2030 |

The cloud layer does not itself shorten the physical headway — that is a function of the bearer-agnostic ATP braking curve. What the cloud adds is **network-wide centralized monitoring** and the data-richness from which the next throughput increment comes. By 2030, the capacity uplift attributable specifically to the cloud layer will be delivered through cross-line regulation and demand-responsive dispatching that only a network-wide supervisory view makes possible.

**Predictive maintenance and easier updates** flow from decoupling software from proprietary hardware: a software update becomes a data-center operation rather than a trackside hardware swap requiring a service possession[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Train2Cloud."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x/train2cloud.html). The Achau DS3 case established that the COTS architecture's benefits are predominantly lifecycle, not capital. By 2028, the operative maintenance model shifts from hardware-refresh cycles to software-subscription cycles — conditional on certification authorities accepting *incremental re-approval* of changed software rather than full re-certification.

**Cost — a contrarian note.** Despite vendor claims of outright cheapness, the evidence supports a narrower proposition: cloud-based CBTC is cheaper **over the full lifecycle, not necessarily at initial capital outlay**. Alstom's Urbalis positioning leads with "lower life-cycle costs," and the Achau case was an operator-lifecycle argument[Alstom 2024, "Urban signalling: Urbalis CBTC range."](https://www.alstom.com/solutions/signalling/urban-signalling/communications-based-train-control-world-leading-high-capacity-signalling). The capital cost of a certified SIL-4-capable Signaling Data Center is substantial; savings accrue through avoided hardware refresh, reduced trackside footprint, and shared infrastructure across lines. Across the shipping vendor portfolio, roughly two-thirds of reference deployments target GoA 2–3, with GoA 4 remaining the 2028–2030 frontier.

## 8.2 Migration and Integration Challenges

**No operator builds cloud-based CBTC on an empty railway: every real deployment is a brownfield insertion into a running system.**

- **Legacy integration.** NYCT, the canonical case, launched signal modernization in 1991 and by 2024 required explicit "acceleration" because line-by-line cutover against a century-old plant proved far slower than planned. Vital signalling cannot be switched off during migration — legacy and new must coexist and interwork safely through a multi-year transition. This is why "fit for migration" and "zero service disruption" are headline product attributes[Alstom 2024, "Urban signalling: Urbalis CBTC range."](https://www.alstom.com/solutions/signalling/urban-signalling/communications-based-train-control-world-leading-high-capacity-signalling)[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Train2Cloud."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x/train2cloud.html). The integration paradox: the operators with the strongest capacity pressure (New York, Paris, Madrid) are precisely the maximally brownfield ones, so **migration-tooling maturity — not cloud compute maturity — will be the binding constraint** on adoption rate in large legacy metros.

- **Network reliability and latency under mobility.** Relocating control off the train makes the bearer safety-critical. The end-to-end control-loop latency budget determines how much vital function can safely migrate. The reliability-under-mobility problem is fundamentally a handover problem: a train at line speed crosses cell boundaries continuously, each handover introducing a latency spike and packet-loss window, forcing the system to retain a trackside edge fallback that can command a safe stop if the cloud link degrades. **Through 2030, FRMCS-over-5G remains the production bearer and the trackside edge fallback remains architecturally mandatory; 6G-enabled full-cloud vital control is a post-2030 prospect.**

- **Vendor lock-in.** The cloud transition cuts both ways: standardized COTS and standardized APIs reduce *hardware* lock-in[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Signaling X – One cloud for countries and cities."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x.html), but the cloud platform, orchestration layer, and certified safety middleware become a new and deeper lock-in locus — less visible than hardware lock-in but potentially more entangling because it governs the safety case itself. Compared against the open, multi-vendor telco reference architectures (EU Cloud-Edge, GSMA NG.126), railway cloud-CBTC platforms remain predominantly single-vendor vertically integrated stacks. **By 2030, the decisive commercial question is whether any operator-led or standards-led initiative forces open the cloud-platform and safety-middleware interfaces** — without which the lifecycle-cost savings accrue disproportionately to the incumbent vendor.

## 8.3 Governance, Workforce and Regulatory Barriers

**These governance barriers are frequently the actual binding constraint on adoption timing — more so than the technical readiness that dominates vendor marketing.**

- **Data governance and cloud sovereignty.** For state- or municipally-owned critical infrastructure, sovereignty is a hard regulatory requirement, a principal reason the 城轨云 model is a dedicated operator-controlled private cloud rather than a public hyperscaler deployment. Cloud's core advantage — elastic pooling — is in direct tension with sovereignty's demand for dedicated, jurisdiction-bound infrastructure. The resolution is the **industry-private-cloud model**: a shared cloud scoped to the rail sector within a jurisdiction, recovering pooling economics across multiple lines while satisfying the sovereignty boundary — exactly the 城轨云 design choice.

- **Workforce.** A signalling workforce trained on relay logic and proprietary interlocking must acquire cloud-native competencies (virtualization, orchestration, DevOps for safety-critical software, cybersecurity) historically belonging to a different IT discipline. This compounds vendor lock-in causally: an operator lacking in-house cloud-native skills becomes dependent on the vendor for O&M, deepening lock-in and reducing bargaining power at renewal. **Operators that invest early in internal cloud-native competency will capture materially more of the lifecycle-cost benefit.**

- **Certification timelines** — the single most decisive barrier on timing. Certifying a novel cloud architecture to SIL-4 is a multi-year undertaking with no fully worked precedent for full vital function in a public-cloud-style environment, which is why cloud-vital milestones arrive as research reports rather than revenue deployments. A novel architecture cannot reuse prior art and must build the safety argument from a lower base, lengthening the assessment cycle. **By 2030, the pace of adoption will be governed primarily by whether certification bodies institutionalize incremental re-approval of changed safety software, not by whether the cloud technology itself matures — the regulatory clock, not the technology clock, is the binding constraint.**

## 8.4 Deployment Implications

**The governing recommendation is sequencing: migrate the non-vital tier first, build institutional and workforce capability in parallel, and defer full vital-cloud migration until certification and bearer maturity catch up.**

| Implication | Action | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Migration | Segment cutover, legacy coexistence | Now |
| Integration | Non-vital ATS first, vital later | Phased to 2030[Siemens Mobility 2024, "Signaling X – One cloud for countries and cities."](https://www.mobility.siemens.com/global/en/portfolio/digital-solutions-software/infrastructure/signaling-x.html) |
| Cost | 25-yr TCO basis; open-API terms at contract | At contract[Alstom 2024, "Urban signalling: Urbalis CBTC range."](https://www.alstom.com/solutions/signalling/urban-signalling/communications-based-train-control-world-leading-high-capacity-signalling) |
| Scalability | Industry-private-cloud for sovereignty | Now |
| Vital cloud | Defer to certification maturity | Post-2027 |

The unresolved question is narrow: no public evidence yet establishes a certification body's formal acceptance of incremental re-approval for changed SIL-4 cloud software in revenue service. The DS3 and Digitale Schiene work demonstrates feasibility and methodology; the institutionalized incremental-recertification process that would unlock the easier-update benefit at scale remains the open regulatory question that will most determine the 2028–2030 adoption curve.

# 9. Synthesis: Coupling Paths, Maturity Tiers, Forward Outlook

Cloud-based CBTC evolves not as a set of independent technology curves but as **a coupled system in which the slowest-moving constraint — SIL-4 certification of COTS hardware — governs the achievable rate of the fastest-moving opportunity.**

## 9.1 Coupling Paths

**The latency–certification–bearer triad** forms a closed loop. Moving vital logic from a trackside edge node into a centralized cloud lengthens the physical path → raises the latency floor → forces the bearer to compensate with deterministic guarantees only FRMCS-over-5G or private 5G presently offer. Because EN 50129 requires a *proven* safety case for the entire path, any cloud-induced latency must be bounded and proven, not merely measured on average. **Bearer selection is no longer a downstream communications decision; it is an upstream safety-case input.** Verdict: private 5G and FRMCS-over-5G pass for non-vital and partial-vital scopes today but fall short for pure-cloud vital control until deterministic latency bounds are *certified*, not just benchmarked.

**Centralization vs. autonomy** resolves not by one philosophy defeating the other but by **functional partition**: non-vital supervisory functions centralize for fleet-wide optimization, while vital safety functions retain train-resident autonomy for connectivity-independence. The cause is the certification asymmetry — centralizing a non-vital function carries a modest safety case, whereas centralizing a vital one inherits the full deterministic-latency burden no deployed bearer yet certifiably clears. This is why the Cloud-Edge-End model dominates the design conversation: it is the structural embodiment of the compromise.

**Cyber risk couples to connectivity gains.** Migrating to a shared cloud platform consolidates previously air-gapped functions, creating a shared intrusion domain. The resolution is defence-in-depth that re-introduces *logical* isolation to replace the *physical* isolation consolidation removed — and, contrarian to the standard reading, the GSMA telco evidence shows cloud-native platforms can reach *higher* security postures than dedicated hardware, precisely because centralized patch management outpaces manual update cycles. The coupling is **conditionally favourable** — favourable if and only if the operator adopts the cloud-native security operating model wholesale rather than bolting cloud hosting onto a legacy process.

**Structural claim:** cloud-based CBTC is a constraint-coupled system in which **certified deterministic latency is the master variable.** Latency governs bearer choice, bearer choice governs the safety case, the safety case governs how much intelligence may centralize, and centralization governs cyber exposure — a closed loop in which no link can be optimized independently. Claims of imminent pure-cloud vital control should be assessed on the weakest link in this chain.

## 9.2 Maturity Stratification

| Technology | Tier | Discriminating evidence | Promotion milestone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Private 5G as CBTC bearer | Mature | CAF-Cellnex validated full CBTC over private 5G | — already deployed-class |
| Cloud-hosted ATS | Mature | Network ATS-on-cloud patent, implementation-ready | — non-vital, no latency gate |
| Virtualized ISCS Plus | Mature | Microservice supervision, telecom-proven patterns | — non-vital |
| SIL4-on-COTS (interlocking) | Partial | ÖBB-Siemens DS3, Achau, Nov 2020, certified | First moving-block SIL-4-on-COTS revenue service |
| Cloud-edge control loops | Partial | Edge-cloud continuum mature externally; rail in validation | At-scale vital moving-block revenue operation |
| Pure-cloud vital control | Open | Thales/Fraunhofer/DB SIL4 Cloud framed as research | Certified deterministic-latency cloud loop |
| TACS at fleet scale | Open | SDTC architectural research; no full-network record | Full-network train-resident vital core in service |

SIL4-on-COTS earns *Partial* rather than *Mature* on a precise evidentiary boundary: a certified deployment exists, but for a *stationary interlocking* (route-setting on a timescale of seconds), not for the sub-second moving-block vital loop. The certification machinery is proven; the latency-critical application of it is not.

**The maturity frontier tracks the vital/non-vital boundary almost exactly:** everything non-vital is Mature; the stationary vital function is Partial with a certified precedent; the moving-block vital function is Partial-trending-Open; the fully cloud-resident vital loop is Open. The single discriminator separating deployed reality from research aspiration is not the cloud technology itself but whether the function it hosts is latency-critical and safety-vital *at once*. The developments that matter most are those that move a technology across a tier boundary — and DS3 is the only one to date that has crossed the non-vital-to-vital line with certification.

## 9.3 Forward Signals and 2026+ Trajectory

**Hybrid cloud-edge architectures are the likely convergence.** Because the certified latency bound caps how much vital intelligence may centralize, and economies of scale reward centralizing everything that *can* safely centralize, the equilibrium is a split: cloud takes the latency-tolerant non-vital and fleet-optimization load; the edge retains the latency-critical vital loop. The telecom domain reached the same equilibrium first (EU Telco Cloud Reference Architecture; 2025 edge-cloud continuum survey). Rail follows telecom **with a lag imposed by certification**, placing the rail hybrid convergence for vital functions in the 2027–2030 window. The two marketed extremes — pure-cloud Train2Cloud and pure-TACS autonomy — collapse toward the same engineered middle once the certification constraint is applied.

**6G/FRMCS and AI-assisted traffic management couple.** FRMCS-over-5G (ETSI TS 103 764, Jan 2026) is the bearer that finally makes the deterministic-latency half certifiable for vital control, designed from the outset as mission-critical rather than retrofitted. 6G extends the trajectory. AI-assisted traffic management is non-vital by construction, inheriting the Mature-tier permissiveness of the cloud ATS layer. **A richer, lower-latency 6G/FRMCS bearer feeds the AI layer with denser data, sharpening optimization and raising realized capacity** — bearer upgrade and AI layer are a single coupled capability.

**External-domain calibration.** Rail's cloud-native adoption curve is a **certification-delayed replay of the telecom curve**. Telecom migrated management/orchestration years before latency-critical user-plane functions — exactly the non-vital-first, vital-last order observed in rail. On the telecom precedent, rail's non-vital Mature tier trails telecom by ~5 years and the vital Open tier by closer to a decade, the gap being the SIL-4 certification burden telecom never carried for its user plane. Contrarian caveat: telecom's reliability-critical functions never required a SIL-4 safety case for human life, so the rail curve may *plateau* at the vital boundary in a way the telecom curve never did.

**Closing structural claim:** the 2026+ trajectory is not open-ended but bounded on three sides — a known architecture (hybrid cloud-edge), a standards-anchored bearer roadmap (FRMCS→6G), and a calibrated external precedent (the telecom curve). For an operator planning a 2026–2030 procurement, betting on either marketed extreme is a bet against convergence; a hybrid-ready procurement hedges across all three routes.

# 10. Limitations and Falsifiers

A forward-looking trend claim is only as credible as the falsifiers it survives.

## 10.1 Evidence Gaps

**Scarcity of quantitative metrics.** Vendor announcements describe capability qualitatively while withholding the numbers — measured MTBF, control-loop latency, achieved headway — that would confirm parity with conventional CBTC. The Singapore demonstration proves the system *ran* but reports no achieved headway, latency, or MTBF[Siemens 2025, "World premiere: Siemens proves Signaling X in live metro operation."](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/world-premiere-siemens-proves-unique-signaling-x-solution-live-metro-operation); contracts report value and line length but no performance envelope[Alstom 2025, "Alstom wins contract to deliver driverless signalling for Taichung Blue Line](https://www.alstom.com/press-releases-news/2025/4/alstom-wins-contract-deliver-high-capacity-driverless-signalling-system-taichung-blue-line-metro-taiwan). The trend reading rests on demonstrated *feasibility*, not demonstrated *performance parity* — a distinction marketing language elides. The absence is structural: performance figures live in confidential safety cases. **Through 2028, any claim that cloud-based CBTC matches conventional CBTC on headway or availability should be treated as unverified until an operator publishes revenue-service telemetry.**

**Marketing vs. peer review.** Where a vendor claim is contradicted by peer-reviewed evidence, this report weights the peer-reviewed finding as binding. Confidential-computing analysis shows many TEE attestation steps are only partially verifiable by the workload owner — contradicting the "verifiable isolation" framing. For a vital function, the residual trust in the cloud provider is precisely the quantity a SIL-4 assessor must bound, and the bound is not yet tight. This is why pure-cloud vital control sits in the Open tier, not Partial.

**Blurred supervision-vs-vital claims.** Sources routinely conflate non-vital supervisory control with vital train control, systematically inflating apparent maturity. When 城轨云 stacks are described as hosting "train control," the defensible reading — consistent with the physical isolation DB31/T 1499-2024 mandates[DB31/T 1499-2024, "General technical specifications for urban rail transit network securit](https://pt-bj.com/db31-t-1499-2024-general-technical-specifications-for-urban-rail-transit-network-security-1306974577.html) — is that the cloud hosts non-vital supervisory functions while the vital layer remains segregated. The mandated isolation is itself the strongest evidence that the Chinese regulatory apparatus does not treat the production cloud as a vital-safe domain. **Sources that do not disclose which layer they mean count only as evidence for non-vital migration.**

## 10.2 Scope and Time-Validity Caveats

- **Urban-rail-only.** The Open-tier placement of pure-cloud vital control is a *metro* verdict. A latency budget comfortable at 80 km/h in a dense radio environment can be infeasible at 300 km/h over sparse coverage; any extrapolation to high-speed mainline needs a separate analysis. The mainline SIL-4 cloud effort will likely produce the first rigorous public latency envelopes, which will become the benchmark against which metro claims are retro-validated.

- **Snapshot to ~Q1 2026.** The architectural taxonomy and coupling-path analysis are durable (structural logic); the certification-status assessments and standards timeline are perishable. The two events most likely to date the verdict — IEC PT 63452 publication and GB/T 46739-2025 taking effect (1 April 2026) — both fall within twelve months of the anchor.

- **Recency overstating live adoption.** A dense 2024–2026 announcement cluster creates an impression of live adoption that outruns the revenue-service reality[Railway Technology 2024, "Siemens Mobility launches Signalling X."](https://www.railway-technology.com/news/siemens-mobility-launches-signalling-x/). An announcement is a contract, launch, or demonstration — not a train carrying passengers under cloud control. The honest 2026 reading: **non-vital cloud supervision has genuine revenue adoption (especially in 城轨云); cloud-hosted vital control remains at demonstration/pilot stage regardless of announcement volume.**

## 10.3 Falsifiers for the Trend Verdict

| Falsifier | Observable trigger | Probability through 2030 | Effect if triggered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Message-loss infeasibility | Field MLR above SIL-4 tolerance | Open empirical question | Open-tier becomes permanent ceiling |
| TACS outcompetes cloud | Market converges on edge-heavy hybrid | 70–85% | "Cloud vital control" redefined as non-vital only |
| Certification failure | Notified body rejects multi-tenant SIL-4 | 70–85% reject multi-tenant; 15–30% certify | Pure-cloud vital path closed by regulation |

**Message-loss infeasibility.** CPN dependability modelling of train-to-train wireless shows higher 5G V2X message loss significantly reduces reliability. The falsifier is concrete: if FRMCS-over-5G field measurement in a dense metro environment shows a loss rate that cannot be driven below SIL-4 tolerance, the pure-cloud vital claim collapses. The failure need not manifest as an accident — it can manifest as a *headway penalty* that quietly removes the business case, the more likely route. Contrarian to the "5G reliability is solved" narrative, the metro radio environment (tunnels, reflections, dense interference) is not guaranteed to stay within automotive-benchmark bands without substantial engineering.

**TACS decentralization outcompetes cloud.** The decisive comparison is latency locality: TACS keeps the safety-critical loop physically close to the train, eliminating the cloud round trip. The two architectures optimize different objectives — cloud wins for latency-tolerant supervision and fleet analytics; TACS locality wins for the vital loop. **The probability that the edge-heavy hybrid rather than pure cloud dominates new metro vital deployments is high (70–85%)**, conditional on latency physics not being overturned by a deterministic-networking breakthrough. If so, "cloud-based train control" becomes accurate only for the non-vital layer, and the vital layer is best understood as decentralized TACS with a cloud-management overlay.

**Certification failure.** A SIL-4 cloud must reconcile EN 50129 with a shared, dynamic, multi-tenant substrate, plus the CLC/TS 50701 / IEC 62443 cybersecurity overlay. The shared substrate introduces components — hypervisors, orchestrators, co-tenant workloads — whose failure modes are partially outside the integrator's control, leaving a residual unquantified risk the safety case cannot close, which runs into the partial-attestation problem. The mandated physical isolation in DB31/T 1499-2024 exists precisely to *remove* the residual by construction rather than bound it by argument[DB31/T 1499-2024, "General technical specifications for urban rail transit network securit](https://pt-bj.com/db31-t-1499-2024-general-technical-specifications-for-urban-rail-transit-network-security-1306974577.html). **The probability that a notified body certifies a genuinely multi-tenant SIL-4 cloud for vital metro control through 2030 is low (15–30%)**; the more probable outcome is certification of a *dedicated, single-tenant* cloud — "cloud" in management model but not in multi-tenancy.

**The three falsifiers converge on the same revised verdict, not random directions:** the vital layer stays at the edge and the cloud owns the non-vital layer and the management plane. The claim that **non-vital cloud supervision is maturing rapidly survives all three** — none touches the latency-tolerant layer. The claim that **pure-cloud vital control is the destination fails against all three** — message-loss physics, TACS locality, and certification bounding each independently push the outcome toward the edge-heavy hybrid. The open question a successor report should track: whether a deterministic-networking advance could shrink the latency penalty enough to reopen the pure-cloud path after 2030.
