# Efficient and Proactive Exploration in Reinforcement Learning under Sparse Rewards and Constraints: Implications for Trajectory Planning

Exploration research in reinforcement learning has split into two maturing, increasingly convergent programs: driving an agent *toward* informative states under reward scarcity, and steering it *away* from unsafe states under cost constraints. The deepest recent progress lies exactly where these two imperatives collide — in trajectory planning.

**The central thesis of this report is that the algorithmic machinery built to solve these two problems — intrinsic motivation for sparse rewards, primal-dual constraint handling for safety — translates directly into reusable primitives for motion planning and MPC trajectory optimization.** Two further claims follow. First, contrary to the common view that intrinsic-motivation bonuses such as RND mark the frontier of sparse-reward exploration, the Go-Explore lineage and constrained primal-dual methods like DOPE show that archival return-then-explore and provably zero-violation exploration outperform bonus-driven curiosity on the hardest deceptive- and constrained-feedback problems. Second, exploration research and safe-RL research are not separate literatures: proactive methods such as preemptive-penalty constrained optimization already fuse both mechanisms, and through 2030 constraint-aware intrinsic reward is likely to become the default design pattern for safety-critical autonomous systems.

This report surveys more than seventy methods across the 2016–2026 window, anchored on canonical hard-exploration benchmarks (Montezuma's Revenge, Pitfall, MiniGrid, MuJoCo, Safety Gym). It tracks ten method families across both regimes, scoring each on a shared rubric weighted toward trajectory-planning relevance.

## 1 Scope, Rubric, and Vocabulary

**Scope and timeframe.** The report covers 2016–2026, with analytical weight on 2022–2026. The 2016 floor marks the emergence of pseudo-count exploration and the first scalable intrinsic-motivation methods to solve hard Atari games. The recency tilt reflects where genuinely new ideas live: constraint-aware intrinsic reward, policy-aware matrix completion, and diffusion-based exploration planners are all 2022-or-later. Earlier work is cited only where it anchors a lineage the recent methods extend. The inclusion filter admits peer-reviewed venues and arXiv preprints reporting concrete quantitative results on named benchmarks; every preprint citation is checked for a reported metric (return, regret bound, violation rate, or speedup) to keep the comparative tables grounded in numbers. Generic RL background appears only where load-bearing for an exploration or constraint mechanism.

**Evaluation rubric.** Six weighted dimensions are applied uniformly in the comparative synthesis:

| id | label | weight |
|---|---|---|

| R-2 | Sample Efficiency | 0.20 |

The 0.40 weight concentrated on Sample Efficiency and Trajectory-Planning Transferability encodes the report's priority: a method that explores brilliantly but cannot be ported into a receding-horizon optimizer scores poorly on the dimension that matters most. Theoretical Guarantees earns full marks only with an explicit regret, sample-complexity, or violation bound; strong empirics without a bound cap at the midpoint. This produces an informative tension — heuristic methods like prediction-error curiosity can outperform principled ones empirically yet score lower on guarantees, but the weighting (guarantees at 0.15) lets a heuristic method that dominates on efficiency and transferability still rank above a principled method that transfers poorly, reflecting actual deployment practice.

**Core vocabulary.** *Sparse-reward RL*: extrinsic reward is zero almost everywhere, non-zero only at rare goal states. *Intrinsic motivation*: self-generated reward for novel/surprising states, typically the error of a learned predictor. *Count-based/pseudo-count*: intrinsic reward inversely proportional to visitation, generalized to continuous spaces via a density model. *Constrained MDP (CMDP)*: an MDP augmented with expected-cost functions, requiring each cost below a budget. *Safe exploration*: maintaining constraint satisfaction throughout learning, not only at convergence. *Proactive exploration*: directing effort toward predicted-informative states rather than wandering randomly; Spark's adaptive branching toward high-uncertainty decision states[Spark: Strategic Policy-Aware Exploration via Dynamic Branching 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20209) is canonical. Notation: extrinsic reward `r`, intrinsic reward `r_int`, expected cost `J_c(π)` with budget `d`, so the constraint reads `J_c(π) ≤ d`. *Regret* is the cumulative gap between optimal and learned-agent return. A *frontier sub-goal* — a state on the boundary between explored and unexplored territory — is the linchpin of the planning bridge, the primitive exploration and motion planning literally share.

## 2 Problem Formulations: Sparse-Reward and Constrained MDPs

Sparse-reward RL and the CMDP are not two flavors of one difficulty but two structurally distinct failures of the standard MDP. A sparse-reward MDP keeps the objective intact but strips the learning signal to a near-zero vector. A CMDP keeps the signal dense but adds a cost scalar and a threshold the policy may not cross, turning maximization into constrained optimization[A Survey of Constraint Formulations in Safe RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02025v1).

**The central claim is that the two regimes demand opposite exploration postures: sparse rewards reward aggressive, directed information-seeking, whereas constraints penalize that aggression and force a conservative, feasibility-first posture.**

| Axis | Sparse-reward MDP | Constrained MDP |
|---|---|---|
| What is scarce | Learning signal (reward ≈ 0) | Feasible region (cost budget) |
| Objective | maximize return | maximize return s.t. cost ≤ threshold[A Survey of Constraint Formulations in Safe RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02025v1) |
| Posture rewarded | Aggressive, directed | Conservative, feasibility-first |
| Canonical failure | ε-greedy never reaches reward | Unsafe exploration violates cost |
| Benchmark | Montezuma's Revenge, MiniGrid | Safety Gym, constrained MuJoCo |

### 2.1 Why Sparse Rewards Make Exploration Hard

Sparse-reward difficulty is not one problem but three compounding ones.

**Vanishing gradient and exponential visitation cost.** In Montezuma's Revenge, the first reward sits behind a long sequence of unrewarded correct actions[Go-Explore: a New Approach for Hard-Exploration Problems](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10995v4). When the reward signal is zero across nearly every trajectory, the policy gradient collapses toward variance-dominated noise, so network weights barely move and the policy stays functionally random for millions of frames. The two effects compound: a random policy needs exponentially many samples to stumble onto the first reward, and until it does, it has no gradient to bias it away from random. If reaching the first reward requires ~100 correct decisions over 18 actions, an undirected walk faces probability on the order of 18⁻¹⁰⁰ — beyond any feasible budget. This is why the Go-Explore family abandoned undirected noise entirely for archiving and deterministically returning to promising states[First return, then explore](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919v6).

**Credit assignment over long horizons.** A reward delivered at step 500 must propagate back to the pivotal decision at step 3[Survey on Sparse Reward in Deep RL](https://www.jsjkx.com/EN/10.11896/jsjkx.190200352). A long horizon inflates the variance of return estimates, which slows value-function convergence and delays the moment the agent can distinguish the pivotal early action from irrelevant ones. Because this chain runs through variance rather than bias, collecting more samples helps only at the square-root rate. Notably, credit assignment and exploration are entangled: offline demonstrations resolve both at once by supplying trajectories that already reach the reward, collapsing the credit horizon and the exploration burden simultaneously[RL with Sparse Rewards using Guidance from Offline Demonstrations](https://openreview.net/forum?id=YJ1WzgMVsMt).

**Failure of undirected exploration.** ε-greedy and Gaussian noise produce a random walk whose expected displacement grows only as √steps, so covering a state space of diameter D by diffusion takes ~D² steps[Exploration in Deep RL survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00824). **The structural defect is that undirected noise has no memory: nothing biases the next action away from already-visited states, so the agent re-explores the same neighborhood indefinitely.** Goal-oriented trajectories fix this by replacing random actions with random goal selection followed by directed steps[Goal-oriented Trajectories for Efficient Exploration](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02078v1) — coverage improves when noise is replaced by temporally extended, committed goal-reaching, because the binding constraint was never the quantity of randomness but its incoherence.

The winning method families attack all three at once: Go-Explore's archive shortens the credit horizon, directs exploration, and defeats diffusion simultaneously.

### 2.2 Constrained MDPs and Safe-RL Formalism

"The constraint" is never a single object but a three-way choice, logically prior to any algorithm.

**Expected-cost vs. hard per-step constraints.** The default CMDP bounds the *expectation* of summed episode cost[A Survey of Constraint Formulations in Safe RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02025v1)[Provably Efficient Model-Free Algorithms for Non-stationary CMDPs](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/wei23b.html). A hard per-step constraint demands the cost at every step respect a threshold — mandatory when a single violation (a collision, a tip-over) is catastrophic and cannot be averaged away. A policy can satisfy an expected-cost bound while violating catastrophically on a minority of episodes. LSVI-AE targets the harder setting, achieving `O(H√(dK))` hard-constraint violation for linear costs[Safe RL with Instantaneous Constraints LSVI-AE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30170). For trajectory planning, where a collision is not amortizable across a fleet, safety-critical stacks will likely converge on per-step formulations rather than the cumulative expectation the RL theory community defaults to.

**Safety during learning vs. at deployment.** Safe exploration requires the constraint to hold *throughout training*, which is far harder than shipping a safe final policy. During learning, the cost model carries large epistemic uncertainty, so a reward-greedy explorer probes exactly the high-uncertainty regions where cost is least known — precisely where violations are most likely. **The very states most informative about the constraint are the states that most risk violating it.** LSVI-AE accepts a bounded `O(H√(dK))` violation during learning as the price of discovering the constraint boundary[Safe RL with Instantaneous Constraints LSVI-AE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30170); demanding zero training violations stalls learning because some boundary-probing is information-theoretically necessary to locate the feasible set.

**Probabilistic constraints.** Chance constraints bound the *probability* of a bad event below a tolerance δ — the natural language of safety under perception uncertainty. The risk-bounded motion-planning framework propagates uncertainty with an Unscented Kalman Filter and applies distributionally robust risk constraints[Risk Bounded Nonlinear Robot Motion Planning](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.01483). Probabilistic constraints sit between expected-cost and hard per-step in strictness, controlling how rare a violation may be. They are the formulation most likely to dominate real-world autonomous navigation through 2030, because they natively express the chance-of-collision tolerances regulators specify, and the UKF-plus-robust pipeline is already tractable.

### 2.3 Efficient vs. Proactive Exploration

The report's two title words name dual objectives attached to dual regimes. **"Efficient" exploration is the objective under sparse rewards — reaching a rare positive event fast; "proactive" exploration is the objective under constraints — avoiding a rare negative event reliably.** The two are duals: the sparse-reward agent chases a needle, the constrained agent avoids a cliff.

*Directed information-seeking* — choosing exploratory actions to reduce the agent's own uncertainty — is strictly more sample-efficient than undirected noise because a value bonus from ensemble disagreement concentrates attention where value is poorly known, then collapses there and redirects to the next frontier[Value Bonuses using Ensemble Errors for Exploration](http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12375v1). *Proactive sub-goal setting* commits in advance to reaching a chosen frontier sub-goal, then switches strategy — the architecture of Go-Explore's "first return, then explore"[First return, then explore](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919v6). By returning deterministically to the edge of the known region, the agent achieves linear rather than quadratic frontier advance, which is why Go-Explore reached superhuman Montezuma scores where intrinsic-motivation methods plateaued. Cell-Free Latent Go-Explore generalizes the archive to a learned latent partition[Cell-Free Latent Go-Explore](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14928v3); Time-Myopic Go-Explore learns the return phase as a time-to-reach representation[Time-Myopic Go-Explore](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05635v1); Intelligent Go-Explore uses a foundation model for state selection[Intelligent Go-Explore](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15143v4).

Real tasks are rarely pure — a warehouse robot faces both a sparse delivery reward and a hard collision constraint — but the two objectives compose rather than conflict, because they operate on different scarce resources. Production motion-planning stacks will likely run an efficient information-gain module and a proactive constraint-shielding module in parallel.

### 2.4 Constraint Taxonomy Dictates Method Choice

| Constraint type | Formal object | Representative method | Violation bound | Planning analogue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Expected cumulative cost | E[Σcost] ≤ d | CPO, Lagrangian[Provably Efficient Model-Free Algorithms for Non-stationary CMDPs](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/wei23b.html) | sublinear cumulative | soft cost-to-go |
| Instantaneous / per-step | cost_t ≤ d ∀t | LSVI-AE[Safe RL with Instantaneous Constraints LSVI-AE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30170) | O(H√(dK)) hard | per-step collision check |
| Probabilistic / risk | P(violation) ≤ δ | distributionally robust UKF[Risk Bounded Nonlinear Robot Motion Planning](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.01483) | chance-bounded | chance-constrained MPC |

The three families are **formally non-interchangeable**: a policy optimal under one can be arbitrarily bad under another. Because the aggregation operators differ — sum-then-expect vs. per-step-max vs. tail-probability — no single algorithm's guarantee transfers across families, which is why LSVI-AE had to prove a fresh hard-violation bound rather than reuse a CMDP regret result[Safe RL with Instantaneous Constraints LSVI-AE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30170).

A constraint's *physical amortizability* determines its correct formal aggregation. A collision is non-amortizable, forcing a hard per-step type; energy is amortizable, permitting expected-cumulative; mismatching the two — encoding collision as expected-cost — produces a policy provably optimal and physically unsafe at once. This yields a decision procedure: expected-cost constraints admit Lagrangian/CPO primal-dual methods; hard per-step constraints demand LSVI-AE-style bounded exploration or a shielding filter; probabilistic constraints demand a distributionally robust optimizer. The strictness ladder runs expected-cost < probabilistic < hard per-step, each step buying tighter safety at the cost of more conservative exploration.

**The load-bearing conclusion is that problem formulation is the decisive design act: the choice between sparse-reward and constrained framing, and within the latter the constraint type, fixes the exploration posture, the provable guarantee, and the admissible algorithm before a line of learning code is written.**

## 3 Intrinsic Motivation and Curiosity

Curiosity-driven exploration is the oldest and most influential response to sparse reward, and it remains the reference against which newer families are measured. By 2026 it has matured from a single prediction-error heuristic into at least three mechanistically distinct signal classes. **The central claim is that prediction error is a proxy for epistemic uncertainty, and the quality of a curiosity method reduces almost entirely to how faithfully that proxy tracks learnable novelty rather than irreducible environmental stochasticity.**

### 3.1 Prediction-Error and Surprise-Based Curiosity

**ICM (2017)** does not predict raw pixels; it learns an inverse dynamics model mapping consecutive states to the action between them, then computes forward-model prediction error in that learned feature space. **By filtering the representation through an inverse model that encodes only what the agent's actions affect, ICM discards uncontrollable environmental features, attenuating spurious curiosity.** It accelerates learning in controllable, low-noise domains but collapses when the feature space still admits stochastic transitions[Environmental Features in Sparse-Reward Deep RL, JAIR 2023](https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/14390/26919/34080)[Exploration in Deep RL survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00824).

**RND (2018)** sidesteps ICM's vulnerability economically: the novelty signal is the error of a trainable network matching a fixed, randomly-initialized target on the observed state[Exploration by Random Network Distillation](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.12894). **Because the target is deterministic, there is no irreducible noise to predict; the only reason the predictor errs is that it has seen a state too few times, making RND's error a clean count-like novelty proxy** — a pseudo-count in all but name. RND achieved the first better-than-human-average Montezuma's Revenge performance without demonstrations. Its limitation: a single scalar error compresses "how novel" too coarsely, so distributional RND (2024) shows the point-estimate design causes bonus saturation and anti-exploration in offline settings[Distributional Random Network Distillation 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09750v4).

**Information-theoretic surprisal** reframes the family under one object: the KL divergence between the agent's beliefs before and after an observation. **Good curiosity should reward the epistemic shift from prior to posterior, not raw prediction error, because only the former is immune to irreducible aleatoric noise** — the single most important conceptual tool for diagnosing every curiosity failure mode. The 2025 AUTODISCOVERY work drives open-ended scientific discovery via Bayesian surprise over an LLM's hypothesis beliefs[AUTODISCOVERY: Open-ended Scientific Discovery via Bayesian Surprise 2025](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/23b127521af7ca7a42f5cdb7507be4f2-Paper-Conference.pdf), and the 2026 Strategy-aware Surprise (SuS) method decomposes surprisal into Strategy Stability and Strategy Surprise for a more stable signal[SuS: Strategy-aware Surprise for Intrinsic Exploration 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10349v1).

The three formulations locate the defense against aleatoric noise differently — in the representation (ICM), the target (RND), or the objective (KL surprisal) — making curiosity research from 2017 to 2026 a systematic migration of the noise-robustness burden across the components of any predictor.

### 3.2 Across-Training vs. Intra-Life Novelty

A question the signal-design work leaves untouched is the *reference frame* against which novelty is judged. Deep Curiosity Search argued that **rewarding novelty relative to the entire training history actively discourages agents from revisiting initially non-rewarding states that could later become stepping stones**[Deep Curiosity Search](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.00553) — a monotone decaying bonus cannot simultaneously attract an agent back to a state and push it onward.

Go-Explore formalized this into two precise failure modes. **Detachment** is losing track of promising frontiers because consuming the novelty bonus erases the gradient back to them; **derailment** is being knocked off course by exploratory stochasticity before reaching a known frontier[Go-Explore: a New Approach for Hard-Exploration Problems](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10995v4). Go-Explore's two-phase decoupling — return deterministically to an archived state, then explore — defeats detachment via an explicit archive and derailment via deterministic return, yielding superhuman Montezuma and Pitfall scores no pure intrinsic-motivation method reached[First return, then explore](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919v6). The lineage from hand-designed cells → learned latent partitions → foundation-model archiving is the clearest single trend line in hard-exploration research.

The deeper lesson: "novelty" is two quantities serving incompatible purposes. Across-training novelty answers "have I ever learned about this state" (what to learn next); episodic novelty answers "is this state new within this attempt" (how to behave without looping). Robust exploration needs both — a persistent term for long-horizon progress and an episodic term to prevent within-episode stalling. This hands motion planning a ready-made principle: separate the persistent frontier memory from the episodic anti-looping term, exactly as adaptive informative-path-planning on dynamic graphs does[Deep RL with Dynamic Graphs for Adaptive Informative Path Planning](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04894).

### 3.3 Recent Advances (2022–2026)

**DISCOVER (2025)** breaks from undirected curiosity toward goal-directed exploration with no prior task information: it selects exploratory goals *in the direction of the target task*, extracting that direction from existing value estimates[DISCOVER: Automated Curricula for Sparse-Reward RL 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.19850). Crucially, it formally bounds the time until the target task becomes achievable in terms of the agent's initial distance to the target — **independent of the volume of the full goal space.** This distance-dependent, volume-independent bound is the chapter's most important theoretical result, the property separating tractable from intractable long-horizon exploration, and it earns DISCOVER a guarantee pass where ICM and RND remain heuristic.

**Episodic Curiosity through Reachability (2018)** forms its bonus from the number of environment steps to reach the current observation from episodic memory[Episodic Curiosity through Reachability](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02274v5). This is immune to the noisy-TV trap: a flickering screen is one step away in reachability terms regardless of pixel unpredictability, so it earns no bonus. Elliptical bonuses generalize this to continuous spaces via Mahalanobis distance in a learned embedding[Exploration in Deep RL survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00824).

**The noisy-TV problem** is the defining unsolved failure mode: a prediction-error agent becomes permanently transfixed by any source of irreducible randomness, mistaking aleatoric noise for epistemic novelty. RND's deterministic target and ECR's reachability distance are both *structural* fixes. Reward hacking is the second-order pathology — any learned intrinsic signal creates an incentive the agent may game, and in multi-agent settings a miscalibrated bonus can actively prevent convergence[Exploration with Unreliable Intrinsic Reward in Multi-Agent RL](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02138v1). **All three limitations are facets of one problem: an intrinsic signal is only as good as its ability to distinguish reducible from irreducible uncertainty, and no current method does this perfectly.** On Safety Control the family earns an explicit fail, because undirected curiosity pushes agents toward unfamiliar and potentially unsafe states — the precise reason constrained methods need a separate cost channel.

| Method | Mechanism Clarity | Sample Efficiency | Guarantees | Safety | Transferability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ICM (2017) | Pass | Pass low-noise / fail stochastic | Heuristic | Fail | High — feature space as reachability |
| RND (2018) | Pass | Strong / fail noisy-TV | Heuristic | Fail | Medium — frontier detector |
| KL surprisal / SuS (2026) | Strong | Mixed | Partial | Fail | Medium — belief-shift as info gain |
| Go-Explore (2021) | Pass | Strong on hard-exploration | Heuristic | Fail | High — archive as frontier set |
| DISCOVER (2025) | Pass | Pass | **Pass — volume-independent bound** | Fail | High — goal-biased sampling |
| Episodic Reachability (2018) | Pass | Pass | Heuristic | Fail | **Highest — reachability is cost-to-go** |

**No curiosity method earns a Safety pass** — the structural reason the constrained regime requires fundamentally different machinery rather than a tuned bonus. The single guarantee-pass method (DISCOVER) is also the one that *directs* exploration toward a goal, suggesting direction, not raw novelty, is the property that admits formal analysis. The highest transferability scores belong to reachability-based methods, confirming that the exploration bonus and the planning cost-to-go are converging on one learned object.

## 4 Count-Based and Pseudo-Count Bonuses

Count-based exploration gives intrinsic motivation a theoretical backbone, converting "visit rare states" into a quantity with regret bounds. The tabular recipe — bonus proportional to `1/√N(s)` — has provably near-optimal regret but collapses when states are images, where no two observations are identical. The modern arc is the story of reconstructing a usable novelty notion without an exact counter.

### 4.1 From Tabular Counts to High-Dimensional Pseudo-Counts

**Bellemare (2016)** showed a visit count can be extracted from any density model: if a model assigns ρ(s) before observing a state and ρ′(s) after, the implied **pseudo-count** is `N̂(s) = ρ(s)(1−ρ′(s))/(ρ′(s)−ρ(s))`, behaving exactly like a tabular count in the limit of a calibrated density model[Unifying Count-Based Exploration and Intrinsic Motivation](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.01868v2). This generalized provably-grounded count-based exploration to the non-tabular case for the first time, converting a hard-exploration agent's random walks into directed visitation of unseen Montezuma rooms.

**Ostrovski (2017)** answered which density model: PixelCNN's per-pixel autoregression supplies a far sharper pseudo-count than the simpler CTS model, dramatically improving the state of the art on hard Atari games[Count-Based Exploration with Neural Density Models](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01310v2). The tradeoff is real — better novelty discrimination arrives bundled with substantial per-step compute, since scoring a frame under an autoregressive model is expensive.

**Tang (2017, #Exploration)** took the opposite route: map each state to a short hash code via SimHash and maintain an ordinary hash table[#Exploration: Count-Based Exploration for Deep RL](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04717v3). This almost trivially simple scheme recovers much of the benefit of neural density models at orders-of-magnitude lower cost — states close in feature space map to the same bucket, so the bucket count approximates a continuous density. The hash code length exposes the central **count-fidelity-vs-scalability** dial. **Despite the field's drift toward elaborate neural density models, #Exploration showed most practical gain on standard continuous control came from hashing over simple features**, suggesting density-model sophistication was often overkill outside truly adversarial Atari titles.

### 4.2 Successor Representations and Coverage

**Machado (2020)** established a surprising identity: the *norm* of the learned successor representation (SR), while still being learned, serves as a novelty bonus[Count-Based Exploration with the Successor Representation AAAI 2020](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/5955). The SR encodes discounted expected future occupancy of every state under the current policy. **Its norm grows for states whose future occupancy is infrequent, so a large norm reads as high novelty** — opposite the naive "big vector means familiar" intuition. The self-extinguishing chain — rare state → large norm → large bonus → biased visitation → raised occupancy → contracted norm — mirrors `1/√N` decay without explicit counting, and is the cleanest justification for treating an SR norm as a legitimate count surrogate.

**Coverage-based novelty** generalizes from "how often visited" to "how much reachable space is covered nearby" — what a frontier explorer actually cares about. Go-Explore is the canonical realization: its defining insight is that **detaching the return phase from the explore phase solves the derailment that cripples bonus-driven agents**[First return, then explore](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919v6). The cell abstraction matured from hand-crafted (original) to learned latent (Cell-Free LGE 2022)[Cell-Free Latent Go-Explore](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14928v3) to reachability-time (Time-Myopic 2023)[Time-Myopic Go-Explore](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05635v1) to foundation-model-judged (Intelligent Go-Explore 2024)[Intelligent Go-Explore](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15143v4). The archive-of-cells structure is nearly identical to the frontier list classical robot explorers maintain, which is why diffusion-based planners like DARE — generating a complete exploration trajectory in one inference step — are already matching conventional frontier planners[DARE: Diffusion Policy for Autonomous Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.16687).

Every surrogate sits on a single **fidelity-vs-scalability axis**: the tabular counter has perfect fidelity and zero scalability; hashing sacrifices fidelity through collisions for O(1) cost; density models occupy the high-fidelity, high-cost end; the SR finds a middle ground by reusing an object maintained for value estimation. For trajectory planning the axis resolves toward scalability, because onboard planning operates under a hard real-time budget — why spatial-hash and occupancy-grid realizations dominate deployed robot exploration.

### 4.3 Optimism and the Regret Connection

The count bonus is **not a heuristic at all in the tabular limit — it is an exact UCB confidence radius.** A low visit count implies high epistemic uncertainty, which under optimism-in-the-face-of-uncertainty licenses value inflation, drawing the policy toward the state and collapsing the uncertainty that justified the optimism[Value Bonuses using Ensemble Errors for Exploration](http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12375v1). The count is the sufficient statistic determining the width of the optimistic confidence interval, and a UCB bonus `c/√N(s,a)` yields cumulative regret growing only as √steps, a worst-case-optimal rate.

**The load-bearing fact is that this guarantee depends entirely on the count being an honest sufficient statistic for uncertainty.** The moment a pseudo-count's relationship to true uncertainty is only approximate, the regret bound becomes aspirational. Bellemare proved pseudo-count consistency under a learning-positive density-model condition[Unifying Count-Based Exploration and Intrinsic Motivation](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.01868v2), but that condition is rarely verifiable for a deep density model. The paradox: **the methods with the strongest regret theory are weakest in the high-dimensional regimes where exploration is actually hard**, because tabular regret scales with state-action cardinality, which diverges in pixel spaces.

Everything that goes wrong in deep RL traces to function approximation breaking the count independence the bound assumes. First, **bonus vanishing**: updating one state's estimate generalizes to neighbors, so visiting a single novel state prematurely collapses the bonus across an entire under-explored region — the deep-network analogue of a hash collision. Second, **detachment/derailment**, which the archive-based fix resolves structurally. Third, **non-stationarity**: the online-trained bonus is a moving target that injects noise into the value function. **Despite the field's long treatment of count-based exploration as the theoretically safe choice, the evidence shows that in deep networks the pure bonus approach is often dominated by archive-based methods on exactly the hard-exploration problems it was supposed to solve.** The deployed pattern is hybrid: a discrete coverage archive for frontier bookkeeping plus a learned bonus for ranking frontiers[Deep RL-based Large-scale Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10833).

## 5 Goal-Conditioned, Curriculum, and Skill-Driven Exploration

Goal-conditioned exploration makes the agent its own teacher: instead of waiting for a rare extrinsic reward, the agent generates, selects, and pursues its own goals. **This is the family whose internal vocabulary — sub-goals, frontiers, reachability, cost-to-go — is already the vocabulary of motion planning**, making it the single most transferable family in the report.

### 5.1 Goal-Conditioned RL and Hindsight Relabeling

**HER (2017)** uses counterfactual relabeling: after an episode fails to reach goal g, the achieved final state is treated as if it had been the goal, turning a zero-reward failure into a success for a different task. This guarantees a constant fraction of every minibatch carries reward signal regardless of task difficulty — why it succeeds where unaugmented off-policy methods stall. Its principal limitation: **relabeling densifies reward along trajectories the agent already visits but provides no pressure to visit new regions.** HER maps cleanly onto the reachability cache of sampling-based planners — each relabeled goal is an empirically verified reachable waypoint, exactly the role RL-RRT assigns a learned policy[RL-RRT: Kinodynamic Motion Planning via Reachability Estimators](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.04799).

**UVFA (2015)** is the architectural substrate that makes relabeling learnable: it approximates `V(s, g)` jointly over states and goals, so experience pursuing one goal generalizes to many related goals[Continuous Goal Sampling for Automatic Curriculum Learning 2022](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Vk9RH9aL1Yv). Without this generalization relabeling merely adds isolated data points; with it, a successful reach informs the value of reaching every nearby state. The goal-conditioned value function is a learned, parametric **cost-to-go map** — the heuristic informed motion planners query, amortized into constant-time network queries.

**Automatic goal generation** turns a passive learner into a proactive one by selecting goals at the agent's frontier of competence using a learning-progress signal[Intrinsically Motivated Goal Exploration Processes IMGEP](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02190v3). The IMGEP framework formalizes this as autotelic learning — the agent as author of its own objectives. The continuous-goal-sampling acceleration (2022) resamples goals within episodes to enrich the curriculum[Continuous Goal Sampling for Automatic Curriculum Learning 2022](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Vk9RH9aL1Yv), and the toddler-inspired transition (2025) shifts reward density from sparse-and-exploratory to dense-and-exploitative as competence accrues[From Sparse to Dense: Toddler-inspired Reward Transition](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17842v1). A curriculum optimized purely for learning progress can starve the agent of rare hard goals, so practice blends progress-driven selection with a constant fraction of task-goal sampling.

These three components form **one closed loop**: a relabeling rule supplies data, a goal-conditioned value function generalizes it, a curriculum chooses where to gather more. The family's power comes from their interaction — HER without UVFA cannot generalize, UVFA without a curriculum cannot prioritize.

### 5.2 Frontier- and Planning-Based Sub-Goal Selection

This family most directly imports motion-planning machinery into RL.

**SIERL (2026)** chooses a sub-goal from the *frontier* — the boundary between known and unknown state space — prioritizing candidates by estimated cost-to-come and cost-to-go[Search Inspired Exploration in RL 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00460). This is a sharp departure from curiosity: SIERL commits to a globally chosen frontier sub-goal before the episode's task-directed exploration[Search Inspired Exploration in RL SIERL](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00460v1). Its cost-to-come/cost-to-go decomposition is grounded in provably complete search algorithms, lending it a search-theoretic justification absent from prediction-error methods. **SIERL's frontier sub-goal is byte-for-byte the frontier of frontier-based robotic exploration, and its prioritization is the A*-family evaluation function** — the most direct import of a planning primitive into RL in the report.

**PEG (2023)** asks a sharper question: rather than selecting a frontier geometrically, it learns a world-model, rolls candidate goals through it under the current policy, and commits to the goal whose rollout promises the most novel territory[Planning Goals for Exploration PEG, 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.13002). Where SIERL says "go to the edge of the known," PEG says "go to the goal that, when pursued, takes you somewhere new" — a prospective signal that subsumes the frontier criterion when the model is good. Its exploration quality is upper-bounded by world-model fidelity. **PEG is informative-path planning with a learned model: its rollout valuation is the expected-information-gain criterion**, and the exploration-via-planning result confirms this cuts samples 2× vs. strong baselines and 200× vs. random[Exploration via Planning for Information about the Optimal Trajectory](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/b90cb10d4dae058dd167388e76168c1b-Paper-Conference.pdf).

The **robotics analogy** is precise, not metaphorical: classical frontier exploration maintains an occupancy map, identifies boundary cells, and dispatches the robot — the RL analogue replaces the occupancy map with a learned state-visitation representation and the geometric frontier with a learned one. The DRL large-scale exploration result runs it the other direction, reducing path length 12%, makespan 6%, and planning time 60%[Deep RL-based Large-scale Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10833). RL-RRT[RL-RRT: Kinodynamic Motion Planning via Reachability Estimators](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.04799), dynamic-graph informative planning[Deep RL with Dynamic Graphs for Adaptive Informative Path Planning](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04894), and DARE[DARE: Diffusion Policy for Autonomous Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.16687) are each simultaneously RL methods and planning methods. The trade-off: replacing a geometric frontier with a learned one forfeits the completeness guarantee, which systems like RL-RRT recover by retaining a classical complete planner as the outer loop. **On Trajectory-Planning Transferability this family passes more decisively than any other — its primitives are already planning primitives.**

### 5.3 Skill Discovery and Hierarchical Exploration

When there is no goal to condition on, the agent must acquire a repertoire of reusable behaviors.

**DIAYN (2018)** maximizes the mutual information between a latent skill variable and visited states, so a discriminator can infer the active skill from state alone — forcing different skills into different state regions. This is intrinsic motivation of a structurally different kind: it rewards the diversity of the repertoire as a whole rather than per-timestep novelty. Its characteristic failure: **maximizing skill diversity does not guarantee usefulness**, so skills may be distinguishable yet behaviorally trivial. A discovered skill is a **motion primitive** — a reusable, temporally extended behavior a higher-level planner can sequence, exactly as a kinodynamic planner sequences pre-computed control primitives[Data-Efficient Learning of High-Quality Controls for Kinodynamic Planning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02254v1).

**Empowerment** generalizes from "make skills distinguishable" to "maximize controllable influence over the future" — the mutual information between actions and resulting future states. This is a contrarian framing: whereas curiosity rewards what the agent *cannot predict*, empowerment rewards what it *can control*, and the two can point in opposite directions, since a maximally unpredictable state carries high curiosity but near-zero empowerment. A high-empowerment state — one from which a large reachable set is accessible — is exactly the quantity a reachability-aware planner computes[PiP: Planning-informed Trajectory Prediction](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11476v2).

**Hierarchical temporal abstraction** lets discovered skills solve long-horizon tasks by factoring the policy into a slow high-level controller selecting skills and a fast low-level executor — compressing the credit-assignment horizon from thousands of primitive steps to dozens of skill selections. HiPER's plan-execute framework instantiates this for LLM agents with hierarchical advantage estimation[HiPER: Hierarchical RL for LLM Agents 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16165), and the PLDI 2026 neuro-symbolic framework converts natural-language tasks to PDDL and verifies high-level plans via SMT before execution[Neuro-symbolic Hierarchical Learning for Long-Horizon Robotic Tasks PLDI 2026](https://pldi26.sigplan.org/details/pldi-2026-papers/95/) — supplying the formal verification the rest of the family lacks. **The high-level/low-level factorization is the planning/control hierarchy of robotics under another name**, making verified-skill hierarchies the probable bridge between heuristic skill discovery and guaranteed planning.

No stage is sufficient alone: diverse skills without composition cannot solve long tasks, and composition without a well-grounded skill set inherits an impossible planning problem. The family's payoff depends on the alignment between what is discovered and what must be composed.

## 6 Model-Based, Optimistic World-Model, and Reward-Shaping Approaches

**The load-bearing claim is that model-based exploration collapses three historically separate levers — directed optimism, imagined rollout planning, and reward densification — into a single computational object: the learned world-model, whose predictions can be queried far more cheaply than the real environment.** Planning in a causal model is accurate but computationally demanding[When Does Model-Based Control Pay Off? PLOS Comp Biol](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1005090).

### 6.1 Optimistic World Models

**Optimistic World Models (2026)** augment a Dreamer- or STORM-class dynamics-learning objective with an "optimistic dynamics loss" biasing imagined transitions toward higher-reward outcomes[Optimistic World Models 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10044). The model is made to "day-dream" that unexplored doors lead to treasure, so the agent walks toward them and finds out. The strong, contestable claim is that this enables efficient exploration "without requiring explicit uncertainty estimates or constrained optimization" — folding both into one auxiliary loss, dispensing with the K-fold compute of an ensemble.

The apparent paradox is that decades of theory insist optimism must be tied to epistemic uncertainty, yet OWM claims to discard it. **The tension resolves by relocating uncertainty: a point-estimate dynamics model is implicitly least accurate where data is sparse, so a fixed optimistic bias has its largest effect in under-visited regions** — uncertainty enters through the back door of model error. But this holds only when model error correlates with visitation; in under-visited-but-smooth regions OWM's implicit signal vanishes. Optimistic Thompson Sampling shows the two postures can coexist within one posterior-sampling scheme[Optimistic Thompson Sampling](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04988v2), suggesting the durable position is hybrid. OWM's Theoretical Guarantees verdict is a qualified fail-relative-to-UCB: it sacrifices the regret bound, and recovering it appears to require re-importing the uncertainty machinery OWM set out to avoid.

### 6.2 Planning-Driven Exploration in Imagination

A single learned world-model can be queried thousands of times per real step, so the agent evaluates candidate behaviors in imagination and executes only the best. **The 200× sample reduction over random exploration[Exploration via Planning for Information about the Optimal Trajectory](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/b90cb10d4dae058dd167388e76168c1b-Paper-Conference.pdf) is the most striking efficiency number in this domain and quantifies why imagined planning, when the model is trustworthy, dominates undirected exploration by orders of magnitude.** The most principled signal is to explore where the model itself is uncertain — treating epistemic uncertainty as the target, because resolving it directly improves downstream planning accuracy, creating a virtuous loop absent from reward-only signals[Value Bonuses using Ensemble Errors for Exploration](http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12375v1). This is the variant most likely to carry a regret bound into deep-RL practice, since its signal is the exact quantity the witness-rank theory requires[Model-based RL in Contextual Decision Processes](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v99/sun19a/sun19a.pdf).

**The paradox is that the longer the imagined horizon — what makes imagination valuable for long-horizon sparse rewards — the more compounding model error corrupts the rollout, so the regime where imagination is most needed is exactly where it is least reliable.** Three partial mechanisms manage this: truncating rollouts and bootstrapping with a value (Dreamer), confidence-weighting imagined returns, and periodic re-grounding with real data — each shortening the effective horizon. Occupancy Reward Shaping offers an escape: rather than roll forward and compound error, it extracts the *occupancy measure* directly from a generative world-model, querying stationary geometry rather than sequential rollout[Occupancy Reward Shaping](https://pith.science/paper/2604.20627).

### 6.3 Reward Shaping and Densification

**Potential-based shaping** is the theoretical bedrock: if the shaping reward is the difference of a potential function across states, the optimal policy of the shaped problem is provably identical to the original. **This policy-invariance condition separates safe densification from reward hacking** — making the reward denser cannot change which behavior is optimal, only how fast it is found. Occupancy Reward Shaping realizes this by applying optimal transport to a generative model's occupancy measure, turning environment geometry into a dense reward that provably leaves the optimum unchanged[Occupancy Reward Shaping](https://pith.science/paper/2604.20627).

**SORS** (self-supervised online reward shaping) learns the shaping function online from a trajectory-ranking objective, claiming policy-invariance under stated conditions[Self-Supervised Online Reward Shaping SORS](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10301964). The 2026 semi-supervised method uses SSL and data augmentation on zero-reward transitions, achieving **up to twice the peak scores in sparse-reward environments versus supervised baselines, with the advantage widening as rewards grow sparser**[Shaping Sparse Rewards: A Semi-supervised Approach](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19128v4). The defining risk is **reward hacking**: a non-potential-based densified reward can introduce spurious optima. The tension is fundamental — the denser and more helpful a shaped reward, the more it encodes the designer's belief, which can diverge from the true objective. Restricting to potential-based form removes the risk at the price of expressiveness; learned methods manage rather than eliminate it; and the toddler-inspired schedule defers densification until coverage is adequate[From Sparse to Dense: Toddler-inspired Reward Transition](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17842v1).

Shaped reward maps onto **cost-field design** in planning: a shaped potential is functionally a navigation cost landscape, and turning reachability geometry into a goal-reaching signal is conceptually identical to the signed-distance-field and neural-potential-field methods that turn obstacle geometry into repulsive MPC costs[Predicted Composite Signed-Distance Fields for Real-Time Motion Planning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00969v2)[Dynamic Neural Potential Field](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.06819).

## 7 Safe and Efficient Exploration under Constraints

Safe exploration inverts the central premise of sparse-reward RL: where prior chapters probed aggressively, the CMDP regime treats large regions as forbidden, and exploration becomes gathering information without crossing a cost threshold. **The defining tension is that optimism drives the agent toward unvisited states to reduce uncertainty, while constraint respect demands pessimism about exactly those unknown states where cost could spike.** Recent work resolves this by splitting the optimism budget asymmetrically across reward and cost.

### 7.1 Lagrangian and Constrained Policy Optimization

Constrained policy optimization is a **spectrum parameterized by where the constraint is enforced.**

**CPO (Achiam 2017)** enforces the constraint at each policy update by solving a divergence-bounded optimization, the first method to deliver near-satisfaction throughout training[Constrained Policy Optimization CPO](https://mattlanders.net/cpo.html). The trust region lets the algorithm linearize both objective and constraint and bound worst-case step cost. It earns a guarantee pass for approximate monotonic-improvement and feasibility, but linearization permits bounded transient overshoot, falling short of true zero-violation. The second-order computation scales poorly to high-dimensional policies — acute near obstacle boundaries.

**Lagrangian primal-dual methods** convert the CMDP into a saddle-point problem: the policy ascends `L(π,λ) = reward − λ·(cost − d)` on a fast timescale while λ climbs slowly when the budget is exceeded. Simpler than CPO and scalable to deep policies, but **raw updates oscillate between over-cautious and unsafe regimes** before settling — which the PID-Lagrangian damps. The guarantee is asymptotic feasibility only, not during-learning.

**RCPO** folds the constraint into a penalized reward `r'(s,a) = r(s,a) − λ·c(s,a)`, letting any actor-critic optimize directly while a multiplier guarantees convergence to the constrained optimum[Self-Supervised Online Reward Shaping SORS](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10301964). Because the cost term is dense even when reward is sparse, RCPO partially mitigates the plateau problem, and its per-step shaping matches the cost-map representation MPC solvers use natively.

All three permit during-learning violation — the gap shielding closes by filtering actions, and optimistic-pessimistic methods close by constructing conservative cost estimates.

### 7.2 Shielding, Barrier, and Lyapunov Methods

Where §7.1 shapes the policy to be feasible in expectation, this family intervenes at action execution — a categorically stronger safety standard that physical planning requires.

**Shielding** wraps the agent in a correctness monitor that checks each action against a temporal-logic specification and substitutes a provably safe alternative[Safe Exploration with Backup Control Barrier Functions](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v283/rabiee25a.html). Because it only restricts the action set, any intrinsic bonus composes on top — the agent explores aggressively within the certified-safe frontier. It provides a genuine during-learning zero-violation guarantee, but only relative to its specification and model: **a shield promises provable safety, yet its guarantee is only as trustworthy as the hand-built model it relies on**, so airtight safety degrades to safety-modulo-model-error. Shield synthesis also scales poorly to high-dimensional continuous spaces.

**Control barrier functions (CBFs)** are the continuous-control answer: a differentiable certificate `h(x) ≥ 0` keeps the system in a safe set, with **forward invariance** guaranteeing it can never leave[Safe Exploration with Backup Control Barrier Functions](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v283/rabiee25a.html). The filter is a quadratic program solved at control frequency, minimizing deviation from the RL policy's proposed action — fast enough for real robots. The backup-CBF approach achieves zero training-time violations by certifying safety through a backup controller that provably returns to safe equilibrium[Safe Exploration with Backup Control Barrier Functions](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v283/rabiee25a.html). The QP can become infeasible when multiple obstacle barriers conflict — a real concern in cluttered planning. **The CBF-QP safety layer is the single most directly transferable construct in this chapter**, already deployed in real-time quadrotor and vehicle trajectory tracking.

**Lyapunov/reachability methods** certify a whole *region* — from which the agent can guarantee it will not be forced into violation. Lyapunov methods construct a forward-invariant sublevel set (a region of attraction); Hamilton-Jacobi reachability computes the certified region by backward-propagating unavoidable-violation states. These certify *recoverability over a horizon*, which matters when safety requires planning ahead. But exact HJ reachability suffers the curse of dimensionality, scaling exponentially and historically confined to ≤5 states. The central tension: exact methods are provably safe but do not scale; scalable learned-Lyapunov methods are only heuristically safe. **Reachability is the theoretical foundation of the RL-RRT reachability estimator** — the same value function that certifies safety in RL predicts time-to-reach for steering in RRT[RL-RRT: Kinodynamic Motion Planning via Reachability Estimators](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.04799).

The strongest safety guarantees come not from shaping the policy but from filtering its actions against a geometrically defined safe set, differing in granularity: shielding enumerates discrete safe actions, CBFs certify instantaneous invariance, reachability certifies recoverability over a horizon. Their shared weakness is dependence on a known model — which the optimistic-pessimistic methods relax.

### 7.3 Optimistic-Pessimistic Constrained Exploration (2022–2026)

The defining advance is treating reward and cost uncertainty *asymmetrically*: optimistic about reward to drive exploration, pessimistic about cost to preserve safety.

**DOPE** proves a single algorithm achieves sublinear regret and zero violation during learning *simultaneously*: an objective regret of `Õ(|S|√(|A|K))` with **zero constraint violation throughout learning**, by combining an optimistic reward bonus with a pessimistic cost constraint[DOPE: Doubly Optimistic and Pessimistic Exploration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00885). The pessimistic estimate inflates the cost of unvisited states, confining exploration to a certified-feasible region (yielding zero violation), while the optimistic bonus inflates the value of those same states, driving the agent to visit them and shrink cost uncertainty (yielding sublinear regret). **This two-pronged optimism-for-reward, pessimism-for-cost construction is the conceptual core the entire 2022–2026 cluster elaborates.** Its limitation is tabular: the bound depends on a finite enumerable state space. DOPE's pessimistic cost frontier is the formal justification for the uncertainty-scaled obstacle buffer conservative planners add.

**MICE** targets cost underestimation — where the cost model is optimistically wrong about an unvisited state and walks into an unpredicted violation. Its **intrinsic cost bonus is the mirror image of an intrinsic reward**, adding a cost penalty proportional to the epistemic uncertainty of the cost estimate, so poorly-known states are treated as expensive until visited enough. This restores DOPE's pessimism in the function-approximation regime where tabular confidence sets break. The calibration tension is the cost-side echo of exploration-exploitation: too large and the agent is paralyzed, too small and underestimation resurfaces. **COX-Q** resolves reward-cost gradient conflicts in action space and adapts the trust region using truncated quantile critics[Off-Policy Safe RL with Cost-Constrained Optimistic Exploration COX-Q](https://openreview.net/forum?id=EHs3tSukHC), a more surgical version of the blunt multiplier; **SBSRL** eliminates explicit bonuses entirely, providing high-probability safety throughout learning and a finite-time sample-complexity bound under regularity conditions[Sampling-Based Safe RL SBSRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19469v1).

## 8 Theoretical Guarantees, Benchmarks, and Evaluation

The methods with the strongest empirical results on hard-exploration Atari rarely carry formal guarantees; the methods with clean regret bounds rarely scale to pixels. The field advances by narrowing, not eliminating, that gap.

### 8.1 Regret and Constraint-Violation Bounds

The cleanest theoretical results come from model-based tabular and linear settings. Sun et al. (2019) formalized the model-based advantage through the **witness rank**, proving it is never larger than the Bellman rank governing model-free OLIVE — implying **model-based global exploration can require exponentially fewer samples than model-free**[Model-based RL in Contextual Decision Processes](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v99/sun19a/sun19a.pdf). The model-based learner fits a transition model, identifies under-determined regions, and directs exploration there, collapsing the posterior faster than reward-driven updates could. RND carries no global regret bound but a *local* consistency guarantee: its bonus provably decays toward zero on repeatedly-visited states[Exploration by Random Network Distillation](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.12894).

Constraint-respecting RL adds a second ledger. The primal-dual CMDP analysis attains **Õ(√K) reward regret and Õ(√K) constraint violation** simultaneously, without trading one off against the other[Learning Policies with Zero or Bounded Constraint Violation for CMDPs](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2106.02684v3.pdf). The striking refinement: if a strictly-feasible policy is known in advance, **violations can be driven to zero with high probability while preserving Õ(√K) regret**[Learning Policies with Zero or Bounded Constraint Violation for CMDPs](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2106.02684v3.pdf) — the feasibility anchor converts an average constraint into a per-episode reachability guarantee. But this requires a strictly-safe policy a priori, exactly the artifact hardest to obtain in high-dimensional domains. Most provable safe-RL results further model the constraint as a *linear* cost function[Safe RL with Linear Approximation Amani 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/amani21a/amani21a.pdf), which makes confidence-set construction tractable but bounds applicability to linear-realizable hazards.

| Bonus-decay | Deep intrinsic reward | Bonus → 0 on visited states | Predictor convergence | RND[Exploration by Random Network Distillation](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.12894) |

**The honest verdict: the bounds hold under assumptions deep-RL systems routinely violate** — tabular or linearly-realizable dynamics, known feasible policies, bounded witness rank[Exploration in Deep RL survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00824). Neural function approximation breaks the closed-form confidence sets, forcing heuristic uncertainty estimates like ensemble disagreement, which pushes evaluation away from regret certificates toward benchmark leaderboards. The first method to carry both a non-trivial regret bound and a top-tier Montezuma score will reset the field's evaluation standard.

### 8.2 Benchmark Environments

Each benchmark family isolates a specific failure mode aggregate reward curves would hide.

**Hard-exploration discrete.** Montezuma's Revenge and Pitfall are canonical because reward is sparse and *deceptive* — greedy following leads away from the goal[Go-Explore: a New Approach for Hard-Exploration Problems](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10995v4). The Go-Explore Nature result reports superhuman scores, establishing them as the de facto bar[First return, then explore](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919v6). MiniGrid is the complementary testbed for pseudo-count methods because it withholds the true counts that would trivialize the problem[LECO: Learnable Episodic Count 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.05409). Through 2027 Montezuma likely transitions from open challenge to *regression test*.

**Constrained continuous.** Safety Gym augments control tasks with explicit cost signals, decoupling task return from cumulative cost so a method achieving high reward by violating constraints is visibly distinguished[A Multiplicative Value Function for Safe and Efficient RL](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04118v1). **Only methods evaluated on cost-instrumented benchmarks can substantiate a Safety pass** — the criterion is unfalsifiable on benchmarks without a cost channel. The optimism that wins on Montezuma is exactly the posture Safety Gym penalizes — the empirical face of the regret-versus-violation tension.

**Embodied testbeds** are where exploration and planning meet most directly, reporting *planning* metrics. Large-scale robot exploration reports 12% path-length, 6% makespan, and 60% planning-time reductions[Deep RL-based Large-scale Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10833); PiP achieves SOTA multi-agent forecasting in a coupled prediction-planning loop[PiP: Planning-informed Trajectory Prediction](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11476v2); DARE generates a complete trajectory in one inference step while generalizing to real-world tests[DARE: Diffusion Policy for Autonomous Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.16687). By 2028 these will likely become the *primary* arena for joint exploration-and-constraint evaluation, since only embodied tasks expose coverage, cost, and planning-time simultaneously.

### 8.3 Performance Criteria

Progress is measured along three non-substitutable axes — sample efficiency, constraint-violation rate, robustness/scalability — and no current method dominates all three. The model-based world-model entity leads on guaranteed sample efficiency (witness-rank improvement); the Lagrangian and barrier entities lead on safety (Õ(√K) / zero-violation); the goal-conditioned entity leads on empirical hard-exploration coverage (superhuman Montezuma); and **the optimistic-pessimistic family is the only one that refuses to sacrifice one axis for another** — the structural hinge between the report's two threads.

Robustness and generalization are hardest to certify: stochastic reward machines extend the formalism to noisy rewards because existing algorithms assumed a noise-free idealization[RL with Stochastic Reward Machines](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14837v1), and DARE's single-inference generalization to real-world tests is the strongest transfer datapoint[DARE: Diffusion Policy for Autonomous Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.16687).

## 9 Comparative Synthesis Across Both Regimes

The exploration literature is organized by a **two-by-two of regime (sparse vs. constrained) crossed with rigor (heuristic vs. principled).** The cells are unevenly populated: sparse-heuristic and constrained-principled are dense, sparse-principled and constrained-heuristic are thin. The empty diagonals are the research frontier.

| Family | Mechanism | Regime | Guarantee | Planning Primitive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intrinsic curiosity | prediction-error reward | sparse | heuristic | frontier attraction |
| Count/pseudo-count | density-model bonus | sparse | near-bound (tabular) | sampling density |
| Goal-conditioned | sub-goal generation | sparse | heuristic | sub-goal tree |
| Model-based / OWM | epistemic uncertainty | sparse | regret bound (some) | reachability estimate |
| Reward shaping | learned dense proxy | sparse | heuristic | cost-to-go field |
| Skill discovery | mutual-information option | sparse | heuristic | macro-action |
| Proactive switching | coordinated mode switch | both | mixed | phase scheduler |
| CMDP policy opt | primal-dual cost bound | constrained | violation bound | cost constraint |
| Safe exploration | forward invariance | constrained | hard safety | barrier field |
| Optimistic-pessimistic | optimism+pessimism | both | regret+violation bound | risk-aware sampling |

**The most empirically successful sparse-reward methods sit entirely on the heuristic side** — Go-Explore's superhuman Montezuma scores carry no regret guarantee[Go-Explore: a New Approach for Hard-Exploration Problems](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10995v4). The constraint column is disproportionately principled because safety certificates are the entire point of the CMDP formulation; the sparse column is disproportionately heuristic because the best empirical methods happen to lack proofs. This correlation between the two axes is the deep reason the literatures evolved separately.

### 9.1 The Heuristic-vs-Guaranteed Trade-off Resolves by Decomposition

**The SIERL critique** indicts heuristic curiosity: prediction-error methods optimize a proxy (novelty) only loosely correlated with task reward, so the policy can converge to a stable suboptimum where novelty is exhausted but the objective is unmet[Search Inspired Exploration in RL SIERL](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00460v1). In stochastic environments, the noisy-TV trap permanently captures a prediction-error agent[Self-Supervised Exploration via Disagreement](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04161v1). **But the critique applies to signal-chasing heuristics and spares memory-based ones**: Go-Explore's archive-and-return stores ground-truth states rather than chasing a signal, which is why the empirical record (superhuman Montezuma) coexists with the suboptimality critique.

**The guaranteed camp pays for certificates in restrictive assumptions** — known cost models, bounded approximation error, linear-MDP structure. The tighter and more honest the CMDP guarantee, the narrower the class of environments where its assumptions hold. The resolution is the optimism-on-reward, pessimism-on-cost decomposition: optimism handles the sparse-reward discovery problem while pessimism handles constraint satisfaction, and the two signals do not interfere because they are computed separately[Off-Policy Safe RL with Cost-Constrained Optimistic Exploration COX-Q](https://openreview.net/forum?id=EHs3tSukHC).

**The routing rule:** when a violation is catastrophic and irreversible, choose a guaranteed method and pay the assumption tax; when a failed episode is a wasted simulation rollout, choose a heuristic method and reap the scalability. The rule collapses in the middle band — exploration in an unknown physical environment, where reward is sparse and constraints are active — because the two pure camps give contradictory commands and only a decomposed method can obey both.

### 9.2 Integrating Sparse-Reward and Constraint Pressures

The **reward-discovery-vs-constraint-satisfaction trade-off** is genuine: aggressive exploration finds reward faster but drives toward the constraint boundary, while conservative exploration respects the constraint but may never discover the reward[Tiered Reward](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03733v3). Naively adding a constraint penalty freezes exploration early when reward is undiscovered; COX-Q resolves the gradient conflict geometrically rather than by a fixed scalar weight[Off-Policy Safe RL with Cost-Constrained Optimistic Exploration COX-Q](https://openreview.net/forum?id=EHs3tSukHC).

**Proactive information gathering under risk** is the bridge to planning: planning to maximize expected information gain about the optimal trajectory cuts samples 2× vs. strong baselines and 200× vs. random[Exploration via Planning for Information about the Optimal Trajectory](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/b90cb10d4dae058dd167388e76168c1b-Paper-Conference.pdf). The viewpoint-recommendation pipeline is the cleanest instantiation — an RL policy handles the information objective while a receding-horizon optimizer handles the collision constraint[Where to Look Next: Learning Viewpoint Recommendations](https://autonomousrobots.nl/assets/files/publications/22-lodel-icra.pdf), reproducing the two-signal decomposition at the planning layer.

No single family certifies efficiency, safety, *and* feasibility at once. The emerging resolution is a **layered architecture**: stack a sample-efficient explorer (information gain) on a safe planner (collision-free guarantee), letting each layer own its certificate and communicate through the trajectory interface. The loop closes — the explorer proposes where to look, the optimizer produces a feasible path, execution reduces uncertainty, the reduced uncertainty feeds the next decision[Where to Look Next: Learning Viewpoint Recommendations](https://autonomousrobots.nl/assets/files/publications/22-lodel-icra.pdf).

| Integration dimension | Sparse contribution | Constraint contribution | Unifying device | Open gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Efficiency | info-gain planning (2×/200×) | trust-region sizing | optimistic signal | scaling to high-dim |
| Safety | — | violation bound (SBSRL, COX-Q) | pessimistic signal | learned cost field |
| Feasibility | reachability estimate | pseudo-optimal safe class | trajectory interface | **no joint proof** |

Efficiency and safety each have a mature contribution and a unifying signal; **feasibility remains the column with no joint proof — the single most important open gap.**

## 10 Mapping RL Concepts to Trajectory-Planning Elements

Every element of a trajectory-planning problem has a named counterpart in the exploration machinery.

| RL element | Planning counterpart | Bridge evidence |
|---|---|---|
| State / archive cell | Configuration / map cell | Latent Go-Explore[Cell-Free Latent Go-Explore](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14928v3) |
| Action / control | Kinodynamic control | RL-RRT[RL-RRT: Kinodynamic Motion Planning via Reachability Estimators](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.04799), kinodynamic controls[Data-Efficient Learning of High-Quality Controls for Kinodynamic Planning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02254v1) |
| Reward / value | Path cost / information gain | IPP objective[IRIS: Information Path Planning via RL and Information-Directed Sampling](https://www.scienced), viewpoint reward[Where to Look Next: Learning Viewpoint Recommendations](https://autonomousrobots.nl/assets/files/publications/22-lodel-icra.pdf) |
| CMDP constraint | Collision / energy budget | flight-time budget |
| Epistemic uncertainty | Belief-space / active sensing | ensemble value bonus[Value Bonuses using Ensemble Errors for Exploration](http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12375v1), info-gain[Exploration via Planning for Information about the Optimal Trajectory](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/b90cb10d4dae058dd167388e76168c1b-Paper-Conference.pdf) |
| Frontier sub-goal | Frontier waypoint | SF landmarks, frontier exploration |

**The cleanest single mapping is CMDP-constraint-to-collision-budget**: both are expected-cost bounds on a physical resource, and the Lagrangian dual that prices a CMDP constraint is formally the shadow price a receding-horizon controller assigns to its constraint margin. Successor Feature Landmarks make the frontier bridge rigorous — the method executes plans to "frontier" landmarks at the edge of explored space, literally using the robotics term. HER's relabeling — manufacturing dense signal from sparse goal achievement — is the learning-side answer to the same sparsity that makes long-horizon informative path planning hard[IRIS: Information Path Planning via RL and Information-Directed Sampling](https://www.scienced)[Deep RL with Dynamic Graphs for Adaptive Informative Path Planning](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04894).

### 10.1 Sparse-Reward Insights for Planning

**Intrinsic reward → exploration-aware objectives.** The sharpest quantitative transfer: an intrinsic-motivation objective re-cast as information about the optimal trajectory buys a 2× sample reduction vs. tuned baselines and 200× vs. random[Exploration via Planning for Information about the Optimal Trajectory](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/b90cb10d4dae058dd167388e76168c1b-Paper-Conference.pdf). The caution from multi-agent work — unreliable intrinsic reward can prevent convergence[Exploration with Unreliable Intrinsic Reward in Multi-Agent RL](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02138v1) — warns that an information-gain objective on a poorly calibrated uncertainty estimate routes toward phantom information, which the ensemble-error value bonus specifically suppresses[Value Bonuses using Ensemble Errors for Exploration](http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12375v1).

**Goal-conditioned RL → long-horizon navigation.** The decisive result is the DRL reactive planner cutting path length 12%, makespan 6%, and planning time 60% while implicitly predicting unknown areas from a learned transition model[Deep RL-based Large-scale Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10833). The design choice — explicit landmark graph (replannable, per-edge cost) vs. one-shot diffusion policy (fast, no mid-trajectory recourse[DARE: Diffusion Policy for Autonomous Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.16687)) — favors landmarks for safety-critical long-horizon navigation and diffusion for coverage sweeps. The likely 2028 stack is hybrid: a learned landmark graph for global sub-goal proposal feeding a fast reactive policy for local traversal.

**World-models → adaptive trajectory optimization.** Composite signed-distance fields are generated in **81–97% less time** than exact SDF calculation, enabling a 7-DoF Panda arm to avoid moving obstacles in real time via GPMP2[Predicted Composite Signed-Distance Fields for Real-Time Motion Planning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00969v2). The world-model's value here is compute *compression*, not accuracy. The Dynamic Neural Potential Field predicts footprint-aware repulsive potentials for an MPC quadratic program, producing safer trajectories than static baselines in dynamic scenes[Dynamic Neural Potential Field](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.06819).

### 10.2 Constraint-Aware Insights for Safe Planning

**Shielding/CBF → collision and kinodynamic handling.** Shielding and reachability estimation are the same operation viewed from opposite ends: shielding blocks the action that leaves the safe set, the reachability estimator certifies the action that stays inside it, and **RL-RRT shows the certification can be learned rather than computed from first principles**[RL-RRT: Kinodynamic Motion Planning via Reachability Estimators](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.04799). Kinodynamic control learning biases waypoint sampling toward reachable states, yielding higher-quality paths[Data-Efficient Learning of High-Quality Controls for Kinodynamic Planning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02254v1).

**Risk-sensitive exploration → belief-space routing.** The contrarian claim: risk-sensitive policies explore *more* efficiently than risk-neutral ones, because the dynamic risk measure steers away from high-variance regions a risk-neutral policy wastes samples on. Transposed, a CVaR belief-space planner both avoids unsafe trajectories and reaches its goal with fewer detours. The viewpoint-recommendation planner demonstrates the belief-reduction payoff, beating greedy next-best-view and MCTS in a receding-horizon loop[Where to Look Next: Learning Viewpoint Recommendations](https://autonomousrobots.nl/assets/files/publications/22-lodel-icra.pdf).

**Energy/time/budget → CMDP constraints.** A UAV flight-time budget is a CMDP expected-cost constraint in everything but name; the online informative path planner respecting it outperforms 3D-coverage and random exploration on both reconstruction error and information metrics. Planning the information-gathering path *against* the budget — rather than covering blindly then truncating — dissolves the apparent coverage-vs-quality dichotomy by allocating every remaining unit of flight time to its highest-information use. A budget-bounded receding-horizon planner can borrow the CMDP trick of pricing remaining budget as a depleting Lagrange multiplier.

### 10.3 Deployment Considerations

| Transfer | Measured gain | Principal risk |
|---|---|---|
| Info-objective planning[Exploration via Planning for Information about the Optimal Trajectory](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/b90cb10d4dae058dd167388e76168c1b-Paper-Conference.pdf) | 2× / 200× sample cut | needs differentiable dynamics |
| Learned-model exploration[Deep RL-based Large-scale Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10833) | 12% path, 60% time | model error off-distribution |
| Composite SDF + GPMP2[Predicted Composite Signed-Distance Fields for Real-Time Motion Planning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00969v2) | 81–97% compute cut | SDF approximation error |
| RL-RRT reachability[RL-RRT: Kinodynamic Motion Planning via Reachability Estimators](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.04799) | faster kinodynamic | no invariance certificate |
| Budget-bounded IPP | dominates coverage/random | per-step dual compute |
| Diffusion one-shot explorer[DARE: Diffusion Policy for Autonomous Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.16687) | parity, faster inference | no mid-trajectory recourse |

**The benefit is almost always a compute-or-sample saving at roughly equal safety, not a safety improvement at equal compute** — meaning the RL transfer pays off most where the incumbent planner is compute-bound. The learned component is consistently placed at the compute bottleneck (SDF evaluation, reachability check, information objective), so the amortized training cost is repaid by per-query savings — provided the deployment query distribution resembles training.

Sim-to-real and partial observability are the two principal risks. DARE's single-shot generation is double-edged: it generalizes across the sim-real gap because it never commits to a brittle per-step estimate, but lacks mid-trajectory recourse[DARE: Diffusion Policy for Autonomous Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.16687). Partial observability is where the sparse-reward literature issues its bluntest warning — deep RL under sparse reward *and* partial observability is doubly hard[Dealing with Sparse Rewards using Graph Neural Networks](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2203.13424v1.pdf), and a belief-space planner importing a value estimate trained under full observability will mis-estimate the belief at deployment. **The safest deployment pattern is a learned proposal wrapped in a classical verifier:** the RL component proposes waypoints, a model-based receding-horizon optimizer certifies feasibility against the physical dynamics before execution[Where to Look Next: Learning Viewpoint Recommendations](https://autonomousrobots.nl/assets/files/publications/22-lodel-icra.pdf).

## 11 Meta-Causal Synthesis and Forward Verdict

The two exploration regimes do not act independently — they **share a common failure geometry centered on the frontier of the explored set**, where novelty is highest, cost is most uncertain, and estimation bias is most consequential.

**Coupling paths.** Reward sparsity *amplifies* the cost of safe exploration: it lengthens the uninformed traversal to the frontier, and every uninformed step under maximal uncertainty is a potential violation[Environmental Features in Sparse-Reward Deep RL, JAIR 2023](https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/14390/26919/34080). Under severe sparsity, a Lagrangian multiplier either collapses exploration (conservative) or permits runaway violation (permissive), because the disciplining reward gradient is absent[From Sparse to Dense: Toddler-inspired Reward Transition](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17842v1). Demonstration-seeded warm starts shorten the uninformed phase and shrink the window in which the multiplier operates blind[RL with Sparse Rewards using Guidance from Offline Demonstrations](https://openreview.net/forum?id=YJ1WzgMVsMt). Constraints *prune* what novelty-seeking can probe: the most novel states sit nearest the constraint frontier, because rarely-visited and rarely-permitted are correlated[First return, then explore](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919v6). **The frontier sub-goal must, in the constrained case, be redefined as the nearest under-explored state that is also collision-free** — exactly the bridge mapping safe-RL cost frontiers onto configuration-space obstacles. Estimation biases *compound*: a cost estimator underestimating (MICE-style) reports the constraint as slack, relaxing the multiplier, permitting deeper pushes that generate exactly the high-cost transitions the underestimate failed to anticipate. The dominant safe-exploration failure through 2026 traces to cost-estimator underestimation rather than reward-estimator error, because cost signals are typically sparser and more delayed.

**Maturity tiers track regime-decoupling, not regime-coverage.**

| Tier | Families | Passing | Blocking |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mature | Count/pseudo-count bonuses, prediction-error curiosity, CPO/Lagrangian | Mechanism Clarity, Sample Efficiency, asymptotic Safety | Coupling — fails when regimes interact |
| Partial | Optimistic world-models, Go-Explore lineage, value-bonus ensembles | Sample Efficiency, Scalability (improving) | Theoretical Guarantees at the frontier |
| Open | Guaranteed safe exploration at scale, unified RL-planning | Aspirational | Scalable guarantee across the fusion boundary |

A method is mature where it can *ignore* the other regime, partial where it handles the other regime heuristically, open where it must guarantee both at once. The open tier is a *connected* frontier: the missing scalable safe-exploration guarantee is precisely what makes the unified framework hard, because a unified framework must carry the guarantee across the fusion boundary. SCALAR and the neuro-symbolic novelty-adaptation architecture are real advances but couple LLM planning and RL as communicating modules rather than fusing them.

**The forward verdict: a role-partitioned hybrid.** The future of trajectory planning under sparse reward and constraints is neither RL replacing planning nor planning resisting RL, but a hybrid in which **RL owns exploration and prediction, a certified classical layer owns constraint guarantees, and a shared representation owns the interface.** Independently motivated lines converge on this: RL-RRT embeds a learned policy in a sampling-based tree[RL-RRT: Kinodynamic Motion Planning via Reachability Estimators](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.04799), the DRL reactive planner beats baselines on planning-native metrics[Deep RL-based Large-scale Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10833), and the Neural Potential Field and composite-SDF results inject learned predictions into classical optimizers in real time[Dynamic Neural Potential Field](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.06819)[Predicted Composite Signed-Distance Fields for Real-Time Motion Planning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00969v2).

External calibration is favorable but conditional. DRL planners beat sampling-based planners on success rate and planning time for industrial arms, but the advantage is conditional on sim-to-real transfer: first-order Markovian policies transfer directly at high inference frequency, while higher-order policies require fine-tuning. The design consequence — constrain the RL component to first-order Markovian structure where the task permits, raise inference frequency before adding policy order.

**Transfer becomes reliable when three conditions hold jointly:** (1) the RL component is confined to exploration and prediction rather than end-to-end control; (2) a certified classical layer carries the hard constraint guarantees; (3) the cost/collision estimator is calibrated at the frontier. When any fails, the coupling pathologies resurface. A further representational condition sharpens reliability: transfer is reliable when the RL policy operates over a representation — a dynamic graph, a learned latent — that the planner can also consume[Deep RL with Dynamic Graphs for Adaptive Informative Path Planning](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04894)[PiP: Planning-informed Trajectory Prediction](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11476v2), so the exploration output is directly actionable rather than requiring translation. **The bottleneck in RL-to-planning transfer is less often policy quality than the representational impedance between explorer and optimizer.**

The narrow but load-bearing open question: whether frontier cost calibration can be achieved in high-dimensional continuous control *without a known dynamics model*, because every method that currently calibrates the frontier well assumes either a model or low-dimensional structure. That single question gates the migration of the open tier into the mature tier.

## 12 Open Challenges

Three structural gaps dominate the RL-exploration/planning intersection through 2030.

**Scalability in high-dimensional spaces.** Sparse-reward machinery fails in high dimensions for three independent reasons, and patching one inherits the other two. *Bonus degeneracy*: count-based episodic terms generalize poorly to large state spaces, flattening the bonus landscape until it no longer distinguishes informative frontier states. **Despite the prevailing view that richer latent metrics are the route to scalable bonuses, the evidence implies the opposite — removing the count-based episodic scaling factor altogether matters more than refining the norm it multiplies**. Policy-Aware Matrix Completion offers a partial escape via low-rank reward factorization, reporting 4100±250 on Montezuma's Revenge against DrQ-v2's 200±50 at ~8% overhead[Policy-Aware Matrix Completion for Sparse-Reward RL 2025](https://openreview.net/forum?id=z8zKRDO2pB). *Credit-assignment blow-up*: the 2025 low-rank reward result proves sample complexity moves from exponential to polynomial under a low-rank assumption[What Fundamental Structure Enables Efficient Sparse-Reward Learning? 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03790v1), but no one has shown long-horizon planning rewards actually possess that structure — the theorem is a conditional guarantee awaiting empirical validation. Spark's uncertainty-triggered branching[Spark: Strategic Policy-Aware Exploration via Dynamic Branching 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20209) and BPO's data-curation flywheel[Beyond Policy Optimization: A Data Curation Flywheel 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03018v2) supply complementary credit-assignment fixes. *Planning compute*: model-based exploration buys its 200× sample efficiency[Exploration via Planning for Information about the Optimal Trajectory](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/b90cb10d4dae058dd167388e76168c1b-Paper-Conference.pdf) with a per-step planning loop that dominates wall-clock time, which amortization addresses — DARE pushing it to a single inference step with constant latency in horizon[DARE: Diffusion Policy for Autonomous Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.16687).

**Safety and theory-practice gaps.** The theory-practice gap is a composition of three gaps — the *assumption gap* (bounds proven for tabular/linear MDPs say nothing reliable about million-parameter networks[Model-based Safe Deep RL via Constrained PPO 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/9a8eb202c060b7d81f5889631cbcd47e-Paper-Conference.pdf)), the *observability gap* (a constraint over full state cannot be enforced when the agent observes only a partial projection), and the *dynamics gap* (contact modeling is the hardest physics to simulate, and a violation bound degrades by exactly the amount the contact model is wrong). **A guarantee survives deployment only if robust to all three simultaneously**, which is why distributionally robust chance constraints — holding against a family of distributions rather than one nominal model — outperform point-estimate guarantees. On a physical robot, manifold projection restricts exploration to a provably safe set from the first interaction, while PCPO's preemptive penalty deliberately explores up to the boundary via a constraint-aware intrinsic reward[Proactive Constrained Policy Optimization with Preemptive Penalty 2026](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/39978); the likely 2028 paradigm is hybrid — projection for the analytically-known core, preemptive penalty for the learned periphery. PAC-Bayesian bounds, applying to stochastic predictors without a realizability assumption, are the most plausible route to guarantees surviving the transition to deep networks[PAC-Bayesian Analysis of the Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off 2011](http://arxiv.org/abs/1105.4585v1).

**The benchmark vacuum.** The field's deepest deficit is the absence of benchmarks directly testing RL exploration on planning problems. Learning methods are evaluated on planning-native metrics (path length, makespan, collision rate) while exploration methods are evaluated on RL-native metrics (return, sample count), and **no shared benchmark reports both**[Motion Planning in Dynamic Environments: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02677). The raw material exists in scattered form[Deep RL-based Large-scale Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10833)[Deep RL with Dynamic Graphs for Adaptive Informative Path Planning](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04894)[RL-RRT: Kinodynamic Motion Planning via Reachability Estimators](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.04799). **The single highest-leverage contribution the community could make by 2027 is a standardized exploration-to-planning transfer benchmark that runs every method family on the same navigation and manipulation tasks and reports all six rubric criteria** — because until such a suite exists, every transferability claim in this report remains partly conjectural. The probability such a benchmark emerges from the motion-planning community rather than the RL community is high, because the planning community already maintains the task suites and planning-native metrics and needs only to add the RL-native ones.

The richest near-term opportunities lie in the connective tissue: risk-aware informative path planning (RL viewpoint selection + UKF uncertainty propagation + distributionally robust constraints[Where to Look Next: Learning Viewpoint Recommendations](https://autonomousrobots.nl/assets/files/publications/22-lodel-icra.pdf)[Risk Bounded Nonlinear Robot Motion Planning](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.01483)[Safe Motion Planning in Unknown Environments](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.05804)), multi-objective integration (learned cost predictor + EIPO-style adaptive exploration weight that scales the intrinsic bonus to zero when extrinsic reward suffices[Redeeming Intrinsic Rewards via Constrained Optimization 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.07627)[Predicted Composite Signed-Distance Fields for Real-Time Motion Planning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00969v2)), and bidirectional planning-informed prediction[PiP: Planning-informed Trajectory Prediction](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11476v2). **The intersection is not blocked by a missing algorithm but by a missing benchmark — the hybrid architectures already exist in isolation, and what prevents their systematic comparison and improvement is the absence of the shared transfer benchmark.**
